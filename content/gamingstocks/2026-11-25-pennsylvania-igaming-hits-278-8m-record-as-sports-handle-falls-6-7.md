## What happened

Pennsylvania's regulator reported on Nov. 17 that October gaming revenue reached [$634,409,091, the highest single month on record](https://gamingcontrolboard.pa.gov/news-and-transparency/press-release/october-revenue-hits-all-time-high). That was up 6.27% from October 2025 and topped the prior high of $625.5 million set in May 2026.

The more useful detail is where the money came from. iGaming (online slots, tables and poker) produced $278.8 million, up 11.05% and a monthly record. The previous iGaming high was $259.7 million in December 2025. Online slots alone were $218.1 million, up 14.26%, also a record.

Sports betting told a different story. Revenue rose 7.30% to $65.1 million, but the amount wagered fell. The Pennsylvania Gaming Control Board (PGCB) put October handle at $903.0 million, [down 6.67% from $968.5 million a year earlier](https://gamingcontrolboard.pa.gov/news-and-transparency/press-release/october-revenue-hits-all-time-high). The October 2025 figure had been [an all-time high for the state](https://gamingcontrolboard.pa.gov/node/663039), so this is a comparison against a hard month.

## Revenue up on less handle

Handle is what bettors stake. Revenue is what the sportsbooks keep after paying winners. When revenue rises while handle falls, the gap is hold, the share of each dollar wagered that operators retain.

The PGCB release does not state a hold rate. Dividing its own revenue figure by its own handle figure gives roughly 7.2% for October 2026 against roughly 6.3% a year earlier. Those are my calculations from the two releases, not a published statistic, and they are only a rough guide. Still, the direction is clear: Pennsylvania sportsbooks kept more of a smaller pile.

That matters because hold is the least repeatable part of the business. Results on the field, and how many bettors happen to pick favorites or parlay long shots, swing it from month to month. Investors who watch DraftKings (DKNG) and Flutter (FLUT), the parent of FanDuel, tend to focus on handle growth and on how much of it converts to durable margin. A month in which revenue grows because of favorable outcomes, not rising wagers, is a weaker signal than one where both move together.

For context, September's sportsbook revenue in Pennsylvania was [$44.7 million, up 50.45%](https://gamingcontrolboard.pa.gov/sites/default/files/2026-10/101926_Sept%20Revenue.pdf), a large swing that also tells you how volatile single months can be. The September release did not include handle.

## The online casino is doing the heavy lifting

Online casino is now the larger engine. By my arithmetic from the PGCB numbers, iGaming was about 44% of October's total gaming revenue, against about 10% for sports wagering.

Tax follows the same pattern. The PGCB reported [iGaming tax of $127.5 million and sports wagering tax of $23.4 million](https://gamingcontrolboard.pa.gov/news-and-transparency/press-release/october-revenue-hits-all-time-high), out of $274.1 million in total tax, itself a monthly record. Dividing those figures by the corresponding revenue implies effective rates of roughly 46% on iGaming and 36% on sports betting. Again, that is my arithmetic, but it illustrates why a dollar of online casino revenue and a dollar of sportsbook revenue are not equal to a state budget, and why operators weigh them differently as well.

Within iGaming, the growth was narrow. Online table games rose only 1.30% to $58.5 million, and iPoker fell 8.97% to $2.3 million. Slots did the work. Retail casinos were mixed: retail slots rose 5.59% to $214.9 million while retail table games fell 9.40% to $68.9 million. Video gaming terminals at truck stops set their own record at $4.9 million, up 39.02%, from a much smaller base.

Among individual licenses, Hollywood Casino at Penn National Race Course led with $129.7 million of combined revenue, up 13.25%, followed by Valley Forge Casino Resort at $117.7 million, up 16.23%. Parx Casino fell 2.50% and Wind Creek Bethlehem fell 12.62%, [per the release](https://gamingcontrolboard.pa.gov/news-and-transparency/press-release/october-revenue-hits-all-time-high). The release ranks casino licenses, not individual sportsbook or online brands, so it does not show which operators won or lost share within those totals.

## New Jersey shows the same split

New Jersey's Division of Gaming Enforcement published its October numbers the same day. [Internet gaming win was $287.4 million](https://www.nj.gov/oag/ge/docs/Financials/PressRelease2026/October2026.pdf), up 10.4% from $260.3 million. Year to date it stands at $2.69 billion, up 12.9%.

Sports wagering was close to flat. Gross revenue of $116.3 million was up just 0.2% from $116.1 million, and the online sportsbook line rose 1.8%. Retail sportsbook lounges fell 32.9%. For the year through October, New Jersey sports wagering revenue is $864.6 million, [down 5.5% from $914.6 million](https://www.nj.gov/oag/ge/docs/Financials/PressRelease2026/October2026.pdf) a year earlier.

Total New Jersey gaming revenue was $649.5 million, up 6.3%, and $6.04 billion year to date, up 5.2%. The overall number is healthy only because online casino is growing double digits while betting is not.

A caution on comparisons: the two states define and report their figures differently, and New Jersey's release does not include handle, so I have not tried to compare their sportsbooks directly or add the states together.

## What to watch

First, whether Pennsylvania's handle decline is a one-month comparison against a record or the start of a trend. The PGCB released September data on Oct. 19 and October data on Nov. 17, so November figures should arrive in mid-December if the pattern holds. A second month of falling handle would be harder to explain away.

Second, whether iGaming keeps absorbing the growth. Slots made up most of the record, and the product is more profitable at scale but also draws more regulatory and tax attention, because states see how much of it they can capture.

Third, how listed operators describe the mix. When DraftKings (DKNG) and Flutter (FLUT) next discuss results, the question is whether sportsbook growth comes from more wagering or from favorable hold, and how much of their growth now rests on online casino in the states where it is legal.

Readers should also remember that regulators report revenue after winnings are paid out, before the operators' own costs for promotions, marketing and taxes, so state totals say little about company profit margins on their own. Two states are not the market, and one month is not a trend. But Pennsylvania and New Jersey are among the largest regulated online markets, and in both, October's gains came from the casino side.
