## What happened

Take-Two Interactive (TTWO) [reported fiscal second-quarter results on Nov. 9](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000946581/000162828026072175/ttwo2q27earningsrelease.htm) for the quarter ended Sept. 30, 2026. It was the last quarter before Grand Theft Auto VI, and the numbers show a company between hits.

Net bookings, the company's headline measure of sales, were $1,601.3 million, down 18% from $1,960.5 million a year earlier. GAAP net revenue was $1,497.0 million, down 16% from $1,773.8 million. The GAAP net loss was $54.0 million, or $0.29 per share, compared with a loss of $133.9 million, or $0.73 per share, in the year-earlier quarter. Non-GAAP EBITDA was $92.2 million against $116.7 million.

For the first six months, net bookings were $2,987.2 million against $3,383.6 million, and the GAAP net loss was $88.1 million, or $0.47 per share, against $145.8 million, or $0.80.

## Where the decline came from

The release splits net bookings by platform, and every one fell. Console bookings were $742.6 million, down from $907.9 million. PC and other bookings were $151.1 million, down from $234.5 million. Mobile bookings were $707.6 million, down from $818.1 million.

Recurrent consumer spending, which covers in-game purchases and virtual currency, was the steadier piece. Its net bookings were $1,302.1 million, down 9% from $1,428.3 million, and it made up 81% of the total, up from 73%. On a GAAP revenue basis it was $1,210.2 million, down 5% from $1,275.9 million.

The share rose because the other part, mainly new full-game sales, shrank faster. That is the usual pattern in the quarter before a major launch, but it is worth stating plainly: the base business is not growing on its own. The release lists NBA 2K, the Grand Theft Auto series, Toon Blast, Match Factory!, Empires & Puzzles and Borderlands among top contributors, and it gives no dollar figure for any individual title, so we cannot say which ones drove the change.

## The guidance does the work

Take-Two [reiterated](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000946581/000162828026072175/ttwo2q27earningsrelease.htm) full-year fiscal 2027 net bookings of $8,000 million to $8,200 million. It guides GAAP net revenue of $7,980 million to $8,180 million, and GAAP net income of $109 million to $142 million, or $0.57 to $0.75 per diluted share. Non-GAAP EBITDA is guided at $1,024 million to $1,082 million.

Because the first half produced $2,987.2 million of bookings, the guidance implies a large second half. Subtracting the first-half figure from the full-year range gives about $5,013 million to $5,213 million for the second half. That is our arithmetic from the release's figures, not a company statement. That is about 1.7 times the first half's $2,987.2 million.

The company also guided the third quarter, which ends Dec. 31 and contains the GTA VI launch. Net bookings are guided at $3,350 million to $3,500 million, GAAP net revenue at $3,270 million to $3,420 million, and net income at $250 million to $276 million, or $1.30 to $1.44 per diluted share. EBITDA is guided at $651 million to $698 million.

Compared with Q2's $1,601.3 million, the Q3 bookings range is more than double. Again, that is our comparison of two numbers in the release.

## What GTA VI timing means

The release gives the launch as Nov. 19, 2026, on PS5 and Xbox Series X|S. CEO Strauss Zelnick said in the release that "the launch of Grand Theft Auto VI is ten days away" with "pre-orders continuing to reach unprecedented levels," and that "we expect to deliver an historic year for our company."

The release gives no pre-order count, and we do not cite one. What the guidance does show is how much of the year sits in the final weeks: Q3 net bookings of $3,350 million to $3,500 million would be about 42% of the $8,000 million to $8,200 million full-year range, matching low end to low end and high to high (3,350/8,000 is 41.9%; 3,500/8,200 is 42.7%). That is our arithmetic. One quarter is expected to carry roughly two-fifths of the year.

One more implication follows. Taking the full-year net income range and subtracting the six-month loss of $88.1 million gives $197.1 million to $230.1 million for the second half. With Q3 guided at $250 million to $276 million, the remaining fourth quarter would be a loss of about $20 million to $79 million. This is our own derivation from the release's ranges, and it could change if the company's quarterly guidance is conservative or if costs are timed differently.

## Cash and balance sheet

Cash used in operating activities in the first six months was $173.5 million, versus $83.7 million provided a year earlier. The release guides full-year operating cash flow above $1,000 million, which, by our arithmetic, implies more than $1,173 million of operating cash inflow in the second half.

Capital expenditures were $163.3 million over six months against $57.0 million, and the full-year guide is about $290 million.

Cash and cash equivalents were $1,149.4 million at Sept. 30, down from $1,545.5 million at March 31, with short-term investments of $524.2 million versus $443.8 million. Short-term debt, net, rose to $629.2 million from $30.0 million, while long-term debt, net, fell to $1,890.6 million from $2,488.0 million. Adding the two gives $2,519.8 million of debt against $2,518.0 million, our sum. Total debt is about flat, but more of it now falls due within a year.

## What to watch

First, the Nov. 19 launch and the first weeks of reported sales, since the quarter's guidance range is wide, at $150 million of net bookings between low and high end.

Second, the January-to-March quarter implied by the guidance, which on our math includes a loss, and how the company explains it when it reports Q3.

Third, recurrent spending, whose net bookings fell 9% in Q2. The release gives no full-year figure for it, so the next quarter's print is the next data point.
