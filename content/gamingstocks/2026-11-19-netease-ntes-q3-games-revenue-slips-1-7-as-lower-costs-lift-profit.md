## What NetEase reported

NetEase (NTES) published its third-quarter 2026 results on November 19, as it [had announced](https://www.morningstar.com/news/pr-newswire/20261105cn89888/netease-to-report-third-quarter-2026-financial-results-on-november-19) earlier this month. The headline is a small decline. According to the company's [Form 6-K release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001110646/000110465926129894/tm2630023d1_ex99-1.htm), net revenues were RMB27.9 billion (US$4.2 billion), down 1.8% from the same quarter of 2025 and down 7.5% from RMB30.1 billion in the second quarter.

Games and related value-added services, the core of the business, brought in RMB22.9 billion (US$3.4 billion). That is a 1.7% decline year over year and an 8.3% drop from RMB25.0 billion in Q2. The company attributes the sequential decline mainly to lower net revenues from certain self-developed games. It does not name which titles slipped.

Net income attributable to shareholders was RMB8.4 billion (US$1.3 billion), a 2.5% decline from RMB8.6 billion a year earlier. Non-GAAP net income, which excludes share-based compensation, was RMB9.2 billion, down 3.6%. Basic earnings were US$1.96 per ADS, against US$2.01 a year earlier.

On the surface that is a flat-to-slightly-weaker quarter. The more interesting part is what sits between revenue and profit.

## Costs fell faster than sales

Gross profit rose 4.8% year over year to RMB19.0 billion even though revenue fell. The reason is the cost line. Cost of revenues was RMB8.8 billion, compared with RMB10.2 billion in Q3 2025, and the company says the year-over-year decrease was mainly due to lower revenue-sharing costs.

For a games investor, that detail matters. Revenue-sharing costs typically move with the mix of titles, particularly games run with outside developers or licensed intellectual property, so a lower figure while revenue holds near flat suggests that more of the quarter's sales came from games NetEase keeps more of. The release does not break this down by title, so that reading is an inference rather than a disclosed fact.

Operating expenses also fell. Total operating expenses were RMB9.6 billion, down 5.4% from RMB10.2 billion in Q3 2025, which the company attributes to lower marketing and general and administrative spending. Against Q2 they rose, which the company says reflects higher marketing spending.

The income statement in the same filing shows operating profit of RMB9.4 billion, compared with RMB8.0 billion in Q3 2025, an increase of about 18% (our calculation from the filing's income statement). Operating profit was higher than a year ago even as net income was lower. The release points to the likely gap: income tax of RMB1.9 billion at an effective rate of 17.8%, against 13.0% in Q3 2025, and swings in other income that the company says were mainly from fair-value changes in listed equity securities.

## Beyond games

The smaller segments were mixed, per the same release:

- **Youdao:** RMB1.4 billion, down 12.3% year over year, which the company attributes to lower sales of smart devices and weaker online marketing services.
- **NetEase Cloud Music:** RMB1.9 billion, down 0.9%.
- **Innovative businesses and others:** RMB1.5 billion, up 7.1%.

Games and related services therefore account for the large majority of group revenue, and the quarter's total tracks the games line closely. Nothing outside games offset the decline.

## The balance sheet and what holders get

The cash position is a large part of the NetEase case. Net cash totaled RMB175.2 billion (US$26.1 billion) at September 30, compared with RMB163.5 billion at the end of 2025. Operating cash flow for the quarter was RMB10.5 billion, below the RMB12.9 billion of Q3 2025 but above the RMB10.0 billion of Q2.

The board approved a Q3 dividend of US$0.117 per share (US$0.585 per ADS), with a December 3 record date. The Q2 dividend, paid in September, was US$0.096 per share (US$0.480 per ADS). The up-to-US$5.0 billion share repurchase program was extended to January 9, 2029. Through September 30, about 24.9 million ADSs had been repurchased for US$2.3 billion in total. The release does not give a Q3-only buyback figure, so it is not possible to say how much of that came in the quarter.

## What to watch next

Three items from the release give the next few months some shape.

**The title pipeline.** NetEase said *Where Winds Meet* passed 100 million players globally in August 2026, and that *Marvel Rivals* added new features and content during the quarter. The release does not quantify how much either contributed to revenue. *Ananta* is scheduled for a multiplatform global launch on January 15, 2027. A new launch is the clearest way for games revenue to turn back to growth, and the company's own wording gives no revenue expectation for it.

**Marketing spend.** Operating expenses rose sequentially on marketing before falling year over year. If the next quarter's marketing rises ahead of the January launch, the cost savings seen in Q3 could narrow. That is a question for the Q4 report, not something the current release answers.

**The tax rate.** The effective rate swung from 13.0% a year ago to 25.5% in Q2 and 17.8% this quarter. A volatile rate makes net income harder to compare quarter to quarter than operating profit, which is why the gap between the two this period deserves attention.

The release offers no formal revenue or profit guidance, so there is no prior outlook to measure the quarter against. NetEase's results also need to be read in RMB; the dollar figures in the release use a convenience rate of 6.7045 per US dollar, which is a translation, not a reported currency.

## Why it matters for the sector

For a games investor, the quarter is a test of profit quality over growth. NetEase's quarter shows revenue slipping, gross profit and operating profit growing, and the net cash pile building. Whether that holds depends on whether lower revenue-sharing costs reflect a durable change in what players are spending on, or a one-quarter swing in mix.
