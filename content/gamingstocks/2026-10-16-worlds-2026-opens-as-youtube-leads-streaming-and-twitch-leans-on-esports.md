## Worlds begins as the streaming map shifts

The League of Legends World Championship started on Oct. 15 with the Play-In stage in Los Angeles, which runs Oct. 15 to 18, according to [esportnow.gg's schedule report](https://esportnow.gg/lol/news/riot-announces-full-worlds-2026-schedule). The Swiss stage follows in Allen, Texas, from Oct. 23. Quarterfinals and semifinals run Nov. 3 to 8, and the grand final is at the Barclays Center in Brooklyn on Nov. 14. [Sheep Esports](https://www.sheepesports.com/de/articles/everything-you-need-to-know-about-league-of-legends-worlds-2026/en) notes it is the first Worlds in North America since 2022, with 19 teams and T1 as defending champion.

For investors, the tournament matters less as a game story than as a live test of where esports viewers choose to watch. Riot Games, which is owned by Tencent ([MCV reported the full takeover in 2015](https://mcvuk.com/esports/tencent-takes-full-ownership-of-riot-games)), will carry Worlds on its own channels on Twitch, YouTube and TikTok. Riot had not yet announced its co-streamers at the time of that preview, and that matters, as the numbers below show.

## What the Q2 streaming data says

The best recent platform-level read is the Streams Charts Q2 2026 report, [covered by Net Influencer on July 15](https://www.netinfluencer.com/q2-2026-livestreaming-sets-record-31-4b-hours-watched-as-world-cup-lifts-youtube/). Global livestreaming hit a record 31.4 billion hours watched, up 8% from Q1, with the FIFA World Cup doing much of the lifting.

The split by platform is the useful part:

- **YouTube** logged more than 15.5 billion hours watched, up 14.8% quarter on quarter, and held 49.5% of tracked hours. But the gain was not gaming. Non-gaming hours rose 19.6% while YouTube Gaming hours fell 12.4% versus Q1.
- **Twitch** total hours were flat, within 1% of Q1, but esports hours watched rose 22.4%. June's peak viewership was the best in 11 months, above 4.4 million concurrent viewers during Summer Game Fest.
- **Kick** grew total hours 7.6%, and its esports hours jumped 66%, mostly on Counter-Strike (the IEM Cologne Major). Twitch's lead over Kick has narrowed to roughly 3x in hours watched, from about 5x a year earlier.
- **Steam** hours watched rose 56%, while Rumble fell 27%.

The report did not give Twitch or Kick share-of-total figures, so treat the 3x ratio as the only direct comparison.

## Why it matters for listed companies

Two large listed parents sit behind the biggest platforms. Twitch is a subsidiary of Amazon (AMZN), which [bought it in 2014 for $970 million](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_(service)). YouTube sits inside Alphabet (GOOGL). Neither company breaks out streaming hours in earnings, so these third-party figures are directional and not a revenue read.

Still, the pattern is worth tracking. YouTube's growth in Q2 came from sports and entertainment, while its gaming hours slipped. Twitch, with flat hours overall, found growth specifically in esports. If that holds, esports is becoming one of the few levers that moves Twitch's engagement, which raises the stakes on events like Worlds and on who is allowed to co-stream them.

Co-streaming is already the main engine of audience size. At this summer's Esports World Cup, [Insider Gaming reported](https://insider-gaming.com/esports-world-cup-hits-new-peak-league-of-legends-viewership/) that League of Legends peaked at about 1.47 million viewers, a record for an EWC LoL match, and drew over 23 million hours watched. Co-streams supplied over 15 million of those hours, more than 60% of the total, with one streamer, Caedrel, contributing 3.5 million. Esports Charts data cited by [esports.gg](https://esports.gg/news/counter-strike-2/ewc-2026-viewership-record/) put the peak at 1.48 million, up 22.3% from EWC 2025.

## The Worlds benchmarks to watch

Worlds is a much bigger audience event than the EWC. Insider Gaming reports that last year's Worlds peaked above 6.7 million, and that G2 Esports' MSI win over T1 peaked above 2.3 million, the most-watched LoL match of 2026 as of that July report. Those are the figures this year's tournament will be measured against.

Counter-Strike offers a useful contrast. [Esports.gg reported](https://esports.gg/news/counter-strike-2/ewc-2026-viewership-record/) that the IEM Cologne 2026 grand final set a CS2 record of 2.75 million peak viewers and 100.24 million hours watched across the event, which is why Kick's esports surge in Q2 tracked so closely with that tournament. One marquee event can move a platform's quarterly esports line, so the Worlds knockout rounds in November will matter for Q4 comparisons.

Three things to watch as it unfolds:

1. **Peak viewers versus 6.7 million.** With the first North American Worlds since 2022, viewing hours may shift. A peak well below last year would suggest the 2026 audience is not growing, even as total streaming hours set records. These figures count peaks, so check which source and method each number uses before comparing.
2. **Where the co-streams land.** Because co-streams carried more than 60% of EWC LoL hours, Riot's co-streaming rules and which platforms the big creators choose will shape how the hours split between Twitch, YouTube and TikTok.
3. **Whether Twitch's esports lift repeats.** Q2's 22.4% esports gain was helped by a busy event calendar. Q3 and Q4 data from Streams Charts will show if Worlds sustains it.

For Tencent, Riot's parent, Worlds is a marketing and ecosystem event for League of Legends, though this article has no verified data on Riot's revenue or Tencent's gaming results, so no read-through is drawn here. Esports audience records do not flow directly into publisher earnings.

## Bottom line

The streaming market is growing in total hours but unevenly. YouTube is gaining from non-gaming content, Twitch is leaning on esports, and Kick is closing the gap on a smaller base. Worlds, running through Nov. 14, will be the next large dataset on whether esports can carry engagement for the platforms and publishers involved. Treat the numbers as engagement signals, not earnings predictions, and watch the first Swiss-stage viewership figures from Esports Charts and Streams Charts for the first real check.
