## What happened

Nebraska voters said no to online sports betting on Election Day. Two linked measures were on the ballot: Initiative 440, a constitutional amendment, and Initiative 441, the statute that would have set the rules. Both had to pass for mobile wagering to become legal, and both were [trailing by midnight](https://communitynews.org/gambling-news/nebraska-online-sports-betting-results/).

In the unofficial count [reported by RG.org](https://rg.org/news/gambling-industry/nebraska-440-441-online-sports-betting-headed-for-defeat) with 930 of 1,322 precincts fully reporting, Initiative 440 had 297,333 votes for and 356,690 against, or 54.5% opposed. Initiative 441 had 300,052 for and 353,892 against, or 54.1% opposed. A [MultiState tally](https://www.multistate.us/elections/results/nebraska-2026) put the final yes share at 45.18% for 440 and 45.61% for 441, though that is not an official canvass. Neither number is certified, but the gap is wide enough that the direction is not in doubt.

The money behind the losing side is the part investors should note. Tax Relief Nebraska, the group that gathered the signatures, raised [nearly $22.5 million](https://rg.org/news/gambling-industry/nebraska-440-441-online-sports-betting-headed-for-defeat), nearly all of it from DraftKings (DKNG) and FanDuel, the U.S. brand of Flutter Entertainment (FLUT). State Treasurer Joey Spellerberg, an opponent, said Save Nebraska Sports was outspent 20-1. His read of the result: "Voters in Nebraska looked right past that."

## What the measures would have done

The proposal was built to be as palatable as a mobile betting measure can be. According to [Covers](https://www.covers.com/politics/nebraska-voters-reject-online-sports-betting-what-comes-next-nov-3-2026), it would have applied the state's existing 20% in-person gaming tax to online sportsbook revenue and sent 70% of the proceeds to property tax relief. A study cited by Tax Relief Nebraska estimated about $238 million in tax relief over 10 years. [GamblingNews.com](https://www.gamblingnews.com/news/nebraska-voters-say-no-to-legalizing-online-sports-gambling-in-the-state/) repeats the 20% rate and the 70% allocation, and it gives a rounded figure of about $240 million in one section.

The market would have been casino-anchored. Each authorized gaming operator could have offered up to two online platforms, allowing as many as 12 mobile sportsbooks across six current or planned facilities. National brands would have needed agreements with licensed Nebraska operators, and the Nebraska Racing and Gaming Commission would have had to adopt online rules by June 1, 2027.

For context, the in-person market has been small. Covers reports that the state's sportsbooks have generated a little more than $4 million in taxes since the first locations opened in summer 2023. All wagering must take place in person at one of the state's [five racetrack casinos](https://rg.org/news/gambling-industry/nebraska-440-441-online-sports-betting-headed-for-defeat), and the restriction on bets on in-state college teams stays in place.

## Why a ballot loss matters more than a delayed launch

Nebraska was never going to be a large contributor to either company's numbers. The state is small, the proposed tax rate was ordinary, and the structure would have forced national operators into partnerships with local casinos. What makes the result notable is the route. When legislatures stall, operators can try to go around them with petitions and ballot language, and Nebraska is a clean test of that approach with a favorable pitch (property tax relief) and a very large spending advantage.

It did not work. The opposing campaign did not need to match the spending. Nate Grasz of the Nebraska Family Alliance said, "This was never about lowering property taxes, this was never about helping Nebraska families." That is a message, not a fact, but it appears to have landed with a majority of voters. The takeaway for the audience that follows these stocks is that a tax-relief framing and a big budget are not a guaranteed path to legalization when voters are asked directly.

The result also fits a pattern in which new markets arrive slowly. [Missouri](https://www.saturdaydownsouth.com/news/betting/missouri-becomes-39th-state-to-launch-legal-sports-betting/) became the 39th state to launch legal sports betting when it went live on Dec. 1, 2025. Nebraska now stays in the group of states that allow wagering only in person, and it is an open question how many easy wins remain.

## What happens next

Covers says a renewed push could come through the legislature or a new petition drive, but the defeat likely blocks both for now, with 2029 as the best-case timeline for mobile betting. Nebraska's Legislature has also seen repeated attempts to legalize online betting fail, so the 2029 date is a best case, not a schedule.

Prediction markets complicate the picture. Covers notes that people physically in Nebraska can still trade event contracts on professional and college sports. That is a reminder that state votes no longer fully decide whether residents can bet on games. The competing venue category is also growing. On Nov. 3, [Sporttrade received CFTC registrations](https://www.covers.com/industry/sporttrade-secures-cftc-registrations-to-launch-prediction-market-business-nov-3-2026) as a designated contract market and derivatives clearing organization. The company had operated as a state-licensed sportsbook in New Jersey, Arizona, Colorado, Iowa and Virginia before exiting those markets in June 2026 to move into prediction markets. It joins a field that includes Kalshi, Polymarket, FanDuel Predicts and DraftKings Predictions.

States are pushing back. In New York, [Covers reports](https://www.covers.com/politics/hochul-wins-new-york-gaming-prediction-markets-november-3-2026) that Gov. Kathy Hochul, who won re-election on Nov. 3, and Attorney General Letitia James sued Kalshi in July, alleging it operated an unlicensed gambling business. Kalshi argues its contracts fall under federal regulation. The same piece says online casino legalization in New York remains unresolved because Hochul has not committed to signing a bill.

## What to watch

- **DraftKings results on Nov. 5.** DraftKings said it will [release third quarter results after the close on Thursday, Nov. 5](https://s21.q4cdn.com/869500724/files/doc_news/DraftKings-to-Release-Third-Quarter-2026-Results-on-November-5-2026-and-Host-Conference-Call-on-November-6-2026-2026.pdf), with a call at 8:30 a.m. ET on Nov. 6. Management commentary on how it weighs ballot campaigns, legislative lobbying and its prediction product is the most direct read-through from this vote.
- **Official canvass.** The Nebraska numbers above are unofficial. Final certified totals could shift the percentages slightly, though not the outcome.
- **The next legalization attempt.** Whether sponsors turn to the Legislature in 2027 or wait for another petition cycle will show how much appetite operators have left after a roughly $22 million loss.
- **State-level fights with prediction markets.** The Kalshi suit in New York and Sporttrade's entry both bear on whether states or federal regulators end up setting the terms for sports contracts.

Nothing here is investment advice. It is a read on how one lopsided vote fits into a map of state markets that is still being drawn.
