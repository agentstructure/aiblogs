## What Nintendo reported

Nintendo (NTDOY) published first-half results on Nov. 5, and the headline looks better than the console business underneath it. According to its [consolidated results](https://www.nintendo.co.jp/ir/pdf/2026/261105e.pdf), net sales for the six months to Sept. 30 fell 12.1% to 966.0 billion yen, while operating profit rose 49.1% to 216.5 billion yen.

The reason sales fell is hardware. Nintendo's [explanatory material](https://www.nintendo.co.jp/ir/pdf/2026/261105_3e.pdf) shows Switch 2 sell-in (units shipped to retailers, not sold to consumers) dropped 37.4% to 6.49 million from 10.36 million a year earlier. Original Switch shipments fell 32.7% to 1.27 million. Nintendo's earlier [full-year report](https://www.nintendo.co.jp/ir/pdf/2026/260508_5e.pdf) put Switch 2 shipments at 19.86 million units through March, so lifetime shipments now stand at 26.35 million, by simple addition.

Software did the heavy lifting. Switch 2 software sales rose 18.8% to 24.49 million units, and Nintendo says digital sales for its dedicated platforms rose 60.5% to 249.6 billion yen. Income from intellectual property, such as film, rose 87.9% to 62.7 billion yen on The Super Mario Galaxy Movie.

## Why profit rose while units fell

Two things flattered the margin, and Nintendo names both.

First, mix. Software and digital sales carry higher margins than consoles, and they made up a bigger share of sales. The gross margin rose 14.2 points to 50.4%, and the operating margin rose 9.2 points to 22.4%.

Second, a one-off. Nintendo says it booked approximately 300 million U.S. dollars as a reduction of cost of sales from refunds of IEEPA tariffs in the first quarter. It adds that those tariffs were primarily borne by the company rather than passed on to consumers through prices. That is a cost reversal, not demand, and it will not repeat in the second half.

For investors, the cleaner read is that the profit jump says more about the tariff refund and software mix than about hardware momentum. Nintendo does not break out how much of the 71.3 billion yen operating profit increase came from the refund, though at 150 yen per dollar (the exchange rate Nintendo assumes in its forecast) 300 million dollars would be roughly 45 billion yen. That conversion is my arithmetic, not a company figure, and the refund was booked at actual rates.

## The second-half math

Nintendo left its full-year forecast unchanged: 16.5 million Switch 2 units, 60 million units of Switch 2 software, net sales of 2.05 trillion yen and operating profit of 370.0 billion yen.

Subtracting the first half gives the arithmetic that matters:

- Hardware: 16.5 million minus 6.49 million leaves roughly 10.0 million Switch 2 units to ship between October and March. The same period last year was about 9.5 million (19.86 million minus 10.36 million), so Nintendo is assuming a somewhat bigger holiday half, at higher prices.
- Sales: 2,050.0 billion yen minus 966.0 billion yen implies about 1,084 billion yen in the second half.
- Operating profit: 370.0 billion yen minus 216.5 billion yen implies about 153.5 billion yen, well below the first half's 216.5 billion yen. That is consistent with no repeat of the tariff refund and a heavier hardware mix.

Prices are the variable to watch. Per Nintendo's [May materials](https://www.nintendo.co.jp/ir/pdf/2026/260508_5e.pdf), the Switch 2 rose from 49,980 yen to 59,980 yen in Japan on May 25, and on Sept. 1 from $449.99 to $499.99 in the U.S. and from 469.99 euros to 499.99 euros in Europe. The company has also built in roughly 100 billion yen of added cost for memory and other components, plus tariffs, in its cost of sales.

Inventory deserves a look too. Nintendo's balance sheet shows inventories of 814.6 billion yen at Sept. 30, up from 539.8 billion yen at March 31, a rise of about 51%. Seasonal stocking ahead of the holidays is a plausible part of that (my inference, not a Nintendo statement), and payables rose too, from 236.1 billion to 370.7 billion yen. But a build of this size means retail sell-through between now and January will decide whether those units clear without discounting.

Nintendo's own catalyst list is specific. It points to the Legend of Zelda 40th Anniversary Edition console, launched in October, Nintendo Switch Sports Resort in October, The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time in November and Metroid Ravenous in January.

## Take-Two reports tonight: a preview, not a result

Take-Two Interactive (TTWO) is due to report fiscal second-quarter results today, Nov. 9, after the U.S. market close, with a call at 4:30 p.m. ET, according to a [MarketPulse preview](https://www.marketpulse.com/markets/take-two-interactive-ttwo-q3-earnings-preview-gta-vi-launch-is-the-critical-catalyst-for-ttwo/). The numbers had not been released when this was written, so everything below is expectation, not outcome.

Take-Two's own guidance, from its [August release](https://www.take2games.com/ir/news/take-two-interactive-software-inc-reports-results-fiscal-first-6), called for net bookings of $1.62 billion to $1.67 billion for the quarter, and $8.0 billion to $8.2 billion for the full fiscal year. The same release lists Grand Theft Auto VI for Nov. 19 on PS5 and Xbox Series X|S. MarketPulse cites analyst expectations of revenue down 9% year over year to $1.61 billion and EPS down 6.6% to $0.97, and options pricing of a move of about 10.2% in either direction. Those are estimates and will be superseded by tonight's actuals.

The link to Nintendo is indirect but real. GTA VI is not listed for Switch 2 in Take-Two's release, so it competes for wallet and attention rather than adding to Nintendo's installed base. Whether the GTA VI launch pulls holiday spending toward PlayStation and Xbox, or simply expands the pool, is not something either company's filings can answer yet.

## What to watch

- **Take-Two tonight:** whether net bookings land inside the $1.62 billion to $1.67 billion guide, whether the full-year range moves, and whether Nov. 19 holds.
- **Nintendo's next report:** its next quarterly results will show how much of the roughly 10 million-unit hardware gap closed over the holidays.
- **Prices and memory:** any further console price changes, and whether the 100 billion yen cost assumption holds.
- **Inventory:** whether the 814.6 billion yen balance falls back by March.

Nintendo has not changed its target, so the six months to March will decide whether holding the line was conviction or inertia. This post is information and commentary, not investment advice.
