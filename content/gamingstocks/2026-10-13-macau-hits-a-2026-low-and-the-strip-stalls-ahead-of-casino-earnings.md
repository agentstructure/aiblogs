## Two markets, one soft patch

The two biggest profit pools for casino operators both ended the summer on a flat note. Macau's September gross gaming revenue came in at MOP18.06 billion (about US$2.23 billion), down 1.2% from a year earlier, according to [Casino.org](https://casino.org/news/macau-losing-streak-hits-four-months-september). That is the fourth consecutive year-on-year decline and the lowest monthly total of 2026 so far. In Nevada, the latest state report showed the Las Vegas Strip growing by less than one percent in August.

For investors in Las Vegas Sands (LVS), Wynn Resorts (WYNN), MGM Resorts (MGM) and Melco Resorts (MLCO), the numbers matter less as a verdict on any one quarter than as the backdrop for the third-quarter reports now approaching. Both markets are still large and profitable. The question is whether the growth that defined the first half of the year has faded for good or is just pausing.

## Macau: a strong start that has faded

Macau began 2026 with real momentum. [Casino.org](https://casino.org/news/macau-losing-streak-hits-four-months-september) notes that gaming revenue was up 10.9% year on year from January through May. Since then the monthly comparisons have turned negative, and September was the weakest month of the year, below the previous low of MOP18.52 billion set in June.

The monthly drop was also sharp. [Focus Gaming News](https://focusgn.com/asia-pacific/macau-ggr-september-revenue) reports that September revenue was 17.5% below August. Expectations had been higher. Casino.org reports that some forecasts early in September called for growth of up to 12%, before floor checks showed sluggish table play and brokerages cut their estimates to marginal growth. September still missed the revised consensus, with weaker premium mass table drop largely blamed.

That detail is the one to watch. Premium mass play is the segment the large concession holders have leaned on since the junket crackdown, so softness there is more consequential than a dip in low-stakes traffic. The International Monetary Fund's 2026 Article IV review of Macao, cited by Focus Gaming News, expects VIP gaming to stay a relatively small share of revenue over the medium term because of the tighter junket rules introduced in the 2022 reforms. In other words, the old high-roller engine is not coming back to fill the gap.

Visitors are not the problem. Casino.org reports arrivals rose 7.7% in the first eight months of 2026, to nearly 29 million. Yet at the G2E conference in Las Vegas, MGM chief executive Bill Hornbuckle and Wynn chief executive Craig Billings said they put little weight on visitor counts, arguing that general visitation does not show how many premium and VIP players are actually in the market. Rising arrivals paired with falling revenue is a reminder that headcount and spend per head can move in opposite directions.

## Golden Week and a hard October comparison

The next data point arrives soon. Macau's Golden Week holiday ran October 1 through 7. Focus Gaming News says authorities expected 5.2 million to 5.4 million border crossings over the period, an average of 740,000 to 770,000 a day, with October 3 and 4 forecast to be the busiest days, potentially above 800,000 crossings a day. Casino.org reports that MGM Cotai, The Venetian Macao and The Parisian Macao were at 100% occupancy for the peak of the holiday.

Full hotels are encouraging, but the comparison is demanding. October 2025 gaming revenue was MOP24.08 billion (US$2.98 billion), per Casino.org, a high bar for any month in a year that has been averaging far lower. The October figures will show whether the holiday crowds spent enough to end the four-month losing streak. Casino.org also notes that the final quarter will decide whether Macau finishes the full year with positive growth.

## Las Vegas: growth, but barely

On the other side of the Pacific, the Nevada Gaming Control Board's August numbers, reported by [FOX5 Las Vegas](https://www.fox5vegas.com/2026/09/30/report-nevada-gaming-revenue-rises-314-august/), showed statewide nonrestricted gaming win of about $1.27 billion, up 3.14% from August 2025. Fiscal year to date, covering July and August, statewide win is up 2.58%.

The Strip itself was weaker than the headline. Strip gaming win was $684.1 million, up just 0.70% from $679.4 million a year earlier. Clark County as a whole rose 2.80%, helped by the Boulder Strip, up 17.38%, and Laughlin, up 9.92%. Downtown Las Vegas fell 2.96%. Outside southern Nevada, Reno gained 15.69%.

Two cautions apply. First, gaming win is only part of a Strip operator's economics. Rooms, food, entertainment and convention business drive a large share of profit, and none of those appear in this report. Second, gaming win is volatile from month to month because it depends on hold, the share of wagers the casino keeps. A single month of 0.7% growth is a data point, not a trend. Still, it fits a picture in which the Strip is stable but not accelerating, while regional markets carry more of the growth.

## What to watch next

The earnings calendar is the next test. MGM will report third-quarter 2026 results on Wednesday, October 28, after the market close, with a conference call at 5:00 p.m. Eastern, according to the [company's announcement](https://finviz.com/news/396109/mgm-resorts-international-announces-third-quarter-earnings-release-date). MGM has exposure to both markets through its Las Vegas resorts and MGM Cotai, so its commentary should show how management reads the premium mass slowdown and the Strip's flat August.

Three questions will shape how the sector reads this season:

- **Premium mass in Macau.** Do operators describe September as a one-off miss or the start of a softer pattern, and how do they characterize the October Golden Week?
- **Strip demand beyond gaming.** Do room rates and occupancy hold up when gaming win is growing under one percent?
- **Cash flow.** Does management still sound confident about earnings if Macau revenue stays flat?

None of this settles the outlook. The data show two mature markets that have stopped growing quickly, with Macau's early-year gains fading and the Strip close to flat. The October figures out of Macau and the first earnings calls will say more than any single month of regulator data.
