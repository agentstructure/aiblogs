## The record, and what it is not

On Sunday, Oct. 11, Steam reached 43,505,724 concurrent logged-in users, according to SteamDB figures cited by [Tech Insider](https://tech-insider.org/steam-43-million-concurrent-users-record-2026/) and [Dot Esports](https://dotesports.com/general/news/steam-all-time-record-43-5-million-concurrent-users). It is the third concurrent-user record of 2026, per [Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/steam-valve-concurrent-users-record-in-game-epic-fortnite-roblox-2000744564): first past 42 million in January, then another half-million in March, and now past 43 million. Tech Insider puts the March mark at 42,686,616, so the new peak beats it by 819,108.

The more useful number sits underneath. SteamDB counted roughly 14.5 million users actually in a game at the peak, itself a new high, per Kotaku. The rest of the 43.5 million were logged in but browsing, chatting or idle. For an investor, in-game users are closer to engagement that turns into spending and, for free-to-play titles, into ad and in-game purchase potential.

Valve is private, and Tech Insider notes that it has not commented on the milestone. So there is no ticker to attach to the headline. The question for this site's audience is which listed companies sit inside the number.

## No single game drove it

Dot Esports and Tech Insider both stress that SteamDB's charts do not show any one title causing the record. The game-level 24-hour peaks around the record day, from Dot Esports, show a spread:

- Counter-Strike 2: 1,404,227
- Dota 2: roughly 1.03 million
- PUBG: Battlegrounds: 713,683
- Aion 2: 392,300
- Apex Legends: 233,682
- Deadlock: 220,575
- WARDOGS: 199,487

The top two are Valve's own games, which makes the record partly a Valve-on-Valve story. Tech Insider also says the record fell outside a major sale and that a Steam Next Fest event was live, which points to discovery and demos as a contributor. That points to a platform effect that accrues first to the store operator rather than to any one publisher.

The takeaway for investors is that platform records and publisher earnings can move apart. A record from a long tail of 40-plus million users, with Valve's two flagships leading, does not map neatly onto a revenue line at a listed publisher.

## Aion 2 is the listed-company link

The clearest listed beneficiary in the data is NCsoft, the Korean publisher of Aion 2. Its official launch on Steam and NC's own PURPLE platform came on Oct. 5, and the game peaked at 397,905 concurrent Steam players that day, per [Inven Global](https://www.invenglobal.com/articles/26837/aion-2-surpasses-400000-concurrent-players-on-steam). That was 226,375 above the 171,530 early-access peak reported a few days earlier. Inven's figure excludes PURPLE users.

The market noticed before launch. [Herald Corp](https://biz.heraldcorp.com/article/10892665) reported that NCsoft closed a Friday session up 6.30 percent at 244,500 won after a 10.29 percent gain the session before, about 17 percent over two sessions. The same report said the stock had fallen to the 190,000-won range the previous month and that a Hyundai Motor Securities analyst raised the price target from 350,000 to 380,000 won, with a price-to-earnings ratio near nine. Those prices are from early October and are not current quotes; check live data before using them.

The context matters. The same Herald piece says NCsoft posted a 109.2 billion won operating loss last year, its first deficit in 26 years, and cut headquarters headcount from about 5,000 to about 3,000. A big Steam launch is a turnaround signal, not yet proof of one.

Revenue evidence is mixed in a useful way. [Mein-MMO](https://mein-mmo.de/en/aion-2-is-an-important-factor-for-ncsofts-record-quarter-but-another-pc-mmo-makes-even-double-the-money,1582087/) reported that NCsoft's PC MMORPG business earned about $243 million in the second quarter, up 8 percent from the prior record and up 216 percent from a year earlier. Aion 2 contributed about $51 million of that, below its Q1 level, while Lineage Classic brought in more than $130 million. So the game that set the Steam record is not even the biggest earner in its own portfolio. Peak concurrency in the first week is a launch measure; subscriptions and retention decide the revenue.

## The streaming side: a share shift, not a winner

Engagement is also moving off Steam and onto streams. [Streams Charts](https://streamscharts.com/news/q3-2026-global-livestreaming-landscape) put total Q3 live-streaming hours watched at 31.698 billion, up about 0.8 percent on Q2 and its highest quarter on record. YouTube remained first at 13.62 billion hours but fell 13.7 percent and slipped to 43 percent share from 49.5 percent. TikTok LIVE rose 19 percent to 10.6 billion hours (a record 33.5 percent share), Twitch grew 4 percent to 4.51 billion, and Kick grew 30.7 percent to 2.04 billion.

Two details bear on gaming. Gaming made up 76.9 percent of Twitch's watch time, and Twitch had its strongest quarter since Q2 2025. And Kick's lead streamer by hours showed unusual audience patterns, which Streams Charts flags as a reason for caution. Growth numbers on new platforms deserve skepticism until audiences are audited.

Most of these platforms are not independently listed: Twitch sits inside Amazon (AMZN), YouTube inside Alphabet (GOOGL), and TikTok and Kick are private. The investable lesson is therefore indirect: watch whether streaming attention helps drive sales of the games that top Steam charts.

## What to watch

- NCsoft's next earnings report: whether Aion 2 subscriptions hold after the launch spike, and whether the stock's early-October gains persist.
- Steam concurrency in the coming weeks. If it stays above prior records without a sale, discovery events are working; if it falls back, the record was an event effect.
- Deadlock, which reached an all-time peak of 262,145 after a Sept. 30 update while still in invite-only testing, per Dot Esports and Tech Insider. Its player count will inform how much Valve's next release can add.
- Whether TikTok LIVE and Kick keep gaining share against YouTube in Q4, which would change where game marketing dollars go.

Engagement records are good news for the industry's top line. They are weaker evidence about which company gets the money.
