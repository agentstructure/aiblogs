## The sticker price is now the story

The cleanest way to see what memory costs have done to the console business is to line up list prices. According to a [Yahoo Finance report on Circana's latest data](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/article/video-game-hardware-sales-plunge-to-13-year-low-as-prices-soar-on-memory-storage-shortage-155535332.html), published Oct. 9, the PlayStation 5 Digital Edition, which launched at $399 in November 2020, now lists at $599. The disc version went from $499 to $649. The PS5 Pro, which debuted at $699 in 2024, is $899.

Microsoft's lineup has moved the same way. The Xbox Series S went from $299 to $499, and the Series X is up $250 to $749. Nintendo's Switch 2 rose from $449 to $499 after tariff increases. By my arithmetic from those list prices, the PS5 Digital Edition is up 50%, the disc model 30%, the Series S 67% and the PS5 Pro about 29% in two years.

Consoles used to get cheaper as they aged. This generation they got more expensive, and for a sector that earns its best margins on software sold to the installed base, that changes how fast the base grows.

## What Circana is seeing

The same report quotes Circana's Mat Piscatella on the demand side. August hardware sales were the lowest for the month in 13 years, hardware spending fell 3% in August, and year-to-date hardware spending is down 11%. Piscatella attributed the weakness to higher prices: "We are seeing the negative impact of those higher price points on the consoles."

He also flagged supply. "We're starting to see some products becoming unavailable, likely because of the RAM and component crisis," he said, adding that "I think there's going to be some sticker shock there if the consoles are even on the shelf to begin with." The report notes some retailers are listing used PS5 Pro units above $1,000, though it says it is unclear whether the memory shortage is the cause.

One caution on reading the data. Higher prices can hold dollar spending closer to flat than unit sales, so a small spending decline is not the same as demand holding up. Fewer buyers paying more each leaves hardware makers dependent on a smaller pool of players.

## Why memory is the cost that matters

The driver is the AI build-out. A February [Telegraph report republished by Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/playstation-6-console-threatened-delay-184327261.html) said Samsung, SK Hynix and Micron (MU) had shifted much of their capacity toward AI customers, leaving less for consumer devices. It cited Counterpoint Research putting RAM chip prices up 600% over the prior year and TrendForce expecting prices to double in the first quarter of 2026 alone. CCS Insight's Ben Wood said he did not expect new supply to ease constraints until well into 2027, possibly early 2028.

Those are forecasts from the spring, so treat them as a framing rather than a current price. The more recent reading is TrendForce's [August contract price summary](https://www.trendforce.com/research/download/RP260831UX), which says PC DRAM contract prices are still rising, with quarterly gains "expanding beyond prior forecasts," and that capacity moving toward servers is strengthening vendors' pricing leverage. TrendForce lists a separate fourth-quarter 2026 memory price forecast, but its contents are not public, so I can't tell you what it projects.

The same Telegraph report said Sony could push the PlayStation 6 to 2028 or even 2029, from an earlier expectation of late 2027, based on Bloomberg reporting. Sony did not comment. That is a report, not a company statement, but it shows why investors are asking how long the PS5 generation has to carry the business.

## Nintendo as the case study

Nintendo (NTDOY) has been the most explicit about the math. In May it said it would raise the Switch 2 to $499.99 in the US effective Sept. 1, and guided for the fiscal year ending March 2027 to revenue of ¥2.05 trillion, operating profit of ¥370 billion and 16.5 million Switch 2 units, down from 19.86 million. It put a ¥100 billion hit on memory prices and US tariffs combined, according to [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/nintendo-switch-2-price-hike-125304028.html). Shares fell 8.4% in Tokyo that day to their lowest since August 2024. Some analysts, including Morningstar's Kazunori Ito, called the outlook overly conservative and estimated 19 million units.

The first quarter, reported Aug. 7, shows the shape of the year. According to [God is a Geek](https://godisageek.com/2026/08/nintendo-switch-2-q1-fy27-sales-forecast/), Switch 2 shipments were 3.82 million in April to June, down 34.4% from 5.82 million in the launch quarter a year earlier, bringing the total to 23.68 million. Net sales fell 9.5% to ¥517.8 billion, yet operating profit rose 150.5% to ¥142.5 billion, helped by digital software sales and roughly $300 million in lower cost of sales from tariff refunds. Nintendo held its 16.5 million unit forecast.

Two lessons. First, a platform holder with a strong software slate can grow profit while hardware volume drops, but the tariff refund is a one-time item that flatters the quarter. Second, that refund is contested: a proposed class action argues Nintendo should share it with customers, and Nintendo has asked a federal court to dismiss the case.

## What to watch next

- **Holiday-quarter hardware data.** Circana's monthly reports will show whether the 11% year-to-date decline deepens when the heaviest buying season arrives with prices at these levels. Availability is the new variable alongside price.
- **Memory pricing.** If contract prices keep rising, further price moves or spec cuts from console makers are a reasonable risk, and so is a later next-generation launch. A turn in DRAM contract pricing would be the clearest relief signal for Sony (SONY), Nintendo and Microsoft (MSFT).
- **Guidance language.** Nintendo's unchanged 16.5 million unit target is a benchmark. Whether its next report keeps it, and what Sony says about PS5 volumes and the PlayStation 6 timeline, will show how much of the squeeze is being absorbed versus passed on.
- **Software and services mix.** The companies cushioned best so far are those where digital revenue offsets thinner hardware. That is the part of the model to track in each filing.
