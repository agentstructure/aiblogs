## The record that hid a reshuffle

Livestreaming set another record in the third quarter, but the headline total conceals who actually gained. [Streams Charts](https://streamscharts.com/news/q3-2026-global-livestreaming-landscape) counted 31.698 billion hours watched across the platforms it tracks, about 0.8% more than the second quarter and the highest quarterly total it has recorded.

Underneath that flat-looking number, the platforms moved in very different directions. YouTube generated 13.62 billion hours, down 13.7% from the prior quarter, and its share of tracked hours slid from 49.5% to 43%. TikTok LIVE rose 19% to a platform record of 10.6 billion hours, a 33.5% share. Twitch grew 4% to 4.51 billion hours. Kick grew 30.7% to 2.04 billion.

For investors in listed gaming and media names, the useful question is less "who is biggest" and more "where is the growth, and is it gaming?" Streams Charts' answer is mostly no, with some important exceptions.

## Why YouTube fell

Streams Charts attributes YouTube's decline to the end of the FIFA World Cup. Viewing was strongest in July and faded through August and September, while gaming content partly offset the drop. The same report says YouTube hit an all-time concurrent peak of 36.06 million viewers during the World Cup final.

The prior quarter shows how much of YouTube's total was event-driven. In the [Q2 report](https://streamscharts.com/news/q2-2026-global-livestreaming-landscape), YouTube had more than 15.5 billion hours, up 14.8% from Q1, and 49.5% of everything tracked. Streams Charts also noted that YouTube's Q2 gaming hours had fallen 12.4% from Q1, so the platform's big quarters were being driven by non-gaming live content.

That matters for how Alphabet (GOOGL) investors read livestream data. A quarter inflated by a global sports final says little about a durable shift in gaming engagement, and the Q3 pullback looks like a return toward a baseline rather than a collapse. Hours watched are also not revenue, and the report does not tie its viewing data to advertising results.

Gaming did hold up better than the rest of YouTube's live mix. YouTube Gaming produced about 1.98 billion hours in Q3. That was 6.2% of all tracked hours, up from 5.6% in Q2, and 14.5% of YouTube's own watch time, up from 11.3%. Streams Charts says the Esports World Cup and The International drove gaming viewership on the platform.

## Twitch is small but steady

Twitch, owned by Amazon (AMZN), posted what Streams Charts calls its strongest quarter since Q2 2025, linked to gaming and esports. Its share of tracked hours edged up from 13.8% to 14.2%. Gaming made up 76.9% of its watch time, with League of Legends drawing substantial attention.

Contrast that with TikTok LIVE, where gaming was 16.2% of the platform's total and the biggest category was Chats. Roblox was among its most-watched games, but the growth Streams Charts links to TikTok LIVE came from non-gaming content taking a larger share. TikTok is owned by the private company ByteDance, so there is no listed stock to attach to that growth, though it is a reminder that the fastest-growing live audience is not primarily a gaming one.

The practical read for Twitch is that it remains the most gaming-concentrated of the large platforms. Its audience is a fraction of YouTube's in the Streams Charts count, yet nearly four in five of its hours are gaming. That makes it a cleaner proxy for gaming engagement than the bigger platforms, even if it is a smaller pool.

## Kick keeps closing the gap

Kick is the fastest-growing major platform in the data. Its 30.7% increase was its third straight growth quarter, and its 6.4% share is a record. Streams Charts says Twitch now generates roughly 2.2 times Kick's watch time, down from roughly 2.8 times in Q2. One caution on that comparison: the Q2 report itself described the Twitch-to-Kick ratio as "roughly three times," so the exact prior-quarter multiple depends on which report you read. The direction, a narrowing gap, is consistent in both.

Esports is part of Kick's story. An Esports Charts analysis, [reported by GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/esports-world-cup-viewership-jumps-37-in-2026/), called Kick the single biggest driver of Esports World Cup viewership growth this year, with EWC hours watched on Kick doubling year over year. The event drew more than 237 million hours across 25 tournaments, up 29.5%, and average live viewership rose 36.9% to 145,100.

There is a data-quality flag. Streams Charts notes that earlier research found unusual audience patterns on the channel of Kick's top streamer, Hani "absi" Al-Qablan, who recorded more than 50 million hours. Treat Kick's hours with some caution until that is resolved. Kick is also privately held, so investors can follow it only through the platforms and rights holders that deal with it.

## Korea: a quiet milestone for Naver

The Korean market has its own order of events. Streams Charts has Naver's CHZZK at 335.2 million hours, up 2.1% for a sixth straight growth quarter, narrowly ahead of SOOP at 333 million. It is CHZZK's first quarterly lead over SOOP.

CHZZK is operated by Naver (035420.KS) and [launched in open beta](https://streamscharts.com/news/platforms-explained-navers-chzzk) on December 19, 2023, after Twitch announced it would leave South Korea. Naver holds exclusive Korean rights to the Esports World Cup for 2025 to 2027, and with SOOP jointly holds League of Legends Champions Korea rights for 2026 to 2030. Those rights are the kind of exclusive content that can move hours between platforms.

Smaller platforms had a worse quarter: Streams Charts says Bigo LIVE and Rumble each lost nearly half of their hours, and four of the six smaller platforms declined.

## What to watch

First, whether YouTube's gaming share holds once there is no World Cup in the comparison. The Q4 data will show whether the 14.5% gaming share of its own watch time was a blip or a floor.

Second, whether Twitch can keep growing without a marquee event. Its 4% gain was modest, and a continued narrowing against Kick would matter more than any single quarter.

Third, the method. Trackers do not agree: [Stream Hatchet's Q2 report](https://streamhatchet.com/reports/q2-2026-live-streaming-trends-report/) uses a different set of platforms than Streams Charts, which counts all YouTube live content, including sports, podcasts and talk shows. Shares are only comparable within a single tracker, so avoid mixing sources.

Finally, the hours-to-revenue link. Neither report ties viewing to advertising or subscription income, and a record in hours watched does not by itself mean a record in monetization. Livestream hours are best treated as one engagement signal among several, not an earnings forecast.
