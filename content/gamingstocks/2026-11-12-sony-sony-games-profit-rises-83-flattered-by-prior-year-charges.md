## The headline number needs a footnote

Sony (SONY) reported September-quarter results on Nov. 5, and the Game & Network Services segment posted an operating income of ¥220.1 billion, up 83% from ¥120.4 billion a year earlier, according to Sony's [earnings materials filed on Form 6-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000313838/000110465926122964/tm2628525d5_6k.htm). Segment sales rose 9% to ¥1,208.5 billion.

Read quickly, that is a blowout. Read the footnotes and it is a good quarter with a flattering comparison. Sony's own explanation for the profit increase starts with what is missing: two charges booked in the year-ago quarter did not recur. One was a ¥31.5 billion impairment on part of Bungie's intangible and other assets. The other was an ¥18.3 billion correction to previously capitalized development costs. Together that is ¥49.8 billion.

[XenoSpectrum's write-up](https://xenospectrum.com/en/sony-ps5-shipments-profit-growth/) strips that out and arrives at roughly a 29% increase on an adjusted base of ¥170.2 billion. That adjustment is the outlet's own arithmetic, not a Sony-reported figure, though it follows directly from the numbers in Sony's filing. On the same basis, the segment's operating margin was about 15% last year (¥170.2 billion on ¥1,113.2 billion of sales) and about 18% this quarter (¥220.1 billion on ¥1,208.5 billion). Those margin figures are our calculations from Sony's reported numbers.

So the real story is a margin that expanded by roughly three points on an apples-to-apples basis, not seven or eight.

## Consoles shrank while the rest of the segment grew

The most useful number for anyone trying to understand the business is not the profit line. It is the PS5 shipment figure. According to [XenoSpectrum](https://xenospectrum.com/en/sony-ps5-shipments-profit-growth/), Sony shipped 2.6 million PS5 consoles in the quarter, down 35% from 4.0 million a year earlier. These are sell-in shipments, meaning units sent to retailers rather than sold to players. Sony's filing says only that hardware sales fell on lower unit sales and does not state a cause beyond that.

The segment breakdown reported by the same outlet shows where the money came from:

- **Hardware:** ¥219.7 billion, down from ¥230.9 billion, a drop of about 5%.
- **Game software:** ¥708.5 billion, up from ¥629.3 billion, a gain of about 13%.
- **Network services:** ¥218.3 billion, up from ¥182.7 billion, a gain of about 19%.

By our arithmetic, software was roughly 59% of segment sales this quarter and hardware and network services about 18% each. A console business that depends on software and subscriptions for most of its revenue behaves differently from one that sells boxes at a loss and hopes to catch up later.

One oddity: hardware revenue fell about 5% while units fell 35%. Sony's materials do not reconcile the two. Currency is part of any answer, since Sony says foreign exchange added ¥73.0 billion to segment sales, but the filing does not break that out by product line, so we would not assign a share to it.

## Engagement is the other support

Sony's user numbers point the same direction. XenoSpectrum reports PlayStation monthly active users of about 127 million in September, up about 7% from 119 million a year earlier. It also reports 88.2 million PS4 and PS5 full games sold worldwide in the quarter, up from 80.3 million, and play time up 4%. First-party titles accounted for 8.2 million of those units, up from 6.3 million.

The first-party figure matters because Sony's profit on its own games is typically higher than on titles from outside publishers, though the filing does not give a split of profit by type. Sony's filing credits higher sales of both first-party and non-first-party software, along with higher network services sales, for the sales increase.

## The forecast was raised, and the reasons are mixed

Sony lifted its full-year outlook for the segment. The filing shows the November forecast at ¥4,830 billion in sales and ¥700 billion in operating income, against the July forecast of ¥4,540 billion and ¥660 billion. That is a ¥290 billion increase in sales and a ¥40 billion increase in operating income, or about 6% each.

Sony attributes the revision to higher sales of non-first-party titles, improved costs and higher network services sales. It partly offsets those with lower first-party software sales, which it says are mainly due to a change in a title's release date. The filing does not name the title in the portion we reviewed, so we are not guessing at which game moved.

For context, the segment earned ¥463.3 billion in operating income on ¥4,685.7 billion of sales in the prior fiscal year, per the same filing. The new forecast implies operating income about 51% higher on sales about 3% higher, which is a margin of roughly 14.5% against about 9.9% the year before. Those comparisons come from our arithmetic on Sony's figures. Because last year's total also carries the Bungie-related charges, part of the implied margin gain is the same base effect described above. Sony's forecast also shows the company raising its group-wide outlook, with operating income now at ¥1.87 trillion against ¥1.72 trillion, [per XenoSpectrum](https://xenospectrum.com/en/sony-ps5-shipments-profit-growth/).

## What to watch next

A few things will decide whether this quarter was a turning point or a pleasant stretch:

- **Hardware unit trend.** A 35% decline in shipments in one quarter is large. If it continues, software and services attach to a smaller installed-base growth rate, which tests how much of the profit lift is durable.
- **The delayed first-party title.** Sony says a release-date change is weighing on first-party sales in its forecast. When that game ships, and how it sells, will affect both the margin mix and the second-half comparison.
- **Network services growth.** An increase of about 19% in a single quarter is strong. Investors following the segment should look for whether that pace holds without a promotional push, which Sony's filing does not discuss.
- **Currency.** With foreign exchange adding ¥73.0 billion to segment sales and ¥8.7 billion to operating income this quarter per the filing, a move in the yen can change reported growth without any change in the underlying business.

None of this is a verdict on the stock. It is a reminder that the 83% figure is the least informative number in Sony's release. The adjusted increase, the shift in revenue toward software and services, and the fall in console shipments together describe a business that is making more money from a base that is growing more slowly.
