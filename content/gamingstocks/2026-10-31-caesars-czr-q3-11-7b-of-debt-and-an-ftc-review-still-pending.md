## What happened

Caesars Entertainment (CZR) reported third-quarter results this week, and for a company with a signed buyout the numbers matter in an unusual way. Shareholders will get $31.00 in cash per share from Fertitta Entertainment whatever the quarter looked like. What the quarter does show is the asset the buyer is taking on, and how much debt sits on top of it.

According to the company's [10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001590895/000159089526000033/czr-20260930.htm), net revenues were $2,940M, up from $2,869M a year earlier. The net loss was $107M, against $39M in the third quarter of 2025. Net loss attributable to Caesars was $123M, versus $55M. A [summary of the results](https://affpapa.com/caesars-q3-2026-revenue-rises-to-2-94b) puts adjusted EBITDA at $865M, down 2.1%, with operating income falling to $457M from $513M as operating expenses rose to $2.48B from $2.36B. Caesars held no earnings call because of the pending sale, per the same report.

Revenue grew, but profit did not follow. That is the basic story of the quarter.

## Where the weakness is

The segments split unevenly.

- **Las Vegas:** revenue fell to $930M from $952M in the 10-Q, a 2.3% decline. Adjusted EBITDA dropped 9.5% to $343M, according to the [AffPapa summary](https://affpapa.com/caesars-q3-2026-revenue-rises-to-2-94b), which cites fewer conventions and weaker leisure travel. Casino revenue rose 10.4% on stronger table-game hold, so the underlying trend is softer than the casino line suggests.
- **Regional:** revenue rose to $1,627M from $1,536M, about 5.9%. The same summary credits the addition of Caesars Windsor in March plus investment in Lake Tahoe and New Orleans.
- **Caesars Digital:** revenue was $326M against $311M. Adjusted EBITDA rose 57.1% to $44M on higher hold and lower marketing costs. Sports betting handle was up 1.2% to $2.51B and iGaming handle up 1.5% to $4.83B.

The digital gain came from hold and cheaper marketing rather than a jump in handle, which keeps the focus on how much of online betting profit depends on hold and promotional spend rather than volume. Vegas is the opposite case: the casino floor won on hold while the rest of the business weakened.

## The balance sheet Fertitta is buying

Total debt at Sept. 30 was $11,659M at face value and $11,563M at book value, per the 10-Q. The AffPapa summary lists net interest expense at $570M for the quarter, which is more than the company's $457M of operating income.

That mismatch explains the net loss and why this is a leveraged deal. [Reported terms](https://www.acquiry.com/deal-intelligence/caesars-fertitta-entertainment-take-private/) put total transaction value at about $17.6B, of which roughly $11.9B is Caesars debt the buyer assumes and about $5.7B is equity. The two debt figures differ slightly because the 10-Q reports debt at Sept. 30 while the deal figure is a headline estimate from that secondary source; treat the $11.9B as approximate.

For lenders and bondholders, the point is that a take-private does not erase the debt. It changes who stands behind it.

## What still stands between signing and closing

The merger agreement is dated May 27, 2026. Shareholders approved it at a Sept. 22 special meeting, with about 65.4% of all shares outstanding voting in favor, per the [Acquiry summary](https://www.acquiry.com/deal-intelligence/caesars-fertitta-entertainment-take-private/). That removes the shareholder risk but not the regulatory one.

Caesars and Fertitta each received a second request from the FTC on Sept. 14, according to Caesars' [8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001590895/000119312526393853/d242276d8k.htm). The waiting period now runs until 30 days after each side has substantially complied, unless the parties extend it or the FTC ends it early. The company said it intends to "continue to work cooperatively with the FTC." The filing does not say how long compliance will take.

The 10-Q lays out the schedule and the money at stake:

- **Ticking fee:** if the deal has not closed by June 26, 2027, shareholders receive an extra amount per share for each additional day, which the 10-Q ties to that date.
- **Outside date:** May 27, 2027, automatically extended to Aug. 27, 2027 and then Nov. 27, 2027 if only regulatory conditions remain unsatisfied.
- **Break fees:** Caesars would pay $200M in certain circumstances. Fertitta Gaming would pay $450M in certain circumstances, including a regulatory prohibition on the merger.

The secondary source cited above puts the ticking fee at $0.00715 per share per day, which would be roughly $2.61 per share over a full year. The 10-Q text I reviewed did not state the daily rate, so that number rests on one outside source.

The 10-Q also does not report where the HSR review or the state gaming approvals stand. It lists the conditions, not their status. That is the main unknown. Change-of-control approvals are still needed in states where Caesars holds licenses, and the same outside source names New Jersey and Pennsylvania, with none reported granted.

## The wider deal picture

Caesars is one of several live gaming transactions, and they point in the same direction: scale and ownership changes.

- **Lottomatica and Cirsa:** an all-share merger announced in early September, with Lottomatica holders owning about 67.5% and Cirsa holders about 32.5%, and Blackstone becoming the largest shareholder at about 24%, according to [Casino.org](https://www.casino.org/news/lottomatica-cirsa-agree-merger-to-create-global-gaming-giant/). Pro forma adjusted EBITDA is about EUR 2 billion, with expected annual run-rate synergies of about EUR 115 million and closing targeted for the second quarter of 2027.
- **Xsolla and GamePix:** on Oct. 29, Xsolla [announced](https://xsolla.prezly.com/xsolla-acquires-gamepix-to-build-a-complete-platform-for-web-and-ai-created-video-games) it acquired GamePix, a browser-game publisher with more than 10,000 titles and over 4,000 developers. The price was not disclosed. It is small, but it shows infrastructure companies buying distribution rather than building it.

Neither deal tells us much about Caesars' timing, but together they show that the pending approvals, not the price, are the common variable across gaming M&A right now.

## What to watch next

- **FTC progress.** Any sign that the review is narrowing, or talk of divestitures where Caesars and Fertitta properties overlap, would shape the closing date. The 8-K gives no timetable, so watch for a new filing.
- **State approvals.** Filings or regulator agendas in New Jersey and Pennsylvania are the next visible checkpoints.
- **The June 26, 2027 marker.** Once the calendar approaches that date, the ticking fee starts to matter to anyone tracking the spread between the share price and $31.
- **Vegas trends.** If convention and leisure softness persists, the case for the buyer's leverage gets harder to make, though the contract's break terms limit how easily either side could walk away.

This is information and commentary, not a recommendation to buy or sell any security.
