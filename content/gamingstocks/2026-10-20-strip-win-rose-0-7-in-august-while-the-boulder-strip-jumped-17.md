## The headline number hides the split

Nevada's casinos won $1,266,105,114 from gamblers in August, up 3.14% from $1,227,573,173 a year earlier, according to the state's monthly report as relayed by [Fox5 Vegas](https://www.fox5vegas.com/2026/09/30/report-nevada-gaming-revenue-rises-314-august/). On its face that is a healthy month. The detail underneath is more interesting for anyone watching casino stocks into earnings.

The Las Vegas Strip, the part of the market most investors picture, won $684,087,062, up just 0.70% from $679,360,873 in August 2025. The growth came from elsewhere. The Boulder Strip rose 17.38% to $83,228,416. Washoe County in northern Nevada climbed 10.44% to $108,415,054, and Reno alone rose 15.69% to $79,426,602. [KSNV](https://news3lv.com/newsletter-daily/las-vegas-strip-gaming-win-august-2026-casinos-tourism-resorts-hospitality) names the Boulder Strip and Washoe County as the growth drivers.

Not everywhere moved up. In the same report, Downtown Las Vegas fell 2.96% to $61,501,577, Sparks fell 5.70%, Elko County fell 2.88% and South Lake Tahoe fell 1.95%. Clark County overall, which includes the Strip, rose 2.80% to $1,062,187,208.

## Doing the arithmetic on who drove the gain

The following is our own calculation from the reported figures. Statewide win rose by about $38.5 million year over year. The Strip contributed about $4.7 million of that, roughly 12%, even though it accounts for about 54% of the state's total win. Working back from the reported percentage changes, the Boulder Strip added roughly $12.3 million and Reno roughly $10.8 million. Those two areas alone, which together made up about 13% of statewide win, more than doubled the Strip's dollar gain.

One caution: gaming win is the casino's take after payouts, so it moves with hold (how much of the money wagered the house keeps) as well as with how much people bet. The state report as covered does not split out baccarat, slots or handle, so this data cannot tell us whether the Strip was soft on volume, on luck, or both. Also, one month is one month. [CDC Gaming Reports](https://cdcgaming.com/nevada-gaming-revenues-post-slight-gains-in-august/) notes that statewide win for the fiscal year that began July 1 is up 2.6%, so the broader trend is modestly positive rather than deteriorating.

The same CDC report says the state collected $72.5 million in percentage fees, down 5.98% from August 2025. That fee is based on the prior month's taxable revenue, so it reflects a different timing than the August win figures, and is a reminder not to read too much into any single line.

## This is a continuation, not a surprise

The pattern fits a story that was already visible in 2025. [The Nevada Independent reported in February](https://thenevadaindependent.com/article/as-strip-slumps-red-rock-and-boyd-find-success-in-las-vegas-locals-market) that in 2025 Strip visitation fell 7.5% while Strip gaming revenue was roughly flat against 2024. Other Southern Nevada markets all grew: Downtown up 2.1%, the Boulder Strip up 3.8%, and unincorporated Clark County up 1.2%.

The same article gave a look at how the locals-focused operators were responding. Boyd Gaming (BYD) said its Southern Nevada properties produced almost $1.1 billion in revenue in 2025, and that its Orleans property lost a combined $13 million in hotel revenue over the last two quarters of 2025 on lower visitation. Red Rock Resorts (RRR) reported Southern Nevada revenue just under $2 billion, and its CFO said the locals model depends on repeat visits rather than conventions or hotel revenue. Its vice chairman said Red Rock is also attracting higher-end customers who used to stay on the Strip. Red Rock was spending on a $200 million Green Valley Ranch renovation and a $385 million Durango expansion, per the same report.

August's numbers are consistent with that split continuing: a Strip that is flat in dollars, and a periphery that is growing. They do not prove it, since the monthly report covers regions rather than individual companies.

## What to watch in the next two weeks

Three Las Vegas-heavy operators report in quick succession, and each is a different read on the same question.

- **Boyd Gaming (BYD):** Thursday, October 22, shortly after 4:00 p.m. Eastern, with a call at 5:00 p.m. Eastern, per [its announcement](https://finviz.com/news/397784/boyd-gaming-to-report-third-quarter-2026-results-host-conference-call-and-webcast-on-october-22). Boyd's Las Vegas locals business is the cleanest test of whether the periphery strength shows up in company results. Listen for commentary on Southern Nevada margins and on its Henderson property, Cadence Crossing, which the Independent said was slated to open in late March.
- **MGM Resorts (MGM):** Wednesday, October 28, after the close, with a call at 5:00 p.m. Eastern, per [the company](https://finviz.com/news/396109/mgm-resorts-international-announces-third-quarter-earnings-release-date). MGM is the largest Strip operator, so its Las Vegas segment should show whether August's flat Strip win was typical of the quarter. Hold and visitation commentary will matter more than the headline.
- **Red Rock Resorts (RRR):** Tuesday, November 3, with a call at 4:30 p.m. Eastern, per [its announcement](https://finviz.com/news/401473/red-rock-resorts-announces-date-of-third-quarter-2026-conference-call-and-earnings-release-date). That is the other half of the locals picture, and the one to compare with Boyd.

We have not listed estimates for these reports because we did not verify current consensus figures. Check the companies' own releases and a market data provider for those.

## The takeaway for the sector

For investors, the useful framing is that "Las Vegas" is no longer one trade. A Strip operator and a locals operator can report the same state-level environment and sit on opposite sides of it, and the August data shows the gap in dollars: a statewide gain of about $38.5 million with the Strip a small slice of it. Whether that holds through the quarter is exactly what these three reports can answer. This is commentary on public data, not a recommendation on any stock.
