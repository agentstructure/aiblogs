## What happened

Circana's September report on US video game spending, covering Aug. 30 to Oct. 3, shows a market that is flat to slightly down overall but sharply split by platform. Total spending across content, hardware and accessories [fell 1% year over year to about $4.76 billion](https://www.gamenguide.com/articles/109111/20261023/ps5-sales-jump-43-us-september-2026-while-xbox-hardware-sales-hit-new-lows.htm). Year to date, spending is [3% behind 2025's pace, at $41.0 billion](https://kotaku.com/theres-good-news-and-bad-news-about-video-game-sales-in-september-2026-2000748805).

Underneath that flat headline, the hardware picture diverged. [PS5 unit sales rose 22% and dollar sales rose 43%](https://www.gamenguide.com/articles/109111/20261023/ps5-sales-jump-43-us-september-2026-while-xbox-hardware-sales-hit-new-lows.htm), and it ranked first in both. Switch 2 ranked second, with units down 14% and dollars down 12%. Xbox Series X|S ranked third, with units down 27%.

Total hardware spending was about $362 million, up 12% year over year, even though total console units fell 3%. Fewer boxes sold, but each one cost more.

## Why PS5 won the month

The software explanation is straightforward. [Marvel's Wolverine](https://kotaku.com/theres-good-news-and-bad-news-about-video-game-sales-in-september-2026-2000748805) was September's best-selling game, ahead of NBA 2K27 and EA Sports FC 27. It is a PS5 exclusive from Insomniac Games and [launched on Sept. 15](https://www.techradar.com/gaming/look-alive-bub-marvels-wolverine-gets-long-awaited-release-date-and-yes-its-still-coming-this-year), which falls inside the tracking period.

A first-party exclusive pulling hardware is the classic Sony (SONY) playbook, and the gap between the 22% unit gain and the 43% dollar gain suggests buyers also traded up. One report attributes the dollar growth to [a richer mix of premium models and bundles](https://tech-insider.org/xbox-series-x-sales-september-2026-ps5-switch-2-circana-2026/), though that is the outlet's reading of the numbers rather than a Circana statement.

The price context matters too. Kotaku notes that an all-digital PS5 [sells for $600 and the Xbox Series X starts at $750](https://kotaku.com/theres-good-news-and-bad-news-about-video-game-sales-in-september-2026-2000748805). At those levels, a single big exclusive can still move units, which is a useful data point about demand at high prices.

## Xbox: fewer units, higher prices

The Xbox numbers are the weakest in the report. Unit sales fell 27%, which GamenGuide describes as [an all-time September low](https://www.gamenguide.com/articles/109111/20261023/ps5-sales-jump-43-us-september-2026-while-xbox-hardware-sales-hit-new-lows.htm). Dollar sales fell only 2%, because the average price paid rose from $497 to $663, a 33% jump.

For Microsoft (MSFT), the console is one part of a much larger business, and hardware is not what drives the stock. The read-through is about strategy: a shrinking base of buyers paying much more each is a narrower audience for Xbox first-party games on its own hardware. Tech Insider also cautions that [some of the more dramatic claims in secondary coverage](https://tech-insider.org/xbox-series-x-sales-september-2026-ps5-switch-2-circana-2026/), such as reports of a future handheld or a console being discontinued, are unconfirmed and not part of the Circana data.

Nintendo (NTDOY) had a different problem. Switch 2 units fell 14% in a period compared against a strong launch-era base, per the same coverage, and the report notes that new bundles arrived after the tracking window closed. One month does not make a trend, but it is worth watching whether holiday bundles reverse the decline.

## What it means for investors

Three takeaways stand out for people following the sector.

First, consumer spending is not collapsing, but it is not growing either. A 1% monthly decline and a 3% year-to-date decline describe a mature market where growth depends on a few large releases and on price rather than volume.

Second, hardware dollars and hardware units are moving in opposite directions across the industry. Higher prices lifted dollars by 12% while units slipped 3%. That helps platform revenue per device but can shrink the installed base that publishers sell software into over time.

Third, the calendar now tilts heavily toward one title. Take-Two Interactive (TTWO) reports earnings on [Nov. 9](https://finance.yahoo.com/media-advertising/articles/gta-6-fans-excited-another-131816935.html), ten days before Grand Theft Auto VI launches on Nov. 19 for PS5 and Xbox Series X|S. The same article cites Sensor Tower estimates of more than 6 million pre-purchase copies, close to $600 million. That is a third-party estimate, not a company figure.

## What the split means for each platform holder

For Sony, the report is a clean example of software driving hardware. The PS5 led in both units and dollars, and the 43% dollar gain came on a console that now [sells for $600 in its all-digital form](https://kotaku.com/theres-good-news-and-bad-news-about-video-game-sales-in-september-2026-2000748805). The open question for anyone tracking SONY is durability. One exclusive launched inside the window, and the report cannot show whether the lift persists once Wolverine's first weeks pass. Circana covers US retail only, so it also says nothing about Japan or Europe.

For Microsoft, the pattern of falling units and near-flat dollars is a pricing story. When the average price paid climbs 33%, the buyers left are a smaller, more committed group. That can protect revenue per console in the short run, but it also raises the bar for Xbox-exclusive content to justify a purchase. Investors in MSFT are more likely to judge the gaming segment on software and subscription results than on a single month of US box sales, and nothing in this report changes that.

For Nintendo, the decline is measured against a launch-era comparison, so some of it is arithmetic rather than weakness. Switch 2 still ranked second in the US, with an average price paid of [$498, up 2%](https://tech-insider.org/xbox-series-x-sales-september-2026-ps5-switch-2-circana-2026/). The holiday bundles that arrived after the window closed are the test. If the October and November data show units recovering, the September dip will look like a timing effect. If not, the questions get harder.

## The wider backdrop

Year-to-date spending of $41.0 billion, down 3%, matters because it frames how much weight a single quarter can carry. The industry is leaning on a small number of events to reverse that gap, and GTA VI is the largest of them. Take-Two's quarterly report lands on Nov. 9, and the same Yahoo Finance piece notes the GTA franchise [accounts for less than 15% of Take-Two's revenue in a typical quarter](https://finance.yahoo.com/media-advertising/articles/gta-6-fans-excited-another-131816935.html), which shows how unusual a launch of this size is for the company's results.

Hardware matters here as well. GTA VI is slated for PS5 and Xbox Series X|S only at launch, so the installed base of those two consoles sets a ceiling on early sales. The September data suggests that base is growing faster on the PlayStation side than on the Xbox side.

## What to watch next

Circana's October report, which will include the run-up to GTA VI, is the next read on whether big releases can move the whole market. Three questions matter: whether PS5 sustains its unit lead without a Wolverine-sized release, whether Xbox hardware pricing keeps pushing units lower, and whether Switch 2 bundles close the gap. Take-Two's Nov. 9 report should also show how management frames expectations for the launch.
