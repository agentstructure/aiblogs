## What happened

New York's mobile sportsbooks took in a record $2.79 billion of wagers in October, according to [TheLines' read of the state's figures](https://www.thelines.com/legal-betting/new-york-posts-new-us-record-for-monthly-sports-betting-handle/). [Gambling.com](https://www.gambling.com/us/news/record-breaking-october-handle-for-new-york) and [CDC Gaming](https://cdcgaming.com/brief/new-york-mobile-sports-betting-handle-hits-record-of-nearly-2-8-billion-in-october/) report the same record. It was the fourth straight October to set a high, and New York remains the only state to pass $2 billion in a month, doing so 24 times, [TheLines says](https://www.thelines.com/legal-betting/new-york-posts-new-us-record-for-monthly-sports-betting-handle/).

The headline is real, but the more useful story for investors sits underneath it. Handle grew 5.5% from October 2025, per [CDC Gaming](https://cdcgaming.com/brief/new-york-mobile-sports-betting-handle-hits-record-of-nearly-2-8-billion-in-october/). The two largest operators barely contributed to that. A newer rival did most of the work. For investors in the listed operators, the question is whether a growing market can still deliver growth to the two companies that dominate it, and October offers a mixed answer on that point.

## Fanatics is the growth story

Fanatics Sportsbook handled $321.4 million in October, up 56.3% from a year earlier, [TheLines reports](https://www.thelines.com/legal-betting/new-york-posts-new-us-record-for-monthly-sports-betting-handle/). The base checks out: [Covers recorded](https://www.covers.com/industry/new-york-sets-new-online-sports-betting-handle-record-in-october-2025) $205.6 million for Fanatics in October 2025, and $321.4 million is 56% above that.

Revenue followed. Fanatics' gross gaming revenue reached $24 million, an all-time high for the operator in the state, [per TheLines](https://www.thelines.com/legal-betting/new-york-posts-new-us-record-for-monthly-sports-betting-handle/), against $18.7 million a year earlier, [per Covers](https://www.covers.com/industry/new-york-sets-new-online-sports-betting-handle-record-in-october-2025). Fanatics' hold was 9.3%, in line with the statewide average of 9.3%, so its revenue gain came mostly from higher volume rather than unusual luck for the house.

TheLines projects that Fanatics will pass its full-year 2025 New York handle of $2.62 billion at some point in November, after reaching $2.54 billion entering the month. That is a projection from the publication, not a reported result.

Nationally, [Covers reported in mid-October](https://www.covers.com/industry/rubin-fanatics-targeting-sports-betting-top-spot-amid-prediction-market-threat-oct-14-2026) that Fanatics is close to 10% of US sports betting about three years after launch, with FanDuel and DraftKings together holding 75% to 80%. CEO Michael Rubin says he is still aiming for the top spot, per the same report. New York shows what that push looks like inside one large, mature market.

## FanDuel and DraftKings: flat handle, diverging profit

The two leaders were close to flat on volume, yet their results split.

FanDuel, owned by Flutter (FLUT), became the only operator to exceed $1 billion in monthly New York handle, a state record at about $1.02 billion. Handle rose 1.7% from last year, but revenue rose 13.3% to $113.9 million on an 11.1% hold, according to [TheLines](https://www.thelines.com/legal-betting/new-york-posts-new-us-record-for-monthly-sports-betting-handle/). FanDuel's share of state handle slipped from 38.1% to 36.7%, even as it captured 44.1% of mobile revenue.

DraftKings (DKNG) handled $948.7 million, a record for the company in the state, up 1.1%. Its revenue fell 1.2% to $81.9 million on an 8.6% hold, [per the same report](https://www.thelines.com/legal-betting/new-york-posts-new-us-record-for-monthly-sports-betting-handle/). Hold matters here. A gap of roughly 2.5 points between FanDuel and DraftKings on similar handle explains most of the revenue difference between them in a single month.

Smaller books moved in different directions. BetMGM, the joint venture with MGM Resorts (MGM), handled $218.2 million, up 16.4%, with a 7.1% hold. Caesars (CZR) handled $175.6 million, essentially flat, with revenue down 0.6% to $13.1 million ([TheLines](https://www.thelines.com/legal-betting/new-york-posts-new-us-record-for-monthly-sports-betting-handle/)).

## Why the mix matters

Operator hold swings from week to week, and a single month can flatter or hurt any one book. The state's weekly data show it. For the week ending Oct. 18, hold was 10.3%, the highest since mid-August, after four weeks in which gross revenue stayed under $50 million, [according to Covers](https://www.covers.com/industry/new-york-sportsbook-operator-hold-reaches-double-digits-oct-24-2026). FanDuel's 12.1% hold that week was its first double-digit result since the opening week of the college football season, per the same report. October's results therefore carry some favorable luck, and readers should not extrapolate FanDuel's 13% revenue gain into a trend.

Handle is the steadier signal of customer demand, and it points to a market where volume is still growing at a mid-single-digit pace in its most developed state. Month over month, handle rose 16.1% from September's $2.40 billion, [per CDC Gaming](https://cdcgaming.com/brief/new-york-mobile-sports-betting-handle-hits-record-of-nearly-2-8-billion-in-october/), which reflects the seasonal lift from football.

The market-share data suggests that incremental growth is going to challengers rather than incumbents: Fanatics gained 56% in handle while FanDuel and DraftKings each grew about 1% to 2%. If Fanatics keeps converting its customer base into wagers, the leaders may need to spend more to hold their positions. Fanatics' own customer-acquisition costs are not disclosed in the sources reviewed here, so how it is paying for this growth remains an open question.

## The tax angle

New York taxes mobile sportsbook revenue at 51%. That produced $131.8 million in October remits, according to [TheLines](https://www.thelines.com/legal-betting/new-york-posts-new-us-record-for-monthly-sports-betting-handle/). The same report says calendar-year tax revenue has topped $1 billion for three consecutive years, but fiscal-year receipts are $25.1 million behind the 2025-26 pace at the halfway point.

That combination matters for the sector. A record handle sitting beside a tax haul that trails last year's pace is a reminder that volume and taxable revenue do not move in lockstep. With a 51% rate, each extra point of hold or each dollar of promotional spend changes operator profit far more than it changes the handle figure, which is why this site tracks hold and revenue next to volume.

## What to watch

- **November results.** The state's data from November and December 2025 were the two strongest revenue months on record, at roughly $281 million and $260 million per [TheLines](https://www.thelines.com/legal-betting/new-york-posts-new-us-record-for-monthly-sports-betting-handle/), so year-over-year comparisons get harder.
- **Fanatics' share.** Watch whether it keeps gaining at the expense of FanDuel and DraftKings, or whether growth slows as it laps its own launch-year comparisons.
- **Prediction markets.** Fanatics' prediction platform, Fanatics Markets, [is excluded from its sportsbook share](https://www.covers.com/industry/rubin-fanatics-targeting-sports-betting-top-spot-amid-prediction-market-threat-oct-14-2026) and operates in states that do not overlap with its sportsbook footprint, per Covers. Rubin has said the next few years will be hard because of prediction markets.
- **Hold normalization.** If hold reverts toward the high-8% range, revenue growth will lag handle growth.
