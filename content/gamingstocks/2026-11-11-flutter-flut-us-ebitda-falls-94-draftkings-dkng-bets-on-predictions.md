## What happened

The two largest US sportsbook operators reported third-quarter results within a day of each other, and the headline is the same in both: customers and wagers are growing, and the profit that growth should produce is being spent on prediction markets.

Flutter Entertainment (FLUT), parent of FanDuel, [reported on Nov. 4](https://finviz.com/news/415228/flutter-entertainment-plc-q3-2026-earnings-release) that US revenue rose 6% to $1,455 million, while US handle (stakes) rose 13% to $12,023 million. US adjusted EBITDA, however, fell 94% to $3 million from $51 million a year earlier. Group adjusted EBITDA fell 25% to $360 million from $478 million, on group revenue of $3,874 million, up 2%.

DraftKings (DKNG) [reported on Nov. 5](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001883685/000188368526000039/q326-prx8kexx991.htm) that revenue rose 16.2% to $1,329.1 million. Its sports consumer volume, a measure that includes prediction markets and excludes market makers, rose 21.9% to $13,900.2 million. Adjusted EBITDA was a loss of $316.9 million, against a loss of $126.5 million a year earlier. The net loss attributable to common stockholders was $468.7 million, or $0.94 per share, up from $256.8 million, or $0.52 per share.

The two companies did not have the same quarter on the sportsbook itself. That is worth separating from the prediction-market story.

## Hold cut one way and helped the other

Flutter's US net revenue margin was 6.8%, down 60 basis points from 7.4%. The company said sports results were a 10 basis point headwind, or about $38 million of revenue, and that its structural revenue margin was unchanged at 12.9%. Management also said handle growth was "well ahead of expectations." In plain terms, Flutter's US book grew faster than it planned and still lost 60 basis points of margin, only 10 of which the company attributed to sports results.

DraftKings went the other way. Its sports net revenue margin rose to 5.6% from 5.2%, which the company named, along with customer engagement, as the driver of revenue growth. Monthly unique payers rose about 13% to 4.1 million, and average revenue per payer rose about 3% to $109.

So one operator had a margin tailwind and still posted a $316.9 million adjusted EBITDA loss. The other had a small margin headwind and posted $3 million of US EBITDA. Margin variance did not decide the quarter for either. That points to the cost side.

## Predictions are the cost line

Both companies are explicit about where the money is going.

DraftKings now guides its 2026 Predictions adjusted EBITDA investment at $650 million to $700 million, with $175 million to $225 million of it in the fourth quarter. It expects Predictions revenue to be "immaterial" this year. Full-year 2026 guidance is $6.35 billion to $6.55 billion of revenue and $175 million to $275 million of adjusted EBITDA. The company says its core business, excluding Predictions, should deliver $875 million to $925 million. The gap between those two EBITDA ranges is the investment: at midpoints, $900 million of core EBITDA less roughly $675 million of Predictions spend lands near the $225 million midpoint of the consolidated range. That is our arithmetic, not a company statement.

Flutter is spending less, but it is not spending nothing. Its release expects a prediction-market adjusted EBITDA loss of approximately $200 million in 2026. FanDuel Predicts had 66,000 average monthly players in the third quarter, up 39% from the second, and $119 million of volume, up 102% sequentially. Revenue was not material in the quarter. October volume was up 271% from July.

Those growth rates come off a very small base. 66,000 players compares with 3,899,000 US average monthly players across FanDuel's core business, which rose 12%. The product is early, and the company is paying to build it before it earns.

## What changed in guidance

Flutter cut its 2026 outlook. At the midpoint, group revenue guidance fell $300 million to $17.61 billion and group adjusted EBITDA fell $160 million to $2.495 billion from $2.655 billion. US revenue guidance fell $60 million to $7.34 billion and US adjusted EBITDA fell $45 million to $715 million. Flutter also said its share repurchase program is paused "for the foreseeable future." The release counts $250 million of 2026 repurchases as complete.

Flutter's quarter also included an $873 million non-cash impairment, made up of $296 million on long-lived assets and $541 million of goodwill. The release attributes $537 million post-tax to Flutter at its 56% ownership. That charge relates to Brazil rather than the US business, so it explains the group loss but not the US margin story above.

DraftKings introduced 2027 guidance, which is where the investment case now sits:

- Revenue of $7.35 billion to $7.75 billion, about 17% growth at the midpoint.
- Adjusted EBITDA of $800 million to $1.0 billion, including $300 million of Predictions investment at the midpoint.
- Core revenue of $6.7 billion to $7.0 billion and core adjusted EBITDA of $1.15 billion to $1.25 billion.
- Predictions revenue of $650 million to $750 million against $250 million to $350 million of investment.

DraftKings ended the quarter with $1,100.9 million of cash and cash equivalents, down from $1,127.5 million at Dec. 31, 2025. It also held $557.0 million of cash reserved for users. The cash balance has been roughly flat despite the losses, though the release excerpt I reviewed does not show how it was funded.

## Why it matters for the sector

For investors following sports betting stocks, the useful change is that "state legalization" is no longer the only growth lever. Both operators are now building prediction products alongside their licensed sportsbooks, and both are paying for it out of current earnings.

That makes the next few quarters a test of three things.

First, does Predictions revenue arrive on the schedule DraftKings has published? Its 2027 range of $650 million to $750 million implies a step from "immaterial" to a business with revenue similar to the size of its investment this year. There is no prior-year base to judge that against.

Second, does core margin hold without help from sports results? DraftKings guided core 2027 EBITDA of $1.15 billion to $1.25 billion, against $875 million to $925 million this year, so the plan needs core earnings to grow while the new product matures.

Third, what happens to buybacks and balance sheets? Flutter paused repurchases. DraftKings did not announce any in the figures I reviewed, and its cash is roughly flat.

## What to watch next

Watch for the fourth-quarter prints against the numbers already given: DraftKings' $175 million to $225 million Predictions spend and Flutter's roughly $200 million full-year Predictions loss. If Q4 spend lands inside those bands, guidance is credible. If either company has to move them again, the sector will start valuing prediction markets as an open-ended cost rather than a funded one.

Also watch FanDuel's US EBITDA. Flutter's full-year US guidance of $615 million to $815 million, with a $715 million midpoint, requires a far stronger fourth quarter than the $3 million posted in the third, and the football and basketball calendar will decide whether that is realistic.
