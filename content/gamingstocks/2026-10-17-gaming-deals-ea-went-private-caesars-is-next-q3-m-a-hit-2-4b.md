## The deal map in one paragraph

Two very different pictures of gaming M&A sit side by side this autumn. Drake Star Partners' [Global Gaming Report for Q3 2026](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/gaming-manda-reached-24bn-across-45-deals-in-q3-2026/) counts $2.4 billion of M&A across 45 deals in the quarter. Meanwhile, the biggest transactions in the sector are not small studio pickups at all. They are take-privates that remove well-known tickers from the market: Electronic Arts, which has already gone, and Caesars Entertainment (CZR), which is in the middle of its regulatory review.

For investors in listed gaming stocks, the useful question is less "who bought whom" than "what is left to own, and what do buyers seem to be paying for?"

## Q3: many small deals, one mid-sized sale

The Drake Star data, as reported by PocketGamer.biz, shows a market of volume rather than size. The largest Q3 deal was Alibaba's sale of Lingxi Games to Trustar Capital for more than $1.5 billion. Kunlun Tech sold mobile chess and card developer Xianlai Interactive Entertainment for about $105 million. Corsair acquired sim racing hardware maker Trak Racer, and Tripledot Studios bought Supersonic from Unity.

Two things stand out. First, a single Lingxi sale accounts for well over half of the quarter's $2.4 billion total, so the 45-deal count overstates how broad the large-deal market is. Second, the sellers include corporate parents pruning portfolios (Alibaba, Kunlun, Unity) rather than founders cashing out of hot assets.

Private capital is flowing elsewhere. The same report puts Q3 private funding at more than $1.6 billion across 106 rounds, with AI and tools taking over 80% of it. Drake Star expects M&A to stay strong through the end of 2026 as buyers look for established IP, scale and recurring revenue. The report also notes that GTA VI's planned November 19 launch could lift investor sentiment heading into 2027, a reminder that Take-Two (TTWO) is a catalyst for the sector's mood even when it is not a party to a deal.

## EA: the template is now complete

Electronic Arts is the largest example of what buyers will pay for scale and recurring revenue. [EA announced](https://www.ea.com/news/ea-announces-completion-of-acquisition) the acquisition by a consortium of the Public Investment Fund (PIF), Silver Lake and Affinity Partners closed on August 4, 2026, with stockholders receiving $210 in cash per share and the stock delisted from NASDAQ. The deal was announced on September 29, 2025, and EA stockholders approved it on December 22, 2025, so the path from announcement to close ran about ten months.

[Game Informer](https://gameinformer.com/2026/08/04/electronic-arts-has-been-fully-acquired-by-trio-of-investors-and-is-now-private) describes the $55 billion transaction as the largest all-cash sponsor take-private investment in history. For public-market investors, the practical effect is simple: one of the few large Western publishers with annual sports franchises such as Madden NFL 27 and FC 27 is no longer investable. What remains in the listed group includes Take-Two, and the exposure to sports and live-service revenue that EA once supplied now has fewer public proxies.

## Caesars: approved by shareholders, now in the FTC's hands

The casino side has its own take-private. Under the [merger agreement filed with the SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001590895/000119312526242995/d143382dex991.htm), Fertitta Entertainment agreed on May 28, 2026 to buy Caesars for $31.00 per share in cash, a deal valued at about $17.6 billion including roughly $11.9 billion of assumed debt. The price was a 49% premium to Caesars' unaffected share price as of February 25, 2026.

The sequence since then:

- **Go-shop.** The agreement allowed Caesars to solicit rival bids through July 11. [SCCG reports](https://sccgmanagement.com/sccg-articles/2026/09/17/caesars-confirms-board-resignations-tied-to-icahn-and-second-ftc-request-on-fertitta-merger/) that Carl Icahn's group, which owns about 5% of Caesars, raised an all-cash offer to $34 per share on the last day of that window, and that it was rejected in favor of Fertitta's $31.
- **Nevada.** In July the Nevada Gaming Control Board [unanimously approved](https://www.hoteldive.com/news/nevada-regulators-approve-fertitta-executives-amid-caesars-acquisition-process/824936/) two Fertitta executives in suitability reviews. Fertitta's general counsel said gaming approvals across Caesars' jurisdictions could take up to 10 months.
- **Shareholders.** [Hotel Dive reports](https://www.hoteldive.com/news/caesars-shareholders-approve-acquisition-by-fertitta-entertainment/831287/) that Caesars shareholders approved the deal at a special meeting held on a Tuesday, which the article's timing indicates was September 22. Holders of about 143.28 million shares, or 70.3% of the outstanding total, voted.
- **FTC.** Caesars and Fertitta each received a second request from the Federal Trade Commission, [confirmed in mid-September](https://www.gambling.com/us/news/caesars-confirms-ftc-second-request-on-fertitta-buyout), and two Icahn-affiliated directors resigned from the Caesars board around the same time. SCCG says the companies have 30 days under the Hart-Scott-Rodino Act to comply with the request.

## What the structure tells you

The Caesars agreement has features worth reading, because they define the risk for anyone holding the stock before closing. Shareholders get $31.00 in cash plus a per-share ticking fee if the deal has not closed by June 26, 2027. The initial end date is May 27, 2027, and it extends automatically, first to August 27, 2027 and then to November 27, 2027, if only regulatory conditions remain unmet. Caesars owes termination fees of $200 million or $100 million in certain cases, while the buyer owes a $450 million reverse termination fee in certain regulatory scenarios.

In other words, the contract anticipates a long review. A stock subject to a cash offer tends to trade on the odds and timing of closing rather than on operations, which is why quarterly results matter less here than regulatory headlines. Note the contrast with the EA case: that one closed about ten months after announcement, with a sponsor-led consortium. Caesars needs gaming approval in every jurisdiction where it operates, which Fertitta's counsel says could take up to 10 months.

## What to watch

- **FTC timing.** Whether the second-request review stays on the schedule the agreement implies, or pushes toward the later outside dates.
- **Gaming regulators.** Each state sign-off is a discrete event. Fertitta's own estimate was up to 10 months from July.
- **Remaining listed gaming names.** With EA delisted and Caesars set to follow, buyers have shown they will pay cash premiums for scale. Whether that appetite extends to other publishers, casino operators or betting companies is the open question for the rest of the year.
- **Take-Two and GTA VI.** The November 19 launch date cited in the Drake Star report is the next big sentiment event for the publisher group.
