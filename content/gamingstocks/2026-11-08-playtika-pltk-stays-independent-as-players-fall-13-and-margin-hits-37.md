## What happened

Playtika Holding (PLTK), the mobile game company behind Slotomania, Bingo Blitz and June's Journey, reported third-quarter results on Nov. 5 and closed a chapter at the same time. Its board [concluded its review of strategic alternatives](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001828016/000182801626000053/a2026q3ex991-earningsrelea.htm) and decided the company will stay independent and publicly traded. On the call, CEO Robert Antokol framed the decision as a bet on the existing plan, and the [transcript](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/11/05/playtika-pltk-q3-2026-earnings-call-transcript/) quotes the board as concluding that "the best path to a long-term value is to execute our plan."

The numbers behind that decision are unusual. Revenue was $673.8 million, down 0.1% from $674.6 million a year earlier and down 7.8% from the second quarter, according to the company's [earnings release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001828016/000182801626000053/a2026q3ex991-earningsrelea.htm). Yet adjusted EBITDA rose 15.9% to $252.1 million, a record margin of 37.4% against 32.2% in the third quarter of 2025 ($217.5 million). Net income was $40.4 million, compared with $39.1 million a year ago.

The Associated Press, using Zacks data, [put adjusted earnings at 32 cents a share](https://wtop.com/national/2026/11/playtika-q3-earnings-snapshot) against a three-analyst average of 20 cents, and revenue above a $664.9 million estimate. Some data providers instead flagged a miss because they compared the 11-cent GAAP figure with that adjusted estimate. The two readings differ because of the yardstick, not the business.

## Fewer players, each worth more

The user data is where the quarter gets interesting. According to the release, average daily active users were 7.1 million, down from 8.2 million a year earlier, a 13.4% decline. Average daily paying users were 333,000, down 5.9% from 354,000 and down 9.3% from the second quarter.

Revenue held flat because the remaining audience spends more. Average revenue per daily active user was $1.03, up from $0.89, a rise of roughly 16% by our calculation. Payer conversion, the share of daily players who pay, reached 4.7%, up from 4.3% a year ago and 4.6% last quarter.

That is the bull case in miniature: a smaller, more loyal base that converts better. Antokol said on the call that the business is durable "because our most loyal players stay with us, and they keep spending." It is also the risk. A casino-style social game lives on a funnel, and a funnel that shrinks 13% in a year has to be refilled somewhere. A [call summary](https://www.gurufocus.com/news/9177951/playtika-holding-corp-pltk-q3-2026-earnings-call-highlights-record-adjusted-ebitda-margin-of-374-as-dtc-revenue-hits-408-of-total-revenue) attributes Bingo Blitz's decline mainly to exiting lower-retention acquisition channels, which fits a cheaper-marketing strategy but does not by itself prove the audience will regrow.

## Direct-to-consumer is carrying the margin

The structural story is Playtika's push to sell directly to players through its own web stores instead of paying app-store fees. Direct-to-consumer (DTC) revenue was $275.1 million, up 31.4% from a year ago and down 4.1% from the prior quarter, per the release. As a share of the $673.8 million total, that is about 40.8% by our calculation, just above the 40% run-rate goal executive Tae Lee said on the call the company set a year ago with a two-year horizon.

This matters for the stock because DTC sales skip most of the platform fee. When a rising share of revenue avoids that cost, margin can expand even if the top line stalls, which is exactly what this quarter showed.
The caution is that fee savings are a one-time step, not a growth engine. Once most payers have moved to the web store, the margin gain stops compounding and the business has to grow players or spend per player on its own.

## Game by game: one engine slowing, one new one running

The release shows a split portfolio:

- **Bingo Blitz:** $133.0 million, down 18.2% year over year and 8.3% sequentially.
- **Disney Solitaire**, from the SuperPlay studio Playtika acquired: $111.2 million, up 88.4% year over year but down 21.9% from the second quarter.
- **June's Journey:** $76.6 million, up 12.1% year over year and 2.4% sequentially.

Disney Solitaire is the swing factor. It more than offsets Bingo Blitz on a year-over-year basis, but the 21.9% sequential drop is a reminder that a young hit can spike and settle. Playtika's growth case leans on titles like it and June's Journey, now in its ninth year, to replace a bingo franchise that is shrinking.

## The balance sheet is the real constraint

Ending the sale review removes one possible catalyst and puts the focus on the debt. At Sept. 30, Playtika held [$594.6 million in cash and short-term investments](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001828016/000182801626000053/a2026q3ex991-earningsrelea.htm) against $2,370.3 million of non-current debt plus $10.8 million of current maturities. Net of cash, that is roughly $1.79 billion by our calculation, or about 2.3 to 2.4 times the $750 million to $790 million adjusted EBITDA range the company reaffirmed for 2026.

Two items need cash. The release lists a $550 million revolving credit facility that expires in March 2027, so refinancing is a near-term task. Management said it plans to address upcoming maturities well ahead of their due dates and does not intend to raise total debt, according to [a call summary](https://www.gurufocus.com/news/9177951/playtika-holding-corp-pltk-q3-2026-earnings-call-highlights-record-adjusted-ebitda-margin-of-374-as-dtc-revenue-hits-408-of-total-revenue). Separately, contingent consideration tied to acquisitions stood at $450.0 million, of which $320.0 million is current, after the company paid $350.0 million in the first nine months. In Q3 alone, an $80.0 million increase in the estimated value of that obligation weighed on general and administrative expense.

Free cash flow is also softer than the margin headline suggests: $177.5 million for the nine months, versus $217.9 million a year earlier, per the release. Margins improved, but cash generation after interest and earn-outs has not yet followed.

## What to watch next

Playtika reaffirmed 2026 guidance of $2.75 billion to $2.85 billion in revenue and $750 million to $790 million in adjusted EBITDA, and said it expects to land near the midpoint of both. Nine-month revenue was $2,149.6 million versus $2,076.6 million a year ago, according to the [Finviz reprint of the release](https://finviz.com/news/416606/playtika-holding-corp-reports-q3-2026-financial-results). At the $2.80 billion midpoint, that implies fourth-quarter revenue of roughly $650 million, below the third quarter's $673.8 million, by our calculation.

The questions for the next report are straightforward. Does daily active user decline narrow once the pullback from weaker acquisition channels is behind it? Does DTC keep rising above 40% of revenue without Disney Solitaire's sequential slide repeating in other titles? And what refinancing terms does the company get on its revolver and upcoming maturities? Those answers will say more about the value of an independent Playtika than the decision to skip a sale.
