## What the October numbers say

Macau's casinos won MOP21.68 billion (about US$2.68 billion) in October, down 10% from MOP24.09 billion a year earlier, according to figures from the territory's gaming regulator reported by [Intergame](https://www.intergameonline.com/land-based-gaming/news/macau-october-ggr-falls-10-to-mop21-68bn). The month contained the mainland's National Day Golden Week holiday, normally the strongest stretch of the autumn, and revenue still fell.

Two other facts frame the number. First, October was up 20% from September's MOP18.06 billion, so the sequential picture is a rebound off a weak month, not a new high. Second, this is the fifth straight month of year-on-year declines, and [Focus Gaming News](https://focusgn.com/asia-pacific/macau-ggr-october-revenue) calls it the second-steepest fall of 2026.

Year to date, the picture is less grim. Revenue for the first ten months reached MOP208.8 billion, up 1.6% from a year earlier but about 15% below the 2019 level of MOP246.7 billion, per [The Standard](https://www.thestandard.com.hk/finance/article/347138/Macau-gaming-revenue-declines-10pc-in-October-despite-Golden-Week-boost). The first ten months averaged MOP20.88 billion a month. The government's full-year forecast of MOP236 billion implies a 12-month average of MOP19.67 billion, the same report notes.

## The slide has been accelerating

The October drop is steeper than the third quarter. Macau's casinos took in MOP60.22 billion in the July to September period, down 3.8% from a year earlier and 1.3% from the second quarter, according to [Yogonet's summary of the regulator's data](https://www.yogonet.com/international/news/2026/10/20/126913-macau-third-quarter-casino-revenue-slips-38-on-weaker-mass-baccarat).

The composition of that quarter matters for stock investors:

- **Mass baccarat** fell 7.9% to MOP33.61 billion and made up 55.8% of casino revenue, versus 58.3% a year earlier.
- **Total mass gaming, including slots,** fell 5.2% to MOP43.31 billion.
- **Slots** rose 18.8% to MOP4.09 billion, a small bright spot.
- **VIP baccarat** was flat, up 0.1% to MOP16.91 billion, though up 9.9% over nine months.

Mass baccarat is the biggest single category in the market, which is why its decline draws the most attention. Those details come from the third-quarter data; I did not find an October split by segment in the sources I reviewed. Sports betting is growing from a tiny base: football betting rose 49% to MOP152 million in the quarter, according to the same report, which is too small to offset anything in baccarat.

The third quarter averaged about MOP20.07 billion a month by my arithmetic (MOP60.22 billion divided by three), so October's MOP21.68 billion is above the recent run rate in absolute terms. That is partly the holiday and partly seasonality, and it does not change the year-on-year direction.

A caution on comparisons: October 2025 was a strong base at MOP24.09 billion. Part of the 10% decline reflects that high bar and not only weak demand. Even so, a quarter-over-quarter slip in Q3 and a bigger year-over-year slip in October point the same direction.

## The recovery gap

The 2019 comparison is the long-run yardstick. Ten-month revenue of MOP208.8 billion sits roughly MOP38 billion below the MOP246.7 billion of 2019 (my subtraction from The Standard's figures), so Macau is still well short of its pre-pandemic scale seven years later. That gap is the bull case for patient investors, since there is room to recover, and the bear case for skeptics, since the recovery has stalled well short of the old peak. October did not settle that argument. It simply added a data point to the bearish side while the year-to-date total stayed slightly positive.

## Why the government's response is worth reading

Macau's Chief Executive Sam Hou Fai said the government is studying whether the decline reflects outside pressures, such as stock and property market corrections in nearby markets, or whether post-pandemic tax revenue has peaked, according to The Standard. Focus Gaming News adds that he said the government would review October's performance before deciding whether to keep its MOP236 billion forecast.

That framing is notable. Officials are openly asking whether the problem is cyclical or structural, which is exactly the question equity investors in Macau operators are asking. The credit research firm CreditSights, quoted by Focus Gaming News, called the weak data "cyclical rather than structural" and said Macau could still meet the target. Both views are on the record, and the next few months of data will decide which one ages better.

## What it means for the listed operators

Las Vegas Sands (LVS), Wynn Resorts (WYNN), MGM Resorts (MGM, through its MGM China stake) and Melco Resorts (MLCO) all draw a large part of earnings from Macau. A market-wide decline of this size does not tell you how any one operator did, because share shifts between concessionaires can offset or amplify it. The monthly regulator data does not break revenue out by company, so operator-level conclusions have to wait for earnings.

The nearest test is Wynn. The company has announced a third-quarter earnings date, which a [Nasdaq-distributed press release](https://www.nasdaq.com/press-release/wynn-resorts-announces-third-quarter-earnings-release-date-2026-10-06) covers; I could only confirm the date through a secondary summary of that release, so check the company's investor page before relying on it. When it reports, the questions are straightforward:

- **Mass hold and market share** at its Macau property relative to the market's 5.2% quarterly mass decline.
- **Management commentary on October and Golden Week**, since the company will now have seen the full month.
- **Margin discipline,** meaning whether lower revenue is flowing through to EBITDA or being cushioned by cost control.

## What to watch next

Three things will clarify the trend soon. The first is November revenue, which the regulator typically publishes at the start of the following month and which will show whether October's rebound from September holds once the holiday boost fades. The second is the government's decision on the MOP236 billion forecast. The third is the October visitor figures, which The Standard says will come later this month and which will show whether footfall is holding up even as revenue slips.

For now the data supports a narrow conclusion: Macau is growing slightly year to date, has now fallen year over year for five months running, and showed no sign in October that a holiday week can reverse it. Whether that is a cycle or a reset is the debate the next set of earnings will have to answer.
