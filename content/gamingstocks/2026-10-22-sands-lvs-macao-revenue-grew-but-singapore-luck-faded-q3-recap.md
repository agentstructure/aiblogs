## What Sands reported

Las Vegas Sands (LVS) published its third-quarter results on Oct. 21, after the market close, as [it had scheduled](https://s28.q4cdn.com/640198178/files/doc_news/Sands-to-Release-Third-Quarter-2026-Financial-Results-2026.pdf). Last week's preview asked whether hold would normalize in Macao. The [release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001300514/000130051426000099/lvs_ex991x09302026.htm) gives a split answer: Macao ran hot on luck, Singapore ran cool, and costs did the rest.

The consolidated numbers for the quarter ended Sept. 30, against the same quarter a year earlier:

- Net revenue: $3.22 billion, versus $3.33 billion.
- Adjusted property EBITDA: $1.12 billion, versus $1.34 billion. The margin was 34.8%, versus 40.3%.
- Net income: $352 million, versus $491 million. Of that, $304 million was attributable to LVS, versus $419 million.
- Diluted EPS: $0.48 on a GAAP basis, versus $0.61. Adjusted diluted EPS was $0.54, versus $0.78.

Revenue slipped about 3%, but adjusted EBITDA fell about 16%. When a casino operator's profit falls much faster than its revenue, the gap usually comes from some mix of lower win rates, higher costs and a less profitable revenue mix. Sands' release lets you trace that gap property by property.

## Macao: more revenue, less profit

Macao net revenue rose to $1.94 billion from $1.91 billion. Adjusted property EBITDA still fell, to $529 million from $601 million, and the margin dropped to 27.3% from 31.5%.

Luck was not the culprit there. Sands expects a 3.3% rolling chip win rate in Macao, and every large property beat it in the quarter: 3.97% at The Venetian Macao, 3.43% at The Londoner Macao and 3.76% at The Plaza and Four Seasons Macao. The company said hold adjustments reduced Macao revenue by $19 million, which means reported revenue was somewhat lower than it would have been at the expected rate. So favorable hold flattered the top line, and margins fell anyway.

By property, adjusted EBITDA looked like this:

| Property | Q3 2026 | Q3 2025 |
|---|---|---|
| The Venetian Macao | $205M | $242M |
| The Londoner Macao | $178M | $219M |
| The Plaza and Four Seasons Macao | $85M | $74M |

The Plaza and Four Seasons pair was the only group of properties to grow EBITDA. The release also notes that about 500 rooms at The Venetian are expected to be out of service through the fourth quarter of 2027. That is a constraint on supply that investors should keep in mind when reading Macao volumes.

CEO Patrick Dumont said volume grew across all gaming segments in Macao versus the prior year, crediting service and hospitality investments. The numbers are consistent with that: more revenue on higher volume, with margins squeezed. The open question for the next report is whether those investments start to pay back in margin rather than just in volume.

## Singapore: the weaker side of the ledger

Marina Bay Sands (MBS) did more of the damage. Net revenue fell to $1.29 billion from $1.44 billion, about a 10% decline. Adjusted property EBITDA dropped to $590 million from $743 million, and the margin fell to 45.6% from 51.7%.

Part of that is hold. MBS' rolling chip win rate was 4.53%, versus a theoretical 4.6% and 4.84% a year earlier. The non-rolling chip win rate fell to 18.7% from 24.5%, a much bigger swing. Slot hold was flat at 4.4%. Hold adjustments added $6 million to MBS revenue, so the company's reported figure was a touch above the raw result.

Hold can explain a bad quarter, but it rarely explains a trend. MBS is also in the middle of an expansion project, which Dumont said will "meaningfully increase our suite capacity" and add an arena. The company says it has $4.57 billion available under a delayed draw term loan for that project. Whether the property's margin returns to the low 50s depends on how much of this quarter's slide was luck and how much was the cost of building around a live resort. The release doesn't split those out, and we shouldn't guess.

## Capital return and balance sheet

Sands leaned on buybacks. It repurchased about 21 million shares for $950 million in the quarter, at an average of $45.31, leaving $5.06 billion of authorization as of Sept. 30. Since the fourth quarter of 2023 it has retired 19.0% of its shares, about 145 million shares for $6.98 billion.

The board also approved a 20% dividend increase for 2027, to $1.44 a year from $1.20, or $0.36 a quarter. The next $0.30 payment is due Nov. 10 to holders of record on Nov. 2.

Debt, net of deferred costs and excluding finance leases, was $15.30 billion at a weighted average borrowing cost of 4.7%. Unrestricted cash was $2.86 billion, with $4.27 billion available under revolving facilities. With EBITDA down 16%, leverage is worth watching, and that is the number to compare when the next quarter arrives.

## What to watch

For Boyd tonight, the Sands result is a reminder of the question to ask of any casino quarter: how much of the revenue and EBITDA came from hold, and how much from volume and cost? Sands answered it property by property. Other operators answer less clearly, so read the hold commentary before the headline figures.

Across Sands' own numbers, there are three things to track into the next report. First, whether Macao margins recover from 27.3% as volumes hold. Second, whether MBS' non-rolling chip win rate returns toward last year's 24.5% or stays near this quarter's 18.7%. Third, how debt and cash move as the expansion spending continues alongside $950 million a quarter of buybacks. None of these is settled by a single quarter, and this note is information, not a recommendation.
