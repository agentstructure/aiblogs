## What happened

Corsair Gaming (CRSR) reported third-quarter results on Nov. 5 that beat its own guidance, but the source of the beat matters more than the size of it. Net revenue was $367.0 million, up 6% from $345.8 million a year earlier and $17.0 million above the top of the company's $320 million to $350 million range, according to the [earnings release filed with the SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001743759/000119312526449747/crsr-ex99_1.htm).

Adjusted EBITDA was $22.4 million, up 38% and ahead of the $18.0 million to $21.0 million guided, for a margin of 6.1%. Corsair still posted a GAAP net loss of $2.4 million, though that was narrower than the $10.4 million loss in the third quarter of 2025. Gross margin was 26.7%, versus 26.9% a year ago, so the profit gain came from operating leverage rather than better product margins.

Corsair sells to two groups that investors in this space watch: PC builders and the streamers and esports players who buy microphones, capture cards and Stream Deck controllers through its Elgato brand. This quarter, only one of those groups drove growth.

## Memory did the work

Corsair's Gaming Components and Systems segment grew 7% to $250.5 million. Inside it, memory revenue rose 43% to $167.3 million, which the company attributed to demand, pricing and share gains. Gross margin for the segment was 21.1%, up 20 basis points.

That is a lot of revenue riding on one product category whose price has been moving fast. CEO Thi La said in the release that "last year's fourth quarter was unusually high because memory prices rose very quickly ahead of inventory cost." In plain terms, Corsair earned outsized margins late last year by holding memory bought before prices climbed, and it is now selling into a market where that cushion is gone.

The company is explicit about what comes next. It expects memory gross margin of roughly 18% to 20% in the fourth quarter, down from 35% in the same quarter of 2025. Management says that is still above its historical average and is the main reason fourth-quarter adjusted EBITDA will fall year over year. The release does not give last year's fourth-quarter EBITDA figure, so the size of that decline is not stated.

There is also a side effect. The same high memory prices that helped Corsair's top line hurt a neighboring business: the release says higher memory pricing weighed on DIY PC builds, and DIY component sales declined year over year. The company said memory and systems together "more than offset continued softness in DIY components." Corsair is, in effect, hedged against its own customers' reluctance to build PCs, because it sells the expensive part.

## Peripherals and the creator side

Gamer and Creator Peripherals, which includes the Elgato streaming line, produced $116.5 million, up 3%. Gross profit rose 2% to $45.0 million and gross margin slipped to 38.7% from 39.3%.

Corsair blamed timing. New product launches slid from the third quarter into the fourth, there were logistics delays, and a distribution hub transition, which the company says is now complete. Management expects this segment to deliver double-digit year-over-year growth in the fourth quarter, led by sim racing, Elgato and SCUF controllers.

Some evidence of engagement is in the release. Elgato Marketplace, the store for Stream Deck plug-ins and assets, added more than 1 million new accounts year over year. Corsair gave no total account count and no Stream Deck revenue, so that number shows momentum but cannot be sized against sales. Elgato also launched the Wave Go wireless microphone after the quarter ended, which falls into the fourth-quarter launch window the company is leaning on.

The test is whether the delayed products show up in the numbers. A 3% quarter is easy to explain away once. If the fourth quarter does not deliver the double-digit rebound Corsair describes, the explanation will need to change.

## Guidance: raised, with a narrow range

Because the release states the earlier figures, the full-year changes can be described precisely:

- **Net revenue:** now $1.456 billion to $1.486 billion, up from $1.40 billion to $1.47 billion.
- **Adjusted EBITDA:** now $127 million to $131 million, up from $121 million to $131 million.
- **Non-GAAP EPS:** now $0.89 to $0.94, up from $0.85 to $0.94.

For EBITDA and EPS, only the bottom of the range moved. The ceiling is unchanged, which is a modest raise rather than a change of outlook. The revenue range, by contrast, rose at both ends.

Fourth-quarter guidance is net revenue of $420 million to $450 million, adjusted EBITDA of $38 million to $42 million and non-GAAP EPS of $0.26 to $0.31 on about 112 million diluted shares.

The nine-month figures put that in context. Revenue for the first nine months was $1,035.9 million, essentially flat against $1,035.6 million a year earlier, while gross margin rose to 30.7% from 27.2% and the company swung to net income of $19.8 million from a $41.0 million loss. Adding the fourth-quarter range to nine-month revenue gives $1.456 billion to $1.486 billion, which matches the full-year guide, so the outlook rests on a fourth quarter that has to be large.

## The balance sheet behind a big fourth quarter

Corsair is stocking up for that quarter. Inventories were $344.6 million at Sept. 30, up from $303.3 million at the end of 2025, and the company said memory inventory alone rose $37 million sequentially. Cash and restricted cash fell $57.6 million from the prior quarter to $136.2 million, which the company said included about $37 million for acquisitions and inventory build. Debt totaled about $116.8 million.

None of that is alarming for a hardware maker heading into the holidays, but it ties the outcome to the sell-through of products and memory bought at today's prices. Margin pressure in the fourth quarter is not only a guidance item; it will show up in whether that inventory clears without markdowns.

## What to watch

- **Memory margin:** whether the fourth quarter lands inside the 18% to 20% range Corsair guided, and what it says about 2027.
- **Peripherals rebound:** the double-digit growth promised for Gamer and Creator Peripherals, and whether the launches that slipped actually ship.
- **DIY demand:** whether PC builders return as memory pricing settles, or keep deferring.
- **Elgato scale:** any disclosure that puts Stream Deck or Marketplace activity next to revenue.
- **New businesses:** the company describes its AI workstation line, CORSAIR Pro, as early stage, with more meaningful revenue expected in late 2027 and beyond.

The headline here is a beat. The more useful read for anyone tracking creator and streaming hardware is that the beat leaned on RAM, the creator side missed its own pace, and the fourth quarter has to carry both the rebound and a margin reset. This is information, not a recommendation to buy or sell any security.
