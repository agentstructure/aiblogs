## What happened

Nintendo (NTDOY; Tokyo: 7974) will publish its six-month results in early November ([ad-hoc-news](https://www.ad-hoc-news.de/boerse/news/corporate-news/nintendo-stock-results-due-november-5-2026-for-nintendo-stock/70393944) lists Nov. 5, while [MarketScreener](https://ca.marketscreener.com/quote/stock/NINTENDO-CO-LTD-6491906/calendar/) shows Nov. 4), and it is the first full test of a strategy the company laid out in May: sell fewer Switch 2 consoles, charge more for each, and absorb a large jump in component costs.

The setup is unusual. Switch 2 has been Nintendo's fastest-selling hardware, with [3.82 million units sold in the April-June quarter and 23.68 million in its first 13 months](https://nintendowire.com/news/2026/08/06/switch-2-sells-additional-3-82-million-units-last-quarter-continues-being-nintendos-fastest-selling-console/). Yet the company's own forecast calls for a smaller year, not a bigger one. Investors will be looking for evidence that the demand is holding up while the price rises and the memory squeeze play out.

## The guidance, in numbers

In its [May 8 explanatory material](https://www.nintendo.co.jp/ir/pdf/2026/260508_4e.pdf), Nintendo reported that Switch 2 hardware sold 19.86 million units in the fiscal year ended March 2026, [about 4.5% above its 19 million forecast](https://www.shacknews.com/article/149058/nintendo-switch-2-unit-sales). Net sales roughly doubled to 2,313.0 billion yen, and operating profit rose 27.5% to 360.1 billion yen, a 15.6% margin.

The forecast for the year ending March 2027 points the other way on volume:

- Switch 2 hardware: 16.50 million units, down 16.9%.
- Switch 2 software: 60.00 million units, up 23.2% from 48.71 million (excluding units bundled with hardware).
- Net sales: 2,050.0 billion yen, down 11.4%.
- Operating profit: 370.0 billion yen, up 2.7%.

Two things stand out. First, Nintendo expects profit to edge up even as revenue falls by more than a tenth, which means it is leaning on software and on higher hardware prices to protect the margin. Second, the 16.5 million hardware figure implies the console is entering its second year with a lower ceiling than its launch year, which is normal for a platform that front-loaded its most eager buyers. Add the 3.82 million already shipped in the first quarter and about 12.7 million units remain to be delivered over the other three quarters, a total that Nintendo Wire also reports as [12.68 million more units to reach 36.35 million lifetime](https://nintendowire.com/news/2026/08/06/switch-2-sells-additional-3-82-million-units-last-quarter-continues-being-nintendos-fastest-selling-console/).

## The cost line: 100 billion yen

The detail that matters most for margins is on the cost side. Nintendo said its forecast includes an impact of approximately 100.0 billion yen from rising component prices, particularly memory, and from tariffs, recorded in cost of goods sold. Set against the 370.0 billion yen operating profit forecast, that is equal to roughly 27% of the profit target (our arithmetic, not a company figure).

This is the same pressure running through the rest of the hardware chain. TrendForce reported last November that consumer DDR4 and DDR5 prices had [more than doubled from their record lows](https://www.trendforce.com/news/?p=48636) on AI and datacenter demand, and that it expected bill-of-materials costs to rise another 5 to 7% or more in 2026. Console makers buy memory in the same market as PC and phone makers, and a handheld hybrid with a fixed price is more exposed than most devices. Nintendo's answer has been to move the sticker price.

## Price increases are now live

Nintendo raised Switch 2 prices in three regions, all of which are [reflected in the forecast](https://www.nintendo.co.jp/ir/pdf/2026/260508_4e.pdf):

- Japan: 49,980 yen to 59,980 yen, effective May 25, 2026 (a 20% rise).
- United States: $449.99 to $499.99, effective September 1, 2026 (about 11%).
- Europe: 469.99 euros to 499.99 euros, effective September 1, 2026 (about 6%).

The percentages are our calculation from Nintendo's published prices. The timing is the useful part for the November report. The Japanese increase covers the whole September quarter, while the US and European increases took effect only on the first day of that quarter's final month. The six-month numbers will therefore show a partial read on Western demand at the higher price, and the full holiday-quarter picture will come in the next report.

## The stock is pricing some of this in

Nintendo shares closed at [8,699 yen in Tokyo on October 22](https://www.ad-hoc-news.de/boerse/news/corporate-news/nintendo-stock-results-due-november-5-2026-for-nintendo-stock/70393944), against a 52-week range of 6,544 to 14,630 yen. That is about 40% below the high, with a market capitalization near 10.0 trillion yen and a price-to-earnings ratio of 21.22, per the same report. The same source notes that on October 17 a US securities firm kept its bullish rating but cut its price target from 10,000 yen to 9,800 yen.

For context, a [separate October 16 report](https://www.ad-hoc-news.de/boerse/news/corporate-news/nintendo-maintains-forecasts-as-switch-2-worries-weigh-on-nintendo-stock/70337110) put the stock at 8,479 yen and said Nintendo kept its annual forecasts unchanged on October 14. That means the company has not yet cut its 16.5 million unit target, nor raised it, despite a first quarter of 3.82 million units that Morningstar called 18% above its own forecast.

## What to watch in the report

- **Guidance changes.** A hardware forecast raised above 16.5 million units would suggest the price increases are not denting demand. A cut would say the opposite. Unchanged is the default given the October 14 reaffirmation.
- **The cost estimate.** Whether the roughly 100 billion yen component and tariff figure moves is the cleanest read on how long the memory squeeze lasts.
- **Software pace.** The 60 million unit software target leans on attach rates. First-half software shipments will show whether it is on track.
- **Currency.** The forecast assumes 150 yen per US dollar and 175 yen per euro, so exchange rates are a swing factor for reported yen revenue.

## Why it matters beyond Nintendo

Nintendo is the cleanest public read on how much of the memory cost surge a console maker can pass to buyers. The same logic applies to PC graphics cards, where TrendForce relayed reports that Nvidia (NVDA) and AMD (AMD) might raise prices or trim lower-end models when memory is scarce. Those reports were secondhand and unconfirmed, so treat them as a risk factor rather than a fact.

If Nintendo shows that a roughly 11% US price increase leaves demand intact, it strengthens the case that the installed base is sticky enough to carry higher hardware prices. If not, the pressure moves to software and to the next fiscal year. This is commentary, not a recommendation.
