## What happened

MGM Resorts (MGM) reports third-quarter results after the market closes on Wednesday, Oct. 28, with a call at 5:00 p.m. Eastern, [according to the company's release-date notice](https://finviz.com/news/396109/mgm-resorts-international-announces-third-quarter-earnings-release-date). It arrives at an awkward moment for its Macau business. Analysts have spent October cutting forecasts for the quarter, and the data that has come in since the quarter ended has not argued with them.

The setup matters beyond one company. MGM is the cleanest read on both halves of the casino trade this season: a Las Vegas Strip portfolio that has been growing slowly, and an MGM China arm exposed to a Macau market where visitors are rising but gamblers are spending less. Wynn Resorts (WYNN) [reports on Nov. 5](https://finviz.com/news/399477/wynn-resorts-announces-third-quarter-earnings-release-date), so MGM's numbers set the first benchmark before the next one lands.

## The Macau backdrop going in

Macau gross gaming revenue (GGR) in September came to MOP18.06 billion (US$2.23 billion), [down 1.2% from a year earlier](https://casino.org/news/macau-losing-streak-hits-four-months-september). That was the fourth straight monthly decline and the lowest monthly total of 2026, undercutting June's MOP18.52 billion. It also missed expectations: [CLSA had estimated MOP18.6 billion in mid-September](https://www.casino.com/news/financial/macau-ggr-falls-1-2-in-september-as-earnings-risks-rise/), and Citigroup had already cut its own forecast to MOP18 billion. Weak premium mass table play was blamed.

For the full quarter, [CLSA expects GGR of MOP60.21 billion, down 3.8% year on year](https://www.ggrasia.com/more-macau-visitors-not-bringing-extra-ggr-as-3q-casino-ebitda-likely-down-11pct-y-o-y-clsa). It expects sector EBITDA to fall 11% to just under US$1.84 billion. The broker sees the margin edging up to 24.6% of GGR from 24.2% in the second quarter, though that is still below the year-ago level. The second quarter showed how the squeeze works: [GGR slipped 0.1% while operating expenses rose 2%](https://www.casino.com/news/financial/macau-ggr-falls-1-2-in-september-as-earnings-risks-rise/), sector EBITDA fell 10.7% to US$1.81 billion, and the EBITDA-to-GGR ratio dropped from 26.8% to 24.2%.

Year to date the market is still ahead: nine-month GGR of MOP187.11 billion is [up 3.2%](https://casino.org/news/macau-losing-streak-hits-four-months-september), but that growth was front-loaded. January through May was up 10.9%, so the later months are running well behind the early ones.

## Visitors up, spending per visitor soft

The strongest signal that something has changed is the gap between traffic and revenue. CLSA noted that [July and August total visitors rose 4.5% to 8.02 million, while overnight visitors fell 2.5%](https://www.ggrasia.com/more-macau-visitors-not-bringing-extra-ggr-as-3q-casino-ebitda-likely-down-11pct-y-o-y-clsa). Golden Week in early October drew just over 1.14 million visitors from Oct. 1 to 7. CICC, citing the Macao Government Tourism Office, [put average daily visitors up 9% on the year](https://www.ggrasia.com/no-clear-inflection-point-for-macau-ggr-and-profitability-amid-subdued-demand-cicc).

Yet CICC estimates GGR for Oct. 1 to 11 [fell 5% on a like-for-like basis](https://www.ggrasia.com/no-clear-inflection-point-for-macau-ggr-and-profitability-amid-subdued-demand-cicc), at about MOP836 million a day. October has a hard comparison: last October's GGR was [MOP24.08 billion](https://casino.org/news/macau-losing-streak-hits-four-months-september). CICC reads the pattern as traffic driven by lower-spending visitors with soft premium demand, and says some operators appear to have frozen hiring for frontline gaming sales roles. It has cut its 2026 Macau GGR forecast to a 1% decline and 2027 to a 7% decline, and expects further consensus downgrades after third-quarter results.

Note that these are analyst estimates and early-month tallies, not final figures. Macau's monthly totals come from the government's gaming bureau, and the October number will not be official until early November.

## MGM's baseline from the second quarter

The Q2 report gives a baseline for what to compare. [Consolidated revenue was $4.45 billion, up 1%](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000789570/000078957026000075/mgmex991q22026earningrelea.htm), a record for a second quarter. Las Vegas Strip Resorts revenue rose 3% to $2.17 billion, with segment adjusted EBITDAR up 3% to $735 million. MGM China is where the pain showed: revenue was roughly flat at $1.10 billion, but adjusted EBITDAR dropped 15% to $257 million from $301 million.

Adjusted earnings per share were $0.59 versus $0.79 a year earlier. The company bought back about 4 million shares for $164 million and had roughly $1.4 billion left on its authorization at June 30. It gave no third-quarter guidance. Management did say its MGM Osaka project is on track for a 2030 opening and that it is directing growth capital toward Las Vegas luxury offerings.

## Wynn offers a counterpoint

The Macau slowdown has not hit every operator equally. In its second quarter, [Wynn's Macau adjusted property EBITDAR rose to $297.0 million from $253.7 million](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001174922/000117492226000051/ex991wrlq22026pressrelease.htm), an increase of about 17%. Wynn Palace drove it, climbing to $201.5 million from $157.2 million, while Wynn Macau was roughly flat at $95.5 million. On the Las Vegas side the story reversed: Wynn's Las Vegas adjusted property EBITDAR fell to $215.2 million from $234.8 million, about 8% lower.

That split is a reminder that a sector forecast of minus 11% is an average. Hold luck, property mix and cost control can push individual operators in different directions, which is why the MGM China line is worth reading closely rather than assuming it tracks the headline.

## What to watch

Four items will decide how the MGM print is received.

- **MGM China EBITDAR against the 15% Q2 decline.** A smaller drop would suggest the operator held up better than the sector estimate implies. A larger one would support CLSA's warning that cost pressure does not dissipate easily.
- **Las Vegas Strip trend.** The Strip grew revenue and EBITDAR 3% in Q2. Whether that held through the summer is the main domestic question.
- **Commentary on October.** Management's read on Golden Week and early fourth-quarter play, set against CICC's estimate of a 5% decline, will matter more to Macau-exposed names than the backward-looking quarter.
- **Capital allocation.** With about $1.4 billion left on the buyback and Osaka spending ahead, any shift in pace will show how management is weighing a weaker Macau.

Wynn follows on Nov. 5, and CICC has already flagged that it expects consensus cuts once the third-quarter prints are in. The next hard data point arrives in early November with Macau's official October GGR, which will show whether the early-month decline held up against a MOP24.08 billion comparison.
