## What happened

Bally's Corporation (BALY) reported third-quarter results on Nov. 16, and the headline number looks strong. [Consolidated revenue was $807.6 million, up 21.7% from a year earlier](https://s29.q4cdn.com/580102441/files/doc_news/2026/Nov/16/BALY-3Q26-FINAL-11-16-26.pdf). The more important sentence for investors tracking consolidation was buried in the CEO's remarks. Bally's Intralot's proposed acquisition of evoke plc, the owner of William Hill and 888, "continues to progress on schedule," but the remaining gaming regulator approvals and the Gibraltar court sanction hearing "are expected to conclude by the first quarter of 2027."

That is a longer runway than the one in June, when the offer was announced. [AJ Bell's report on the offer](https://www.ajbell.co.uk/news/articles/evoke-agrees-ps243-million-takeover-offer-ballys-intralot) put expected completion in the fourth quarter of 2026. By August, [Casino.org](https://www.casino.org/news/evoke-shareholders-back-328m-ballys-intralot-takeover/) was reporting a window of Q4 2026 or Q1 2027. Bally's own November language points to the later end of that range. It is not a collapse of the deal, but it keeps a large piece of European online gambling in limbo for another quarter or more.

## The evoke deal in brief

According to [AJ Bell](https://www.ajbell.co.uk/news/articles/evoke-agrees-ps243-million-takeover-offer-ballys-intralot), the June 5 offer gives evoke holders 0.537 new Bally's Intralot shares per evoke share, worth about 52 pence each and about £243.1 million in total. A cash alternative at 52 pence is capped at £117.1 million. The same report says Bally's Intralot expects roughly £180 million of pretax cost and capex savings by the end of the second year after completion, and that evoke's board unanimously recommended the bid.

Shareholders have done their part. [Casino.org reports](https://www.casino.org/news/evoke-shareholders-back-328m-ballys-intralot-takeover/) that on Aug. 17, 99.91% of scheme shares voted at the court meeting backed the deal, with 268.2 million shares in favor and 236,504 against. [Bally's says](https://s29.q4cdn.com/580102441/files/doc_news/2026/Nov/16/BALY-3Q26-FINAL-11-16-26.pdf) shareholders of both companies have approved the transaction and that "a number of competition and regulatory conditions have already been satisfied."

What is left is procedural but not trivial: the remaining gaming regulator approvals and the Gibraltar court sanction. Until both land, the combination cannot close, and evoke holders cannot receive the Intralot paper.

## What the quarter shows about the buyer

The growth in the quarter is largely a story of what Bally's has bought. In the [segment table](https://s29.q4cdn.com/580102441/files/doc_news/2026/Nov/16/BALY-3Q26-FINAL-11-16-26.pdf), Bally's Intralot B2B revenue was $76.6 million, against $5.5 million a year earlier. The release says the prior-year figure was only a royalty stream from a divested business, because the Intralot transaction completed in the fourth quarter of 2025. Bally's Intralot B2C revenue rose 22.8% to $257.5 million, helped by UK growth and the addition of Intralot's Turkish sports betting business.

Profit did not keep pace in the online unit. B2C segment Adjusted EBITDAR was $77.0 million, down from $86.4 million. Bally's attributes a gross hit of about $39 million in the quarter to the higher UK gaming duty, and says roughly 55% of that impact, measured year over year, was offset by revenue growth, marketing efficiency and cost control. That UK tax pressure matters beyond Bally's. [AJ Bell's account of evoke's rationale](https://www.ajbell.co.uk/news/articles/evoke-agrees-ps243-million-takeover-offer-ballys-intralot) says evoke cited UK tax changes that could raise its annual tax bill by £125 million to £135 million, which helps explain why a sale looked attractive to its board.

The land-based business was steadier. Casinos & Resorts revenue rose 3.2% to $408.6 million, while its Adjusted EBITDAR was $107.8 million, essentially flat against $107.9 million. North America Interactive revenue rose 25.6% to $62.7 million, with a segment Adjusted EBITDAR loss of $3.2 million versus a $6.0 million loss a year earlier.

## The balance sheet is the real constraint

Any acquirer's ability to close and integrate a deal depends on its funding, and Bally's has been explicit about pressure here. In its second-quarter 10-Q, [the company described a conditional waiver](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001747079/000174707926000084/baly-20260630.htm) of its consolidated net leverage ratio covenant, tied to a minimum liquidity requirement. [Fox Business reported](https://www.foxbusiness.com/markets/major-casino-operator-discloses-substantial-doubt-over-future-debt-pressures-mount) that the same filing expressed "substantial doubt" about the company's ability to continue as a going concern, and that Bally's is pursuing asset monetization, an equity sale and debt financings, none of which it said had been finalized. Note that evoke would be acquired by the Bally's Intralot subsidiary rather than by Bally's directly, and the exact credit linkage between the two is something this post has not verified.

The third-quarter numbers show why the issue persists. Per the [Q3 release](https://s29.q4cdn.com/580102441/files/doc_news/2026/Nov/16/BALY-3Q26-FINAL-11-16-26.pdf), long-term debt including the current portion was $4.84 billion at Sept. 30, up from $4.50 billion at year-end, and the current portion rose to $537.9 million from $37.3 million, mainly because the revolving credit facility was reclassified as current. The [Q3 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001747079/000174707926000108/baly-20260930.htm) shows cash and equivalents of $497.3 million, down from $798.4 million at Dec. 31, and a total net loss of $117.0 million for the quarter versus $106.2 million a year earlier. Net cash used in operating activities was $399.6 million over nine months.

Meanwhile, the spending list is long. Bally's says its permanent Chicago casino is on schedule to open in spring 2027 and that it will meet and likely exceed the $1.34 billion minimum capital commitment. The $4.0 billion Bronx project is targeted to open by 2030, with a $400 million term loan closed Oct. 1 and $160 million of delayed-draw commitments. Management says it is fielding interest from equity and debt partners for the rest.

## What to watch next

- **The Gibraltar sanction hearing.** Bally's guides to completion of regulatory approvals and the hearing by Q1 2027. A firm date, or a further slip, would be the clearest signal on timing.
- **Financing.** Any asset sale, equity raise or new debt at Bally's would show how the company plans to fund Chicago, the Bronx and its leverage covenant at the same time. [Gaming.net reports](https://www.gaming.net/evoke-shareholders-back-ballys-intralot-takeover-as-buyers-liquidity-woes-deepen/) that Bally's told the SEC it is working toward new financing by early 2027; that is a single secondary report and worth confirming in the filing.
- **UK margins.** If Bally's keeps offsetting the UK duty increase at roughly the pace it reported, B2C profit can stabilize. If not, the synergy case for evoke carries more weight.
- **Chicago in spring 2027.** The opening is the next large test of whether capital spending turns into cash flow.
