## Two ways to read the same tournament

The group stage of the biggest event in esports has just produced a split verdict. According to figures from Esports Charts, reported by [Strafe](https://www.strafe.com/news/read/lol-worlds-2026-swiss-stage-average-viewership-rises-8-despite-lower-peak/) on Nov. 2, the League of Legends World Championship's Swiss Stage grew average viewership by 8.15% over last year and total hours watched by 10.5%. Yet peak viewership fell. In 2025, three Swiss Stage matches topped 2 million concurrent viewers. In 2026, none did.

[White Market](https://news.white.market/latest/worlds-2026-average-viewership-rises-8-percent-as-peak-falls/) reported the same numbers the same day, including the top match: T1 against Dplus KIA at 1,836,125 peak viewers. Both outlets are relaying Esports Charts data, so this is one underlying dataset rather than two independent counts, and Esports Charts' own pages were not retrievable when this post was written. Treat the figures as reliable but singly sourced.

Why does the gap between average and peak matter? Peaks measure a few nights of the biggest rivalry matches. Averages and hours watched measure how many people stayed across the whole stage. For anyone trying to judge what an esports property is worth to sponsors, broadcasters and the platforms carrying it, breadth is arguably the steadier number, though advertisers often price on headline moments.

## What moved the numbers

The top five Swiss Stage matches, per the same reports, were:

- T1 vs. Dplus KIA: 1,836,125
- G2 Esports vs. Hanwha Life Esports: 1,785,416
- T1 vs. CTBC Flying Oyster: 1,784,678
- T1 vs. Movistar KOI: 1,736,719
- Bilibili Gaming vs. Hanwha Life Esports: 1,688,935

T1 appears in three of the five. The reports attribute the lower peak mainly to weaker Korean-language viewership. Two reasons are offered: T1 qualified for the Knockout Stage early and so played fewer Swiss matches, and the US host location put games in less convenient time slots for several markets. Those are the outlets' explanations, not measured causes.

The growth came from elsewhere. Viewership rose in every broadcast language except Korean. French-language broadcasts grew 114.5% and Portuguese-language broadcasts 106.3%, helped by Karmine Corp and LOS, which both reached the 2-2 stage of the Swiss format. Portuguese and French audiences are not the traditional centre of gravity for League of Legends, so the event is leaning on a wider base of regional fans rather than one star team.

## The platform backdrop

Where those viewers watch is shifting too. [Streams Charts](https://streamscharts.com/news/q3-2026-global-livestreaming-landscape) published its Q3 2026 livestreaming report on Oct. 13. Tracked platforms logged a record 31.698 billion hours watched, about 0.8% above Q2. Underneath that nearly flat total, the mix changed:

- YouTube: 13.62 billion hours, down 13.7% from Q2, with its share of tracked hours falling from 49.5% to 43%. It remains the largest platform.
- TikTok LIVE: 10.6 billion hours, up 19%, a record 33.5% share.
- Twitch: 4.51 billion hours, up 4%, with share rising from 13.8% to 14.2%. Streams Charts says gaming is 76.9% of Twitch's watch time and calls it the platform's strongest quarter since Q2 2025.
- Kick: 2.04 billion hours, up 30.7%, a third straight quarter of growth and a record 6.4% share.

Twitch is owned by Amazon (AMZN) and YouTube by Alphabet (GOOGL). Neither company breaks out these platforms' hours, so Streams Charts' third-party tracking is the main public yardstick, and it has caveats. [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/twitch-gains-4-to-4-51bn-hours-in-q3-as-kick-grows-30-7/), summarizing the report, notes that the Q2 2025 comparison period predates Twitch's August 2025 viewbot detection change, and that Streams Charts did not say whether its hours are adjusted for bots. Streams Charts itself flags unusual audience patterns on the channel of Kick's top streamer, so Kick's growth should be read with that in mind. PPC Land also points out that Amazon Ads' claim that Twitch holds 60% of the global livestreaming landscape uses an undefined scope; the 14.2% figure is a share of all tracked hours, including YouTube and TikTok LIVE.

Gaming is a smaller slice of the larger platforms. Streams Charts puts gaming at about 14.5% of YouTube's watch time (up from 11.3% in Q2), with YouTube Gaming at roughly 1.98 billion hours and a 6.2% share of tracked hours. On TikTok LIVE gaming is 16.2% of watch time. On this evidence, Twitch's 76.9% gaming mix makes it the most concentrated in the category, while YouTube's gaming hours are growing as a proportion of a shrinking total.

## What it means for this audience

The angle for investors tracking the engagement side of gaming is that attention is fragmenting across more places while the headline event still draws large crowds. A tournament whose average audience grows 8% even as peaks fall suggests the audience is broadening, and that the sponsorship and media-rights value of esports depends on which metric counts. It also shows how much a single team can swing a peak: with T1 playing fewer matches, the biggest numbers fell.

For the platform owners, the Q3 data is a reminder that publicly traded parents do not report livestream hours, so claims about share come from trackers with differing methods. A separate Streamlabs and Stream Hatchet Q2 2026 report, for instance, uses a different scope and gives a very different split, so share numbers from different trackers are not comparable.

## What to watch next

Worlds is not over. Per the reports, the Knockout Stage began Nov. 3 with best-of-five series, one match per day, through the Grand Final on Nov. 14. As of this writing, no knockout-stage viewership figures were located, and the Grand Final number does not yet exist. That is where the year's real peak will be set, and it will test whether the Swiss Stage's lower peaks were a scheduling and team-draw effect or a sign of a cooler Korean audience.

Three markers are worth following: the knockout-round peaks once Esports Charts publishes them, whether T1's presence again lifts a match above 2 million, and Streams Charts' Q4 report, which will show whether Kick's and TikTok LIVE's gains hold and whether YouTube's decline reverses after a tournament-heavy quarter. Until then, the numbers say the audience is larger on average, narrower at the top, and spread across more platforms than a year ago.
