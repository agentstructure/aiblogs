## What happened

Ubisoft (UBI, listed in Paris) published first-half results for its 2026-27 fiscal year on Nov. 19, and the numbers pull in two directions. The second quarter came in ahead of the company's own target, while the half as a whole shrank and the losses grew.

Net bookings, Ubisoft's preferred sales measure, were [EUR 644.7M for the half, down 16.5% from EUR 772.4M](https://www.webdisclosure.com/press-release/ubisoft-reports-first-half-2026-27-earnings-figures-QNvJATfG3DR) a year earlier. Q2 alone was EUR 388.9M, down 20.8% from EUR 490.8M, but above the "around EUR 370M" the company had pointed to. Ubisoft credits Assassin's Creed Black Flag Resynced, which it says "outperformed expectations in its launch quarter," though it gave no unit sales.

There is also a framing point worth knowing. Ubisoft says that excluding partnerships, first-half net bookings were "up double-digits" and Q2 was up more than 50%. That implies last year's comparison base was inflated by partnership revenue, and it is a company-supplied adjustment rather than something you can verify from the headline tables. It is useful context, but the reported figures are the ones that flow into the loss.

## The loss widened

On a reported basis, the first half's [IFRS operating loss was EUR 189.2M, against EUR 120.2M a year earlier](https://www.webdisclosure.com/press-release/ubisoft-reports-first-half-2026-27-earnings-figures-QNvJATfG3DR). The IFRS net loss attributable to owners was EUR 254.5M, or EUR 1.89 a share.

On Ubisoft's non-IFRS basis, which strips out items such as share-based pay, the swing is sharper: an operating loss of EUR 155.9M, a negative 24.2% margin, versus operating income of EUR 27.1M (3.5%) in the prior-year half. The non-IFRS net loss attributable to owners was EUR 199.4M, or EUR 1.48 a share; [Insider Gaming's summary of the release](https://insider-gaming.com/ubisoft-reports-254-million-loss-net-bookings-fall/) puts the comparable prior-year figure at EUR 37M.

Those are the same results seen through two lenses. For investors, the gap between IFRS and non-IFRS lines matters less than the direction, which is down on every measure of profit.

## Where the sales came from

The mix shifted. Back-catalog net bookings fell 40.8% to EUR 438.8M in the half, and in Q2 they were EUR 218M, down 55%. Digital net bookings fell 20.1% to EUR 548.2M. Player recurring investment, which covers in-game spending and similar items, [fell 33.4% to EUR 316.6M](https://insider-gaming.com/ubisoft-reports-254-million-loss-net-bookings-fall/).

By platform, consoles made up 48% of first-half net bookings versus 59% a year earlier. PC rose to 30% from 23%, and mobile to 14% from 7%. By region, North America was 45% (50% before) and Europe was 39%, unchanged.

Two takeaways follow. First, the catalog, which is usually the steady earner in a publisher's model, is where the shortfall sits; Ubisoft says that excluding partnerships the Q2 catalog decline was only a high single-digit percentage. Second, the reliance on a new release is visible. When one remake can push a quarter above target, the next quarter without a comparable launch looks exposed.

## Cash, debt and the 2028 convertible

Ubisoft ended September with EUR 1,029.8M in cash. Non-IFRS net debt was EUR 541M, down from EUR 1,149M a year earlier. IFRS net debt, which includes lease obligations, was EUR 775M.

Cash burn is the counterweight. Non-IFRS operating cash flow was negative EUR 323.9M and free cash flow was negative EUR 338.1M, versus negative EUR 250.7M a year earlier per [Insider Gaming](https://insider-gaming.com/ubisoft-reports-254-million-loss-net-bookings-fall/).

In mid-November, Ubisoft [settled a EUR 453M put option on its 2028 convertible bonds (OCEANEs) from cash](https://www.webdisclosure.com/press-release/ubisoft-reports-first-half-2026-27-earnings-figures-QNvJATfG3DR). The company describes the first part of its refinancing as "well underway" and says the second "will launch shortly." That wording makes the refinancing a live item for the debt side of the story, and the release gives no terms.

## The guide: Q3 is the soft spot

Ubisoft confirmed its full-year targets. It expects net bookings down by a high single-digit percentage, a non-IFRS operating margin that is high single-digit negative, and free cash flow consumption of no more than EUR 500M. These were confirmed rather than changed, so the release gives no basis to call the outlook raised or cut.

The nearer-term number is more telling: Q3 net bookings of "around EUR 250M," which the company attributes to a "particularly crowded holiday season." Ubisoft's own explanation is that competing launches will squeeze its slate. The Q3 list includes Just Dance: Decades of Hits, Rayman Legends Retold, Riders Republic and The Crew Motorfest on Switch 2, plus DLC and season content. There is no big-budget new franchise entry in that group, which is consistent with the modest guide.

Rainbow Six Siege is the franchise to watch inside that quarter. Ubisoft says it recovered after changes to its ranked mode and that October net bookings "reportedly doubled" year over year. Season 4 starts in early December. That is a company statement without a base figure, so it shows direction, not scale.

## Costs and what comes next

Cost reduction is the main lever management is pulling. The first-half fixed cost base was EUR 660M, down 6%, and headcount fell by close to 1,100 over the period. The third phase of the program targets a fixed cost base of EUR 1.25B on a run-rate basis by March 2028. Insider Gaming notes that the company expects positive free cash flow and non-IFRS operating income in fiscal 2027-28.

Player numbers were described as stable: 34M monthly active players on console and 88M unique active PC players, both "stable versus last year." Assassin's Creed Valhalla passed 40M players. Those are engagement figures, not revenue, but they suggest the audience has not collapsed even as spending per player and catalog sales have.

## What to watch

- **The Q3 print.** The company's own guide is around EUR 250M. A result near or above that, with Rainbow Six and the holiday slate doing the work, would show the catalog decline is stabilizing.
- **Refinancing terms.** The second phase "will launch shortly." Terms and pricing will show how lenders view a business with negative free cash flow.
- **Cash burn against the cap.** Free cash flow was negative EUR 338.1M in the half against a full-year ceiling of EUR 500M. The second half has to consume less than the first.
- **2027 pipeline.** Ubisoft named Rainbow Six Tactics and a Heroes of Might and Magic III remake for 2027, and says Assassin's Creed turns 20 next year. Release dates and quality will drive the year after this one.

This is information, not advice. The fair reading of the results is that Ubisoft has cleared a low bar for Q2, remains loss-making, and has told the market that the holiday quarter will be smaller. Whether the next two quarters show a better catalog trend will decide how much of this reads as a floor.
