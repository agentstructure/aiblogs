## What happened

Light & Wonder (ASX: LNW), the slot machine, iGaming and social casino group, reported third-quarter results on Nov. 4, and the headline numbers pulled in two directions. Net income rose 17% to $133 million, or $1.72 per diluted share, up 28%. Adjusted EPS rose 17% to $2.12. Revenue, however, fell 2% to $824 million from $841 million a year earlier, according to the company's [results release filed with the SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000750004/000075000426000045/lnw09302026erandtables.htm).

Revenue also landed below expectations. Capital Brief, citing a Visible Alpha consensus, [put the revenue estimate at $844.9 million](https://www.capitalbrief.com/briefing/light-wonder-q3-net-income-lifts-17-to-190m-lowers-full-year-guidance-cfa9a9e3-ea92-40d2-813a-4953aa6dd269/) and the consensus for net income at $114.7 million. The profit beat and the sales miss are the same story: a company converting a smaller top line into more earnings through mix and lower costs.

The part that matters most for the stock is the outlook. The company now expects full-year consolidated adjusted EBITDA growth "toward the lower end" of its previously announced mid-to-high single-digit range, per the [Business Wire release](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/light-wonder-inc-reports-third-212300368.html). As recently as Aug. 4, it had said it [maintained its full-year outlook](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000750004/000075000426000032/lnw06302026erandtables.htm) with growth in the mid-to-high single digits. Three months later, the low end is the new base case.

## Where the pressure came from

The company named two headwinds: weakness in social casino and customers delaying some investments and capital spending. Both show up in the segment table.

SciPlay, the social casino unit, generated $173 million of revenue, down 12% from $197 million. Its adjusted EBITDA fell 6% to $67 million. In the second quarter SciPlay revenue fell 9% to $182 million, which the company blamed on a softer free-to-play social casino market, so the decline is widening rather than stabilizing. The unit is about 21% of group revenue, which is enough to drag the consolidated line even when other businesses grow.

The gaming systems business, which sells casino management software and hardware, fell 24% to $55 million, mainly on lower hardware sales. Table products slipped 7% to $52 million. That points to casino operators holding back on discretionary technology spending, which fits the delayed-investment language in the outlook.

## What held up

Not everything softened. The core slot business was steady to better:

- **Gaming operations** (leased machines and recurring revenue) rose 3% to $248 million.
- **Gaming machine sales** rose 5% to $199 million, with 8,906 new units shipped globally, including about 5,800 in North America, at the high end of prior guidance per [Capital Brief](https://www.capitalbrief.com/briefing/light-wonder-q3-net-income-lifts-17-to-190m-lowers-full-year-guidance-cfa9a9e3-ea92-40d2-813a-4953aa6dd269/).
- The North American premium installed base grew by 2,657 units year over year, the 25th straight quarter of growth. Premium units are now 58% of the North American installed base, which totals 36,974 units excluding Grover, the charitable gaming business. Grover has more than 12,750 units, up 1,487.
- **iGaming** revenue rose 13% to $97 million, with adjusted EBITDA up 9% to $37 million. The release cited record quarterly wagers of $31.7 billion.

Consolidated adjusted EBITDA rose 2% to $383 million, a 46% margin. That is a sharp slowdown from the second quarter, when it rose 9% to $383 million on $828 million of revenue. The dollar figure is flat quarter to quarter, while revenue went from growing 2% to shrinking 2%.

Cash generation was the strongest part of the report. Adjusted free cash flow rose 21% to $181 million in the quarter and 49% to $545 million year to date. Net cash from operating activities rose 28% to $236 million. Capital expenditure was $90 million, against $79 million a year earlier, as the company keeps funding leased machines and Grover.

## The balance sheet is the priority

Light & Wonder is using that cash to pay down debt. Principal debt fell $153 million in the quarter to about $5.0 billion, and net debt leverage eased to 3.3x from 3.4x at June 30. The target is about 3.0x by the end of 2026 and below 3.0x in the first half of 2027.

The company made no share repurchases in the third quarter. It has bought back about $156 million of stock this year and has about $180 million of authorization left, roughly 12% of the program. CFO Oliver Chow said that "as signaled last quarter, we prioritized deleveraging over share repurchases" and that this order will hold "in the fourth quarter and beyond."

For shareholders, that changes how to read the EPS growth. The company expects adjusted EPS to grow faster than adjusted NPATA, "aided by prior buybacks," but with repurchases paused the per-share tailwind from a shrinking share count will fade. Future EPS growth has to come from operations and lower interest costs.

## What to watch next

First, the fourth quarter. Management expects gaming machine sales to accelerate, citing positive customer feedback after the AGE and G2E trade shows, stronger purchase intentions and recent industry data. The reduced outlook leans on a seasonally strong fourth quarter and recurring-revenue growth to offset social casino weakness and delayed customer spending, so the quarter has to deliver on both. CEO Matt Wilson said the company is "encouraged by the momentum heading into the fourth quarter."

Second, social casino. Another quarter of falling SciPlay revenue, after declines of 9% and 12% in the last two, would turn a segment issue into a thesis issue. One bright spot in the quarter: direct-to-consumer reached 31% of SciPlay revenue and average monthly revenue per paying user rose 5%, so paying users are monetizing better even as total revenue falls. Whether that is enough to stabilize the segment is the open question.

Third, iGaming. At 12% of revenue and growing in the low teens, it is the fastest-growing piece of the mix. The release ties that growth to first-party content and a wider partner network, and it is the segment most likely to benefit if operators lean further into online channels.

Finally, deleveraging. Moving from 3.3x toward 3.0x in one quarter requires continued free cash flow. The company's year-to-date adjusted free cash flow of $545 million gives it room, but a lower EBITDA path also raises the leverage ratio at the margin, so the target and the guidance cut pull against each other.

None of this is a call on the shares. The takeaway is that a recurring-revenue story with 25 straight quarters of premium installed base growth is running into two cyclical pressures at once, and the next two reports will show which one dominates. The company has not published numeric guidance for systems, SciPlay or iGaming, so the segment trends in each release are the main evidence investors have.
