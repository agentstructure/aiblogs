## Two publishers, two answers to the same question

The video game business is quietly arguing about whether the boxed game has a future. Sony Group (SONY) has given its answer: [new PlayStation games will be digital-only from January 2028](https://engadget.com/2205792/sony-will-stop-making-disc-based-playstation-games-starting-2028/), according to its July 1 announcement. Ubisoft (UBI, Euronext Paris) gave the opposite signal this month, when its CFO said the company will keep releasing physical editions.

For investors, this is more than a collector's quarrel. Disc sales shape margins, retail relationships, inventory risk and the used-game market. How much of a publisher's sales still comes in a box decides how much the 2028 cutoff will matter to it.

## What Sony announced

Sony said it will stop producing discs for new PlayStation games starting in January 2028. Per [Gamefile's report](https://www.gamefile.news/p/sony-drops-playstation-discs-2028-ps3-vita-stores-closing), the policy covers all PlayStation games released from that point, including those from third-party publishers. Games released before January 2028 are unaffected, so 2027 releases can still ship on disc.

Sony cited consumer preference. Gamefile reports that, by Sony's most recent figures, nearly four in five purchases of full PS4 and PS5 games in the past year were digital. Sony will keep selling games at physical retailers, though Gamefile notes it is unclear what form those retail products will take, such as boxes holding download codes. Gamefile also says Sony did not discuss the economics of the decision.

Two caveats apply. Different outlets describe the scope slightly differently, and Engadget says its report does not specify first-party versus third-party. Gamefile's account says all games. Readers should treat the third-party detail as reported rather than as a direct Sony quote.

## Ubisoft's counterpoint

At its annual investors meeting, Ubisoft CFO Frédérick Duguet said the company will continue producing games for physical distribution case by case, depending on a title's profile, audience and platform, according to [Tech4Gamers](https://tech4gamers.com/ubisoft-continue-selling-physical-games/) (published Oct. 6, 2026). The same report says Assassin's Creed Shadows sold nearly half of its copies as discs in several countries.

That is not a blanket claim for every title, and the report does not say which countries. It does fit Ubisoft's own numbers. In its [first-quarter 2026-27 release](https://mfn.se/one/a/ubisoft-entertainment-sa/ubisoft-reports-first-quarter-2026-27-sales-3afe2ec5), Ubisoft reported net bookings of EUR 255.8 million, down 9.2% year on year. Digital net bookings were EUR 206.2 million, or 80.6% of the total, down from 88.8% a year earlier. The release does not give a physical figure, but the remainder implies roughly 19%.

Ubisoft attributed the bookings decline to a high prior-year comparison, when Assassin's Creed Shadows launched, and it did not explain the mix shift. Do not read one quarter as a trend. It does show that at Ubisoft a meaningful slice of revenue still comes in a box, in a way Sony's headline digital share does not suggest.

## The July comment that makes this awkward

Ubisoft was not always at odds with Sony. On its July earnings call, according to [Wccftech](https://wccftech.com/sony-playstation-disc-shutdown-ubisoft-cheaper-digital-consoles/), Duguet said that from Ubisoft's perspective the impact of Sony's move would be small. He argued that on PC, digital "helped to grow the market" and that all-digital machines could make consoles more accessible and cheaper. He also said the change "will not disturb too much the industry."

Read together, the two sets of comments are consistent if you separate the platform from the publisher. Ubisoft can argue that a digital-only PlayStation is fine for the market while still wanting the option to sell discs on other platforms and for games that suit collectors. What is not settled is what happens to Ubisoft's physical share on PlayStation once the cutoff arrives in 2028.

## Why it matters for the stocks

There are four things to separate.

**Margins.** Digital sales avoid manufacturing, shipping and retailer margin. A publisher moving mix from disc to download generally keeps more of each sale, though platform fees apply. Whether that helps depends on the publisher's mix.

**Used-game market.** Wccftech reports that investors asked Ubisoft whether losing the used market would create a demand headwind. Used discs undercut new-game prices. Removing them could lift full-price sales or deter buyers who rely on resale. Nobody has data on that yet.

**Retailers.** Engadget expects negative effects on retailers and the second-hand market. That is a read from the reporter, not an announced figure, but it is the natural channel through which the change reaches listed companies outside gaming.

**Ubisoft's balance sheet.** The company [confirmed its full-year guidance](https://www.abcbourse.com/marches/ubisoft-confirme-ses-perspectives-annuelles-apres-une-baisse-de-son-activite-au_700233) on July 23: net bookings down by a high single-digit percentage, a negative high single-digit non-IFRS operating margin, and free cash flow consumption of up to EUR 500 million. It expects second-quarter net bookings of about EUR 370 million. A company in that position has reasons to defend any revenue channel that still works, which may be part of why its CFO is keeping discs on the table.

## What to watch next

- **Ubisoft's second-quarter sales.** The company has guided to about EUR 370 million. Its release did not state the reporting date, so check its investor site. Look for whether the digital share rebounds after the Assassin's Creed Black Flag Resynced launch, which the company said exceeded annual expectations within two weeks.
- **Other publishers' disc plans.** Tech4Gamers says others are expected to follow Sony, but names none. A named publisher committing to a digital-only date would be the first real test of that claim.
- **Sony's retail format.** Whether physical shelves hold download-code boxes or something else determines what happens to retailers. Sony has not detailed it.
- **Used-game pricing.** If used copies of 2027 releases start trading at a premium as the cutoff nears, that would be early evidence the market expects scarcity.

## The takeaway

The disc cutoff is a 2028 event, but the argument over it is happening in 2026 earnings calls. Sony's own data say digital is already the norm on PlayStation. Ubisoft's data say it is not universal across a publisher's mix. For investors the useful question is not who is right but which companies have the most revenue exposed to a format shift they do not control. This is information and commentary, not a recommendation on any security.
