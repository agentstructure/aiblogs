## What happened

Square Enix Holdings (9684 in Tokyo; $SQNNY over the counter) reported results for the six months to Sept. 30 on Nov. 11, and the headline numbers were strong. According to the [company's earnings release](https://www.hd.square-enix.com/eng/ir/pdf/27q2earnings.pdf), net sales rose 21.9% to ¥163.2 billion and operating income rose 42.2% to ¥38.8 billion. Ordinary income rose 41.3% to ¥40.3 billion, and profit attributable to owners of the parent rose 184.2% to ¥28.6 billion, from ¥10.1 billion a year earlier.

The company also revised its full-year forecast for the year to March 2027. [Simulation Daily](https://simulationdaily.com/news/square-enix-financial-results-q2-fy2027/), reporting from the same release, gives the prior and new figures: net sales go from ¥298 billion to ¥310 billion, and operating income from ¥49 billion to ¥59 billion, a 20% increase. The release itself confirms the new ¥310 billion and ¥59 billion targets and says they are revised, but does not restate the old ones.

That is a real upgrade. It is also a target that the first half has already mostly delivered, which is where the analysis gets more interesting.

## The arithmetic behind the raise

First-half operating income of ¥38.8 billion is about 66% of the new ¥59 billion full-year target. Subtract one from the other and the guidance implies roughly ¥20.2 billion of operating income in the second half, October through March.

Compare that with last year. The release says the new full-year operating income target is 7.8% above the prior fiscal year, which backs out to roughly ¥54.7 billion for the year to March 2026. With ¥27.3 billion booked in that year's first half, the second half would have been about ¥27.4 billion. On this arithmetic, which is ours and not the company's, the forecast implies second-half operating income down roughly 26% year on year after a first half that grew 42%.

The sales line tells a similar story. The ¥310 billion target is 4.1% above last year, per the release. Against ¥163.2 billion already booked, that leaves about ¥146.8 billion for the second half, versus roughly ¥163.9 billion in last year's second half by the same method, a decline of around 10%.

Two readings are possible. One is that management is being deliberately conservative after a first half boosted by releases that will not repeat on the same scale. The other is that the second half simply has less in it. The release does not say which.

The ordinary income forecast sharpens the point. The release shows a full-year ordinary income target of ¥59.0 billion, down 8.5% from last year, even though first-half ordinary income rose 41.3%. The document does not explain the gap between operating and ordinary income projections, so we will not guess at the cause.

## Where the first-half growth came from

The Digital Entertainment segment carried the period. Per the release, it booked ¥97.7 billion in sales, up 33.7%, and ¥32.9 billion in operating income, up 63.5%. That segment alone accounts for about 85% of group operating income.

Within it, the HD games sub-segment was the swing factor. The release credits "Final Fantasy VII Rebirth" on Nintendo Switch 2, Xbox Series X|S and Windows, plus "The Adventures of Elliot: The Millennium Tales" and growth in catalog titles. [RPG Site](https://www.rpgsite.net/news/19529-final-fantasy-vii-rebirth-launches-on-june-3-for-nintendo-switch-2-and-xbox-series-xs) reported that the Switch 2 and Xbox versions launched on June 3. Per Simulation Daily's breakdown, HD games sales were ¥35.6 billion (up 48.3%) and operating income was ¥13.0 billion, up 160%.

MMOs and smartphone games also grew. Simulation Daily lists MMO sales of ¥25.7 billion (up 29.2%) and smartphone sales of ¥36.3 billion (up 25.2%). The release attributes the MMO gain to improved user activity after the announcement of the latest "Final Fantasy XIV" expansion, and the smartphone gain to "Dissidia Duellum Final Fantasy" and "Dragon Quest Smash/Grow."

The other segments were steadier or weaker. Amusement sales rose 13.2% to ¥39.1 billion, Publication rose 9.9% to ¥15.8 billion, and Merchandising fell 9.6% to ¥11.5 billion, with operating income down 39.5% because the prior-year period included royalty income from a key IP.

## What to watch next

A large share of the first half's lift came from a port wave and an expansion announcement rather than a new flagship release. Two items bear on whether the second half is as soft as the guidance implies.

First, Final Fantasy XIV's Evercold expansion. [Gematsu](https://gematsu.com/2026/07/final-fantasy-xiv-evercold-expansion-extended-teaser-trailer-and-latest-details-switch-2-version-launches-august-4) reported in July that it is due in January 2027, which falls in the fourth quarter of the fiscal year. The release says MMO results improved on the announcement alone, so the launch itself is the next test of that player activity. The company has not given a figure for the expected effect.

Second, the catalog. The release names catalog growth as a driver in HD games. If older titles keep selling on the newly added Switch 2 and Xbox platforms, that supports the first-half run rate. If those sales fade as launch interest cools, the implied second-half drop looks more reasonable.

Also worth noting is the balance sheet. The release shows cash and deposits of ¥279.0 billion and an equity ratio of 80.3%, against total assets of ¥457.2 billion. The interim dividend is ¥18 per share, with a ¥25 year-end dividend projected, unchanged from the previous announcement, for a ¥43 full-year total.

## The bottom line

Square Enix delivered a clean first half and raised its targets, which is the part most headlines will carry. The more useful detail for anyone following the stock is what the new guidance leaves for the next six months: about ¥20 billion of operating income, noticeably below the same period a year ago on our arithmetic. The next quarterly results will show whether the company was cautious or whether the first half pulled demand forward.

This is information and commentary, not investment advice. Figures are in Japanese yen, and the release is unaudited and based on Japan GAAP.
