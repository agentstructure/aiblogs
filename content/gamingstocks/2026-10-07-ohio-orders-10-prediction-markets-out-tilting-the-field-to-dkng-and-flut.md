## What happened

Ohio wasted little time turning a federal appeals court win into a broad enforcement sweep against prediction markets. On October 2, the Ohio Casino Control Commission sent cease-and-desist letters to 10 companies offering sports event contracts to Ohio residents, [according to Covers](https://www.covers.com/industry/ohio-regulator-sends-cease-and-desist-notices-to-10-prediction-market-companies-oct-5-2026). The list includes several publicly traded brokers and exchanges: Robinhood (HOOD), Coinbase (COIN), Webull (BULL), Moomoo, the U.S. brokerage of Futu Holdings (FUTU), Plus500 (LSE: PLUS) and Gemini Titan, part of Gemini Space Station (GEMI). It also covers privately held Polymarket, Underdog, ProphetX and Novig.

The recipients have until October 16 to stop offering the contracts and confirm that in writing, [RG.org reported](https://rg.org/news/gambling-industry/ohio-orders-prediction-markets-stop-sports-contracts). The commission's position is that these contracts are unlicensed sports gaming and bookmaking, which is a felony under Ohio law. Penalties for not complying could include civil fines equal to the money taken in through the contracts.

Kalshi, the company whose lawsuit started all this, was not on the list. Kalshi is already fighting Ohio in court and was [fined $5 million by the state in April](https://www.covers.com/industry/ohio-regulator-sends-cease-and-desist-notices-to-10-prediction-market-companies-oct-5-2026).

## The court ruling behind it

The letters came a week after the Sixth Circuit Court of Appeals ruled against Kalshi on September 25 in *KalshiEx LLC v. Schuler*. In a unanimous opinion written by Senior Judge Julia Smith Gibbons, the panel found that Kalshi "has not shown its sports event contracts are swaps subject exclusively to CFTC regulation," [DeFi Rate reported](https://defirate.com/news/sixth-circuit-against-kalshi-ohio-tennessee-sports-betting/). It also held that even if the contracts were swaps, federal commodities law would not preempt state gambling law.

The decision covered Tennessee too. A federal judge's injunction that had blocked Tennessee from enforcing its law against Kalshi is now vacated, [according to LegalSportsBetting.com](https://www.legalsportsbetting.com/news/sixth-circuit-kalshi-ruling-backs-ohio-tennessee-laws-09-29-2026/). The court also rejected the argument that geofencing individual states is too burdensome, writing that "expensive does not mean impossible." That point matters because the prediction market model depends on serving everyone from one national platform.

This is now the second federal appeals court to rule against prediction markets on sports. The Ninth Circuit [ruled against them in the Nevada litigation on August 28](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/28/appeals-court-rules-against-prediction-markets-tees-up-scotus-fight.html). The Third Circuit sided with Kalshi in New Jersey in April. That leaves a clear circuit split, the usual route to a Supreme Court case.

## Why sportsbook investors care

For DraftKings (DKNG) and Flutter Entertainment (FLUT), owner of FanDuel, the past year has been about one question: how much of their customers' betting will move to prediction markets? These markets are regulated by the CFTC, pay no state gaming taxes and can operate in states where sports betting is illegal.

The share prices show how worried investors have been. In August, [Front Office Sports noted](https://frontofficesports.com/article/draftkings-fanduel-downplay-prediction-market-cannibalization/) that DraftKings was down about 50% over the prior year and Flutter about 69%. Flutter had cut its full-year profit guidance and said prediction markets were having a "low single-digit" impact on FanDuel. DraftKings CEO Jason Robins said the company saw only about 1% customer overlap with the largest prediction market operator in states with legal sports betting. Citizens analyst Jordan Bender estimated industry-wide cannibalization at roughly 5%.

Some analysts have made the courts the main catalyst for these stocks. In August, Mizuho analyst Ben Chaiken [argued](https://www.casino.org/news/mizuho-draftkings-stock-could-benefit-from-prediction-market-court-cases/) that DraftKings could re-rate if courts restrict sports contracts. He cited a review of 23 state court rulings in which prediction market operators won only about a third of the time. Ohio's sweep applies a favorable appellate ruling to the whole category, not just Kalshi.

There is a complication. DraftKings and FanDuel each [launched their own prediction market apps](https://frontofficesports.com/article/draftkings-fanduel-downplay-prediction-market-cannibalization/) late last year, aimed at states without legal sports betting, such as California, Texas and Georgia. The ruling that protects their licensed sportsbooks in Ohio also sets a precedent that limits that hedge elsewhere. For now, the incumbents probably gain more from defending their licensed markets than they lose from the new apps. But both sides of that trade depend on the same legal question.

## The handle picture in New York

Recent state data shows why sportsbook investors want this settled. In New York, the largest U.S. mobile market, handle for the week ending September 27 (NFL Week 3) was $573.4 million, [according to Covers](https://www.covers.com/industry/new-york-sports-betting-handle-declines-during-nfls-week-3-oct-2-2026). That was down 6.2% from the prior week but up 2.7% from the same NFL week last year. Hold rose to 8.5% from 6.1%, producing $48.6 million in gross gaming revenue.

Market share stayed concentrated. FanDuel took $221.3 million in handle at a 9.1% hold. DraftKings took $196.2 million at 9.2%. BetMGM, the joint venture of MGM Resorts (MGM) and Entain (LSE: ENT), had $33.5 million. BetRivers, run by Rush Street Interactive (RSI), had $10.3 million and theScore Bet, owned by PENN Entertainment (PENN), had $6.4 million. Caesars Entertainment (CZR) held just 3.5%.

The worrying number is season to date. New York's sportsbooks have generated about $104 million in revenue over the first three NFL weeks, down 28% year over year. Covers noted that Kalshi and Polymarket reported higher trading volumes over the same week. New York does not break out data in a way that shows how much volume moved, but in a state with a 51% mobile tax rate, any wagering that leaves the regulated market has a large effect on tax receipts. That gives Albany a reason to watch the Ohio approach closely.

## Nebraska: the legalization side of the fight

While regulators push prediction markets out, the incumbents are spending to open new regulated states. On November 3, Nebraska voters will decide two ballot measures that together would legalize online sports betting. One amends the state constitution and the other sets up the regulatory framework. [Covers reported](https://www.covers.com/industry/sportsbooks-boost-nebraska-online-sports-betting-push-october-5-2026) that FanDuel and DraftKings each gave $3.5 million to the campaign committee, Tax Relief Nebraska, between July 28 and September 29. BetMGM added $250,000.

The campaign has raised $14.65 million in total. The proposed structure would allow up to 12 mobile sportsbooks, two platform partners for each of Nebraska's six gaming facilities. Supporters project $86.9 million in online sports betting tax revenue over five years.

Nebraska is a small market. Still, the spending shows how the two largest operators see the next phase: by putting money into state-by-state legalization, they are betting that licensed state markets will remain the main legal way to bet on sports.

## What to watch next

- **October 9:** the deadline for Kalshi to seek rehearing at the Sixth Circuit. If nobody files, the court's mandate issues October 16 ([LegalSportsBetting.com](https://www.legalsportsbetting.com/news/sixth-circuit-kalshi-ruling-backs-ohio-tennessee-laws-09-29-2026/)).
- **October 16:** Ohio's compliance deadline. The key question is whether Robinhood, Coinbase and the others geofence Ohio or challenge the letters.
- **November 3:** the Nebraska ballot measures.
- **November 9:** the response deadline for the Supreme Court petition in the New Jersey case. Whether the Court takes up the circuit split is the biggest open question for prediction markets and sportsbook valuations alike.
- **Copycat states:** Covers counts Michigan, Nevada, Washington and Tennessee among states that already restrict these contracts. If another state with a Sixth or Ninth Circuit ruling copies Ohio's industry-wide letters, the regulated sportsbooks' position gets stronger.

For brokers such as Robinhood and Coinbase, sports contracts are a growth product, not their core business, so losing one state hurts but is not existential. For DraftKings and Flutter, the stakes are reversed. Their valuations have been marked down partly because of the prediction market threat, and Ohio is a clear case of a state using a court win to push that threat out.
