## What happened

Shareholder votes are the next gate for the Lottomatica and Cirsa merger. Lottomatica (LTMC) holds its extraordinary shareholders' meeting on [23 November, and Cirsa's holders meet on 25 to 26 November](https://en.ilsole24ore.com/art/lottomatica-2311-cirsa-merger-agm-closing-confirmed-for-q2-2027-AJfB6feB), according to Il Sole 24 Ore. Both boards approved the merger plan, and the terms were confirmed in a joint statement on [8 October](https://casinobeats.com/2026/10/11/lottomatica-cirsa-merger/), per CasinoBeats. Closing is planned for the second quarter of 2027.

The structure is an all-share deal in which Lottomatica absorbs Cirsa through an EU cross-border merger and stays the surviving company. Announced in early September, it would create what [iGaming Business](https://igamingbusiness.com/strategy/ma/lottomatica-cirsa-agree-merger/) calls the second-largest listed global gambling operator by combined adjusted EBITDA, behind Flutter.

## The terms

Cirsa holders receive 0.668 new Lottomatica shares for each Cirsa share. After completion, Lottomatica shareholders would own about 67.5% of the group and Cirsa shareholders about 32.5%. Blackstone, Cirsa's largest shareholder, would become the largest shareholder of the combined company with about 24%, [iGaming Business reports](https://igamingbusiness.com/strategy/ma/lottomatica-cirsa-agree-merger/). The board grows from 11 to 13 directors, and Blackstone can nominate two of them, per [CasinoBeats](https://casinobeats.com/2026/10/11/lottomatica-cirsa-merger/).

On price, iGaming Business says Cirsa's implied value is about 6x its expected 2026 EBITDA of EUR 800 million to EUR 820 million. Combined pro forma adjusted EBITDA is around EUR 2 billion.

Before the merger takes effect, Cirsa plans an extraordinary dividend of EUR 1.56 per share, about EUR 262 million in total. CasinoBeats also reports a potential EUR 744 million return to shareholders, delivered as a special dividend, a voluntary partial tender offer, or a mix. iGaming Business cites a larger ceiling: the enlarged group could return up to EUR 4 billion over the three years after completion.

## Revenue is not the same as ownership

The ownership split looks lopsided at first glance. On FY2025 revenue, Cirsa was the larger business: EUR 2.34 billion against EUR 2.26 billion for Lottomatica, per [iGaming Business](https://igamingbusiness.com/strategy/ma/lottomatica-cirsa-agree-merger/). Yet Lottomatica holders keep two-thirds of the combined equity.

The sources reviewed do not explain the gap, but earnings and debt are the obvious places to look. Lottomatica reported net debt of EUR 2,164 million at the end of the third quarter, or 2.4 times last-twelve-month run-rate adjusted EBITDA, a multiple that is pro forma for bolt-on deals and run-rate synergies, according to [Gambling.com](https://www.gambling.com/news/lottomatica-q3-adjusted-ebitda-up-3-to-200m). The sources I reviewed do not give Cirsa's leverage, so a full comparison of the two balance sheets is not possible from the public coverage reviewed here.

The deal's cost savings are modest relative to the combined size. The companies target about EUR 115 million of pre-tax cash synergies a year, from operating-cost and interest savings, by the third full year after completion, per iGaming Business. That is roughly 6% of the EUR 2 billion pro forma EBITDA figure, simple arithmetic on the two sourced numbers. Much of the case therefore rests on scale and the capital return rather than on cost cuts alone.

## The 5% exit trigger

The detail most worth watching is the withdrawal right. Cirsa shareholders who vote against the plan can take cash of EUR 13.20 per Cirsa share, reduced by any dividends or distributions paid before the merger takes effect. Completion is conditional on exits not exceeding 5% of Cirsa's issued shares, per [CasinoBeats](https://casinobeats.com/2026/10/11/lottomatica-cirsa-merger/). BDO Auditores, appointed by the Barcelona Commercial Registry, found the exchange ratio reasonable and the withdrawal compensation sufficient, according to the same report.

A simple conversion shows how the exit works. At 0.668 Lottomatica shares per Cirsa share, a EUR 13.20 cash exit equals what the shares would fetch if Lottomatica traded at about EUR 19.76 (13.20 divided by 0.668). If Lottomatica trades above that level around the vote, staying in the deal is worth more than taking the cash. If it trades below, the exit becomes more attractive, and the 5% threshold gets harder to hold. This is arithmetic, not a forecast, and it ignores the dividend adjustment.

## Operating trends heading into the vote

Both companies reported third-quarter numbers in the past two weeks, and both pointed to the high end of their 2026 ranges.

Lottomatica's third-quarter revenue rose 5% to EUR 537 million. Online revenue grew 13% to EUR 256 million, while sports revenue fell 1% to EUR 101 million and gaming revenue fell 2% to EUR 180 million. Reported adjusted EBITDA rose 3% to EUR 200 million. Normalized for a sports payout closer to assumed levels (80.5% retail and 85.5% online), it was EUR 234 million, up 17%. Online share of gross gaming revenue was 31.7%, up 0.9 points, all per [Gambling.com](https://www.gambling.com/news/lottomatica-q3-adjusted-ebitda-up-3-to-200m). Lottomatica confirmed its 2026 guidance and still expects normalized adjusted EBITDA at the top end of the range.

Cirsa's third-quarter revenue rose 12% to EUR 628 million, with operating profit up 6.8% to EUR 195 million, or EUR 203 million (up 11.1%) excluding merger-related costs. Online wagers rose 43.4% and online net revenue rose 19%. Cirsa said it expects to finish 2026 at [the upper end of the range previously communicated](https://igamingbusiness.com/finance/cirsa-targets-upper-end-2026-guidance-lottomatica-merger/), though the report does not state that range.

## What to watch

- **Nov. 23 and Nov. 25 to 26:** whether both meetings pass on first call, and whether any dissent is disclosed. Lottomatica holders will also vote on a 2027 to 2029 share option scheme, per Il Sole 24 Ore.
- **Withdrawal tally:** the share of Cirsa stock exercising the EUR 13.20 exit against the 5% cap.
- **Regulators:** filings have been submitted in Italy, Spain, Mexico and Morocco, plus a European foreign-subsidies review, per CasinoBeats. None of the reports I reviewed describes a decision yet, and the deal could still face conditions.
- **Capital return form:** whether the up-to-EUR 4 billion framing converts into a concrete special dividend or tender offer after completion.
- **Online mix:** online is already the growth engine at both companies, while Lottomatica's retail sports and gaming lines were down in the third quarter.
