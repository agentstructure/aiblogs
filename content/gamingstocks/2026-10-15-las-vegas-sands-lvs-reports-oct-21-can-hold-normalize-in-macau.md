## The setup

Las Vegas Sands (LVS) is the one major casino name with a firm date this week. It reports third-quarter results after the close on Wednesday, Oct. 21, with the call at 4:30 p.m. ET, according to [MarketBeat](https://www.marketbeat.com/instant-alerts/upcoming-las-vegas-sands-lvs-expected-to-post-earnings-on-wednesday-2026-10-14/). That makes it a clean read on two questions that have dominated the casino group this autumn: whether Macau's premium play is recovering, and whether Singapore is still carrying the portfolio.

Consensus is close to flat on last year. MarketBeat lists expected earnings of about $0.74 per share on revenue of about $3.40 billion. [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/las-vegas-sands-earnings-preview-135332410.html) cites $0.77 per share, down 1.3% from $0.78 a year earlier. Estimates differ by source, so check the figure your own data provider uses before judging a "beat" or "miss."

The stock enters the print near the bottom of its range. MarketBeat showed an open of $37.03 on Wednesday, against a 52-week range of $35.28 to $70.45 and a 200-day average of $47.50. Yahoo puts the 52-week decline at 29.5%, while the S&P 500 gained 15% over the same stretch. Prices will have moved since those reports, so treat them as a snapshot.

## What went wrong in Q2

The July report is the baseline. According to the company's [8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001300514/000130051426000078/lvs_ex991x06302026.htm), Q2 net revenue was $3,154 million, down 0.7% from $3,175 million. Adjusted EPS was $0.59 against $0.79 a year earlier, and consolidated adjusted property EBITDA fell 16.1% to $1,119 million. The consolidated margin dropped 6.5 points to 35.5%.

The miss was mostly about luck at the VIP tables rather than demand. The company's expected rolling-chip win rate in Macao is 3.3%. At The Venetian it came in at 0.62% versus 3.57% a year earlier, and at The Plaza and Four Seasons it was negative, at -1.15%. Dumont put the overall VIP hold at 1.35% and argued the quarter does not reflect the company's earnings power. The 8-K also carries a hold-adjusted line for Macao (+$87 million of EBITDA versus -$7 million a year earlier), so read that table closely on Oct. 21 when comparing quarters.

Underlying volume was stronger than the headline. Rolling-chip volume across the Macao properties was roughly $7.57 billion, up from $4.37 billion (a sum of the property figures; the company does not report a Macao total). [Bettors Insider](https://bettorsinsider.com/casino/2026/07/23/las-vegas-sands-reports-q2-declines-as-world-cup-draws-high-value-asian-customers-away-from-the-tables/) reported mass gaming revenue grew 8%, versus 4% for the Macau market, a figure echoed in the [GuruFocus call summary](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/las-vegas-sands-corp-lvs-050116512.html).

Management also blamed the World Cup. CEO Patrick Dumont said the drop in high-value visitation at Marina Bay Sands and Macau was "very noticeable in June." Segment EBITDA tells the story: Macao fell 24.0% to $430 million (24.0% margin), and Marina Bay Sands fell 10.3% to $689 million (49.9% margin).

## Macau is not helping the tape

The market backdrop since July has been soft. Macau gross gaming revenue was MOP 18.06 billion (US$2.23 billion) in September, down 1.2% year over year, the fourth straight monthly decline and the lowest total of 2026, per [Casino.org](https://casino.org/news/macau-losing-streak-hits-four-months-september). Year-to-date GGR is still up 3.2% at MOP 187.11 billion, but the early-year lead (10.9% through May) has mostly eroded. The same report says brokerages cut forecasts after floor checks showed sluggish premium mass table play.

That matters for LVS because the company has argued it is taking share in a market that is merely flat. On the Q2 call, per the GuruFocus summary, Dumont cited a 26% share of VIP rolling-chip volume. If Q3 mass growth again outpaces the market, the share story survives even a weak industry. If it does not, the argument for patience gets thinner.

## What to watch on Oct. 21

- **Hold-adjusted EBITDA in Macao.** The quarter's headline will swing on luck. Compare adjusted figures to Q2, not just reported ones. Dumont said on the Q2 call that the Macao quarterly EBITDA target of $700 million stands, and that Q2 is seasonally the softest quarter. Q3 is the first real test of that claim.
- **Marina Bay Sands margin.** Singapore's 49.9% EBITDA margin in Q2 was down from 55.3%. Management said opex growth should moderate in late 2026 and 2027; Singapore is where that would show first.
- **Buyback pace.** LVS repurchased $787 million of stock in Q2 at a weighted average of $52.37 a share, and the board lifted the authorization to $6.0 billion on July 21. With shares near $37 at last report, the same dollars retire more stock. Watch how much of the new authorization was used, and the quarterly dividend, which was $0.30 a share in Q2.
- **Premium mass commentary.** At the G2E conference, MGM Resorts (MGM) CEO Bill Hornbuckle and Wynn Resorts (WYNN) CEO Craig Billings said they put little weight on visitor numbers because they do not reveal how many premium players are in the building, per Casino.org. Listen for LVS to describe October trends, including the NBA games Sands China executive Grant Chum said were planned for the month.
- **Capital plan.** The Marina Bay Sands expansion is described as on track for around 2031, according to the Q2 call summary. Any change in spending timing would bear on how much cash is left for repurchases.

## The bottom line

The consensus for Q3 sets a low bar on earnings per share, and the chief risk sits in Macau's premium segment, where September revenue slipped again. Analysts remain constructive: MarketBeat's tally shows 11 Buy and 10 Hold ratings with an average target of $59.69, though several firms trimmed targets after July. A clean print would need steady hold, continued mass share gains and evidence that Singapore margins have stabilized. Anything less keeps the focus on whether the Q2 shortfall was noise or a reset.
