## The number that matters is not hold

Most coverage of US sportsbook earnings stops at "hold": the share of each dollar wagered that the operator keeps. The Q2 filings from the two largest US operators are more useful than that, because they show what sits between a sportsbook's built-in edge and the margin it reports.

Start with FanDuel. In its [Q2 2026 earnings release](https://flutter.com/media/g23an0ae/flutter-q2-2026-earnings-release.pdf), Flutter (FLUT) reported US sportsbook handle of $11,958 million, up 2%, and a US sportsbook net revenue margin of 8.7%, down 170 basis points from 10.4% a year earlier. US sportsbook revenue fell 15% to $1,039 million. Total US revenue fell 6% to $1,683 million, cushioned by iGaming revenue growth of 14%.

The margin drop did not come from a worse underlying product. Flutter said its structural revenue margin, the edge built into the odds and bet mix, rose 40 basis points to 14.0%, helped by heavy Same Game Parlay use during the FIFA World Cup. Sports results were a smaller factor: Flutter put the year-over-year swing at 70 basis points, with Q2 2026 results about 10 basis points favorable against 80 favorable a year earlier. That worked out to roughly $21 million of unfavorable in-quarter revenue impact versus a $90 million benefit in Q2 2025.

## Promotions are where the margin went

The largest line item was promotional spend. Flutter reported that promotions rose 140 basis points to 5.4% of handle, which it attributed to new state launches and World Cup investment.

A simple subtraction shows how much that matters. A 14.0% structural margin less 5.4% of handle in promotions leaves roughly 8.6%, close to the 8.7% reported once the small favorable results effect is included. That is our arithmetic, not a figure Flutter presents, and it ignores other adjustments. But it points to the direction: the margin an operator reports is increasingly a choice about how much to spend on customers, not just a function of how often favorites cover.

The company is leaning into that choice. Its [release](https://flutter.com/media/g23an0ae/flutter-q2-2026-earnings-release.pdf) says it is moving "from a focus on margin growth, to prioritizing AMP growth and customer value," where AMPs are average monthly players. US AMPs were 3.8 million, up 9%, with sportsbook AMPs up 8%.

## FanDuel budgets for it

The guidance revision is where this turns from description into a number investors can track. Flutter cut its US 2026 midpoints to $7.4 billion of revenue and $760 million of adjusted EBITDA, reductions of $395 million and $210 million. Within that, it listed net investment to strengthen the proposition of about $385 million of revenue and $270 million of adjusted EBITDA, plus a $75 million revenue and $50 million EBITDA phasing hit from a one-week delay to the 2026/2027 NFL season start.

Flutter also said it expects roughly 20% of full-year US revenue in Q3 and US adjusted EBITDA of about breakeven in the quarter. It described the sportsbook backdrop plainly: a "long sequence of customer-losing weeks" since Q4 2025 drove high churn, and its forecasts assume H2 market growth in line with the mid-single-digit pace of H1. Flutter reports a 39% US sportsbook and 27% iGaming GGR share across the states where FanDuel operates, so a company defending the top position is choosing to spend. Flutter has said it will share more details at its Q3 results in November.

## DraftKings tells a similar story with different mechanics

DraftKings (DKNG) reported Q2 on Aug. 6. Its [8-K exhibit](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001883685/000188368526000027/q226-prx8kexx991.htm) shows revenue of $1,443 million, down 4.6%, and sports revenue of $891.9 million, down 10.6%. Sports net revenue margin fell to 6.8% from 8.7%. The company attributed the decline to "customer-friendly sport outcomes" and higher promotional spending to acquire customers across sportsbook and prediction markets.

Adjusted EBITDA was $114.6 million versus $300.6 million a year earlier, and the quarter ended with a net loss of $67.6 million. Average revenue per monthly unique payer was $132, down about 13%, while monthly unique payers rose roughly 9% to 3.6 million. iGaming revenue grew 7.5% to $461.9 million.

DraftKings kept its 2026 guidance of $6.5 billion to $6.9 billion of revenue and $700 million to $900 million of adjusted EBITDA. Its CFO said the core business remains on track for about $1 billion of adjusted EBITDA this year, which leaves room to invest in Predictions.

The two companies are not directly comparable. DraftKings' $13.1 billion of "Sports Consumer Volume," up 15%, includes both sportsbook wagers and prediction market trades, whereas FanDuel's handle figure covers its sportsbook. Treat the growth gap between them with care.

## Prediction markets are now part of the margin discussion

Both companies framed prediction markets as an acquisition channel. DraftKings said Predictions launched in December 2025 and is "growing faster than we anticipated." Flutter said FanDuel Predicts lets it acquire customers ahead of sports betting regulation in new states, but conceded operational progress in H1 "has been slower than planned." It also said FanDuel Predicts revenue in Q2 was not material and that H2 gross revenues will be offset by customer acquisition costs before increasing in 2027.

On the question investors keep asking, Flutter said it continues to see "limited cannibalization impact" on its regulated sportsbook customer base, while adding that it will keep monitoring the growth of prediction markets. Flutter also expects about $50 million of 2026 revenue from market-making, providing liquidity for combination markets across prediction platforms. DraftKings did not quantify cannibalization in the release we reviewed.

## What to watch

Three items will test whether this spending buys durable growth.

First, Q3 results. Flutter points to November; we could not verify a date for DraftKings. The key lines are sportsbook net revenue margin against promotional spend as a share of handle, and whether Flutter's roughly breakeven US EBITDA guide for the quarter holds.

Second, the NFL season effect. Flutter explicitly guided to a phasing hit from the season start, which makes the first weeks of football a useful read on whether the extra spending is drawing players.

Third, state-level data. Monthly regulator reports will show whether handle growth is tracking Flutter's mid-single-digit market assumption, and whether promotional intensity is easing or building as new states mature.

For investors, the takeaway is structural rather than cyclical. When the edge in the odds rises but the reported margin falls, the gap is a spending decision, and both leading operators have signaled more of it. Whether that buys share and lasting customer value, or merely resets the cost of staying in place, is what the November prints should begin to answer.
