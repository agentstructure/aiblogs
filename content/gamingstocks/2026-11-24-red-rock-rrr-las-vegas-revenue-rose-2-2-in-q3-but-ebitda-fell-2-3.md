## What happened

Most Las Vegas casino coverage this autumn has been about the Strip and tourists. Red Rock Resorts (RRR), which runs the Station Casinos locals properties in Las Vegas, sells mostly to residents instead, and its third quarter shows a different version of the same squeeze: demand holding up, profit per dollar of revenue slipping.

According to the [company's Nov. 3 earnings release](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/red-rock-resorts-announces-third-quarter-2026-results-302927929.html), Las Vegas net revenue was $479.1 million, up 2.2% from $468.6 million a year earlier. Las Vegas Adjusted EBITDA was $204.6 million, down 2.3% from $209.4 million. On the call, CFO Stephen Cootey said the quarter had [the highest third-quarter gaming revenue and the second-highest third-quarter profitability in the company's history](https://www.tradingkey.com/news/transcripts/262272576-).

Consolidated net revenue rose 2.8% to $488.7 million. Consolidated Adjusted EBITDA fell 4.3% to $182.6 million, a figure that includes $5.8 million of one-time expenses tied to the company's 50th anniversary, per the same release.

## The $79.1 million line that hid the quarter

The headline loss needs context. Red Rock reported a net loss of $17.7 million, against net income of $76.9 million in the third quarter of 2025. The release attributes the swing "primarily" to $79.1 million of share-based compensation. That relates to stock awards for what the release describes as substantially all full-time and part-time employees.

On the call, Cootey described the grant as more than $72 million of Class A stock and called the associated expense, which includes payroll tax, one-time in nature and not part of EBITDA. That matters for how to read the numbers: the loss is largely an accounting charge, and the operating story sits in the Las Vegas segment.

One gap is worth flagging. The release cites $6.9 million of other one-time anniversary costs while showing only $5.8 million inside Adjusted EBITDA, and it does not explain the $1.1 million difference. Anyone modeling normalized earnings should note it.

## Revenue up, margin down

Using the release's own figures, Las Vegas Adjusted EBITDA margin works out to roughly 42.7% in the quarter versus roughly 44.7% a year earlier. That is my arithmetic from the reported revenue and EBITDA, not a company-stated margin.

Cootey offered two explanations on the call. One was a bad debt recovery in the prior-year quarter, which he said accounted for about 102 basis points of the margin decline. The other was a $1.2 million increase in utilities cost; he said NV Energy raised its seasonal peak demand rate, which applies from June 1 to Sept. 30. Management also said new-customer momentum helped: Durango Casino and Resort [has added more than 168,000 customers to the database](https://www.tradingkey.com/news/transcripts/262272576-), and executive Scott Kreeger said new sign-ups rose year over year and that uncarded play posted one of its strongest quarterly increases in some time.

The cost side is the longer-term concern. Cootey said that with expenses and payroll rising roughly 2% to 3%, the company would need about 3% to 4% revenue growth to keep margins stable. A quarter with 2.2% revenue growth does not clear that bar. The more useful read on locals-market operators is whether top-line growth can outrun labor and utility inflation, and this quarter says not yet.

## Spending is climbing

The release gave no capital spending figures, but the call did. Cootey said Red Rock now expects 2026 capital expenditures of approximately $550 million to $570 million, made up of $425 million to $435 million of investment capital and $125 million to $135 million of maintenance capital. He tied the increase to construction delays and cost overruns.

Two projects illustrate it:

- **Green Valley Ranch.** Cootey said the renovation budget started at $200 million and ended up close to $250 million. Frank Fertitta said the rooms came on later than expected, though Lorenzo Fertitta said the team was already seeing positive trends from the hotel.
- **Durango North.** The estimated investment is now approximately $430 million, up from $385 million, with an opening anticipated in the third quarter of 2027. Analysts quoted by [Casino.org](https://www.casino.org/news/red-rock-resorts-garners-analyst-support-following-post-earnings-slide/) said management maintains a targeted 20% return on investment over time and is studying a possible hotel expansion at Durango, though that account is secondhand.

Cootey also said 2027 should be a heavier capital year as Durango North and Sunset Station are finished.

## Balance sheet and shareholder returns

Per the release, total debt principal was $3.7 billion at quarter-end against $128.3 million of cash, and the board declared a $0.26 Class A dividend payable Dec. 31 to holders of record on Dec. 15. On the call, Cootey said the net debt-to-EBITDA ratio was 4.4x, that the company added $500 million to its Term Loan B and used the proceeds to bring revolver borrowings to zero, and that it repurchased about 470,000 Class A shares in the quarter at an average of $56.94 under its $900 million program.

Higher leverage with rising capital spending is the combination to track. It is not alarming on its own for a company that has historically funded new casinos from cash flow, but it narrows the room for a soft quarter.

## The wider Las Vegas backdrop

The Strip data are modest. Per [Casino.org's report on Nevada Gaming Control Board figures](https://www.casino.org/news/tight-slots-lift-las-vegas-strip-gaming-revenue-september/), Strip gaming win in September was $693.5 million, up 0.8% from a year earlier, with slot win of $452.6 million and table and sports-betting win of $240.9 million, down 3%. Statewide win was about $1.28 billion, flat. Visitor volume was about 3.1 million, flat, according to the same report. The article did not break out the locals market, so it does not directly measure Red Rock's customer base.

## What to watch next

- **Fourth-quarter Las Vegas EBITDA.** Cootey described the quarter as "steady as she goes," so the test is whether margin pressure persists with only low-single-digit revenue growth.
- **Upcoming Nevada Gaming Control Board reports.** Low-single-digit revenue growth looks different against a Strip market that is itself close to flat.
- **The next development announcement.** Management signaled on the call that a new project could come, per the Casino.org account, which would add to spending that is already rising.
- **Capital discipline.** With a $550 million to $570 million guide for 2026 and a heavier 2027 flagged, watch whether budgets stop moving.

None of this is a recommendation to buy or sell anything. It is a snapshot of a locals operator whose revenue is growing but whose costs are growing nearly as fast, at a time when it is spending more to expand.
