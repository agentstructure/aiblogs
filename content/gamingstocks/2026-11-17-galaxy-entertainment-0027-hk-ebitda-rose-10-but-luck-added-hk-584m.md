## What happened

Galaxy Entertainment Group (0027.HK), one of Macau's six casino concessionaires, reported third-quarter results on Nov. 6 that look strong at first glance and weaker on a second read. Adjusted EBITDA rose 10% year on year to HK$3.69 billion from HK$3.34 billion, according to [RTTNews](https://www.rttnews.com/3711165/galaxy-entertainment-q3-adj-ebitda-revenues-rise-normalized-adj-ebitda-drops.aspx?type=bn). Net revenue rose 4% to HK$12.69 billion from HK$12.16 billion.

The company's own adjustment tells a different story. Galaxy said gaming operations were lucky in the quarter, which added about HK$584 million to adjusted EBITDA, per [AGBrief's account of the release](https://agbrief.com/news/macau/06/11/2026/galaxy-entertainment-q3-adjusted-ebitda-rises-10-to-hk3-7-billion-as-good-luck-adds-hk584-million/). Without that, normalized adjusted EBITDA was about HK$3.1 billion, down 7% year on year and down 9% from the second quarter.

That gap is the story. Casino earnings swing with how often the house wins on high-stakes play, and Macau operators strip that noise out to show underlying performance. On that basis Galaxy's earnings went backward in a quarter when the whole market was soft.

## Why the comparison is fair

A fair question is whether last year's base was also distorted. It was not much. In its [Q3 2025 announcement](https://www.galaxyentertainment.com/uploads/investor/57584cd9732d9b158029fe4da1836826c185bdea.pdf), Galaxy said luck had added only about HK$14 million to adjusted EBITDA that quarter, and put normalized Q3 2025 adjusted EBITDA at HK$3.3 billion. So the 7% normalized decline compares a nearly neutral year-ago quarter with a lucky one that has been adjusted back down. It is a like-for-like drop, not a statistical artifact.

Trailing earnings look healthier. Latest-twelve-month adjusted EBITDA was HK$14.9 billion, up 11% year on year, per AGBrief, so the slowdown is a recent turn rather than a long slide. But a trailing figure that still includes strong earlier quarters can lag a market that has now been shrinking for five months.

The quarter-on-quarter figures point the same way. Headline EBITDA rose 9% from the second quarter, while the normalized figure fell 9%, so the sequential improvement was also hold.

## Where the pressure sits

Most of Galaxy's profit comes from one property. Galaxy Macau's adjusted EBITDA was HK$3.54 billion, up 15% year on year, on net revenue of HK$10.84 billion, up 7.4%, according to [SCCG's summary of the results](https://sccgmanagement.com/sccg-articles/2026/11/06/high-vip-hold-and-premium-additions-drive-galaxy-entertainment-groups-4-revenue-growth-to-us-1-62-billion-in-q3-2026/). AGBrief reports that this included about HK$576 million of favorable hold, which leaves normalized EBITDA of roughly HK$3.0 billion. In other words, almost the entire luck benefit sat at the flagship.

The underlying demand data are mixed. SCCG reports that rolling chip volume at Galaxy Macau fell 5.6% year on year, while mass table win slipped 1.3% to HK$8.10 billion. Slot win rose 16.7% to HK$677 million. The rolling chip win of HK$2.86 billion came on a win rate of 4.7%, and that win rate rather than volume growth is what lifted VIP revenue.

StarWorld, the older peninsula property, is the weak spot. Its adjusted EBITDA fell 24% to HK$279 million, according to AGBrief, and SCCG reports revenue down 10.6% to HK$1.13 billion. Galaxy attributes roughly HK$20 million of the EBITDA hit to renovation, with upgraded rooms and suites due in the first quarter of 2027.

Not everything is soft. Galaxy said Capella, its ultra-luxury hotel at Galaxy Macau, ramped up well, and the Horizon Plus premium area expanded from six to ten private salons. Hotel occupancy across the nine hotels at Galaxy Macau averaged 99%. Those additions target the premium player, the segment the property is leaning on.

## A market that is moving the other way

Galaxy's results landed against a shrinking Macau. Market gross gaming revenue in the third quarter was HK$58.5 billion, down 4% year on year, per AGBrief. Galaxy's own gross gaming revenue rose 5.2% to HK$12.88 billion, per [SCCG](https://sccgmanagement.com/sccg-articles/2026/11/06/galaxy-entertainment-group-reports-third-quarter-2026-adjusted-ebitda-of-us-470-6-million-up-10-4-percent/). By our arithmetic from those two figures, that is roughly a 22% share of market gaming revenue, though neither source states a market share.

The market then got worse. Macau's October gross gaming revenue fell 10.0% to MOP21.68 billion from MOP24.09 billion a year earlier, according to the regulator's data as reported by [AGBrief](https://agbrief.com/news/macau/01/11/2026/macau-october-ggr-falls-10-to-mop21-68-billion-despite-golden-week/). [Focus Gaming News](https://focusgn.com/asia-pacific/macau-ggr-october-revenue) calls it the fifth consecutive monthly year-on-year decline and the second-largest drop of the year. October is normally lifted by the National Day Golden Week, and AGBrief notes the result fell outside the range of market forecasts, which ran from a 3.5% decline to 4% growth.

Year to date, January to October gross gaming revenue is MOP208.8 billion, up 1.6%, so the cushion built earlier in the year is thinning. CICC, in an Oct. 17 note quoted by AGBrief, saw "no clear near-term inflection point" in market revenue or sector profitability.

## What it means and what to watch

For investors tracking Macau operators, the lesson is to read the luck line before the headline. Galaxy's reported 10% EBITDA growth and its normalized 7% decline describe the same quarter. Which one you treat as the base for the next few quarters changes how the stock looks, and operators with less favorable hold in the period would show the reverse pattern.

Galaxy itself is measured about the outlook. Chairman Francis Lui said GGR growth has moderated in the near term, citing possible taxation policy changes, slower global economic growth and geopolitical uncertainty, while remaining confident in Macau's medium- to longer-term outlook, according to SCCG. The company did not give figures for any of these, and we have not found sourcing on what tax change he means.

The balance sheet gives it room to wait. AGBrief reports cash and liquid investments of HK$36.4 billion against HK$2 billion of debt, a net cash position of HK$34.4 billion, after an interim dividend of HK$0.90 per share paid on Sept. 15. Galaxy Macau Phase 4, adding about 1,350 rooms and suites and a 5,000-seat theater, is scheduled for completion in 2027.

Three things to watch:

- **November revenue.** The regulator's table for November is due around Dec. 1. The question is whether the October slide persists or reflects a one-off.
- **Hold.** If Galaxy's luck reverses in the fourth quarter, headline EBITDA will fall below the normalized figure, and the 7% decline becomes the visible number.
- **StarWorld's renovation.** The Q1 2027 completion is the first test of whether the peninsula property can stop shrinking.
