## What happened

MGM Resorts (MGM) reported third-quarter results on Oct. 28, and the headline numbers were soft. Revenue was $4.21 billion, down 1% from $4.25 billion a year earlier, and consolidated adjusted EBITDA fell to $402.8 million from $505.8 million, a drop of about 20%, according to the company's [earnings release filed with the SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000789570/000078957026000106/mgmex991q32026earningrelea.htm). Adjusted EPS was a loss of $0.11 versus a profit of $0.24 a year ago.

MGM reported a net loss attributable to the company of $17.1 million, or $0.07 a share, against a loss of $285.3 million, or $1.05 a share, in the prior-year period.

## The Strip is a rate problem, not an occupancy problem

Las Vegas Strip revenue slipped 2% to $1.95 billion, but segment adjusted EBITDAR fell 12% to $530.8 million from $600.9 million. The cause was rooms. Occupancy was 89% in both periods, yet average daily rate dropped 8% to $218 and RevPAR dropped 8% to $193, per the release.

On the earnings call, as summarized in an [Investing.com transcript](https://www.investing.com/news/transcripts/earnings-call-transcript-mgm-resorts-posts-q3-2026-eps-miss-as-stock-steadies-93CH-4981087), management said rate declines deepened through the quarter: down roughly mid-single digits in August and high-single digits in September. An analyst pointed out that RevPAR has now declined for eight consecutive quarters. CEO Bill Hornbuckle conceded that "Las Vegas has been hurt more than most," according to [iGaming Business](https://igamingbusiness.com/finance/mgm-pares-losses-in-q3-2026/). Management also pointed to airfares into Las Vegas running about 45% above last summer.

There were offsets. Table games win rose 17% to $363 million, per iGaming Business, and the group and convention channel delivered more than 500,000 room nights in the quarter, ahead of internal expectations, according to the call summary. Management said October "is looking a lot more like July," when room rates were roughly flat year over year, and expects the fourth quarter to beat the third, helped by Formula One in November. December faces a convention rotation that sales teams are still working to fill. A new Strip competitor is expected in 2027, and management said it is investing in premium product ahead of that.

The open question is whether October is a bottom or a bounce. COO Ayesha Molino said it is too early to tell whether leisure demand has bottomed.

## Regionals and Macau diverge

Regional operations were the other soft spot. Revenue fell 8% to $883.2 million, but that includes the April sale of Northfield Park; same-store revenue rose 1%. Segment adjusted EBITDAR fell 18% to $242.6 million, with casino revenue down 10% to $626 million. iGaming Business reports slot win fell 11% and that Molino called the casino underperformance "material." Management attributed part of the same-store decline to hold at two properties and prior-year insurance proceeds, per the call summary.

Macau told the opposite story on volume. MGM China revenue rose 5% to $1.14 billion, and the release cites a record quarterly market share of 17.1%. Profit did not follow: segment adjusted EBITDAR fell 7% to $264.0 million, partly because an intercompany branding license fee more than doubled to $41 million from $19 million. Management described the Macau market as promotional and competitive and put margin at about 26.9%, within its guided range, per the call summary. The same summary says Golden Week visitation was up 10% year over year.

This is a useful reminder for the sector. Market share gains in Macau are being bought with promotion and high-end play, so share and earnings are not moving together.

## BetMGM improves, with a caveat

BetMGM North America, the online joint venture with Entain (ENT), reported its own third quarter earlier this month: net revenue of $710 million, up 7%, and adjusted EBITDA of $86 million, up 112%, according to [Capital.com](https://capital.com/en-int/news/betmgm-q3-net-revenue-rises-7-to-710-million-adjusted). The venture reiterated full-year guidance of $2.9 billion to $3.1 billion in net revenue and $300 million to $350 million in adjusted EBITDA, while saying it expects results near the lower end of those ranges, per the same coverage.

On the call, CFO Jonathan Halkyard argued that the MGM China stake plus BetMGM implies about $4.1 billion of value, which he said assigns zero value to MGM Digital and MGM Osaka. That is management's framing, not an independent valuation.

## The Diller chapter closes

The corporate news may matter more than the quarter. Barry Diller's People Inc. offered on June 1 to buy the MGM shares it did not own for $48.30 each, then withdrew the proposal in late September. [Hargreaves Lansdown](https://www.hl.co.uk/shares/stock-market-news/company--news/mgm-resorts-tumbles-as-people-inc-pulls-takeover-proposal) reported MGM shares fell 9.1% to $34.42 the day after, from a prior close of $37.85. [iGamingExpert](https://igamingexpert.com/features/people-mgm-resorts-proposal-off/) reported People still held about 66.8 million shares, roughly 27% of the company.

Per iGaming Business, Hornbuckle said on Oct. 28 that the idea of MGM acquiring People is off the table too: "we're done, and that's our position and that's People's position." The same day the companies amended their voting agreement. People agreed not to exceed 29.9% ownership and to vote with the board's recommendation on any change-of-control transaction, while MGM agreed not to take defensive measures adverse to People or Diller.

MGM bought back no stock in the quarter because of the talks. Share count stood at 251.6 million on Sept. 30, down from 258.3 million at year end. Management said those constraints have ended, and iGaming Business reports more than $1 billion remains under the authorization. The Investing.com call summary adds a limiter: Japan spending of roughly $1 billion a year in 2027 and 2028 will constrain buybacks somewhat, and management said it could take on more leverage if the stock stays undervalued. Cash was $2.40 billion at quarter end, and net long-term debt fell to $5.67 billion from $6.23 billion at year end, per the release.

## What to watch

- **Room rates.** If October's flat-ish ADR holds into November and December, the eight-quarter RevPAR slide may end. If not, the Strip margin problem becomes a 2027 story, with a new competitor arriving.
- **Buyback pace.** The authorization is back in play, but Osaka capital needs and leverage tolerance will set the speed. Osaka is about 11% complete and, per Hornbuckle, on time and on budget for a 2030 opening.
- **Macau margins.** Record share with falling EBITDAR needs to reverse for the China business to carry more of the valuation argument.
- **Regional slots.** An 11% drop in slot win is the line to track for signs of a consumer pullback beyond Las Vegas.
- **People Inc. at 29.9%.** The cap and voting agreement reduce takeover optionality that supported shares earlier this year. iGaming Business reports shares closed at $27.27 on Oct. 28, down 2.5% on the day and well below the June spike above $50.

None of this is a verdict on the stock. It is a quarter in which the business was weaker than a year ago, the takeover premium is gone, and the next catalyst is whether fourth-quarter Strip pricing actually improves.
