## Two reports, two days

Casino earnings season ends this week with the two operators most exposed to the premium customer. Melco Resorts & Entertainment (MLCO) will [release third-quarter results on Wednesday, Nov. 4](https://finviz.com/news/409819/melco-announces-earnings-release-date), with a call at 8:30 a.m. Eastern. Wynn Resorts (WYNN) follows on [Thursday, Nov. 5 after the close](https://finviz.com/news/399477/wynn-resorts-announces-third-quarter-earnings-release-date), with a call at 4:30 p.m. Eastern.

Both arrive after the sector's large-cap reports, so investors already know the shape of the quarter: soft Macau, a Strip that is holding its gaming win but not its pricing. What they do not know is how much of that shows up in two companies with very different mixes. Wynn earns its profit from three places, Las Vegas, Macau and Boston. Melco is almost entirely a Macau story. The same quarter should look quite different on each income statement.

## The Strip: win is steady, pricing is not

The Las Vegas numbers released last week give Wynn's Las Vegas segment a mixed backdrop. Strip gaming win was [$693.47 million in September, up 0.82%](https://news3lv.com/news/local/las-vegas-strip-gaming-win-september-2026-casinos-nevada-tourism-visitors-travel-gambling) from a year earlier. According to [Casino.org](https://www.casino.org/news/tight-slots-lift-las-vegas-strip-gaming-revenue-september/), slots did the work, producing $452.6 million at an 8.23% hold versus 8.04% a year ago. Table games and sports betting fell 3% to $240.9 million, and table game handle dropped 5%. Baccarat, the game that matters most to Wynn's high-end rooms, rose 12% to $58.6 million.

The weaker part was the room. The same report says visitor volume was flat at nearly 3.1 million, the Strip's average daily rate fell almost 11% to $185, and Strip RevPAR dropped nearly 7% to $139.51. A flat gaming line with falling rates is a margin problem: the property has the same customers paying less for rooms, while labor and operating costs do not fall with them.

Wynn's own second-quarter report shows how that played out before the latest data. Las Vegas adjusted property EBITDAR was [$215.2 million, down 8.3%](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001174922/000117492226000051/ex991wrlq22026pressrelease.htm) from $234.8 million, even though segment revenue rose 0.7% to $643.2 million. Revenue held up and profit did not. If September's pattern of soft rates and flat visitation was typical of the quarter, the question for Thursday is whether that gap narrowed or widened.

## Macau: a low bar, and a new hotel

Macau's gross gaming revenue was [MOP18.06 billion (about US$2.23 billion) in September, down 1.2%](https://focusgn.com/asia-pacific/macau-ggr-september-revenue). That was the fourth straight monthly year-on-year decline and the lowest monthly total of 2026. October was worse: GGR fell 10% to [MOP21.68 billion from MOP24.09 billion](https://focusgn.com/asia-pacific/macau-ggr-october-revenue), according to figures reported by [Intergame](https://www.intergameonline.com/land-based-gaming/news/macau-october-ggr-falls-10-to-mop21-68bn) and [The Standard](https://www.thestandard.com.hk/finance/article/347138/Macau-gaming-revenue-declines-10pc-in-October-despite-Golden-Week-boost). Jefferies had expected [weakness to continue into November](https://focusgn.com/asia-pacific/macau-ggr-jefferies-forecast), with a recovery in December. The same article cites a CLSA forecast of an 11% year-on-year drop in third-quarter EBITDA across Macau operators, to US$1.84 billion. The CLSA figure is a projection, not a result, and the operators will say how close it came.

Melco went into the quarter with the most to prove. Its [second-quarter revenue fell about 6% to US$1.25 billion](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001381640/000119312526350178/d152386dex991.htm) and adjusted property EBITDA dropped to US$303.8 million from US$377.7 million. City of Dreams carried most of the damage: adjusted EBITDA of US$147.8 million versus US$225.6 million a year earlier on revenue of US$632.2 million versus US$710.5 million. Studio City held up better, at US$95.5 million versus US$105.2 million.

Wynn's Macau results were steadier in the second quarter. Wynn Macau adjusted property EBITDAR was $95.5 million, down only 1.0% from $96.5 million, on revenue that rose $7.3 million to $351.1 million. That makes Macau the smaller part of Wynn's problem, if the comparison holds.

Melco also has a new variable. Its REM hotel was due to begin a [phased opening in the third quarter](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001381640/000119312526350178/d152386dex991.htm). Expect management to be asked what it costs to open and how quickly it can lift volumes on a Cotai floor that has been losing ground.

## Balance sheets and buybacks

Both companies have been returning cash, which is the second thing to watch. In the second quarter Wynn repurchased 741,098 shares for $75.0 million, an average of $101.20, and had [$326.1 million of authorization left](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001174922/000117492226000051/ex991wrlq22026pressrelease.htm). It also paid a $0.25 per share dividend. Total debt was $10.72 billion, of which $5.76 billion was Macau-related, against $1.57 billion in cash and equivalents, excluding $527.4 million of short-term investments held by Wynn Macau.

Melco bought back about 22.4 million ADSs for roughly US$120.6 million between April 1 and Aug. 12 and had about US$589.6 million of authorization left. It reported net debt of US$7.05 billion and about US$2.80 billion of liquidity at June 30.

Weak profit and ongoing repurchases pull in opposite directions. If third-quarter EBITDA falls again, the pace of buybacks becomes a sign of how confident each board is about the next two quarters.

Wynn also carries a long-dated commitment. Its 40%-owned Wynn Al Marjan Island resort in the UAE is [set to open in September 2027](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001174922/000117492226000051/ex991wrlq22026pressrelease.htm). Wynn contributed $48.1 million to the project in the second quarter, bringing its life-to-date contribution to $1.06 billion. Any update on timing or remaining funding will matter to investors weighing the capital the company still has to spend.

## What to watch

- **Wynn Las Vegas margins.** After an 8.3% EBITDAR drop in the second quarter, does the third quarter show the same revenue-up, profit-down pattern, or does lower room pricing finally pull revenue down too?
- **Melco's City of Dreams.** The property lost about a third of its adjusted EBITDA year over year in the second quarter. Another drop of that size would show a structural share problem, not a one-quarter slump.
- **Management tone on November.** After October's 10% drop, Jefferies' forecast of continued weakness will either be echoed or rebutted by the people with the daily numbers.
- **Capital return and spending.** Buyback pace, the Wynn Al Marjan timeline and the REM opening cost all compete for the same cash.

Neither report will settle the debate on its own. Together they will show whether the weakness seen at the large-cap operators last week was specific to those companies or part of a wider slowdown at the premium end. This is information, not investment advice.
