## Two neighbors, opposite results

Two state regulators published September numbers on Oct. 19, and they look like they describe different industries. In Pennsylvania, sports wagering revenue rose to [$44.7 million, up 50.45% from $29.7 million a year earlier](https://gamingcontrolboard.pa.gov/news-and-transparency/press-release/pa-gaming-control-board-reports-revenue-september), even though handle fell 6.09% to $798.7 million. Next door, New Jersey's sports wagering revenue [fell 14.3% to $77.0 million, on handle that dropped 7.3% to $725.5 million](https://www.nj.gov/oag/ge/docs/Financials/PressRelease2026/September2026.pdf).

Handle moved the same way in both states: down mid-to-high single digits. Revenue did not. The gap is almost entirely hold, the share of each wagered dollar the sportsbook keeps after paying winners. For investors in DraftKings (DKNG), Flutter (FLUT), MGM Resorts (MGM) and Penn Entertainment (PENN), it is a reminder of how much a single month of results can swing reported revenue without any change in customer demand.

## Doing the hold math

Neither regulator's headline publishes a hold percentage, so this is simple division on their reported figures. Treat the results as approximations, since each state defines revenue under its own rules.

- **Pennsylvania:** $44.74 million of revenue on $798.74 million of handle is about 5.6%. In September 2025 it was $29.74 million on $850.55 million, or about 3.5%.
- **New Jersey:** $76.98 million on $725.54 million is about 10.6%, versus $89.82 million on $782.39 million, or about 11.5%, a year earlier.

Pennsylvania's ratio rose by roughly two points. New Jersey's slipped by about one. A swing of that size on roughly $800 million of handle is worth many millions of dollars in a single month, which is why analysts talk about "customer-friendly" and "operator-friendly" outcomes and treat them as noise around the underlying trend.

[The Lines' coverage](https://www.thelines.com/legal-betting/pennsylvania-reports-jump-in-sports-betting-revenue-for-september/) of the Pennsylvania release did not offer an explanation for the jump, and neither regulator does. Revenue for individual venues was lumpy: Hollywood Casino at the Meadows reported $12.62 million of sports revenue, up 207.90%, while Valley Forge Casino Resort, the handle leader at $284.2 million, reported $15.77 million, down 0.51%. The PGCB release does not break results out by sportsbook brand, so it is not possible to say from this report which apps produced the swing.

## The year-to-date picture in New Jersey

New Jersey's regulator gives a longer view. Sports wagering revenue through September is [$748.4 million, down 6.3%](https://www.nj.gov/oag/ge/docs/Financials/PressRelease2026/September2026.pdf), on year-to-date handle of $8.30 billion, down 4.1%. So the state has been shrinking on both lines all year, not just in September. One month of weak hold did not cause that.

Online casino is the counterweight. New Jersey internet gaming win rose 13.0% in September to $274.8 million and is up 13.2% year to date at $2.40 billion. Total New Jersey gaming revenue rose 2.8% to $579.6 million for the month. Internet gaming win was about 3.6 times sports wagering revenue in September.

Pennsylvania shows the same shape. iGaming revenue was [$249.3 million, up 6.82%](https://gamingcontrolboard.pa.gov/news-and-transparency/press-release/pa-gaming-control-board-reports-revenue-september), more than five times the state's sports wagering revenue. Online slots rose 8.99% to $197.4 million. Retail tables fell 18.07%. Statewide gaming revenue rose 4.07% to $555.0 million.

## Why the sector keeps coming back to hold

The most recent company-level evidence is DraftKings' second quarter. The company reported revenue of $1.443 billion, [down 5% from a year earlier](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001883685/000188368526000027/q226-prx8kexx991.htm), and attributed the decline mainly to customer-friendly sport outcomes and higher promotional spending. Its Sports Net Revenue Margin fell to 6.8% from 8.7%, even as Sports Consumer Volume grew 15% to $13.1 billion. Adjusted EBITDA was $114.6 million, against $300.6 million a year earlier. DraftKings maintained its 2026 guidance of $6.5 billion to $6.9 billion in revenue and $700 million to $900 million in adjusted EBITDA.

That is a company growing volume while margin per dollar fell. The September state data, with the caveat that it is two states and one month, shows the margin side can swing back without volume recovering. It also shows the opposite is possible: New Jersey, a large and mature market, posted lower hold than last year.

The same release put numbers on a second pressure point. DraftKings Predictions launched in December 2025, and the company said it is growing faster than it anticipated, with monthly unique payers up about 9% to 3.6 million, partly due to Predictions. Prediction markets do not show up in state sportsbook reports, so the handle and revenue figures regulators publish capture less of the total customer wallet than they used to. Pennsylvania's fantasy contest category is also shrinking: revenue was $1.49 million, down 23.75%, with DraftKings' share at $1.11 million, down 22.73%.

## What to watch next

- **Whether the Pennsylvania hold holds.** One month at 5.6% against 3.5% last year does not make a trend. October and November data, as the NFL calendar matures, will show whether it was a favorable stretch or a structural change.
- **Handle in both states.** Falling handle in two mature, adjacent markets is the more durable signal. If the declines persist, growth has to come from new states, iGaming or prediction products rather than from existing sportsbooks.
- **iGaming momentum.** Double-digit online casino growth in New Jersey and nearly 9% online slots growth in Pennsylvania keep pushing revenue mix toward a higher-margin product with fewer outcome swings.
- **Third-quarter reports.** Operators will report July through September results. Compare their reported sports margin with the state-level hold figures above, and look at whether guidance changes, given that DraftKings reiterated its full-year range in August.
- **Massachusetts and other states.** The Massachusetts Gaming Commission's most recent release covers August, so September data from that market is still to come.
