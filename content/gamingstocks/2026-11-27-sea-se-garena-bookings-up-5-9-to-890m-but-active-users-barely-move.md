## What Garena reported

Sea Limited (SE) is best known for Shopee, but its Garena division, the publisher of Free Fire, is the part of the company that speaks most directly to gaming engagement. In its [third-quarter 2026 results, released Nov. 10](https://finviz.com/news/419799/sea-limited-reports-third-quarter-2026-results), Sea said Garena bookings were $890.0 million, up 5.9% from a year earlier. Bookings count what players spend in the period, before accounting for how revenue is recognized over time.

The details underneath that number are where the story sits. Per the same release, Garena's quarterly active users were 671.7 million, compared with 670.8 million in the third quarter of 2025. That is a gain of 0.9 million players on a base of roughly two-thirds of a billion. The audience, in other words, did not grow in any meaningful way.

Spending did. Quarterly paying users rose 4.1% to 68.6 million. The paying user ratio, which is the share of active users who spent money, reached 10.2%, against 9.8% a year earlier. Average bookings per user were $1.33, compared with $1.25.

Put together, the release describes a Garena that grew by converting a slightly larger share of a flat player base into payers, and by getting a bit more from each of them. Management's own summary was that Garena "delivered a solid third quarter, with bookings continuing to grow and profitability continuing to improve."

## Profit grew faster than bookings

The profit lines moved more than the bookings line. Garena's GAAP revenue was $840.1 million, up 28.6% from $653.0 million. Adjusted EBITDA was $458.2 million, up 45.9% from $314.0 million, according to the release.

Revenue can outrun bookings when earlier player spending is recognized in a later period. The release gives a hint of how large that effect was this time: the net effect of deferred revenue changes and related cost was $52.0 million in the quarter, against $151.9 million a year earlier. A smaller deferral build means less of the quarter's player spending was parked for later, so the gap between bookings and revenue narrowed. Investors who compare Garena's revenue growth with its bookings growth should keep that accounting in view, because the two lines are measuring different things.

The practical reading is that the 28.6% revenue figure overstates the pace at which players are currently spending. The 5.9% bookings figure is the cleaner read on demand, and it is a more modest one.

## The company around it

Garena is now a smaller part of a much larger group. Sea reported total revenue of $8.6 billion, up 43.4%, gross profit of $3.9 billion, up 51.4%, and net income of $502.5 million, up 34.0%. Total adjusted EBITDA was $931.3 million, up 28.9%. Garena's adjusted EBITDA of $458.2 million therefore accounts for a large share of group profit even though its revenue is a fraction of the total, which is part of why its engagement trends matter to the whole stock.

The company also said it had used about $997.2 million of its $1.0 billion repurchase program, buying 11.1 million shares, and that its board approved a further $1.0 billion authorization on Nov. 9 with no expiration. Timing and amounts depend on market conditions, per the release. A buyback does not change how Garena's players behave, but it does show where management is putting spare cash while the gaming audience is flat.

## Why a flat audience matters

Free Fire's business model depends on a very large free-to-play base, with a small paying minority funding everything. When the base stops growing, the levers left are the paying ratio, spend per payer and new content. The release shows Garena improved the first two this quarter. The risk is that each has a ceiling. A paying ratio of 10.2% can edge higher, but it cannot rise forever, and per-user spend usually depends on a steady stream of collaborations and events.

That makes new audience sources the question to watch. The release shows no meaningful expansion in the player count, so any bookings acceleration that does not come from a larger base has to come from deeper monetization, which tends to be harder to repeat each year. Readers tracking the stock should watch whether quarterly active users start to move in coming reports, and whether the paying ratio keeps climbing from the 10.2% level.

## What esports data says about the wider audience

Garena does not report esports viewership, and nothing in Sea's release ties its player numbers to tournament audiences, so treat this section as backdrop rather than a link. Still, the esports data from the past few months shows how uneven engagement is across the industry.

Esports Charts, a third-party data firm that tracks live viewership, reported that the Esports World Cup in 2026 generated more than 237 million hours watched across 25 tournaments, a 29.5% increase, according to [GamesBeat's summary](https://gamesbeat.com/esports-world-cup-viewership-jumps-37-in-2026/). Average live viewership rose 36.9% to 145,100, while peak viewership came in 6% below the prior year's record. Esports Charts attributed the growth to non-English broadcasts and expansion on Kick, where hours watched doubled.

The League of Legends World Championship went the other way. Preliminary Esports Charts figures, which exclude mainland China streaming platforms, put the 2026 peak at 3,981,986 concurrent viewers, described as a 41% drop from 2025's 6,752,585, per [Strafe](https://www.strafe.com/news/read/worlds-2026-viewership-drops-41-outside-china-but-bilibili-draws-millions/). Esports Charts pointed to U.S.-based match times that were less convenient for parts of the Asian audience and to the absence of T1 from the final as possible factors. Bilibili separately said more than 20 million people watched BLG's championship on its platform, a figure the article notes cannot simply be added to the international peak because the metrics differ.

The common thread is that engagement is shifting toward specific regions, languages and platforms rather than rising evenly. For an operator like Garena, whose players are concentrated in markets where mobile is the main screen, that is consistent with a business that can grow spending without growing its headline user count.

## What to watch next

- **Active users.** Garena's 671.7 million quarterly actives were almost level with last year. A move either way in the fourth-quarter report would change the debate.
- **Paying ratio and spend per user.** The 10.2% ratio and $1.33 in average bookings per user are now the benchmarks for the next print.
- **Bookings versus revenue.** With a smaller deferred revenue build this quarter, the gap between revenue growth and bookings growth should be watched so that accounting does not get mistaken for demand.
- **Capital return.** The new $1.0 billion authorization gives management room to keep buying shares, with timing at its discretion.
