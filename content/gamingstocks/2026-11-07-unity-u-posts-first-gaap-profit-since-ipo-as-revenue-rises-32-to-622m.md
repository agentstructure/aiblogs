## What happened

Unity Software (U) reported third-quarter results on Nov. 5, and the quarter is the kind a company wants on the table when a suitor appears. According to the [company's earnings release filed with the SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001810806/000181080626000053/a2026q3ex-991.htm), total revenue rose 32% to $622.1 million from $470.6 million a year earlier. GAAP net income was $121.6 million, a 20% margin, against a loss of $126.8 million in the third quarter of 2025.

CEO Matt Bromberg called it Unity's "strongest ever" quarter and "the first GAAP profitable quarter since our IPO," which took place in 2020. Adjusted EBITDA reached $256.6 million, a 41% margin, up from $109.5 million a year earlier. Operating cash flow was $224.0 million and free cash flow was $219.7 million.

The timing matters because of a separate report. [PocketGamer.biz, summarizing a Reuters story](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/silver-lake-and-pif-explore-25bn-deal-to-acquire-unity/), says Silver Lake and Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund (PIF) have held talks about acquiring Unity at a valuation of about $25 billion. That report rests on unnamed people familiar with the matter. Unity has not confirmed it in anything cited here, and the same write-up says no deal has been agreed and terms could change. Treat it as a reported approach, not a transaction. The earnings, by contrast, are filed numbers.

## The mix shift is the story

Unity now reports two segments that behave very differently. Per the release, "strategic" revenue, which is the business Unity wants to keep, was $596.6 million, up 59%. "Non-strategic" revenue, the legacy businesses being wound down, fell 73% to $25.5 million from $95.7 million.

Inside strategic revenue, the split is lopsided:

- **Strategic Grow** (advertising and monetization tools) was $431.5 million, up 90%.
- **Strategic Create** (the game engine and related tools) was $165.1 million, up 11%.

The release's narrative section describes Grow Solutions at $457 million, up 44%, and Create growth of 8%. Those figures use a different scope than the strategic lines above, so the two sets should not be mixed. Either way, the direction is the same: advertising is growing several times faster than the engine business.

Bromberg said Vector, Unity's ad platform, grew 126% year over year and 40% from the prior quarter. That puts Unity in more direct competition with AppLovin, a point PocketGamer.biz also makes in its coverage of the reported approach.

## Beating the guide

In August, Unity guided to strategic revenue well below what it delivered. [Pulse 2.0's August report on the company's outlook](https://pulse2.com/unity-strategic-grow-revenue-targets-up-to-385-million-in-q3-as-vector-ai-drives-68-to-70-growth/) put the third-quarter range at $540 million to $550 million. The actual strategic figure in the filed release is $596.6 million, roughly $47 million above the top of that range. Because the actual figure is now out, the guidance is a historical benchmark and not a forecast.

Why it matters to this audience: a company that beats its own guide by that margin while crossing into GAAP profit changes how its stock is valued. Unity was long judged on potential. A 41% adjusted EBITDA margin and about $220 million of quarterly free cash flow give investors something closer to a conventional earnings multiple to apply, and that is the same math any buyer would run.

## Balance sheet and the November deadline

Unity ended the quarter with $2,651.5 million in cash, cash equivalents and restricted cash, up $587 million from $2,064 million at Dec. 31, 2025. Convertible notes totaled about $2.24 billion, split between $557.5 million classed as current and $1,680.9 million long-term.

The current portion is due Nov. 15, 2026, and the release says management plans to pay it off. The cash balance is roughly four and a half times that current amount, so repayment looks well covered on the filed numbers. It also means that, soon after the date, Unity's near-term debt load shrinks materially. A buyer would still inherit the long-term notes, and those terms would matter in any change of control, though the release does not detail them.

## Fourth-quarter guidance

The company gave the following outlook for the fourth quarter, which is guidance and not a result:

- Strategic revenue of $683 million to $691 million, up 65% to 67%.
- Strategic Grow of $515 million to $520 million, up 105% to 107%.
- Strategic Create of $168 million to $171 million, up 4% to 6%.
- Non-strategic revenue of about $5 million.
- Adjusted EBITDA of $290 million to $295 million, up 132% to 136%.

The Create line is the number to watch. Guidance implies the engine business grows in the single digits while Grow more than doubles. That is a very different company from the one that listed in 2020, and it leans on the ad market and on Vector holding its pace.

## Why the reported suitors are familiar

If the report is accurate, the buyers are not strangers to the sector. The same investor group, PIF, Silver Lake and Affinity Partners, [closed its $55 billion buyout of Electronic Arts on Aug. 4, 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leveraged_buyout_of_Electronic_Arts) at $210 per share in cash, a 25% premium. That deal added roughly $20 billion of debt to EA, according to the same source. PocketGamer.biz adds that Silver Lake already owns about 8% of Unity and that PIF's Savvy Games Group has a memorandum of understanding with Unity to support game development in Saudi Arabia.

That shared history is context, not confirmation. Large shareholders approaching a company, and a company hiring a bank to weigh options, are common steps that often end without an agreement.

## What to watch next

- **Confirmation or denial.** Whether Unity, Silver Lake or PIF comment on the reported talks, or whether an offer becomes public.
- **The Nov. 15 repayment.** A clean payoff would remove the largest near-term claim on the cash balance.
- **Vector's pace.** The fourth-quarter guide needs Grow to roughly double. A miss there would undercut the profitability story that makes the company attractive.
- **Create growth.** Single-digit engine growth is the weakest part of the mix and the part a buyer might price most cautiously.
