## The deal is signed. Then what?

Most M&A coverage stops at the announcement or the closing date. This week gave investors three examples of what comes after, and they point in different directions.

On Oct. 21, Electronic Arts told staff it is shutting down BioWare, the studio behind Mass Effect and Dragon Age, and cutting about 400 jobs, roughly 3% of its workforce, according to [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/ea-shuts-bioware-400-job-cuts). [Cox Media Group's report](https://www.wdbo.com/news/trending/electronic-arts-cut-400-jobs-shut-down-development-studio-bioware/KUK6GRHMBNCVXC45LB7KGMOXLU/) puts EA's pre-layoff headcount at about 14,500. On Oct. 19, Supercell said it had completed its purchase of Merge Mansion maker Metacore. And Caesars Entertainment (CZR), whose shareholders have already approved a sale to Fertitta Entertainment, is still waiting on antitrust and gaming regulators.

None of these is a signing-day story. Each is about the stretch where the financing, the regulators and the integration plan meet reality.

## EA: 11 weeks from closing to closure

EA's $55 billion take-private [closed on Aug. 4](https://thewrap.com/industry-news/deals-ma/electronic-arts-goes-private-55-billion-buyout-closes), at $210 in cash per share. The buyers are Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund (PIF), Silver Lake and Jared Kushner's Affinity Partners. TheWrap describes it as the largest leveraged buyout in history. The financing was about $36 billion of equity, including PIF's rollover of its 9.9% stake, plus $20 billion of debt from J.P. Morgan Chase, of which $18 billion was funded at closing. [PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/eas-55bn-buyout-complete-in-deal-led-by-saudi-arabias-pif/) reports PIF holds 93% of the company.

That means roughly a third of the purchase price, $18 billion of $55 billion, sat on EA's books as debt on day one. Eleven weeks later, EA is cutting. Its memo, quoted by [Insider Gaming](https://insider-gaming.com/ea-shutting-down-bioware-cut-400-jobs-across-the-company/), says the company is making "deliberate choices to align talent and investments" where it sees the greatest opportunities.

What got cut, per The Next Web and Insider Gaming:

- BioWare closes. About 50 of the 400 lost roles are at its Edmonton studio.
- The next Mass Effect game, in early development, is cancelled.
- A Sims project codenamed Project Lotus is cancelled.
- The team on the skate. game shrinks.
- Sports games, Apex Legends, Battlefield and Star Wars Jedi continue, and Respawn and DICE will work on a new project.

Insider Gaming also reports that more cuts are expected over the next 12 to 18 months. The Next Web says EA has told investors it planned to save up to $700 million, partly by trimming research and development. That figure comes from a single outlet, so treat it as reported, not confirmed by EA in a document we have seen.

One caution. EA's note to staff says the review took "several months," and The Next Web notes that BioWare's recent games, Anthem and Dragon Age: The Veilguard, underperformed, with deep staff cuts at the studio in 2025 after Veilguard missed sales targets. This is not a case of a new owner inventing a problem. But the timing shows how quickly a leveraged owner moves from deal logic to portfolio logic.

## Why it matters for public gaming stocks

EA no longer trades, so there is no ticker to watch. The read-through is for what remains public.

First, a private EA with an $18 billion funded debt load competes for talent and franchises without quarterly earnings pressure, but also without much slack. Cutting a prestige RPG studio to protect sports and live-service franchises is a signal about where the money is going. Public peers will be measured against that mix.

Second, the buyer is a sovereign fund that already owns Savvy Games. The Next Web notes that PIF has reportedly considered merging Savvy with EA, though that is a report, not an announcement. If that consolidation came, it would be one of the few remaining large combinations available in the sector.

Third, this is a reminder to read the cost side of any announced take-private. Headline premiums get attention. The cuts that fund the interest bill arrive afterward.

## Caesars: shareholders said yes, regulators have not

Caesars (CZR) agreed on May 28 to be acquired by Fertitta Entertainment for $31 per share in cash, a deal [valued at about $17.6 billion](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001590895/000119312526242995/d143382dex991.htm) including about $11.9 billion of assumed debt. The company said the price was a 49% premium to its unaffected share price on Feb. 25, 2026.

At the Sept. 22 special meeting, [133.3 million shares voted in favor](https://www.gaming.net/caesars-stockholders-approve-fertitta-entertainment-merger/), about 65.4% of outstanding shares, against 4.3 million against. The same report says both Caesars and Fertitta received an FTC second request on Sept. 14, and that the antitrust waiting period stays open until 30 days after each company substantially complies.

Gaming approvals are the other gate. Per [Hotel Dive](https://www.hoteldive.com/news/caesars-shareholders-approve-acquisition-by-fertitta-entertainment/831287/), Fertitta's executives won unanimous approval from the Nevada Gaming Control Board in July, and Fertitta's general counsel said other jurisdictions "could take up to 10 months." The $31 price applies if the deal closes by June 26, 2027, with additional compensation if it closes later.

What to watch: the date the companies certify substantial compliance with the second request, and whether any state regulator attaches conditions. Neither company has given a closing date.

## Supercell and Metacore: a smaller deal with the same pattern

[Supercell completed its acquisition of Metacore](https://mobilegamer.biz/supercell-has-completed-its-acquisition-of-merge-mansion-maker-metacore/) on Oct. 19, after expecting to finish at the end of September. Terms were not disclosed. Metacore has cut 159 staff in Helsinki and closed its German and Swedish offices since the deal was announced in May. AppMagic estimates Merge Mansion has earned about $490 million since its 2020 launch.

The sequence matches EA's: buy the asset, then restructure it around the franchise that earns.

## The broader tally

Drake Star's Q3 data, reported by [PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/gaming-manda-reached-24bn-across-45-deals-in-q3-2026/), counted $2.4 billion of gaming M&A across 45 deals. Alibaba's sale of Lingxi Games to Trustar Capital, at more than $1.5 billion, was the largest, which means that single deal was over 60% of the quarter's value. EA's $55 billion closing came in the same quarter but sits far outside that tally's scale, which shows how lopsided the sector's deal flow is.

## What to watch next

- Further EA cuts, which Insider Gaming says to expect over 12 to 18 months, and any move on Savvy Games.
- The FTC process for Caesars (CZR), and the gaming-regulator timeline Fertitta described.
- Whether other studio owners with weak performers follow EA's template of cutting flagship-but-underperforming teams to protect proven franchises.
