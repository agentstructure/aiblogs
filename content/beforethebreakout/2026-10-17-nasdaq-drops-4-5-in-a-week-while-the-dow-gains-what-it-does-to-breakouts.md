## What happened this week

The tape that looked calm for most of October broke on Friday. According to the [AP's Friday market close](https://www.lanacion.com.ar/usa/how-major-us-stock-indexes-fared-friday-10162026-nid16102026/), the S&P 500 fell 1.1% to 7,659.44, the Nasdaq Composite dropped 2.1% to 26,140.03 and the Russell 2000 lost 1.7% to 2,721.37. The Dow Jones Industrial Average slipped only 0.3% to 52,002.41.

The weekly numbers show how uneven the damage was:

- **Nasdaq Composite:** down 1,226.14 points, or 4.5%, for the week.
- **S&P 500:** down 152.10 points, or 1.9%.
- **Russell 2000:** down 85.61 points, or 3%.
- **Dow:** up 347.46 points, or 0.7%.

The S&P 500 and the Nasdaq each had their worst week since June, per the same report. The prior week had been the opposite. On Oct. 9 the AP said stocks [finished a record week](https://www.lanacion.com.ar/usa/how-major-us-stock-indexes-fared-friday-1092026-nid09102026/), with the S&P 500 at 7,811.54 and the Nasdaq at 27,366.17. So the index that was sitting at a record on Oct. 9 gave back about 2% in five sessions, and the Nasdaq gave back more than twice that.

For breakout traders, the more useful fact is the shape of the damage, not the size. The Dow gained on the week while the Nasdaq fell hard. That is a market where a handful of heavily weighted names are moving the headline numbers, and where a chart in one sector can be healthy while the group around it is falling apart.

## Where the selling was concentrated

Friday's selling was a chip-and-megacap story. [AP data reproduced by DTN](https://www.dtnpf.com/agriculture/web/ag/news/world-policy/article/2026/10/16/us-markets-weighed-extended-sell) listed these declines:

- Intel (INTC): down 5.8%
- Advanced Micro Devices (AMD): down 6.0%
- Micron Technology (MU): down 5.6%
- Broadcom (AVGO): down 4.5%
- Meta Platforms (META): down 3.7%
- Alphabet (GOOGL): down 2.5%
- Tesla (TSLA): down 2.1%

Netflix (NFLX) fell 2.9% after a leadership change in its content unit and a planned layoff of about 6% of its workforce, so that one has its own story. The market-wide cause AP gave was doubt about how long the AI infrastructure boom can keep being financed, with bond yields at multi-year highs around the world.

Breadth on the day was less ugly than the index moves suggest. Slightly more than half of S&P 500 stocks declined, and all but seven of the index's 75 tech-sector stocks closed lower. Read that carefully: the damage was close to total inside technology but the rest of the index was nearly split down the middle. Energy did the opposite of tech. Chevron (CVX) rose 2.6% and Exxon Mobil (XOM) rose 2.5% as Brent crude gained 3.7% to settle at $109.86 a barrel.

That sector rotation is why the Dow could rise on the week. It is not a broad-based decline, and it is not a broad-based advance either. It is a transfer.

## The macro backdrop that matters for bases

Two numbers frame every setup right now. The 10-year Treasury yield eased to 5.09% on Friday from 5.10% on Thursday, per the AP report, after earlier topping 5.30%, its highest level since 2002. And the Fed raised its key rate a quarter point to about 3.9% in September, its first increase in three years. The CME Group data cited by AP put the odds of a December hike at 74.3%, up from 50.2% a month earlier. Markets expect the Fed to hold in October.

AP also noted that consumer and wholesale inflation rose more than economists expected this week, yet yields fell anyway. That matters because the market is pricing risk from two directions: higher discount rates on long-duration growth stocks, and a cost of capital problem for companies that need to borrow to fund AI buildouts.

Brent is up from about $72 in late February, before the Iran war began, to nearly $110. With no U.S.-Iran deal, the oil premium is still in the tape.

## What this does to breakout odds

None of the following is a prediction or a trade recommendation. It is a checklist for judging setups when leadership is under pressure.

**1. Check who your breakout is trying to lead.** A base in a semiconductor name that fell 5% on Friday is not the same setup it was on Thursday. When a whole group sells off on a theme rather than on company news, a breakout attempt in that group has to fight the group's tape. Setups outside the sold-off theme, such as energy names that were rising on Friday, are being tested by a different set of forces, mostly oil.

**2. Watch the depth of pullbacks inside bases.** A tight range that gets cut open by a 2% to 6% gap down is no longer a tight range. Traders who define a base by the narrowing of daily ranges and drying volume should note which bases broke their lows this week and which held. Holding while the group fell is the more interesting data point.

**3. Separate index weakness from stock weakness.** The Dow gained on the week. Stocks that held up on their own charts while the Nasdaq dropped 4.5% showed relative strength. Stocks that only looked fine because the index was fine are the ones to watch for follow-through failures.

**4. Respect the small-cap signal.** The Russell 2000 lost 3% on the week after the Oct. 9 report had it already down 0.9% for that week. With the 10-year yield above 5%, small caps are under pressure, and many swing-trade setups live there, so a weak Russell 2000 thins the pool of clean candidates. The index is still up 9.6% for the year, per AP, so this is a pullback from strength rather than a collapse.

**5. Look for failed breakouts.** In a week like this one, any breakout that triggered earlier and reversed on Friday is a candidate for a failure. Whether the stock closes back above its pivot over the next few sessions matters more than the volume on the original breakout day.

## What to watch next week

Earnings season continues, and AP expects strong profit growth across sectors. Results from AI-exposed companies will be read through the financing lens: not just whether demand is there, but how it is being paid for. On the macro side, watch the 10-year yield around the 5% line, since it topped 5.30% earlier and closed Friday at 5.09%, and any news on a U.S.-Iran agreement, which AP identified as a key driver for oil. The Fed's October meeting is expected to bring no change, so the focus is on what officials say about December.

For breakout hunters, the practical question is whether Friday was a one-day flush or the start of a longer reset in leadership. One session cannot answer that. A few things can: whether chips find a floor and build new bases above Friday's lows, whether energy and other leaders keep their relative strength, and whether the Russell 2000 stops falling before it gives back more of its 2026 gain. Until then, a market where the Dow rises and the Nasdaq drops 4.5% is one that rewards patience over aggression.
