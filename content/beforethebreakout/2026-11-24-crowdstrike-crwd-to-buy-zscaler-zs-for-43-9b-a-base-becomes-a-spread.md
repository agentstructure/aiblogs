## What was announced

CrowdStrike (CRWD) agreed to acquire Zscaler (ZS) in an all-stock transaction that the companies value at an enterprise value of approximately $43.9 billion, according to the [deal press release filed with the SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001713683/000119312526471211/d115803dex991.htm). Each Zscaler share converts into 1.135 CrowdStrike shares. At CrowdStrike's Nov. 20 close, the release puts that at about $260.41 per Zscaler share, roughly a 34% premium to Zscaler's close that day.

The announcement came Monday, Nov. 23, and the release says it replaced a joint conference call that had been scheduled for Dec. 1. The same release notes that CrowdStrike's fiscal third-quarter results and Zscaler's first-quarter results were issued separately. This post does not rely on either set of earnings figures, because the deal terms are the part that changes how the two charts behave.

The pieces that matter for a chartist, all from the [announcement release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001713683/000110465926130801/tm2630454d2_ex99-1.htm):

- **Ownership:** CrowdStrike shareholders would hold about 84.1% of the combined company and Zscaler shareholders about 15.9%.
- **Support:** Jay Chaudhry and affiliates, holding about 34% of Zscaler's outstanding shares, have signed voting agreements supporting the deal.
- **Timing:** Closing is expected in the second half of CrowdStrike's fiscal 2028, subject to a majority Zscaler shareholder vote, regulatory approvals and customary conditions.
- **Scale:** Zscaler had $3.9 billion of annual recurring revenue (ARR) in its most recently completed quarter, and the companies say the combined business would have more than $10 billion of ARR at closing.

## Why a fixed ratio changes the chart

Most breakout setups assume a stock is trading on its own story: earnings, guidance, sector flows. An all-stock deal with a fixed exchange ratio replaces that story. Once Zscaler holders are due 1.135 CrowdStrike shares per share, Zscaler's price is tied to CrowdStrike's price, less whatever discount the market applies for the chance the deal fails or drags.

That has three practical consequences for anyone reading ZS charts.

First, ZS's moves from here mostly reflect CRWD's moves plus the deal spread. A tight range in ZS no longer says much about accumulation in Zscaler itself. It may only say CRWD is quiet. A breakout in ZS that is not a breakout in CRWD would be a change in deal-completion odds, not a change in business demand.

Second, volume reads differently. Heavy turnover in a target after a deal announcement is often merger-arbitrage positioning and index or fund rebalancing, not the kind of institutional accumulation that traders look for in a base. A high-volume day in ZS after the announcement is less informative about future demand than the same day would be in a normal setup. The release does not give trading data, and this post does not either: check the actual volume yourself against the 50-day average before reading anything into it.

Third, the pressure moves to the acquirer. CrowdStrike would issue enough stock to leave its own holders with about 84.1% of the combined company. That issuance, plus the integration questions the filings flag, is what CRWD holders are now underwriting. The [8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001713683/000119312526471217/d115803d425.htm) lists risks including failure to satisfy closing conditions, integration challenges, litigation, management distraction and higher-than-expected transaction costs.

## What can make the spread move

The merger agreement spells out what has to happen, and each item is a dated catalyst the spread will react to.

**Zscaler shareholder vote.** A majority of outstanding Zscaler common stock must approve. With about 34% already committed through voting agreements, the vote is a lower hurdle than in a deal with no insider backing, though it still has to happen.

**Regulatory clearance.** The 8-K lists expiration or termination of Hart-Scott-Rodino Act and other antitrust or foreign-investment waiting periods as a condition. It also makes the absence of a "Burdensome Condition" imposed by a governmental authority a closing condition.

**Registration and listing.** CrowdStrike must have a Form S-4 declared effective and the new shares approved for listing on the Nasdaq Global Select Market. The S-4 filing itself is the first place readers will get a fuller picture of the companies' rationale and any financial projections.

**The clock.** Either side can terminate if the deal has not closed by Nov. 23, 2027, with an extension to Feb. 23, 2028 for antitrust or foreign-investment issues, plus up to 60 days for a government shutdown. Zscaler would owe a $1.4 billion termination fee in specified circumstances, including a change in its board's recommendation or a superior proposal.

The filing also says the 2028 convertible notes of Zscaler become convertible into CrowdStrike shares, cash or a combination at CrowdStrike's election, which is one more piece of ZS-linked paper that will trade off CRWD.

## What it means for the rest of the group

The companies frame the deal around AI. CrowdStrike CEO George Kurtz said in the release that "Security used to be about keeping the adversary out. Today, safety is required to let AI in," and Chaudhry said "AI agents are rapidly becoming the new weakest link." The release also cites 8,000 Zscaler customers who are not yet CrowdStrike customers as a cross-sell path and projects about $1 billion of annualized revenue synergies within 30 months of closing and $500 million of annualized cost synergies within 18 to 24 months. Those are the companies' own targets, not independent estimates.

For traders watching other security names, the deal creates a read-across question rather than an answer. A large buyout of a sector leader can pull attention to peers that are also building bases, or it can simply mean one fewer independent leader in the group. Nothing in the filings settles that. What the charts will show is whether peers hold their own prior pivots on rising volume in the next several sessions, which is the same test any base faces.

## What to watch next

- **CRWD's reaction relative to its own base.** An acquirer that gaps down on a stock-funded deal and then fails to reclaim the gap is behaving differently from one that digests it within a tight range. Mark the pre-announcement pivots and use the Nov. 20 close, the deal's reference price, as one anchor.
- **ZS's distance from the implied value.** The release gives the deal value per share at the Nov. 20 close. As CRWD moves, that implied value moves with it at the 1.135 ratio. A persistent gap between ZS and the implied value is the market's price for deal risk and time.
- **The S-4 and any regulatory filings.** Each is a scheduled information event.
- **Unusual-volume screens.** Expect ZS to keep showing up on them for deal reasons. Filter it out of breakout lists unless CRWD is confirming.

This is commentary on a developing situation, not a recommendation about either stock. The figures above are the companies' own deal terms as filed with the SEC, and a pending merger can change or fail.
