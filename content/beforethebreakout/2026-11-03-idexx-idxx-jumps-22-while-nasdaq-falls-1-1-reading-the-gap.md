## What happened

On Monday, November 2, IDEXX Laboratories (IDXX) closed up 21.85% after its third-quarter report, according to [Capp Notes' closing recap](https://cappnotes.substack.com/p/closing-look-monday-november-2-2026). The recap's other top gainers were Nextpower (NXT), up 14.87%, and PHINIA (PHIN), up 12.51%.

The backdrop makes the move more notable. The Nasdaq Composite fell 1.07% to 25,164.36 and the S&P 500 lost 0.51% to 7,541.40, its third straight decline, per [BBN Times](https://www.bbntimes.com/technology/nasdaq-today-composite-falls-to-25-164-36-as-oracle-amd-and-marvell-lead-fresh-tech-rout). The Dow rose 0.41%. Technology sank 2.95% as a group while Consumer Discretionary gained 2.41%, and the VIX rose 8.91% to 21.15, according to Capp Notes.

So this was a stock that rose by roughly a fifth on a day when the index most traders watch for risk appetite was heading the other way. For anyone who screens for relative-strength leaders, that is exactly the kind of print that lands on the list. The harder question is what kind of leader it is.

## The numbers behind the gap

IDEXX's [press release](https://ir.idexx.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/420/idexx-laboratories-announces-third-quarter-results) reported third-quarter revenue of $1,214 million, up 10% both as reported and organically. Diluted EPS was $4.01, up 18%, and comparable EPS was $3.96, up 22%. CAG Diagnostics recurring revenue grew 11% organically, helped by what the company called strong same-store volume growth.

The company also raised its 2026 outlook. Revenue guidance moved to $4,741 million to $4,767 million, and EPS guidance to $14.96 to $15.16, which the release describes as $0.25 above the prior midpoint. The company reported placing more than 1,900 inVue Dx analyzers in the quarter, bringing the installed base to nearly 11,000.

Two details complicate a clean "beat and raise" read. First, operating margin guidance was trimmed to 32.1% to 32.3% from 32.3% to 32.5%, per the same release. Second, on the earnings call, as summarized by [GuruFocus on Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/healthcare/articles/idexx-laboratories-inc-idxx-q3-210253436.html), management said U.S. same-store clinical visits fell an estimated 1.8% in the quarter. The call summary also puts the net price benefit in the diagnostics recurring revenue outlook at about 4%, and cites the CoVet acquisition as about $0.13 per share dilutive in 2026.

In plain terms, growth is leaning on new instruments, test volume per visit and price rather than on more foot traffic in clinics. Management argued that growth does not depend on positive visit trends. Whether the market keeps paying for that story is the open question after a gap this large.

## Where the stock came from

The price history matters more than the headline percentage. A 21.85% gain to a $671.64 close implies a prior close near $551. A [Timothy Sykes / StocksToTrade write-up](https://www.timothysykes.com/news/idexx-laboratories-inc-idxx-news-2026_11_02-2/) describes the stock moving from a close near $513 on October 8 to around $668 to $672, with a pullback into the low $550s around October 30 before the break higher. It also says the stock opened in the low $620s and finished near the top of its range.

That secondhand description is worth treating with care, since it comes from a single retail-oriented site and I could not corroborate the October 8 level elsewhere. But the broad shape is plausible and easy to check on a chart: a stock that climbed, pulled back into the low $550s, and then jumped well above that level in one session.

Two things stand out for base watchers:

- **The gap skipped the base-building step.** A classic setup tightens over weeks and breaks on rising volume. A gap compresses that into a single bar, which means there is no tight consolidation underneath the new price to anchor risk.
- **Closing near the high of the day is the constructive part.** Per the Sykes write-up, the stock held most of its opening gain and finished near the top of its range, which is the opposite of a gap that fades by the close.

I could not find a verified share-volume figure for the session, so I am not making a claim about how the volume compared with its average. That is the first thing to look up on your own platform, because a gap on light volume and a gap on several times normal volume are different signals.

## What the Street did with it

Analysts did not simply chase the move. According to the same Sykes summary, Goldman Sachs initiated at Buy with a $740 target, JPMorgan cut its target from $775 to $750 while keeping Overweight, UBS cut from $670 to $627 and stayed Neutral, and Jefferies cut from $795 to $705. KeyBanc initiated at Sector Weight. The article puts the average target around $719.

Notice how many of those targets sit near or above the new price while several were reduced. Targets being trimmed even as the stock gaps is a reminder that the rally partly reflects a reset from depressed expectations rather than a wave of new optimism. These figures also come from a single secondary source, so verify them before relying on them.

## What to watch next

The session after a large earnings gap often tells you more than the gap itself. A few things that would help separate a durable move from a one-day event:

- **Does the gap area hold?** Watch whether IDXX can trade in a tight range above the reported opening area in the low $620s rather than filling the gap quickly.
- **Does volume dry up on pullbacks?** Quiet pullbacks after a high-volume gap are what constructive follow-through usually looks like. Heavy-volume selling would argue the other way.
- **Visit trends.** The 1.8% decline in U.S. clinical visits is the soft spot in the story. A further deterioration would test the idea that volume per visit and pricing can carry growth.
- **Relative strength versus the tape.** The market backdrop is shaky. BBN Times notes the Nasdaq is about 9% below its October 6 record close of 27,599.89, and the week still holds Palantir results, the midterm elections and Friday's October jobs report. A stock that holds its gains if the tape weakens further shows more than one that merely rose on a calm day.

## Bottom line

IDEXX is a clean example of a stock showing strength against a weak group: a double-digit earnings gap on a day when technology fell almost 3% and the Nasdaq lost more than 1%. That earns it a place on a relative-strength watchlist. It does not, by itself, make a base. The company beat and raised, yet trimmed its margin outlook and reported fewer U.S. clinic visits, and the move arrived in one bar rather than through weeks of tightening.

For traders who study these setups, the useful work is in the days ahead: whether the stock builds a new, tight structure above the gap, what volume does on the way, and whether the broader market lets a defensive-leaning healthcare name keep its footing. This is information and commentary, not a recommendation to buy or sell any security.
