## A bounce with a narrow base

The Nasdaq Composite rose 1.1% on Monday to 26,430.89, and the S&P 500 added 0.4% to 7,690.73, according to the [Oct. 19 index table](https://www.lanacion.com.ar/usa/how-major-us-stock-indexes-fared-monday-10192026-nid19102026/). The Dow Jones Industrial Average slipped 0.1% to 51,966.03, and the Russell 2000 gained 0.3% to 2,728.30.

On the surface that is relief after the worst week for the S&P 500 since June. Underneath, it was thin. The AP reported that [most U.S. stocks outside AI and tech declined](https://ny1.com/nyc/all-boroughs/ap-top-news/2026/10/19/ai-stocks-lead-a-rebound-on-wall-street-as-european-markets-drop-following-protests) while Nvidia (NVDA) rose 1.4%. Nvidia is the largest U.S. stock by market value, so a small move there carries an outsized weight in the index.

For anyone scanning for setups, that distinction matters. An index rising on a handful of heavyweights tells you very little about whether the average chart is repairing. Monday's headline gain and Monday's breadth said different things.

## What the prior session set up

Friday's damage explains why the bounce was cautious. The S&P 500 fell 1.1% to 7,659.44 and the Nasdaq fell 2.1% to 26,140.03, [per the Oct. 16 closing table](https://www.lanacion.com.ar/usa/how-major-us-stock-indexes-fared-friday-10162026-nid16102026/). The Russell 2000 dropped 1.7%.

[Reuters reported](https://www.kitco.com/news/off-the-wire/2026-10-16/wall-street-tumbles-worries-about-ai-trade-and-high-oil-prices-weigh) that the selling centered on debt-heavy AI infrastructure names. The worry was that these companies may not turn profitable in time to repay borrowing, particularly as rates are expected to rise. CoreWeave fell about 12% that day, and its bonds sold off to yields above 15%. Reuters also noted hotter-than-expected consumer and producer inflation reports and a more-than-3% jump in oil.

Reuters' breadth snapshot, written before the close, showed the Nasdaq with 49 new highs against 263 new lows. The counts may have changed by the bell, but the lopsided ratio is the point: Friday was not a handful of stocks breaking down, it was broad damage on the exchange.

CoreWeave on Monday is a good illustration of a weak bounce. It rose more than 7% early, turned negative, and [closed down 0.4%](https://ny1.com/nyc/all-boroughs/ap-top-news/2026/10/19/ai-stocks-lead-a-rebound-on-wall-street-as-european-markets-drop-following-protests), after a 25.2% drop the week before. Buyers showed up at the open and could not hold the gain. A move that fades from a morning high after a steep decline is not the price action of a base forming.

## The cleaner signal: an earnings gap outside AI

The standout was Cleveland-Cliffs (CLF), up 16.3% after a larger-than-expected quarterly profit, per the AP. That is a stock with no connection to the AI debt story rising sharply in a session when most non-tech stocks fell.

The numbers behind the gap come from the company's [8-K exhibit](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000764065/000076406526000112/clf-202609308xkex991.htm):

- Revenue of $5,612 million and GAAP net income of $165 million.
- Adjusted EBITDA of $612 million, with adjusted diluted EPS of $0.28.
- An average net selling price of $1,177 per net ton, up from $1,124 in the second quarter and $1,032 a year earlier.
- Fourth-quarter adjusted EBITDA guidance of approximately $715 million.

CEO Lourenco Goncalves called it "our strongest quarter in three years." Rising realized prices plus guidance above the quarter just reported is the kind of fundamental change that can keep institutions buying for more than a day.

## How to read a gap like this

A single-day jump is an event, not yet a setup. The useful questions are about what happens next, and they are the same ones that apply to any earnings gap.

**Where does it close relative to the day's range?** A gap that holds near the high on heavy turnover says buyers kept absorbing supply. A gap that fades says early buyers sold into strength. Check the closing position on your own chart; this post does not cite a verified volume figure for Cliffs.

**Does it hold the gap area?** The price zone where the stock jumped from becomes the first reference for support. Orderly pullbacks that stay above it, on lighter trading, look different from a full round trip.

**Is the strength confirmed by peers?** One steel name rising on its own numbers is company-specific. If other industrial and materials names begin to hold up while the index chops, that is the rotation story. Without it, treat the move as isolated.

**Did it come from a base?** A gap out of a long, tight consolidation is a different animal from a gap that is merely recovering a prior breakdown. Look left on the weekly chart.

## The backdrop still matters

The macro tape is not friendly to extended moves. The 10-year Treasury yield rose to 5.14% Monday from 5.09% on Friday, after touching 5.30% earlier this month, its highest level since 2002, per the AP. Brent crude settled at $109.55. European indexes fell more than 1% after violent protests in France.

Reuters reported that Barclays expects 25-basis-point rate hikes in December and January, though markets mostly expect the Fed to hold this month. Higher yields pressure the valuations that growth and AI stocks rely on, which is the channel that drove last week's selling.

## What to watch this week

Earnings are the catalyst list. The AP noted that Meta Platforms (META) and Lam Research (LRCX) both report Wednesday, and Reuters named Tesla (TSLA) and Procter & Gamble (PG) among the earnings starting this week. FactSet analysts forecast 29% profit growth for S&P 500 companies year over year, per the AP, so the bar is high.

Three things will say whether Monday was a turn or a pause:

1. Whether the Nasdaq can add to Monday's gain without the 10-year yield pushing higher.
2. Whether the chip index, which Reuters said was heading for an eighth straight daily decline Friday, can stop falling.
3. Whether stocks like Cleveland-Cliffs hold their gaps while the tape is split, or give them back.

When leadership is narrow and the winners are all in one theme, a relief rally is easy to mistake for a new trend. A stock that moves on its own fundamentals while the rest of the market is soft is giving cleaner information. That is what relative strength is supposed to look like, and it is worth tracking through the next few sessions.
