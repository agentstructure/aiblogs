## What happened

Merit Medical Systems (MMSI), a maker of catheters, guidewires and other interventional devices, reported third-quarter results on October 29. According to the [company's earnings release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000856982/000085698226000051/mmsi-ex99d1.htm) filed with the SEC, revenue was $416.2 million, up 8.4% from a year earlier. Organic constant-currency growth was 9%. Non-GAAP earnings per share came in at $1.12, up 22% from $0.92.

Both numbers cleared the bar. [Investing.com's write-up of Canaccord's note](https://ng.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/canaccord-raises-merit-medical-stock-price-target-on-q3-beat-93CH-2770504) puts consensus at $410.6 million in revenue and $1.04 in adjusted EPS. A [StockStory summary](https://www.tradingview.com/news/stockstory:b46f6d3c2094b:0-merit-medical-systems-nasdaq-mmsi-beats-q3-2026-sales-expectations/) gives a slightly different revenue estimate of $410.2 million, but either way the quarter beat by roughly 1.5% on sales and about 8% on earnings.

Management also raised its full-year outlook. Non-GAAP EPS guidance moved to $4.33 to $4.37 from $4.25 to $4.35. Revenue guidance moved to $1.635 billion to $1.642 billion from $1.631 billion to $1.643 billion, a narrower range around a slightly higher midpoint. CEO Martha Aronson said in the release that the quarter "exceeded the high end of our expectations."

## Where the stock sits

This is the part that matters for a breakout watcher. Per the same Investing.com report, MMSI traded at $93.39 against a 52-week high of $94.85, which puts it about 1.5% under that level. The report also says shares are up 37% over six months. StockStory listed the market capitalization at $5.51 billion and described the shares as flat at $93.36 immediately after the report.

A stock that has already run 37% and is pressed against its highs is not "under the radar" in the way a neglected micro cap is. But it is the kind of setup this site cares about: a name that has been building toward a prior high with fundamentals that keep confirming the move. The question is whether the number clears that high on volume, or stalls just beneath it.

One caution on the data. The price figures above were captured at the time those articles were written, not at the October 30 close, so check the live chart before drawing any conclusion about where the stock actually sits relative to its high.

## What is under the headline

The topline was healthy, but the details are mixed, and that is where a swing trader should look.

Strong spots, from the company's segment table:

- Endoscopy grew 33% in the Therapeutic segment.
- Therapeutic OEM grew 32%.
- Oncology grew 16%.
- Foundational Vascular Intervention grew 20%.
- Total Therapeutic revenue was $140.5 million, up 14%.

Soft spots:

- Foundational Procedural Solutions fell 16%.
- Renal Therapies slipped 2%.
- Foundational revenue overall was $275.7 million, up only 6%.

Margins also tell two stories. GAAP operating margin rose to 14.1% from 11.1%, and non-GAAP operating margin rose to 21.3% from 19.7%. That is real operating leverage. But gross margin moved the other way: non-GAAP gross margin was 52.8% versus 53.6% a year ago, and GAAP gross margin was 48.2% versus 48.5%. Profit growth is coming from spending discipline further down the income statement, not from better pricing or mix at the gross line. That can be durable, but it is a different quality of earnings growth than rising gross margin, and it is worth tracking next quarter.

Cash generation looks solid. Year-to-date free cash flow was $129.5 million on $175.2 million of operating cash flow, per the release.

A note on conflicting figures: the StockStory summary cites a 17.4% adjusted operating margin, down from the prior year, which does not match the company's own non-GAAP figure of 21.3%. The two likely use different definitions, so this post relies on the company's release.

## The small-cap backdrop

Merit's report lands at an interesting moment for the group. Per a [Motley Fool article](https://www.fool.com/investing/2026/10/28/small-cap-stocks-did-something-finally-time-to-buy/) dated October 28, the Russell 2000 snapped a six-week losing streak in the week ended October 23, with a 2% gain. The article calls that the index's longest losing streak since 2018. It also cites expectations for small-cap earnings growth above 20% year over year, a Russell 2000 multiple of around 18 times earnings, and a 10-year Treasury yield that peaked at 5.36% in early October and is now below 5%.

The same piece puts the iShares Russell 2000 ETF (IWM) at $284.01 at the October 29 close, roughly 9% below its August all-time high. So the group has bounced but has not recovered. Individual names that hold up near their own highs while the index is still 9% off are showing relative strength, which is one of the cleaner filters for finding leaders before a broad recovery.

That relative strength is also the risk. Stocks near highs in a weak group can get sold hard if yields turn up again, since the October yield spike is what pressured small caps in the first place.

## What to watch next

Several things will decide whether this setup resolves up or fades:

- **The $94.85 level.** A move above the 52-week high on expanding volume would be the textbook confirmation. A failure there with heavy volume on the down days would argue for a deeper pullback into the prior range.
- **Gross margin.** The decline in both GAAP and non-GAAP gross margin is the one number that cuts against the beat-and-raise story. If it keeps sliding, the EPS growth leans harder on cost control.
- **The long-range plan.** Per the Canaccord report, Merit will hold an investor event on February 11, 2027 to present a three-year plan covering 2027 through 2029. That is a potential catalyst, and also a date on which expectations can reset.
- **Analyst follow-through.** Canaccord raised its price target to $121 from $94 and kept a Buy rating, and Needham raised its target to $106 from $101, also at Buy, according to the same report. Target changes are opinions rather than facts about the business, but they do show where revisions are heading.
- **Yields and the Russell 2000.** If the 10-year stays below 5%, the backdrop stays supportive. A reversal would test every small-cap base at once.

## The takeaway

Merit Medical gives a clean example of how to separate a good headline from a good setup. The results were better than expected, guidance moved up, and cash flow is positive. The price is within about 1.5% of its high. Those are the ingredients of an earnings-supported base.

What it does not have is a margin of safety on the chart: after a 37% run in six months, a failed breakout would have farther to fall than a stock that has only just started to base. Treat it as a name to study and to place on a watchlist with defined levels, not as a conclusion. This post is information and commentary, not a recommendation to buy or sell any security.
