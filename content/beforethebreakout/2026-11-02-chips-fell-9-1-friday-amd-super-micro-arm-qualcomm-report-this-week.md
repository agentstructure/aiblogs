## What happened

The iShares Semiconductor ETF (SOXX) tumbled 9.1% on Friday and finished the week down 2.4%, according to [CNBC's week-ahead outlook](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/30/stock-market-next-week-outlook-for-nov-2-6-2026.html). The same piece says the major averages lost ground for the week, led by tech after weak results from Amazon (AMZN) and Intel (INTC). Alphabet (GOOGL) fell 12% on Thursday, its worst one-day drop on record, then lost another 1.7% Friday.

Most of the 11 S&P 500 sectors still gained for the week, with health care and consumer staples drawing defensive buying. That split matters for a breakout trader. Money did not leave the market. It left the part of the market that had been carrying it.

Now the same group has to report. Four chip and chip-adjacent names, AMD (AMD), Super Micro Computer (SMCI), Arm Holdings (ARM) and Qualcomm (QCOM), report in a 48-hour window, and [CNBC counts 138 S&P 500 companies](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/30/stock-market-next-week-outlook-for-nov-2-6-2026.html) reporting this week overall.

## The calendar, in order

- **Tuesday, Nov. 3, after the close:** AMD and Super Micro. AMD's call is [set for 5 p.m. ET](https://ir.amd.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/1300/amd-to-report-fiscal-third-quarter-2026-financial-results), and so is [Super Micro's](https://ir.supermicro.com/news/news-details/2026/Supermicro-Provides-First-Quarter-of-Fiscal-Year-2027-Preliminary-Business-Update/default.aspx). Tuesday is also Election Day.
- **Wednesday, Nov. 4, after the close:** [Arm](https://newsroom.arm.com/news/arm-announces-earnings-release-date-for-second-quarter-fiscal-year-ending-2027), with a 5 p.m. ET call, and [Qualcomm](https://investingnews.com/qualcomm-schedules-fourth-quarter-and-fiscal-2026-earnings-release-and-conference-call/), whose call begins at 1:45 p.m. Pacific.
- **Friday, Nov. 6, 8:30 a.m. ET:** the [October jobs report](https://www.bls.gov/schedule/news_release/empsit.htm).

The sequence is the point. Two reports land before the next session opens on Wednesday, two more land before Thursday, and the labor data closes the week. A chart that survives Friday's flush could be tested three times before the weekend.

## AMD: the guide is public, so the bar is clear

AMD gave its third-quarter outlook on Aug. 4. In its [second-quarter release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000002488/000000248826000121/q22026991.htm), the company reported revenue of $11.5 billion, up 50% from a year earlier, and data center revenue of $6.7 billion, up 107%. It guided third-quarter revenue to about $13 billion, plus or minus $300 million, with non-GAAP gross margin of about 56%.

That gives the market three numbers to check: revenue against the $12.7 billion to $13.3 billion range, gross margin against 56%, and the data center growth rate against the 107% pace. On CNBC, Jim Cramer called AMD's report [another key test for the semiconductor sector](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/30/jim-cramer-week-ahead-apple-amd-jobs-report.html) after Intel's disappointing results, and said he expects AMD to post better numbers than Intel. That is one commentator's view, not a forecast anyone has to accept. What a chart reader can say is that the stock's reaction to the guide will likely say more than the headline beat or miss.

## Super Micro: the number is already out

Super Micro is the odd one this week because much of its answer was published two weeks ago. On Oct. 20 the company issued a [preliminary business update](https://ir.supermicro.com/news/news-details/2026/Supermicro-Provides-First-Quarter-of-Fiscal-Year-2027-Preliminary-Business-Update/default.aspx) for the quarter ended Sept. 30. It estimated revenue of $18.5 billion to $19.5 billion, against its own guidance of $14.5 billion to $15.5 billion, "primarily due to increased demand for AI platforms."

It also estimated gross margin of 12.0% to 12.5%, against guidance of 10.4% to 10.8%, "primarily due to customer and product mix."

Those figures are unaudited. The company says its auditor has not reviewed them and that actual results may differ materially. Because the headline range is known, Tuesday's attention should move to what is not: the outlook for the current quarter, margin durability, and how the company talks about customer concentration, which it lists among its risk factors. A stock whose quarter is pre-announced can trade on guidance alone, which is a cleaner read on whether a base is holding.

## Arm and Qualcomm: the second wave

Arm and Qualcomm report Wednesday after the close, so the market will have AMD's and Super Micro's reactions in hand first. I have not verified current consensus figures for either name, so none appear here. The setup question is simpler: if AMD and Super Micro move the group Wednesday morning, do Arm and Qualcomm trade ahead of their own prints in sympathy, and does that move hold into the reports?

For a swing trader, the useful discipline is to separate the gap from the base. A stock that gaps on earnings has made one large, single-day move. A base is the weeks of tightening that came before it. When SOXX can fall 9.1% in a session, the sector's bases were stressed whether or not a given stock has reported.

## The macro overlay: midterms and payrolls

Two non-earnings events sit on top. The midterms are Tuesday. According to CNBC, early polling and prediction markets suggest Democrats have a shot at winning both the House and Senate, and possible market effects include a pullback in defense spending, more pressure on drug prices and new data center regulation. Investors broadly expect limited near-term impact, though Siebert Financial's Mark Malek expects "volatility." CNBC also cites the Stock Trader's Almanac for a historical gain of 2.8% in the Dow across the eight trading days around midterm election days since 1934. History is context here, not a promise.

Friday's jobs report comes with a [FactSet consensus of 55,000 payrolls and a 4.2% unemployment rate](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/30/stock-market-next-week-outlook-for-nov-2-6-2026.html), per CNBC. September came in at 29,000. Cramer, for his part, expects strong hiring with limited wage growth, a mix he says could ease inflation concerns. After last week's Fed hold, the labor print is the data point most likely to move rate expectations.

## What to watch

- **Reaction versus result.** Note where each stock closes the session after its report relative to its pre-earnings range, not only the first-minute gap.
- **Volume on the follow-through day.** A gap that holds on heavy volume reads differently from one that fades into the close.
- **Group behavior.** If one name reverses a Friday low and others confirm, that is sector evidence. A lone mover is just one stock.
- **Earnings quality.** CNBC cites FactSet showing companies beating estimates by an average of 1.4% this season, against 7.5% over five years. A modest beat may not carry the weight it once did.
- **Support levels.** BTIG's Jonathan Krinsky expects the S&P 500 to test its 200-day moving average at 7,306, more than 5% below Thursday's close. That is one analyst's call, but it marks where index-level stress would become visible.

This is information and commentary, not investment advice. The reports will land in the next 48 hours, and each one is a chance to see which bases the selloff actually broke and which it only bent.
