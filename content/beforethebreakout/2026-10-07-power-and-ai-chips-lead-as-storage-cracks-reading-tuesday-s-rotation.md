## The tape under the record

On the surface, Tuesday was a clean up day. The S&P 500 closed at a record 7,818.93, up 0.58%, the Nasdaq Composite added 0.45% to 27,599.79 and the Dow rose 0.49%, [according to TheStreet's market wrap](https://www.thestreet.com/stock-market-today/stock-market-today-dow-jones-sp-500-nasdaq-updates-oct-06-2026). Treasury yields eased, with the 10-year settling near 5.275%, and WTI crude slipped 1.86% to $87.77.

Underneath, the picture was narrower. The Russell 2000 fell 0.57% on the same session. Utilities and real estate led the sector board, while health care was the weakest group, with only 48 of 428 Russell 2000 health care names finishing higher. That split matters more to swing traders than the headline record, because it tells you where institutional money is actually pressing and where it is stepping away.

Three groups stood out: nuclear-linked power producers, AI networking and compute semiconductors, and, on the other side of the ledger, hard-drive makers. Each one is at a different stage of the base-building process.

## Power producers: a catalyst lands on repaired charts

The spark was Constellation Energy (CEG). On Tuesday it signed a 20-year agreement with Alphabet's Google to add 890 megawatts of new nuclear capacity through uprates at existing plants in Illinois, Pennsylvania and New Jersey, [as ESG Today reported](https://www.esgtoday.com/constellation-signs-20-year-deal-with-google-to-unlock-890-mw-of-new-nuclear-capacity/). Constellation will invest $4.3 billion, the new capacity is due by the end of 2032, and a separate 15-year agreement covers 2,700 megawatts from Constellation's existing PJM fleet. The deal came roughly a week after Constellation announced a 20-year, 690-megawatt agreement with Amazon (AMZN) tied to its Calvert Cliffs plant in Maryland, [per TIKR](https://www.tikr.com/blog/googles-4-3-billion-bet-on-nuclear-comes-just-days-after-amazons).

CEG closed up about 12.3%, according to TheStreet. The sympathy moves were the more interesting part. [Stocktwits reported](https://stocktwits.com/news-articles/markets/equity/ceg-vst-tln-jump-google-s-power-deal-sparks-a-nuclear-rally/cZDtQh2RBlL) gains of about 10% in Vistra (VST), about 11% in Talen Energy (TLN), 8% in Oklo (OKLO) and NANO Nuclear Energy (NNE), and 6% in the VanEck uranium and nuclear ETF (NLR). The read-through logic is that Google's money signals value for any nuclear fleet in the same PJM region, though [24/7 Wall St. pointed out](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/10/06/constellation-energy-soars-12-on-google-nuclear-deal-for-890-mw-vistra-jumps-8-talen-energy-climbs-7/) that only Constellation and Google are parties to the contract.

From a setup perspective, the key detail is where these stocks started. 24/7 Wall St. noted CEG was still down about 15% for the year even after the jump. Vistra closed at $159.57, its highest level since July 27, and cleared its 200-day moving average (about $154.77) on volume roughly 4.8 times its typical average, [per Benzinga](https://www.benzinga.com/trading-ideas/movers/26/10/62202028/whats-going-on-with-vistra-stock-today). Benzinga also reported that the administration was weighing roughly $4 billion in loans to help finance upgrades at three Vistra nuclear plants in Ohio and Pennsylvania. Its 52-week high, though, is $217.10, set in October 2025, so the stock is still about 26% below that peak.

That makes this group a recovery story, not a group already breaking out to new highs. A gap on four to five times normal volume through the 200-day is the type of move that can start a new base, but it also leaves overhead supply from buyers who bought higher earlier in the year. The constructive version from here is a tight, low-volume pullback or sideways drift that holds most of Tuesday's gap. A quick fade back below the 200-day would suggest the move was a one-day news reaction.

## AI semis: leadership extending, with Marvell in a base

Technology has been the steadier leader. [Nasdaq's September review](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/september-2026-review-and-outlook) showed large-cap technology up 4.5% and communications up 4.3% for the month, with tech finishing within 1% of its all-time high while nine of 11 large-cap sectors declined. That is textbook relative strength: a group holding near highs while the rest of the market corrects.

Tuesday added fresh evidence. Marvell Technology (MRVL) used its investor day to raise its fiscal 2028 revenue outlook to $20 billion from $18 billion and lay out a fiscal 2031 target of $70 billion to $90 billion, [per The Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/coverage/stock-market-today/2026/10/06/stock-market-today-oct-6-marvell-stock-is-up-as-the-company-raises-fy2028-revenue-outlook-to-usd20-billion/). The stock closed up 5.81% at $287.01 on 50.1 million shares, about 122% above its three-month average.

For base watchers, the useful numbers are the range. Marvell's 52-week high is $329.88, which puts Tuesday's close about 13% below the peak. That is a normal correction depth for a high-growth semiconductor, and a heavy-volume up day off the lower part of a base is the kind of accumulation signal that often shows up before a stock works back toward its highs. Whether it builds a proper right side, with volume drying up on pullbacks, is the thing to track.

The group's leaders confirmed the move. Advanced Micro Devices (AMD) rose 2.80% to $649.42 after [Citi raised its price target](https://www.fool.com/coverage/stock-market-today/2026/10/06/stock-market-midday-oct-6-s-and-p-500-sets-new-high-nuclear-stocks-surge/), and Nvidia (NVDA) touched an all-time high, [according to Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/live/stock-market-today-tuesday-october-6-dow-sp-500-nasdaq-080526166.html). Yahoo also noted that Nvidia, Apple (AAPL) and Microsoft (MSFT) now make up more than 21% of the S&P 500, a reminder that index records are increasingly a statement about a handful of names.

## Storage: a leader group takes a supply hit

Not every AI-adjacent group is acting well. Seagate Technology (STX) fell 7.98% and Western Digital (WDC) dropped 6.92% on Tuesday, per TheStreet. [24/7 Wall St. tied the selling](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/10/06/western-digital-sinks-6-as-toshibas-drive-expansion-hits-the-group-seagate-drops-8-micron-holds-flat/) to Toshiba's plan to ramp hard disk drive output for AI infrastructure, which raised fresh supply concerns. Western Digital is still up 141% this year, so this is a big winner getting hit, not a laggard.

The distinction inside the memory complex is worth noting. Micron Technology (MU) edged up 0.3% to $1,067.02 while the drive makers sold off, suggesting the selling was specific to hard drives rather than a broad exit from memory. For anyone tracking extended leaders, a supply-driven break on heavy volume is the kind of event that can turn a leadership group into a group that needs a new base before it leads again.

## Small caps and rates: the backdrop to respect

The broader backdrop remains a headwind for anything small and rate-sensitive. Nasdaq's review noted the Russell 2000 fell 5.3% in September, its worst month since March 2025, as the 10-year Treasury yield rose 54 basis points during the month to 5.29%. Small-cap sectors averaged a 14.9% drawdown from their 52-week highs, compared with about 9% for large-cap sectors.

That helps explain why Tuesday's rally stayed concentrated in large-cap tech and in power names with an obvious catalyst. With the 10-year still above 5.2%, broad small-cap bases have a harder time holding together.

## What to watch next

- **Follow-through in power producers.** Whether CEG, VST and TLN hold most of Tuesday's gains over the next several sessions, and whether volume contracts as they do.
- **Marvell's range.** How MRVL trades between Tuesday's close and the $329.88 high, and whether pullbacks come on lighter volume.
- **Semiconductor breadth.** Whether strength spreads beyond the mega-caps or stays concentrated in Nvidia, AMD and a few others.
- **Storage stabilization.** Whether STX and WDC find support after the Toshiba headline, or whether the selling spreads to other memory names.
- **The 10-year yield.** Any move back toward or above September's 5.29% level is likely to weigh on small caps and rate-sensitive groups, while easing yields would give lagging groups room to repair.

Rotation rarely announces itself in one session. Tuesday showed money concentrating in AI compute and power, and stepping away from hard drives, health care and small caps. The next few sessions will show whether that concentration starts to build proper bases or fades.
