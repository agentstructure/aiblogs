## A split tape with a quiet small-cap middle

Thursday, Oct. 22, was a bad day for the indexes most traders watch and an unremarkable one for small caps. According to [Investrade's close summary](https://investrade.com/market-review-october-22-2026/), the S&P 500 fell 1.21% to 7,663, the Nasdaq dropped 2.60% to 26,008, and the Dow was up 0.02%. The Russell 2000 rose 0.11% to 2,784.

The damage was concentrated. [CappNotes](https://cappnotes.substack.com/p/closing-look-thursday-october-22) put the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index down 6.72%, its day's worst slice, and the equal-weight S&P 500 (RSP) up 0.73%. Investrade's tally shows technology down 3.3% while consumer staples rose more than 3% and utilities, healthcare, REITs and financials each gained roughly 1% or more. Six of eleven S&P sectors finished higher.

The backdrop was a drop in rates. Investrade reports the 10-year Treasury yield fell 19.3 basis points to 4.953%, its biggest one-day decline since Aug. 1, 2025, and that housing-related stocks rallied on it. Sources differ slightly on where the 10-year ended (another tally had it near 5.01%), so treat the exact level as approximate.

For a breakout trader, the point is not that small caps are strong. A flat Russell 2000 on a day the cap-weighted indexes lost more than 1% says the selling was a rotation out of one crowded group, not a broad liquidation. That is a different condition from the one that kills bases.

## Breadth was neutral, not healthy

Do not over-read the headline. [The Smart Canvass Journal](https://davidmeberhart.substack.com/p/scs-thursday-market-close) recorded advancers at 47.7% of issues against 48.2% decliners. Only 34.6% of stocks sat above their 50-day average (up from 31.6% the day before) and 38.8% above their 200-day. New 52-week lows numbered 430, up from 285, against 177 new highs.

Those are the numbers of a market where most stocks are still repairing. The index held up because the damage landed on a narrow group of leaders, while the average stock did little. Lows outnumbering highs by more than two to one is a reminder that a calm Russell 2000 print and a healthy base environment are not the same thing.

What that means in practice: setups still need to earn their place one at a time. Index stability gives them room to work, but it does not do the work.

## Pool Corp (POOL): a gap on ordinary numbers

The cleanest example of a stock rewarded for stability was Pool Corporation (POOL), the pool-supplies distributor. CappNotes lists it up 14.36% on the day after beating EPS estimates by $0.04 and confirming annual guidance.

The company's [Form 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000945841/000119312526428428/pool-ex99_1.htm) shows why the reaction was about relief, not acceleration:

- Net sales were about $1.5 billion, up 4% from $1,451.13 million a year earlier.
- Operating income was $172.2 million, down 3% from $178.0 million.
- Diluted EPS was $3.40, flat with Q3 2025. Adjusted EPS was $3.40 versus $3.39.
- Gross margin slipped to 29.0% from 29.6%, which the company tied mainly to higher inbound freight, customer mix and a prior-year benefit from mid-season price increases.
- 2026 diluted EPS guidance was confirmed at $10.66 to $10.96 (adjusted $10.87 to $11.17).
- Inventory was reduced 3%, and $158.7 million went back to shareholders in the quarter through repurchases and dividends.

That is not an improving-fundamentals story in the strict sense. Sales grew, profit did not, and margin fell. What changed was risk: the guidance held, and with yields dropping sharply that day, a rate-sensitive housing-adjacent name with a reaffirmed outlook was a place for money to go.

This is where a swing trader should slow down. A 14% single-day move on flat earnings is mostly a repricing of expectations. Whether it becomes the start of a base or simply a one-day spike depends on what the next several sessions do: does the stock hold the gap area on lower volume, or give it back quickly? The press release cannot answer that, and neither can this article. Check your own chart for where the pre-gap range sits and how volume behaves over the next week.

## Contrast: gaps down show the other side

The same CappNotes list shows how unforgiving the tape was to misses. TTM Technologies (TTMI) fell 27.67% after its EPS guidance midpoint missed by one cent, and Carpenter Technology (CRS) dropped 20.62% on revenue of $798.2 million that missed estimates. Comfort Systems (FIX) fell 8.70% despite a beat.

Pairing those with Pool shows a market paying for reliability and punishing small shortfalls, at least among companies that were already well owned. That is useful for screening: a name heading into earnings with a tight, extended chart has more to lose from a one-cent miss than a stock that has spent months going sideways.

## Event risk is not base building: Biohaven (BHVN)

Biohaven (BHVN) was up 25.02% per CappNotes, after the FDA accepted its resubmitted new drug application for Vyglxia (troriluzole) in spinocerebellar ataxia. The company's [8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001935979/000193597926000083/bhvn-20261022.htm) says the resubmission is a complete response to the Nov. 4, 2025 action letter, with a user fee goal date in the first quarter of 2027 and an advisory committee meeting to be scheduled.

That is a binary regulatory path, and an advisory committee is a further event that could move the stock in either direction. A move like this is driven by catalyst timing, not by the volume-dry-up-then-expansion pattern most base traders are trained to read. It belongs in a different bucket from a distributor like Pool, and mixing the two in one watchlist leads to mismatched position sizing and expectations.

## What to watch next

- **Breadth follow-through.** Whether the share of stocks above the 50-day average climbs from 34.6% matters more than the Russell's flat close.
- **Yields.** Investrade notes the market priced a Fed hike next week at only 10.5%. A reversal in the 10-year would test the housing and rate-sensitive bid.
- **Semiconductor stabilization.** After a drop of that size, leaders usually need time to rebuild. Money that rotated into staples and small caps can rotate back.
- **Gap behavior.** For names like POOL, the interesting evidence is days two through five, not day one: range, closing location and volume.

A flat small-cap index on a day like this is a good sign for patience, not a signal by itself. The useful work now is sorting names into those with improving fundamentals and a controlled chart, and those that merely popped.
