## Three reports, three different bars

A short trading week still carries real catalysts. Workday (WDAY) has confirmed it will report after the close on Tuesday, November 24, with a call at 4:30 p.m. ET, [according to its own announcement](https://investor.workday.com/news-and-events/press-releases/news-details/2026/Workday-to-Announce-Fiscal-2027-Third-Quarter-Financial-Results-on-November-24-2026/default.aspx). Deere (DE) will hold its fourth-quarter call on Wednesday, November 25, at 9:00 a.m. Central, [per its November 11 notice](https://finviz.com/news/421094/deere-to-announce-fourth-quarter-2026-financial-results). Dell Technologies (DELL) is listed for Tuesday, November 24 on [a third-party earnings calendar](https://news.ifyouspent.com/en/news/thanksgiving-week-earnings-dell-workday-zoom-hp-deere-november-2026) that cites Dell's investor relations page. We could not open Dell's own notice, so confirm the date and time with Dell before planning around it.

For traders watching bases, the useful way to read these reports is through the guidance each company already put on the record. A stock that is tightening in a range ahead of earnings is, in effect, pricing that guidance. The report either confirms it or forces a repricing. This post lays out what each company said last time and what the next print has to show. It is information, not a call on direction.

## Dell: the highest bar of the three

Dell's [second-quarter release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001571996/000157199626000039/exhibit991earnings8kq2fy27.htm) set expectations high. Revenue for the quarter ended July 31 was $47.0 billion, up 58% from a year earlier, and non-GAAP diluted EPS was $7.04. The company booked a record $60.9 billion of AI orders and ended the quarter with a record $95 billion AI backlog. AI-optimized server revenue was $16.4 billion, up 100%.

The raise was explicit, and the prior figures are in the release. Full-year fiscal 2027 revenue guidance went from $167.0 billion to $192.0 billion, and AI-optimized server revenue guidance from $60.0 billion to $74.0 billion. Non-GAAP EPS guidance moved from $17.90 to $25.50.

For the quarter that ended October 30, Dell guided to revenue of $49.0 billion, up 81%, and non-GAAP diluted EPS of $6.50. Those two numbers are what the market will grade first.

Two details in the release matter for how the report might trade:

- **Cash conversion.** Cash from operating activities was $2.2 billion in the second quarter, down 13% from a year earlier, even as revenue rose 58%. Adjusted free cash flow was $8.1 billion, up 224%. The two measures tell different stories, and the call is where management reconciles them.
- **Mix.** Infrastructure Solutions Group revenue was $31.8 billion, up 89%, with operating income of $4.8 billion. The Client Solutions Group, the PC side, grew 20% to $15.0 billion with operating income of $1.1 billion. A report that holds the infrastructure growth rate while the client business stays steady is the clean version of the story.

A base that has been quiet into a report with this much growth already in guidance leaves little room for a result that is merely in line. That cuts both ways, and the reaction is usually set by the AI backlog and the next full-year numbers rather than the quarter itself.

## Workday: steady growth, a margin target and a buyback

Workday's [second-quarter release](https://investor.workday.com/news-and-events/press-releases/news-details/2026/Workday-Announces-Fiscal-2027-Second-Quarter-Financial-Results/default.aspx) was calmer. Total revenue was $2.649 billion, up 12.8%. Subscription revenue was $2.471 billion, up 13.9%. The 12-month subscription revenue backlog was $9.034 billion, up 14.2%, and non-GAAP operating margin was 31.1%.

The third-quarter guide is the benchmark: subscription revenue of $2.515 billion, up 12%, and a non-GAAP operating margin of 30.0%. Full-year subscription revenue guidance is $9.940 billion to $9.950 billion, up 13%, with a 31.0% margin. The release calls the full-year figures an update but does not state the earlier numbers, so this post does not describe them as a raise.

Workday also repurchased about 9.8 million Class A shares for $1.3 billion in the quarter and authorized up to $4.0 billion more. It said more than 5,500 customers use at least one of its organic AI agents, up more than 35% from the prior quarter.

What matters for a setup:

- **Growth rate against the guide.** The third-quarter guide implies growth slowing from 13.9% to about 12%. A print that lands above 12% with backlog growth near 14% supports the case that the deceleration is shallow.
- **Margin.** The company guided the third quarter to 30.0%, below the second quarter's 31.1%. Watch whether the actual margin lands above that.
- **Agents.** AI adoption is the narrative driver for software names. A new customer count on the agents is the number most likely to move the discussion.

## Deere: a cycle-bottom claim gets tested

Deere's [third-quarter release](https://s22.q4cdn.com/253594569/files/doc_financials/2026/q3/DE-3Q26-News-Release.pdf) showed net income of $1.379 billion, or $5.10 a share, on net sales and revenues of $12.608 billion, up 5%. The company said it improved net income guidance to a range of $4.75 billion to $5.00 billion. The release does not state the previous range, so this post does not call it a raise.

The segments pulled in different directions. Production & Precision Agriculture sales fell 6% to $3.998 billion, with operating margin of 13.2%. Small Agriculture & Turf rose 12% to $3.383 billion at an 18.4% margin. Construction & Forestry rose 18% to $3.618 billion, with margin of 12.1% against 7.7% a year earlier. Tariff recoveries were $110 million in the quarter and $382 million over nine months.

CEO John May said the company continues to believe 2026 will mark the bottom of the current ag equipment cycle, citing early order program trends and improving used-equipment inventories. The fourth-quarter report is the first chance to see whether that language holds, and the company's initial look at its next fiscal year, if it gives one, will carry as much weight as the quarter. For a stock that has been building a base, the question is whether management's cycle-bottom view gets more specific or more hedged.

## How to frame the week

Three habits help with earnings-week bases:

- **Know the guide before the print.** Each number above is a line the stock has already priced to some degree. A beat that merely matches an already-elevated expectation can still sell off.
- **Look at the first-day volume, not just the gap.** A gap on heavy volume that holds the next session carries more information than a gap that fades.
- **Respect the calendar.** With U.S. markets closed on Thanksgiving Day, the post-earnings reaction for Tuesday and Wednesday reports plays out in a compressed week, and thin holiday sessions can exaggerate moves.

None of this predicts direction. It marks the levels of information each report is likely to deliver, so a reaction can be read against something concrete instead of the headline alone.

## What to watch next

- **Dell:** the quarter's revenue and EPS against $49.0 billion and $6.50, the AI backlog versus $95 billion, and any change to the $192.0 billion full-year revenue guide.
- **Workday:** subscription revenue against $2.515 billion, margin against 30.0%, and the agent customer count.
- **Deere:** net income against the $4.75 billion to $5.00 billion range, and the tone on the cycle bottom.

Check each company's own release as it lands, since the first headline figures are often adjusted in the detail.
