## What Friday left behind

Friday's session was a tech problem, not a broad one. The S&P 500 fell 0.40% to 7,561.67 and the Nasdaq Composite lost 0.91% to 25,136.27, while the Dow edged up 0.10% to 52,347.55, according to [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/article/nvidias-first-post-jensen-earnings-and-anthropics-ipo-what-to-watch-this-week-110000732.html). [Eurasia Business News](https://eurasiabusinessnews.com/2026/11/14/stock-market-today-november-13-2026-tech-stocks-fall-as-nvidia-earnings-and-anthropic-ipo-loom) reports the same closes and adds that most S&P 500 sectors advanced while technology fell.

That split matters for anyone watching bases. When the selling is concentrated in one group, money often has somewhere to go, and the stocks that held tight ranges during the dip are the ones worth studying. Next week's calendar gives a clean test of that idea, because the biggest names on it are retailers rather than chip makers.

Nvidia (NVDA) still dominates the headlines. Yahoo Finance reports that Jensen Huang resigned as CEO on Thursday and that Colette Kress will host Tuesday's call as interim CEO. Nvidia reports after the close Tuesday, and any large move in the index leaders will bleed into every other chart. For this post, though, the more useful question is what happens to the stocks that are not in that blast radius.

## The retail calendar

Four large retailers report in three sessions. According to [StockTitan's earnings calendar](https://www.stocktitan.net/articles/earnings-calendar-week-of-november-16-2026), the schedule is:

- **Home Depot (HD):** Tuesday, Nov. 17, before the open, with a 9:00 a.m. ET call.
- **Target (TGT), Lowe's (LOW) and TJX (TJX):** Wednesday, Nov. 18, before the open.
- **Walmart (WMT):** Thursday, Nov. 19, before the open, with results at 7:00 a.m. ET.

StockTitan lists Nasdaq-compiled consensus earnings per share of $2.03 for Target (against $1.78 a year ago), $2.86 for Lowe's (against $3.06), $1.33 for TJX (against $1.28) and $0.64 for Walmart (against $0.62). StockTitan notes that the basis for those estimates is not stated, so they may not match the adjusted figures companies report. Consensus for Home Depot differs between sources I checked, so I am leaving a single number out.

Scale is part of the story. [Opening Bell Daily News](https://www.openingbelldailynews.com/p/stock-market-week-ahead-retail-earnings-nov-14-2026) lists market values as of Friday's close of $1.02 trillion for Walmart, $348 billion for Home Depot, $148 billion for TJX and $128 billion for Lowe's. Those are among the heaviest consumer and housing-linked weights in the market, so reactions there tend to set the tone for the rest of the group.

Note the Lowe's line in particular. A consensus that sits below last year's figure means the bar is lower on the bottom line, which can make the reaction hinge on guidance and comparable sales rather than the headline beat.

## The macro backdrop these reports land in

Retailers are reporting into a rising-rate, rising-price tape. Both Yahoo Finance and Eurasia Business News put the 10-year Treasury yield near 5.15%. Eurasia Business News reports that October consumer prices rose 0.4% on the month, with core up 0.3%, each 0.1 percentage point above forecasts. It also reports that October producer prices rose 0.3% on the month, slightly below the 0.4% consensus, while the annual rate accelerated to 6.5%, which it describes as the highest since November 2022. Yahoo Finance gives the same 6.5% annual figure and says a December Fed rate hike looks more likely. I could not find the underlying government release to confirm the 6.5% reading independently, so treat it as reported by these two outlets.

Why this matters for base-builders: higher yields compress the multiples that long-duration growth names can hold, which is part of why tech has been the pressure point. Retailers face a different squeeze. Input costs flow through to margins, and the consumer is the one absorbing the price increases. A retail stock that has spent weeks tightening its range into these reports is telling you the market already expects some of that pain. The earnings call is where that expectation gets checked.

Yahoo Finance also flags retail sales data on Tuesday and University of Michigan consumer sentiment on Friday. Those two releases bracket the retail reports and give traders a second read on the same question: whether the shopper is still spending.

## What a constructive reaction looks like

No one can say how these reports will land, and nothing here is a call on any stock. What swing traders can do is define, ahead of time, what would count as confirmation and what would count as failure. A few framing points:

**Judge the reaction against the base, not the gap.** A stock that gaps up from inside a tight, multi-week range is in a different position than one that gaps into resistance after a run. Mark the top of the pre-earnings range for each name on your watchlist and note where the previous breakout attempts stalled.

**Volume tells you who showed up.** Earnings-day volume well above the stock's recent average, with the close in the upper part of the day's range, reads differently from a spike that fades into the close. The Friday fade in the Nasdaq is a reminder that strong opens do not always hold.

**Watch the follow-through day.** The session after a report often shows whether institutions kept buying or used the gap to sell. For Home Depot and Target that means Wednesday and Thursday, and for Walmart it means the Friday session.

**Guidance can outweigh the quarter.** With cost inflation running hot, outlook language on margins, tariffs and consumer demand tends to move these stocks more than a penny of earnings difference. The Walmart print Thursday, as the largest of the group, will likely color how the whole sector trades into the weekend.

## Beyond retail

Other names on the calendar can move related charts. Per [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/article/nvidias-first-post-jensen-earnings-and-anthropics-ipo-what-to-watch-this-week-110000732.html), Disney (DIS) reports Tuesday before the open alongside Home Depot, and Palo Alto Networks (PANW) reports Wednesday. StockTitan lists Intuit (INTU) and Ross Stores (ROST) after the close Thursday and Palo Alto after the close Wednesday. Software and security names that have been basing quietly could use those reports as a catalyst or a reason to reset.

## What to watch next

The sequence is simple. Tuesday brings Home Depot before the open, retail sales, and Nvidia after the close. Wednesday adds Target, Lowe's and TJX. Thursday closes the retail run with Walmart, and Friday's consumer sentiment reading follows.

If the index leaders stabilize after Nvidia and the retailers hold their ranges through earnings, the market will have shown that leadership can rotate without a broad break. If the retailers gap down on margin worries while tech is still unsettled, the pool of stocks with intact bases gets a lot smaller. Either way, the levels that matter are the ones you can mark today: the top and bottom of each stock's recent range, and the volume it needs to clear them.
