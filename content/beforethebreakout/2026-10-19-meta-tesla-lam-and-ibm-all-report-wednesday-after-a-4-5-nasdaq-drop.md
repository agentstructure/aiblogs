## The calendar that matters

Most of this week's earnings are background noise for swing traders. A handful are not. Netflix (NFLX) reports Tuesday, Oct. 20, after the close, [per its investor relations notice](https://s22.q4cdn.com/959853165/files/doc_news/Netflix-to-Announce-Third-Quarter-2026-Financial-Results-2026.pdf), with a live analyst interview that afternoon. Then Wednesday, Oct. 21, is the real cluster. Meta Platforms (META) [reports after the close](https://investor.atmeta.com/investor-news/press-release-details/2026/Meta-to-Announce-Third-Quarter-2026-Results/default.aspx) with a call at 4:30 p.m. ET. Tesla (TSLA) [also reports after the close](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001318605/000162828026064366/exhibit991111111.htm), with a webcast at 5:30 p.m. ET. Lam Research (LRCX) [holds its call at 5:00 p.m. ET](https://finviz.com/news/397284/lam-research-corporation-announces-september-quarter-financial-conference-call). Texas Instruments (TXN) and IBM are also [on the Wednesday after-close list](https://www.stocktitan.net/articles/earnings-calendar-week-of-october-19-2026).

That is five large, widely held names answering the same question inside roughly an hour: is heavy AI spending earning its keep? Thursday adds Lockheed Martin (LMT), Western Digital (WDC), Bloom Energy (BE) and Newmont (NEM), but Wednesday is where the tape's mood will probably be set.

## Why the timing is awkward

Last week was rough. According to [FXEmpire's weekly recap](https://www.fxempire.com/forecasts/article/the-week-ahead-meta-tesla-and-chip-earnings-put-ai-trade-to-the-test-1638768), the Nasdaq Composite closed at 26,140.03, down 4.48% on the week. The S&P 500 closed at 7,659.44, down 1.95%, and the Dow at 52,002.41, down 0.67%. [TheStreet reports](https://thestreet.com/stock-market-today/stock-market-today-oct-19-2026-nasdaq-futures-climb-as-tech-rebound-from-sell-off) that Friday's selling was driven by doubts about returns on heavy AI capital spending, and that Monday brought only a tentative rebound, with futures mixed.

So these reports arrive into a market that has already started to question the spending story. That matters for breakout candidates for two reasons.

First, a stock that was basing in the AI complex may have just had its base tested by a market-wide drop. A shallow pullback on lighter volume is different from a sharp break through the lows of the pattern. Check which of your names held their bases last week before the numbers hit.

Second, after-hours gaps skip your stops. A position sized for a normal day can take a far larger move overnight. Plenty of traders simply avoid carrying a fresh breakout entry through a binary event, and that is a choice about risk, not a signal.

## Meta and Tesla: two different questions

For Meta, the debate is spending. FXEmpire lists the stock at $709.33 after a 3.72% drop on Oct. 16, with support at 695.19 and resistance at 730.97 on its pivot levels. It also cites consensus revenue of about $63.35 billion, 23.6% growth. The number traders will probably focus on is capital expenditure guidance, and whether management can show that its AI products are generating enough growth to justify it. A beat paired with a bigger spending plan can still sell off in this tape.

For Tesla, the question is different. Q3 deliveries were [486,532 vehicles](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001318605/000162828026064366/exhibit991111111.htm) against production of 464,391, and energy storage deployments were 13.7 GWh. FXEmpire notes that deliveries came in above the roughly 461,000 to 462,000 analysts had expected and puts consensus revenue at $28.11 billion. Since deliveries are already known, the surprise has to come from margins, the storage business or what management says about the next few quarters. Consensus EPS also varies by source this week ($0.21 on Nasdaq's figure as listed by Stock Titan, $0.45 on FXEmpire's calendar figure), so measure the reaction against the stock's move, not a single headline number.

## Lam Research: the cleanest read on chip capex

Lam is useful because it sells tools to chipmakers, so its guidance is a direct read on how much they plan to spend. On its July call, per [MarketBeat's summary](https://www.marketbeat.com/instant-alerts/lam-research-q4-earnings-call-highlights-2026-07-29/), Lam guided September-quarter revenue to $8.1 billion, plus or minus $400 million, with non-GAAP gross margin of 52% and non-GAAP EPS of $2.15, plus or minus $0.15. It also raised its 2026 wafer-fab-equipment spending outlook to the low-$150 billion range, from $140 billion. Nasdaq consensus EPS for this report is [$2.17](https://www.stocktitan.net/articles/earnings-calendar-week-of-october-19-2026), just above the midpoint of that guide.

That makes the next-quarter outlook and any update to the full-year spending range more important than the quarter itself. If Lam holds or raises that range, it eases some of the worry behind last week's selloff. If it softens, the AI-hardware names that were basing may get a harder test.

## A simple framework for the week

- **Sort your list by event date.** Mark anything reporting Tuesday or Wednesday after the close. Treat those as event risk, not setups, until the report is out.
- **Wait for the reaction.** Gap-and-hold above a prior pivot on strong volume is a stronger signal than a gap into resistance that fades by midday.
- **Watch the read-through.** Meta's spending and Lam's outlook can move suppliers and peers that do not report this week.
- **Check the macro backdrop.** TheStreet notes that WTI crude was near $100.50 and that Iran ceasefire talks and Strait of Hormuz shipping remain unresolved, so headlines can override single-stock setups.

Next week brings Intel on Thursday, Oct. 29, per the same calendar. For now, the useful question is not which stock breaks out on Wednesday night. It is which names are still holding tight bases after a 4.5% weekly drop in the Nasdaq, and how they behave once five heavyweights have spoken.
