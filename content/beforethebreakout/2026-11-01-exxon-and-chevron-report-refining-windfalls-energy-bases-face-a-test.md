## What happened

Friday was a lopsided tape. The S&P 500 closed at 7,580.07, down 1.82%, and the Nasdaq Composite fell 2.86% to 25,437.00, according to [Yahoo Finance's market snapshot](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/live/stock-market-today-friday-october-30-dow-sp-500-nasdaq-amazon-intel-ai-sell-off-191429143.html). The Dow lost 1.09% and the Russell 2000 slipped 1.66%. [Investrade](https://investrade.com/market-review-october-30-2026/) puts the Nasdaq's October decline near 5.3%, with Yahoo's tally at roughly 5.2%, so call it a drop of more than 5% on the month.

Inside that, one group moved the other way. Exxon Mobil (XOM) and Chevron (CVX) both reported third-quarter results before the open, and both beat. Investrade lists the two among energy names that hit 52-week highs Friday. Fortune reports [Exxon gained about 1% and Chevron a little over 1% on the day](https://fortune.com/2026/10/30/exxon-chevron-massive-profits-trump-brags-energy-companies-making-freaking-fortune-midterms/), against a tape that was down nearly 2%.

That is the first thing a base watcher should register. Relative strength on a heavy-selling day, in a stock that is still near its highs, is the kind of behavior the audience of this site screens for. The harder question is whether the reason behind it can last.

## The numbers behind the move

Exxon's [filing with the SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0002115436/000211543626000018/livef8k3q26991.htm) shows GAAP earnings of $15.4 billion ($3.72 a share) and adjusted earnings of $17.5 billion ($4.23 a share). Operating cash flow was $25.7 billion and free cash flow $19.1 billion. Adjusted Energy Products earnings were $9.05 billion, ahead of $7.31 billion from Upstream. Reuters notes this is [the first time in at least six years that Exxon's refining profit has exceeded its upstream profit](https://kfgo.com/2026/10/30/exxon-chevron-earnings-propelled-by-war-driven-refining-surge/).

Chevron's [8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000093410/000009341026000196/a09302026ex9918-k.htm) reports earnings of $9.9 billion ($5.06 a share) and adjusted earnings of $10.4 billion ($5.28), compared with $3.5 billion a year earlier. Downstream earnings were $4.9 billion, against $5.7 billion upstream. Free cash flow was $12.0 billion, and net debt fell to $20.7 billion from $34.5 billion at the end of 2025.

Fortune puts the year-over-year picture plainly: Exxon's profit more than doubled from $7.5 billion, and Chevron's rose from $3.5 billion. Exxon's was just shy of its record quarter in 2022.

## Why refining, not crude, drove it

Reuters ties the surge to disruption: the war involving Iran, the effective closure of the Strait of Hormuz, and attacks on refineries in Saudi Arabia and Russia that removed fuel-making capacity. Chevron CEO Mike Wirth said, in Fortune's account, that [the world has 10% of its refining capacity offline](https://fortune.com/2026/10/30/exxon-chevron-massive-profits-trump-brags-energy-companies-making-freaking-fortune-midterms/). Exxon CEO Darren Woods said it will take "well through next year" before conditions get closer to normal.

Oil itself is firm. Investrade has WTI settling at $104.12 and Brent at $117.64, with WTI up more than 15% on the month. Reuters adds that Brent swung between $68 and $132 during the third quarter alone, which shows how wide the range has been.

Peers fit the pattern. Fortune reports Phillips 66 posted $5.6 billion and Valero $6.8 billion, both their most profitable quarters ever, and Reuters says Shell and TotalEnergies beat on Thursday while BP reported a record quarterly net profit on Friday.

## What the cash is doing

The way the money is used matters for how durable a trend looks. Reuters reports both companies are directing cash mainly at debt reduction rather than larger payouts. Exxon cut debt by $8 billion in the quarter and says it will repurchase $20 billion of shares in 2027, the same level as the previous two years. Chevron bought back $2.5 billion in the quarter.

For a swing trader, conservative capital allocation reads two ways. It signals management does not treat these margins as permanent, which tempers the case that earnings simply keep compounding. It also strengthens balance sheets, which tends to matter when a market is under pressure.

## What the tech side is showing

Contrast the energy picture with what is happening underneath growth stocks. Investrade says the semiconductor index (SOX) closed below its 200-day moving average of 10,785 and is down more than 15% in October, while the technology sector fell about 5% on Friday. Yahoo's snapshot lists Nvidia (NVDA) about 12% below its 52-week high and Tesla (TSLA) about 19% below its own.

Yahoo also reports that Intel (INTC) fell roughly 26% to 29% on a weak outlook, and that Amazon (AMZN) shares slipped after a rare earnings miss, with trailing-twelve-month free cash flow negative by more than $42 billion. We covered Friday's Intel move already; the point here is that leadership is narrowing, and money has to go somewhere.

## Levels and conditions to watch

I can't source exact pivot prices for the energy names from the reports above, so treat the following as conditions, not trade levels.

- **Holding the highs.** A stock near a 52-week high that holds most of its gains on a down day for the index is behaving constructively. A close back below the prior consolidation range after an earnings pop would be the warning sign. Check where each name's base sits on your own chart.
- **Volume on the next push.** The best breakouts come with volume well above average. A new high on thin volume, particularly in an event-heavy week, is weaker evidence.
- **Oil and the Hormuz headline.** The refining margin story rests on supply disruption. Any sign of reopening, or a demand scare, would hit this group harder than a normal earnings miss.
- **The semiconductor line.** If SOX cannot reclaim its 200-day average, the pressure on the Nasdaq likely continues, and index-level breakouts stay a low-odds proposition.
- **Tuesday's midterm elections.** Reuters notes fuel prices are a political issue going into the vote, and it reports a diesel export ban was considered in September before being ruled out in early October. Policy headlines can move the group quickly in either direction.

## The takeaway

October ended with the market telling two different stories: a growth complex breaking down on AI-spending worries and a refining-driven energy group posting profits that were close to or above prior records. Relative strength like this is worth tracking, but a crowded, news-driven leader can reverse fast when the news changes. The signal is price and volume holding through the next pullback, not the earnings headline itself.
