## The index is flat; the leadership is not

The S&P 500 closed Tuesday at 7,805.45, down 0.02%, according to [TheStreet's market wrap](https://www.thestreet.com/stock-market-today/stock-market-today-dow-jones-sp-500-nasdaq-updates-oct-13-2026). That is almost exactly where it finished Monday, when it slipped 0.1% to 7,807.24 by [Zacks' count](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/stock-market-news-oct-13-131300201.html). A flat index hides a lot here. Beneath it, energy and utilities led Tuesday's sector table while healthcare and technology sagged.

Monday showed the same split. Eight of 11 S&P 500 sectors closed higher, yet the Nasdaq fell 0.4%. The sector ETFs tell the story: energy (XLE) gained 1.5%, consumer staples (XLP) 1.2% and communication services (XLC) 1.1%, while technology (XLK) lost 0.9%.

For a breakout trader, this is the useful part. The headline index says "nothing happened." The sector tape says capital is being moved out of last quarter's crowded winners and into groups that were ignored.

## Memory and storage are the pressure point

The damage is concentrated. On Monday, Western Digital (WDC), SK Hynix (SKHY), Seagate (STX) and Sandisk (SNDK) fell sharply and Micron (MU) dropped almost 4%, [per Schwab](https://www.schwab.com/learn/story/stock-market-update-open). Schwab attributes the pressure to another drop in South Korea's tech-heavy market on concerns about Chinese chip competition.

Tuesday brought more of it. TheStreet's live tracker showed WDC down about 7.7%, STX down about 7.3% and Micron down roughly 4% to 5% at various points in the session. Those are intraday readings from a live blog, so treat the exact figures as approximate. The direction is not in doubt: the memory and storage group is being sold for a second day.

This matters because these names were among the market's strongest performers this year. An August report on the market noted that Sandisk was up more than 500% year to date and that Micron and Western Digital had roughly doubled, with the [leadership group already wobbling](https://www.itiger.com/news/1104954447) at that point. Extended names do not need bad news to correct. They need buyers to step away, and a rotation provides that.

For a base-hunter, the lesson is about what not to chase. A stock that has run several hundred percent and then drops 5% to 7% in a day is not building a base. It is breaking one. The setups worth studying are on the other side of the trade.

## Where money is landing

Energy is the clearest beneficiary, and it has a reason. Brent settled Monday at $106.65, its highest close since Sept. 15, after attacks around Riyadh's airport and a Houthi-claimed attack, Zacks reports. ConocoPhillips (COP) rose 2.6% and ExxonMobil (XOM) 1.9% on the day.

Oil then eased early Tuesday on a temporary relaxation of U.S. restrictions on Russian diesel, with WTI near $92 to $93 in early trading. That is a reminder that this leadership is headline-driven. Energy strength tied to geopolitics can reverse in one session, so a group move like this is more reliable when the individual charts are also tightening.

Banks are the second pocket, with a split verdict. Goldman Sachs (GS) jumped more than 4% after earnings of $17.01 per share against $12.86 expected, per Schwab. JPMorgan (JPM) rose almost 1% despite missing at $5.57 versus $5.90. Wells Fargo (WFC) fell 1% and Citigroup (C) fell 1.2% even though Citi beat. The market is paying for trading and capital-markets strength and not rewarding banks that merely meet expectations. Within financials, that is a stock-picking market, not a sector bid.

Healthcare, meanwhile, is mixed. UnitedHealth (UNH) rose after beating and raising guidance, while Johnson & Johnson (JNJ) fell despite a beat and a raised outlook. Sector ETFs average over that, and the average hides the setups.

## Breadth is repairing from a low

The most constructive number in the tape is not a price. Schwab notes that about 36% of S&P 500 stocks trade above their 50-day moving average, up from a six-month low of 20%. The share of stocks outperforming the index over the trailing month rose to 26% from 17%.

That is repair, not strength. A majority of stocks are still below their 50-day lines. But it is the right order of events for a base-builder: breadth washes out, then a rising minority of stocks reclaims its intermediate trend while the index goes sideways. Rotation is how that happens. Money leaving the leaders has to go somewhere, and it is going into groups that spent weeks consolidating.

Schwab also flags that a record share of S&P 500 stocks showed negative day-to-day beta, meaning many names are moving against the index. That is another description of the same thing: the market is trading stock by stock and group by group, not as one block.

## What to watch before the bell and after

The next catalyst is on the calendar. September CPI is due at 8:30 a.m. ET Wednesday, with consensus at 3.7% headline and 2.5% core, per Schwab. Producer prices follow Thursday. Rate-hike odds matter here: CME FedWatch put the chance of an October hike at nearly 22% on Monday, up from 17.7% on Friday, per Zacks. The 10-year yield was around 5.19% at Tuesday's open.

A hot print would pressure rate-sensitive groups and stretched growth. A soft one could let yields fall further, as they did Tuesday after Fed Governor Christopher Waller said higher yields were curbing demand in rate-sensitive sectors. Housing is the obvious test case: Freddie Mac's 30-year mortgage rate reached 7.4% last Thursday, its highest since November 2023, according to Schwab.

A practical checklist for the days ahead:

- **Does the memory selloff stop making new lows?** A group that stabilizes on lighter volume starts a base. One that keeps gapping down does not.
- **Does energy hold when oil dips?** Leadership that survives a down day in crude is more durable than leadership that needs the headline.
- **Does the 50-day breadth reading keep rising?** Another step up from 36% would confirm repair; a roll back toward 20% would say the bounce failed.
- **Which individual charts are tightening inside the leading groups?** Look for narrowing daily ranges and drying volume in names that held their 50-day lines through the chip selloff.

This is analysis of market conditions, not a recommendation to buy or sell any security.
