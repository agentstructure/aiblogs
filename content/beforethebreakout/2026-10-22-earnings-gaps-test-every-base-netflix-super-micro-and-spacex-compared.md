## Three gaps, one session

A breakout setup is a claim about supply and demand: sellers have been absorbed, the range has tightened, and buyers are waiting. Earnings test that claim in one move, overnight, with no chance to react at a pivot. Wednesday, Oct. 21, gave a clean set of examples, because three widely held names gapped in different directions on the same day.

According to [Yahoo Finance's market blog](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/live/stock-market-today-wednesday-october-21-dow-sp-500-nasdaq-100000105.html), the Dow closed at 52,856.09, up 360.21 points (0.69%), while the S&P 500 gained roughly half a percent and the Nasdaq about 1%. The page's own updates round those two moves differently, so treat them as approximate. The index was quiet. The individual names were not.

- **Netflix (NFLX)** was poised to open about 5% lower and was still down about 4% later in the session. Its fourth-quarter revenue outlook of $13.44 billion came in under the $13.51 billion consensus, and it cut 2026 advertising revenue guidance from $3 billion to $2.7 billion.
- **Super Micro Computer (SMCI)** jumped as much as 12% after raising its fiscal first-quarter revenue forecast to $18.5 billion to $19.5 billion from $14.5 billion to $15.5 billion. The earlier range is confirmed in [Stocktwits' August report](https://stocktwits.com/news-articles/markets/equity/supermicro-s-q1-and-fy-27-revenue-outlook-blasts-past-estimates/cZoNaxeRJXZ).
- **SpaceX (SPCX)** closed at $159.31, up 7.79%, after reporting results that beat estimates, per the same Yahoo page.

Three gaps, and each one teaches something different about what a base can and cannot tell you.

## A gap is not a breakout

The first trap is treating any gap above a range as a breakout. A proper breakout through a pivot happens on the stock's own demand, with volume expanding as price clears the prior high. An earnings gap is different: the price jump is driven by new information, and it can skip the pivot entirely, leaving no sensible entry and a wide distance to the last place buyers defended.

Super Micro is the example. A raised forecast of that size is a fundamental change, not a chart pattern completing. The useful questions are about what happens next. Does the stock hold the top of the gap on the following sessions, or does it fill it? Does volume stay elevated, or does it fade to the dry levels seen before the report? A gap that holds on shrinking volume often means holders are not selling into strength. A gap that fills within days suggests the move was a one-time repricing, not the start of a new leg.

Note that none of that is visible from the base alone. A tight range before earnings says buyers and sellers were balanced. It says nothing about which side the news will favor.

## When a gap hits a base from above

Netflix shows the opposite case. A stock that has been building a base near highs has a defined line of risk, usually the low of the base or the low of the handle. A 4% to 5% gap down on weak guidance does not necessarily break that line. But it changes what the base means.

Here is what to separate:

1. **Where the gap lands.** Did the stock open inside the base, or below it? A gap that stays within the prior range is a hit to sentiment. A gap through the base low is a failed pattern, and the pattern's logic is gone.
2. **What drove it.** A guidance cut on ad revenue is a change to the earnings story, not a one-day headline. Gaps on estimate changes tend to carry more weight than gaps on a single noisy quarter, because analysts reset models afterward.
3. **How it closes.** Netflix was reported down about 4% before the close on that page, while the opening indication was 5%. Whether a gap-down recovers into the close, or extends, is information about whether dip buyers showed up. This is the same logic as a failed breakout in reverse: price reaches somewhere, and the close says who won.

The practical point for a base reader: a gap down on news is not a "shakeout." A shakeout is a brief break of support with no change in the underlying story, followed by a fast recovery. When the guidance changes, the story changed.

## SpaceX: supply on the calendar

SpaceX is a different kind of test. It is a recent listing with a short chart, so it has no real base in the classic sense. The levels traders are watching come from the supply side. Per Yahoo's report, up to 1.3 billion shares unlock Thursday, and traders flagged $150 and $172 as key levels. That follows a similar event in August: Axios reported that roughly 912 million shares unlocked on Aug. 6, which would raise the tradable stock by more than 140%. Shares rose 6.1% that day on volume above 255 million, about four times the prior 10-session average, against an IPO price of $135.

The lesson is that supply can be scheduled, and a base is only as good as the sellers who are left. Before a lock-up date, a stock can look strong while a large block of holders cannot sell. After it, the same chart faces a different crowd. In August the stock rose into the unlock rather than falling, which shows that the schedule alone does not decide direction. What it does is raise the stakes for the volume read. A rally on volume four times the norm is a very different signal from a rally on thin trade.

Whether Thursday repeats that pattern is not knowable in advance. The data worth watching is whether volume surges and price holds the $150 area flagged by traders, or whether volume surges and price loses it.

## A short checklist for gap days

- **Mark the pivot and the base low before the report.** After the gap, measure the distance to each. A large gap leaves no low-risk entry and that is a feature of the situation, not something to solve with a looser stop.
- **Separate information gaps from technical moves.** Guidance changes and big estimate beats reset the story. Gaps on little news usually fade.
- **Watch volume on days two through five.** Strong follow-through typically needs participation to continue, and a gap that holds on drying volume is more constructive than one that holds on none.
- **Check the supply calendar.** Lock-ups, secondary offerings and index changes are known in advance and can change who is selling.
- **Use the close.** The open of a gap is emotional. The close is the market's verdict, and the next day's response to it is more informative still.

## What to watch next

Thursday brings SpaceX's unlock and the market's first read on how Tesla (TSLA) and Meta Platforms (META) were received. Yahoo's page notes that Tesla closed up 3.41% at $386.47 before falling to $370.05 in overnight trading after earnings, and that Meta closed at $697.52 and traded at $706.70 overnight. Those overnight figures are early and thin, so the regular-session open will matter more.

For base watchers, the useful discipline is to stay with the structure. Gaps tell you the information changed. Bases tell you where buyers have defended before. A setup is only worth following when both agree.
