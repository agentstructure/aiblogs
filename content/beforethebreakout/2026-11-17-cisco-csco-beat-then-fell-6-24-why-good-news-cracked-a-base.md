## What happened

Two of last week's biggest AI-hardware reports landed on the same night, and the stock reactions did not match the headlines.

Cisco (CSCO) [reported fiscal first-quarter results on Nov. 12](https://investor.cisco.com/news/news-details/2026/CISCO-REPORTS-FIRST-QUARTER-EARNINGS/default.aspx): revenue of $18.5 billion, up 24% from a year earlier, with non-GAAP earnings of $1.40 a share, up 40%. Product revenue rose 32%. Management then raised fiscal 2027 revenue guidance to $74.8 billion to $75.8 billion, from the $72.2 billion to $73.4 billion it gave in August, and lifted non-GAAP EPS guidance to $5.47 to $5.53 from $5.05 to $5.11. By my arithmetic, that moves the revenue midpoint up about 3.4% and the EPS midpoint up about 8.3%.

The next session, [CSCO fell 6.24% to $95.64](https://www.tikr.com/blog/cisco-raised-its-earnings-outlook-8-the-stock-fell-6-anyway), according to TIKR, a figure [Stock Titan's Nov. 13 market wrap also reports](https://www.stocktitan.net/articles/stock-market-today-live-november-13-2026). That was a closing move, not an intraday swing. On the same Friday the S&P 500 [slipped 0.4% to 7,561.67 and the Nasdaq Composite lost 0.9%](https://www.lanacion.com.ar/usa/how-major-us-stock-indexes-fared-friday-11132026-nid13112026/), so part of the drop was a soft tape. Cisco's 6.24% decline was still far larger than the market's.

## Why a raised guide did not hold the stock up

The AI number was not the problem. Cisco [lifted its expected fiscal 2027 AI infrastructure revenue from hyperscalers to $9 billion](https://investor.cisco.com/news/news-details/2026/CISCO-REPORTS-FIRST-QUARTER-EARNINGS/default.aspx), which the company says is more than double the fiscal 2026 total. TIKR puts the prior target at $7.5 billion.

The pressure points were elsewhere. According to TIKR, gross margin came in at 65.6%, down 2.5 points, and the company guided it to "inside 65% to 66%" for the next report. TIKR attributes the squeeze to hardware mix and memory costs, though it gives no dollar figure for the memory effect. CFO Mark Patterson also said the second half of the fiscal year carries "tougher compares and a little bit less visibility," per the same source.

That is a familiar pattern for anyone watching bases form around earnings. When a stock has already run on a theme, the market stops paying for the revenue line and starts pricing the quality of that revenue. More sales at lower margins, with visibility fading later in the year, can read as a reason to sell strength even when every headline number is higher.

It also was not new for this stock. After the August report, Cisco [closed down 8.9% at $113.25](https://www.webull.com/news/15397609947145216) even though revenue hit a record $17.3 billion. Against that August close, $95.64 is roughly 15.5% lower, by my calculation. Two strong reports in a row have each been followed by a lower price. TIKR also notes the forward price-to-earnings ratio has compressed to 17.2x from 27.7x at the June peak, which suggests much of the decline has come from a shrinking multiple rather than lower estimates.

## Applied Materials: strong numbers, a quieter read-through

Applied Materials (AMAT) reported the same evening. According to [Crypto Briefing's summary of the release](https://cryptobriefing.com/applied-materials-q4-revenue-jumps-53-percent/), fiscal fourth-quarter revenue was $10.41 billion, up 53%, with non-GAAP EPS of $4.16, up 92%. Non-GAAP gross margin rose 250 basis points to 50.6%. Full-year revenue reached $34.45 billion, up 21%, and operating cash flow of $8.87 billion was a company record.

The contrast with Cisco is worth noting. Applied's margin moved up while Cisco's moved down, which is the cleanest explanation for why the two reports could earn different treatment even with the same AI demand backdrop. Equipment makers sell the capacity build; networking-hardware makers absorb component inflation while delivering it.

Guidance needs a careful read. [FourWeekMBA reports](https://fourweekmba.com/ai-applied-materials-posts-10-4b-guides-11-1b-in-14-weeks/) that Applied guided first-quarter fiscal 2027 revenue to $11.1 billion, plus or minus $500 million, and that the quarter runs 14 weeks. The same analysis calculates that this is roughly 1% lower per week than the fourth quarter. That per-week figure is a third-party calculation, not a company number. I could not find a reliable source for AMAT's share-price reaction, so this post does not characterize it.

## What it means for base-watchers

The takeaway for traders who study price action is about what to measure, not which stock to own.

First, a beat-and-raise is not a catalyst by itself. Stocks that ran into earnings near highs often need more than higher numbers. Quality of the beat, including margins and guidance shape, determines whether buyers show up on the gap or into the following days.

Second, relative strength can hide a weak base. A stock can look strong against a falling market and still break its own structure the moment a heavy-volume sell day appears. Watch how Cisco behaves around the $95.64 close and whether it can reclaim the $102.01 level from the prior session. A recovery that happens on thin volume tells a different story from one that comes with a surge in participation. Neither tells you what happens next, and neither is a recommendation.

Third, compare peers inside the same theme. If component-cost pressure is the driver, expect it to show up in other hardware names' margins. If it is Cisco-specific, the pressure should stay contained. The sector's next round of reports will help separate those cases.

## What to watch next

- **Cisco's next report:** TIKR cites a Feb. 10, 2027 report date, with management guiding gross margin "inside 65% to 66%." Margins staying in that band would address the concern that drove last week's drop.
- **Hyperscaler spending signals:** Cisco's $9 billion target depends on cloud-customer orders. Any change in those budgets feeds directly into the guide.
- **Memory-cost commentary:** Watch whether other hardware makers cite memory pricing as a margin headwind.
- **Volume on rebounds:** Heavy-volume gains that return a stock to its pre-earnings range carry more information than quiet drifts.

Last week showed that positive fundamentals and a healthy chart can part ways quickly. For anyone tracking AI-hardware bases, Cisco's reaction is a useful reminder to read the price action first and the headline second.
