## What happened

The Federal Reserve held its target range at 3.75% to 4.00% on Wednesday, as expected. The [official statement](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20261028a.htm) shows the vote was 10 to 2, and both dissenters, Lisa Cook and Beth Hammack, preferred a quarter-point increase. The statement describes economic activity as expanding at a strong pace and inflation as elevated.

Stocks took it calmly. According to the [Motley Fool market wrap](https://www.fool.com/coverage/stock-market-today/2026/10/28/stock-market-today-oct-28-markets-edge-higher-as-fed-holds-rates-steady/), the S&P 500 rose 0.28% to 7,743.98, the Nasdaq Composite gained 0.27% to 26,549.85, and the Dow added 0.19% to 52,475.10. Energy led the sector gains while real estate and utilities declined. The 10-year Treasury yield rose to 5.02%, which [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/stock-market-today-oct-28-220317072.html) noted crossed the 5% level as investors weighed the decision.

Index moves of roughly a quarter of a percent hide what happened underneath. That is the part that matters for anyone watching bases.

## The tape was quiet; the single stocks were not

The wrap listed several large single-stock moves on a day when the indexes barely moved. Vertiv (VRT) jumped almost 17% on long-term AI infrastructure demand forecasts. [StockTitan](https://www.stocktitan.net/articles/stock-market-today-live-october-28-2026) put the close at $243.12, up from $208.56, a gain of 16.6%. Oracle (ORCL) rose 11% after an investor day where it set new financial targets. F5 (FFIV) fell more than 20% on its guidance and an analyst downgrade.

Three stocks, three gaps of 11% to 20% or more, and an index that moved less than 0.3%. When that happens, the index is telling you very little about the quality of individual setups. Dispersion is high, and the move is being driven by company-specific catalysts rather than by a broad tide.

For a base reader, that has a practical consequence. A gap on news is not the same event as a breakout from a quiet, tightening pattern. The first is a repricing in one session. The second is a gradual process where supply dries up over weeks. Wednesday's moves reinforce that distinction.

## Why a 5% yield matters to base structure

A 10-year yield above 5% is a competing return for every dollar sitting in equities. It does not make bases fail by itself, but it changes how forgiving the market is of a loose one.

Consider what a healthy base looks like: ranges contract from left to right, volume dries up in the quiet stretch, and any pullbacks within the pattern are shallower than the ones before. In a calm rate environment, a base that is slightly sloppy can still resolve higher because money keeps flowing into equities. With yields at 5% and two voting Fed members already asking for a hike, there is less cushion. Sloppy structure has less margin for error.

Three things to check on any base you are watching:

- **Depth relative to the prior move.** A base that retraces most of the advance that preceded it is behaving more like a rounded top than a pause.
- **Where volume dries up.** A true dry-up happens near the lows of the pattern and in the handle or the final tight range, not randomly across the whole structure.
- **How the pivot is approached.** Tight closes near the pivot on light volume are the setup. Wide, heavy-volume bars into the pivot suggest distribution rather than accumulation.

## What F5 shows about failed setups

F5's drop of more than 20% is a reminder of what an event gap can do to a chart that looked fine the day before. A pattern is a statement about supply and demand up to a certain point. It says nothing about an earnings release or a guidance cut that arrives afterward.

That is the argument for knowing the calendar before reading the chart. Any base with an earnings date inside the next few sessions carries event risk that the pattern cannot measure. The same logic applies to the after-hours session on the same day. According to the Fool wrap, Microsoft (MSFT) beat expectations, led by its Azure cloud division, while Alphabet (GOOGL) shares slid after revenue and sales fell short of analyst predictions. StockTitan's after-hours table showed Fortinet (FTNT) sharply lower as well. After-hours quotes are thin and can reverse by the open, so treat those moves as provisional until Thursday's regular session shows how much of them holds.

The lesson is not that these companies' charts were wrong. It is that the chart and the catalyst are two separate inputs, and a swing trader who only reads one of them is exposed to the other.

## Where the December question sits

The December meeting is on [December 8 and 9](https://www.stocktitan.net/articles/stock-market-today-live-october-28-2026). Sources disagree about how likely a hike is. StockTitan's summary cited Yahoo Finance as reporting about an 85% chance of a December hike, while saying Reuters, in the same article, described expectations as leaning toward a pause, and the piece did not reconcile the two. Yahoo's own market wrap, for its part, said officials indicated that upcoming data would guide the December decision and gave no probabilities. Given the conflict, the honest reading is that the market has not settled on an answer, and that the data between now and December will do the settling.

That makes the next several weeks a data-driven stretch. Inflation and growth readings can move yields, and yields can move the multiple the market is willing to pay for growth stocks. Bases in rate-sensitive groups are the ones most exposed to that chain.

## What to watch next

- **Thursday's reaction to the after-hours earnings.** If Alphabet's and Fortinet's gaps hold at the open, the sessions that follow will show whether sellers keep pressing or buyers step in near the new lows.
- **The 10-year at 5%.** A close back below the level would ease one source of pressure. Continued closes above it keep the headwind in place.
- **Whether AI infrastructure strength broadens.** Vertiv and Oracle both led on Wednesday. Watch whether their suppliers and peers build bases of their own, or whether strength stays confined to a few names that already moved.
- **Volume on up days in the indexes.** Quarter-percent gains on thin volume are a weaker signal than the same gains on heavier turnover.
- **Energy.** It led sector gains on Wednesday while real estate and utilities, the rate-sensitive groups, lagged. If that pairing persists, it supports the idea that rates are driving the rotation.

None of this is a forecast. It is a framework for reading what is in front of you: a market that held steady on the surface, a Fed with a hawkish minority, a 10-year at 5.02%, and single stocks that moved far more than the index. In that setting, the quality of the base, and the calendar around it, matter more than the headline number.
