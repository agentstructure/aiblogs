## What the tape said on Friday

The major indexes ended the week lower, but the way they fell matters more than the size. The S&P 500 dropped 0.52% to 7,623.68 on Friday, the Nasdaq Composite slipped 0.21% to 25,955.34, and the Dow lost 1.23% to 52,215.16, according to [BusinessToday's market wrap](https://www.businesstoday.com.my/2026/10/24/wall-street-closes-lower-to-end-rocky-week/). For the week, the S&P 500 fell 0.5% and the Nasdaq 0.7%, while the Dow gained 0.4% for a third straight weekly advance, per [Reuters-sourced coverage](https://www.world-today-news.com/wall-street-closes-lower-over-ai-costs-earnings-and-fed-meeting/).

The detail worth a swing trader's attention is volume. Roughly 15.4 billion shares traded on Friday, below the 20-day average of 17.2 billion, [BusinessToday reported](https://www.businesstoday.com.my/2026/10/24/wall-street-closes-lower-to-end-rocky-week/). That is about 10.5% under average. A down day on lighter-than-normal turnover is not the same as a down day on heavy turnover. Heavy-volume declines suggest institutions are selling into strength. Light-volume declines usually mean buyers have stepped back without sellers becoming urgent.

That is one session, and one session proves nothing. But it frames what to look for in the stocks you follow this week.

## Why a quiet dip can be constructive for a base

A base is a period of digestion. Price stops advancing, the range narrows, and selling pressure is absorbed. The healthiest versions show a specific volume signature: pullbacks inside the pattern happen on contracting volume, and the heavier-volume days are the up days.

Friday's market-wide picture fits only part of that. Breadth was negative, with NYSE decliners outnumbering advancers 1.72 to 1 and the Nasdaq ratio at 1.57 to 1, per the [same wrap](https://www.businesstoday.com.my/2026/10/24/wall-street-closes-lower-to-end-rocky-week/). Yet the S&P 500 still logged 18 new 52-week highs, according to [SRN News](https://srnnews.com/wall-street-futures-dip-after-ai-rout-telecoms-slide/). Leadership did not vanish. It narrowed.

Useful checks when reviewing a candidate over the weekend:

- **Did the last decline come on volume below the 50-day average?** If so, the base is behaving.
- **Is the range tighter now than three weeks ago?** Contraction in daily high-to-low spread is the setup's core feature.
- **Where did the stock close relative to its pivot?** A stock sitting within a few percent of a clearly defined prior high has a visible level to watch. A stock far below it does not yet have a setup.
- **Did the stock hold up on Thursday?** The Nasdaq had its largest one-day drop since June on Thursday, [per Reuters](https://www.world-today-news.com/wall-street-closes-lower-over-ai-costs-earnings-and-fed-meeting/). Names that barely moved that day showed relative strength.

## The tape was a split, not a rout

Under the surface, Friday was a rotation day. Energy rose 2.6% and technology 0.49%, while consumer staples fell 1.92%, communication services 1.78% and consumer discretionary 1.66%, according to [Capp Notes](https://cappnotes.substack.com/p/closing-look-friday-october-23-2026). The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index gained 0.44% to 10,779.46 and the Russell 2000 slipped 0.29% to 2,775.90. Those two index levels are useful reference points: semiconductors held up while the broader tape sagged, and small caps were nearly flat.

Single-stock reactions show how violently earnings can reshape a chart. Robert Half (RHI) jumped 21.74% after an earnings beat, while LKQ (LKQ) fell 22.47% after weak preliminary results and withdrawn guidance, per [Capp Notes](https://cappnotes.substack.com/p/closing-look-friday-october-23-2026). Bloom Energy (BE) fell 11.24% even after a beat and raised guidance. SLB (SLB) rose about 7% after reporting third-quarter revenue of $9.45 billion, up 6% year on year, and adjusted EPS of $0.64, according to its [earnings release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000087347/000119312526429435/d151271dex99.htm).

Two lessons for anyone holding a name through a report. First, a beat is not a guarantee of a gap up, as Bloom showed. Second, a tight base does nothing to protect against an earnings gap in either direction. Position size and the date on the earnings calendar are part of the setup.

## News gaps that no chart could have flagged

Not every large move came from earnings. AT&T (T) fell 7.96% and T-Mobile (TMUS) 7.71%, while Crown Castle (CCI) gained 9.06%, on a report that SpaceX is in talks to lease cell towers, per [Capp Notes](https://cappnotes.substack.com/p/closing-look-friday-october-23-2026). [SRN News](https://srnnews.com/wall-street-futures-dip-after-ai-rout-telecoms-slide/) said the report concerned a fourth wireless carrier, with Verizon (VZ) down 1.2% and American Tower (AMT) up 4%. Whatever the base looked like in those stocks, the pattern was irrelevant to the outcome.

American Express (AXP) offers a quieter version. It fell 3.7% despite raising its profit forecast, as it also announced plans to cut 5% of its global workforce, [SRN News reported](https://srnnews.com/wall-street-futures-dip-after-ai-rout-telecoms-slide/). Good headline numbers can still meet selling when the surrounding story changes.

SLB's quarter had a cleaner profile. Beyond the revenue and EPS figures, the company reported $1.51 billion in operating cash flow, $828 million of free cash flow and $656 million of share repurchases, according to the [release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000087347/000119312526429435/d151271dex99.htm). That gives fundamentals-minded traders something to weigh beside the 7% move, though a one-day gap says nothing about whether it holds.

## The week ahead stacks the risk

The next five sessions carry unusual event density.

- **Fed decision, Wednesday.** The Federal Reserve is widely expected to hold rates steady, with investors watching for hints about December, [Reuters-sourced reports say](https://www.world-today-news.com/wall-street-closes-lower-over-ai-costs-earnings-and-fed-meeting/).
- **Megacap earnings.** About one-third of S&P 500 companies report, including Alphabet (GOOGL), Microsoft (MSFT) and Amazon (AMZN). Their capital-spending forecasts are the focus after Thursday's AI-cost selloff.
- **Aggregate earnings growth.** S&P 500 companies are on track for third-quarter earnings growth above 32%, per LSEG IBES data [cited by Reuters](https://www.world-today-news.com/wall-street-closes-lower-over-ai-costs-earnings-and-fed-meeting/). A bar that high makes in-line results easy to punish.
- **Midterm elections, Nov. 3**, which sit on investors' radar as well.

Event clusters like this tend to compress ranges beforehand and expand them after. A base that tightens into Wednesday is not a signal on its own. It may simply reflect traders waiting. The information arrives with the reaction.

## What to watch

Rank candidates by the quality of their last pullback rather than by how close they sit to a breakout. Then note three things for each: the pivot level, the average volume a clean move would need to exceed, and the next earnings date.

On the index side, watch whether volume expands on rallies after the Fed and megacap prints, and whether the 18-highs figure grows or shrinks. If new highs thin out while volume rises on down days, that argues for patience. If volume picks up on advances and breadth improves from Friday's roughly 1.6-to-1 negative ratio, bases have a better backdrop.

This is commentary on market conditions, not a recommendation to buy or sell any security.
