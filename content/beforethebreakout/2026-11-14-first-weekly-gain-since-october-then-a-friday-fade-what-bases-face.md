## The week in numbers

Wall Street closed the week ended November 13 with gains, but not a clean finish. According to the [AP index table](https://krmg.com/2026/11/13/how-major-us-stock-indexes-fared-friday-11-13-2026/), the S&P 500 rose 2.5% on the week to 7,561.67, the Dow Jones Industrial Average gained 2.1% to 52,347.55, the Nasdaq Composite added 2.3% to 25,136.27 and the Russell 2000 climbed 1.3% to 2,790.49. AP called it the market's first winning week since early October.

Friday itself was mixed. The S&P 500 slipped 0.4%, the Nasdaq fell 0.9% and the Russell 2000 lost 0.3%, while the Dow edged up 0.1%. AP attributed the drag to technology stocks, with Nvidia (NVDA) falling again a day after its chief executive resigned.

[Investrade's recap](https://investrade.com/market-review-november-13-2026/) and a [Cardano Capital note](https://cardanocapital.substack.com/p/nasdaq-snaps-a-four-week-slide-up) both add that the Nasdaq's weekly gain snapped a four-week losing streak. That matters for context: a one-week bounce after four down weeks is a repair job, not a trend change.

## Where the year-to-date scoreboard stands

The AP table also shows how uneven the year has been. The Russell 2000 is up 12.4% year to date, the S&P 500 is up 10.5%, the Dow is up 8.9% and the Nasdaq Composite is up 8.2%. The Nasdaq, the index most traders associate with leadership, is the laggard of the four.

That ordering is worth keeping in mind when scanning for breakouts. Small caps have been rewarded more than the tech-heavy composite this year, yet the Russell 2000 gained the least of the four indexes this week. Leadership is not sitting in one place, and that makes the "buy the index, find the setups" shortcut less reliable.

The S&P 500 also remains below its high. Cardano Capital's note puts the record close at 7,818.93 on October 6. By simple arithmetic, Friday's 7,561.67 close sits about 3.3% below that mark. A move back to the record would not require a new regime, only a recovery of what was lost in the October slide.

## Breadth: broad on the week, narrow on Friday

Investrade reports that all eleven S&P 500 sectors finished the week higher. By its figures, energy led at +5.37%, followed by industrials (+3.73%), communication services (+3.72%) and technology (+3.3%). Those are the numbers of a single secondhand source, so treat the exact percentages as approximate, but the direction of the story is consistent with AP's account of a rebound that reached beyond one corner of the market.

The same recap says technology fell about 2% on Friday, its second straight daily decline, and that AI-linked groups such as memory, data-center and server names saw heavy selling over the last two days. It also says Nvidia hit new 52-week lows. Neither the AP table nor the other recaps I reviewed published advance/decline or new-high/new-low counts, so I can't say how many individual stocks participated. That is a gap worth closing with your own screener before acting on any index-level read.

The practical takeaway for base-builders: a week where every sector rises is constructive, but a Friday where the market's most-owned group sells off is a reminder that index leadership can reverse quickly. Bases in industrials and energy that held their lows through the week look different from tech bases that are now being tested again.

## Rates are still the pressure point

The 10-year Treasury yield finished near 5.15%, up about 0.05 percentage point on Friday, according to [VistaP Global](https://vistapglobal.com/weekly-stock-market-summary-november-13-2026-nasdaq-slides-as-inflation-and-ai-risks-shape-wall-street-ant-eprx-googl-gs-modd-msft-nvda-ser-spcx/) and Investrade. AP noted that Treasury yields rose on Friday. VistaP also says markets are pricing a quarter-point Federal Reserve rate increase at the December 8 and 9 meeting, and describes that as a market expectation rather than a policy decision.

Sources differ on how far yields rose over the week, so I'm leaving that figure out. What is not in dispute is the level: a 10-year yield above 5% raises the bar for every growth stock trying to hold a breakout, because the discount rate on future earnings is high and the competition from risk-free income is real.

For setups, that tends to favor names with current earnings power and tight, orderly bases over speculative stories. It also means a failed breakout can unwind faster than usual if yields keep climbing.

## What to watch next week

Three events could shape breakout odds.

First, the AI complex. According to Investrade, Nvidia's leadership change prompted some analyst price-target cuts and weighed on its supply chain. Cardano Capital's note says Nvidia reports on Tuesday, which would be November 17, though that is a single-source date and should be confirmed with the company's investor relations page. Whatever the number, the reaction will tell you whether Friday's selling in AI names was a pause or a break.

Second, the Anthropic IPO. Both Investrade and Cardano Capital say pricing is expected November 18 with trading on the Nasdaq on November 19 under the ticker ANT, subject to regulatory clearance. Investrade reports a planned sale of 825 million shares at $70 to $78, implying a valuation of $1.3 trillion to $1.45 trillion. A large listing can pull attention and capital toward one theme at once, and traders should watch whether it lifts or drains liquidity from other AI-related bases.

Third, the 10-year yield. A move that holds above 5.15% keeps the pressure on tech bases; a pullback would help them.

## How to read it

None of this is a call on direction. The facts are simple: indexes ended a losing stretch, leadership is split between small caps, energy, industrials and a wounded tech group, and rates are high. A breakout that works in that setting tends to show strong volume, a close near the highs of the day and relative strength against the S&P 500 rather than merely tagging a pivot.

Quiet bases that tighten while the indexes wobble are the ones to keep on a watchlist. Stocks that break out on thin volume the same week that tech is being sold are the ones more likely to give the move back. This is commentary, not advice, and every setup should be checked against your own rules and risk limits.
