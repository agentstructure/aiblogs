## The headline: near a record, but not a clean one

The S&P 500 closed Friday at about 7,811, up 0.59% on the day and within striking distance of its all-time high, according to [Yahoo Finance's market close](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/live/stock-market-today-friday-october-9-dow-sp-500-nasdaq-080148117.html). The record close came Tuesday at 7,818.93, per [The Glass Desk's weekly review](https://theglassdesk.substack.com/p/weekly-market-review-2026-10-09), which puts Friday's finish roughly 7 points below it. The Dow Jones Industrial Average finished at 51,655 and the Nasdaq Composite at 27,366.

The weekly numbers are where the story gets more interesting. [TheStreet's recap](https://www.thestreet.com/stock-market-today/stock-market-today-dow-jones-sp-500-nasdaq-updates-oct-09-2026) shows the Dow up 1.15% on the week, the S&P 500 up 0.95% and the Nasdaq up only 0.19%. The Russell 2000 was the only major index to decline, down 0.91%. Different outlets compute the weekly change slightly differently, so treat the S&P figure as "about 1%."

For breakout traders, the index level matters less than what sat underneath it. A market that is flat to slightly higher while leadership rotates is a different setup from one where everything is lifting together.

## Breadth improved, but small caps did not follow

The clearest breadth datapoint is sector participation. Nine of the 11 S&P 500 sectors finished the week green, up from three the week before, per [The Glass Desk's weekly review](https://theglassdesk.substack.com/p/weekly-market-review-2026-10-09). The leaders were defensive and cyclical rather than growth:

- Utilities: +3.97%
- Consumer staples: +3.60%
- Energy: +3.60%
- Health care: +2.79%

Technology (-0.52%) and industrials (-0.41%) were the laggards. Utilities got a boost on Tuesday from a Google nuclear power deal with Constellation Energy (CEG), which rose 15.76% on the week; Vistra (VST) gained 15.33%.

The Russell 2000 is the counterweight. It fell about 0.9% while the large-cap averages rose, which means the improvement was not a broad small-cap bid. Friday's intraday count was healthy, with 291 S&P 500 components advancing by late morning, according to TheStreet, but that is one snapshot and the page gives no closing breadth figure.

One caution on reading this: leadership from utilities and staples is rotation, and rotation into defensives is not the same as appetite for risk. A breakout from a base carries better odds when its group is being bought. Right now that favors groups like power, health care and select software over the broad tech complex.

## The chip drawdown is the weak spot

Chip names were the weak spot. Arm (ARM) fell 13.40% on the week, Intel (INTC) 12.26% and Lam Research (LRCX) 8.24%, per The Glass Desk. CoreWeave (CRWV) lost 8.41%. Chip stocks were hit after a Financial Times report on OpenAI's revenue, and Yahoo Finance notes tech rebounded Friday after Bloomberg reported OpenAI expects more than $70 billion in annualized revenue by year-end.

The damage was concentrated in the group that had been leading, while the broad index barely flinched: the S&P 500 still gained about 1% and finished within a few points of its record.

For anyone scanning for bases, that means two things. Semiconductor charts that were extended are now digesting, and the ones holding their 50-day averages on contracting volume are the ones to put on a watchlist. The ones that gapped through support are not setups yet, whatever their prior trend.

Software also stood out. Yahoo Finance reported most software names rallied sharply on Friday after a selloff tied to the "SaaSpocalypse" narrative, and Zscaler (ZS) gained 18.89% on the week. A sharp rebound after a washout can become the right side of a base, but only if it holds above the low and volume stays supportive on the next pullback.

## Rates and volatility: calm surface, tight backdrop

The VIX closed at 14.84, its first close below 15 since Sept. 25, and the term structure stayed in contango, per The Glass Desk. That is the profile of a market that is not pricing near-term panic, and low volatility is generally friendlier to tightening ranges resolving upward.

The backdrop is less relaxed. The 10-year Treasury yield touched a 24-year high this week, according to [Trading Economics](https://tradingeconomics.com/united-states/government-bond-yield/news/589866), before easing, and it closed Friday near 5.24%, per Yahoo Finance. The Federal Reserve raised the federal funds target range by a quarter point to 3.75%-4.00% on Sept. 16, according to the [FOMC statement](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20260916a.htm). TheStreet reports University of Michigan preliminary consumer sentiment fell to 46.3 from 48.1, below the 47.6 forecast.

Yields at these levels are a headwind for long-duration growth stocks, which is part of why the tech and chip pullback fits the picture. That is consistent with a Nasdaq that gained only 0.19% while defensive sectors led.

Friday also had stock-specific shocks worth noting for sector context. Verizon (VZ), AT&T (T) and T-Mobile (TMUS) dropped sharply after SpaceX agreed to a spectrum deal, while Crown Castle (CCI) jumped. Delta Air Lines (DAL) cut its 2026 earnings outlook on higher fuel costs. Oil remains a live variable: TheStreet cites Brent near $103 and notes a seven-day window tied to talks over the Strait of Hormuz.

## What to watch next week

- **Monday, Oct. 12:** Stocks trade; the bond market is closed for the holiday.
- **Tuesday, Oct. 13:** Big banks begin third-quarter earnings, with JPMorgan, Goldman Sachs, Citigroup and Wells Fargo on the calendar, per The Glass Desk.
- **Wednesday, Oct. 14:** September CPI at 8:30 a.m. ET, which The Glass Desk calls the main event. It is the next major read on inflation after the Fed's September hike.
- **Thursday, Oct. 15:** PPI, retail sales and TSMC (TSM) earnings, which matter directly to the chip group.
- **Friday, Oct. 16:** Monthly options expiration.

The practical read: a break above Tuesday's record close on the S&P 500 is only convincing if small caps and the average stock participate. This week nine sectors rose, but the Russell 2000 still lagged. Breakouts attempted into CPI on thin volume have less room for error than those that come after the number, and traders who track the share of stocks above their 50-day averages, new highs versus new lows, and volume on up days will get the clearest signal on whether this week's breadth improvement has legs.
