## A clean beat that the chart did not reward

Ross Stores (ROST) reported fiscal third-quarter results after the close on Thursday, and on the numbers alone it was a strong print. Sales rose 12% to $6.3 billion, and comparable store sales rose 8% on top of a 7% gain a year earlier, with the company saying customer traffic was the primary driver, according to its [earnings release filed with the SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000745732/000074573226000047/q326exhibit991.htm).

Diluted earnings per share came in at $1.91, against company guidance of $1.75 to $1.83 and $1.58 a year ago. Operating margin was 12.3%, up 70 basis points and above the 11.7% to 12.0% the company had planned.

The stock still fell. According to [StockAnalysis price data](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/rost/history/), ROST closed Friday, Nov. 20, at $213.46, down 2.92% on the day, on volume of about 6.0 million shares. That was the heaviest volume of the 15 sessions the page lists, and the close was the lowest the page shows since it begins on Sept. 14. For traders who track bases, a stock that sells off on heavy volume after a beat is a specific kind of information, and it is worth separating what the company reported from what the market chose to price.

## What the market was reading

The headline quarter is not where the debate sits. The forward guidance is.

Ross kept its fourth-quarter comparable sales outlook at 4% to 5%, against a 9% gain in the same quarter last year, per the Q3 release. That is a visible step down from the 8% it just delivered. Fourth-quarter EPS guidance is $2.16 to $2.25. In its [August release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000745732/000074573226000038/q226exhibit991.htm), the company had guided that quarter to $2.17 to $2.26, so the range moved down by a cent at each end while the full-year figure moved up.

Full-year EPS guidance is now $8.75 to $8.84. The August release set the range at $8.61 to $8.77. Two details matter for interpreting that. First, the company says full-year guidance includes an approximate $0.60 per share benefit from about $253 million of IEEPA tariff refunds recognized in the second quarter. Second, a good part of the raise comes from a third quarter that beat its own plan, not from a stronger fourth quarter.

In other words, the company beat, banked the beat, and left the holiday quarter essentially where it was. StockAnalysis's [quote page](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/rost/) summarizes press coverage saying the shares fell as the holiday-quarter outlook was held, and notes that several analysts trimmed price targets while keeping their ratings. That is secondhand, so treat it as context rather than a verified read on any one analyst's view.

## The balance sheet details that bear on the guide

A few other items in the release help explain why a conservative fourth-quarter guide can carry weight.

- **Inventory:** merchandise inventory was $3.54 billion, up from $3.13 billion a year earlier. Ross is a store-count growth story, and stores rose to 2,377 from 2,273, so some of that is expected. But inventory growing into a holiday quarter with a slower comp plan is something to watch.
- **Store growth:** the company opened 51 stores in the quarter (41 Ross and 10 dd's DISCOUNTS) and completed its 115-location plan for 2026.
- **Buybacks:** it repurchased 1.4 million shares for $319 million in the quarter, and says it remains on track to repurchase $1.275 billion of stock in fiscal 2026.

## The levels in the chart

Price context here comes from a third-party data provider, StockAnalysis, so treat the exact figures as that firm's data. Its quote page lists a 52-week range of $160.34 to $257.00. In the 15 sessions on its history page, the highest close was $245.74 on Nov. 4. From there the stock drifted lower, closing at $241.27 on Nov. 11, $231.68 on Nov. 13, $222.99 on Nov. 16 and $227.64 on Nov. 18, before the report.

On Thursday, the day of the release, the stock closed at $219.88. The earnings reaction then took it to $213.46 on Friday. The page also shows month-end closes of $233.40 on Sept. 30 and $228.28 on Oct. 30.

Reference points on closing prices:

- **$213.46:** Friday's close, and the lowest on the page since Sept. 14. A close below it would extend the pattern of lower closes. It is the line the post-earnings reaction drew.
- **$219.88 to $222.99:** the closes on Nov. 19 and Nov. 16. This is the zone the stock fell out of on Friday, and a recovery back above it on calmer volume would show the selling was absorbed.
- **$227.64 to $228.28:** the Nov. 18 and Oct. 30 closes, a second overhead area where supply may show up.
- **$245.74:** the Nov. 4 closing high on the page, and the number a real breakout would have to reclaim.

The 52-week high of $257.00 sits above all of these.

## What a base needs here

Ross did not set up a tight range ahead of this report. The prior two weeks were a steady slide from the Nov. 4 high, which is not the quiet, low-volume contraction that breakout traders usually look for. The earnings gap also did not form a base. It broke down through the area where buyers had stepped in earlier in the month.

That reframes the question from "when does it break out" to "what repairs it." A few things would matter:

1. **Volume behavior.** Friday's roughly 6.0 million shares was the largest of the sessions listed. Follow-through selling on similar volume would be a negative sign. A quiet drift sideways on volume closer to the 1.5 to 3.5 million shares seen through most of November would suggest sellers are finished.
2. **Comp trends.** Ross has guided the fourth quarter at 4% to 5%, and its record on beating its own guide this year is on the page: Q2 EPS of $2.66 against guidance of $1.85 to $1.93, and Q3 EPS of $1.91 against $1.75 to $1.83. Whether the market gives credit for clearing conservative bars is a separate question.
3. **Tariff noise.** The $0.60 refund benefit inflates the year-over-year EPS comparison, and the release itself warns of risks from "tariff increases (or threats of increases)." Any new trade headline hits a retailer with Ross's import exposure directly.

## What to watch next

The tests are price-based: whether $213.46 holds on a retest, whether the stock can recover the Nov. 16 to Nov. 19 closes, and whether volume fades.

A company that beats its own plan and sees its stock fall to a multi-week low is a reminder that bases are built by price and volume, not by headlines. Ross has the earnings record. The chart has not yet shown the repair.
