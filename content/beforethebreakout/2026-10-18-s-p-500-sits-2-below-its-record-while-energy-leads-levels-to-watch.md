## What Friday changed

The S&P 500 closed Friday at 7,659.44, down 1.1% on the day and 1.9% on the week, according to [AP's market table](https://www.lanacion.com.ar/usa/how-major-us-stock-indexes-fared-friday-10162026-nid16102026/). The Nasdaq Composite fell 2.1% to 26,140.03 and lost 4.5% for the week. The Dow slipped 0.3% on the day but still gained 0.7% on the week. The Russell 2000 dropped 1.7% to 2,721.37.

Compare that with the high. The S&P 500 set its record close of 7,818.93 on [Oct. 6](https://www.lanacion.com.ar/usa/how-major-us-stock-indexes-fared-tuesday-1062026-nid06102026/), and the next day's [close of 7,801.77](https://www.lanacion.com.ar/usa/how-major-us-stock-indexes-fared-wednesday-1072026-nid07102026/) was not a record. Friday's close sits about 2.0% below that peak. The Nasdaq Composite also closed at a record that day, at 27,599.79, which puts Friday's close roughly 5.3% below it.

That gap matters for anyone watching bases. A two-percent pullback in the S&P 500 is shallow, and it is far too small to call a correction. A five-percent pullback in the Nasdaq is a different thing. It is deep enough to damage the chart of almost any stock that was extended on the way up.

## The damage was concentrated

Friday was not a broad selloff. By [Strategitz's close recap](https://strategitz.substack.com/p/global-markets-close-october-16-2026), the Nasdaq 100 fell 2.50% on the day and 5.49% on the week, while the equal-weight S&P 500 (RSP) fell only 0.42%. The software ETF (IGV) dropped 4.80%, and the PHLX Semiconductor Index fell 3.83%. Technology (XLK) lost 3.03%.

The money went elsewhere. Energy (XLE) rose 2.52%, utilities (XLU) gained 1.34% and staples (XLP) added 1.08%. Individual chip names took the hits: AMD (AMD) fell 5.95%, Intel (INTC) 5.79%, Micron (MU) 5.63% and Broadcom (AVGO) 4.52%. CoreWeave (CRWV) lost 11.91% and Nebius (NBIS) 12.18%.

The volatility index (VIX) jumped 21.45% to 19.99, per the same recap. A day earlier, [Zacks reported](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/stock-market-news-oct-16-115900532.html) the VIX at 16.46, with the S&P 500 up 0.3% to 7,744.06. In other words, Thursday looked calm and Friday did not.

The 10-year Treasury yield barely moved on Friday, at 5.09% (down 1 basis point). Still, it has stayed above 5% all week, and Zacks put it at about 5.06% on Thursday after a drop from 5.19%. Growth stocks with long-dated earnings have a harder time basing under yields like these.

## What is actually on the new-high list

If tech is breaking down, the next question is where the new highs are. The [Trefis list](https://www.trefis.com/stock/spy/articles/618819/the-52-week-high-list-18-sp-500-names-on-wednesday-2/2026-10-15) for Oct. 14 counted 18 S&P 500 stocks at 52-week highs, and energy was the largest group with 7 of them. The ten largest names were Apple (AAPL), Exxon Mobil (XOM), Chevron (CVX), Coca-Cola (KO), Philip Morris (PM), Gilead (GILD), ConocoPhillips (COP), Pfizer (PFE), Marathon Petroleum (MPC) and Valero (VLO). EOG Resources (EOG) and Mettler-Toledo (MTD) were also on it.

That is a mix of energy, staples and health care, and the trading problem with it is plain. Stocks printing 52-week highs are mostly past their breakouts. They are not setting up. Exxon, for example, hit [$169.64 on Oct. 9](https://tradingeconomics.com/xom:us/news/570807), its highest level since March 2026 according to Trading Economics, and Valero's one-year gain on the Trefis table was 190.9%. A stock up that far in a year rarely offers a tight, low-risk entry. The more useful question is whether these leaders will build a new base after the run or simply keep climbing on oil.

Oil is the common thread. [Strategitz](https://strategitz.substack.com/p/global-markets-close-october-16-2026) shows WTI up 3.82% to $101.71 and Brent up 3.74% to $109.88 on Friday. The energy group's trend depends on crude holding up, so it can reverse quickly if crude falls. Thursday is a reminder: XLE fell 1.6% that day, per Zacks, before rising 2.52% on Friday.

## Levels to watch on Monday

These are reference points taken from closing prices, not forecasts.

- **7,818.93 on the S&P 500.** This is the Oct. 6 record close. A close above it would end the pullback and put the index back in blue sky.
- **7,718.94.** This is the Oct. 14 S&P close implied by Thursday's 25.12-point gain to 7,744.06. It marks where the index sat before Thursday's bounce and Friday's drop, so it is the first overhead area a rebound has to reclaim.
- **7,659.44.** Friday's close. It is the level to hold; a close below it extends the pullback.
- **27,599.79 on the Nasdaq Composite.** Its Oct. 6 record close is about 5.3% above Friday's 26,140.03, so there is a long way to go before tech leadership looks repaired.
- **5% on the 10-year yield.** The yield was 5.09% on Friday. A move well below 5% would be a tailwind for growth bases, and a move back toward Thursday's starting 5.19% would not be.

## How to use this in practice

First, separate the stocks that held from the stocks that merely fell less. A base that holds up while its sector falls 3% is worth watching, and a stock that drifts down 1.5% with the group is not. Watch for tight closes, shrinking daily ranges and quiet volume on down days.

Second, treat tech and energy as two different set-ups. Software and chips fell hard on Friday, so any bases there are being tested by a falling tape, and a failed test is more informative than a pass. Energy names are extended, so the realistic setup is a pause after the run rather than a breakout.

Third, watch breadth. The equal-weight S&P 500 held up much better than the Nasdaq 100 on Friday, which says the damage was narrow. If the equal-weight index starts falling alongside the cap-weighted one, the selling has widened and breakouts everywhere get less reliable.

The tape is not signaling that the uptrend is over. It is signaling that leadership is being reset, and bases that form after a reset tend to be more useful than the ones that form before it. The next week or two will show which groups build them.
