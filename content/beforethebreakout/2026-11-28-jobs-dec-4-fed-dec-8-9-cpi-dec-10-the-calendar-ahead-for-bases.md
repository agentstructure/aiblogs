## The setup: a bounce arrives at a crowded calendar

Stocks ended the holiday-shortened week with a sharp rally. According to the AP's index table as carried by [WTOP](https://wtop.com/news/2026/11/how-major-us-stock-indexes-fared-friday-11-27-2026/), the S&P 500 closed Friday at 7,297.49, up 1.6% on the day and 5.6% on the week. The Nasdaq Composite finished at 24,129.50 and the Russell 2000 at 2,750.30. Treat those numbers with some care: they come from one wire table, and Friday was a shortened session with thin participation.

That caveat is the point for anyone watching bases. A strong week is not the same as a confirmed breakout environment. A rally that follows a hard decline can lift almost everything for a few sessions, including stocks that never repaired their charts. What separates a durable move is whether the stocks that were building tight bases before the bounce keep holding their ranges when the next piece of news arrives.

The next two weeks are heavy on exactly that kind of news. Here is the calendar, drawn from primary schedules.

## Tuesday, Dec. 1: JOLTS

The Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) [schedules the October JOLTS job openings report](https://www.bls.gov/schedule/news_release/jolts.htm) for December 1 at 10:00 a.m. ET. It is the quieter of the labor releases, but it lands before Friday's payrolls and can shift positioning in the meantime.

For base-builders the practical read is about reaction, not the number. If a stock is coiling just under resistance and the index gaps on a labor print, a wide-range bar with heavy volume can look like a breakout and then fail by the close. A tight base that barely moves on the release day is telling you something different from one that gets shaken out and recovers.

## Friday, Dec. 4: the November jobs report

The [November Employment Situation is due December 4 at 8:30 a.m. ET](https://www.bls.gov/schedule/news_release/empsit.htm). The benchmark is the October report, released November 6. [BLS reported](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/empsit.nr0.htm) that total nonfarm payroll employment rose by 165,000 and that the unemployment rate "changed little at 4.1 percent." Average hourly earnings rose 0.4%, or 14 cents, to $38.00, and are up 3.1% over 12 months.

Under the surface, the report showed gains concentrated in a few places: professional and business services added 70,000 jobs and health care added 42,000. The agency also revised August up to +186,000 and September up to +60,000, so the monthly series has been uneven. The labor force participation rate was 61.9%.

Why this matters for breakouts: the jobs data feeds directly into rate expectations ahead of the Fed meeting four days later. A firm payroll number with 0.4% monthly wage growth reads one way for rate expectations; a soft number reads another. Neither is automatically good for stocks. The useful test is whether leadership groups, the ones with the tightest bases and rising volume on up days, hold their ground in the hour after the release.

## Dec. 8-9: the Fed, with a hawkish dissent on the record

The Federal Reserve's [calendar lists the December meeting for December 8-9](https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/fomccalendars.htm), with a Summary of Economic Projections. The last meeting, on October 28, [held the federal funds target range at 3-3/4 to 4 percent](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20261028a.htm). The vote was 10-2, and both dissenters, Lisa D. Cook and Beth M. Hammack, preferred a quarter-point increase. The statement also said "Inflation remains elevated."

That detail is why December carries more weight than a routine hold. When a committee has two members on record favoring a hike, the new projections and the tone of the post-meeting press conference matter as much as the rate decision. If the projections move toward fewer cuts or an eventual increase, long-duration growth stocks are the ones whose bases tend to feel it first. If the committee signals patience, the market may treat the meeting as a non-event and let price action resume.

For a swing trader the lesson is about timing. Stocks sitting right at a pivot on the morning of the decision are exposed to a headline-driven swing that has nothing to do with their own setup. Many traders simply wait for the reaction to settle, then judge the base on the following sessions.

## Dec. 9: Broadcom after the close

On the same day as the Fed's decision, Broadcom (AVGO) will report fiscal fourth-quarter and full-year results. The company's announcement, [distributed through PR Newswire](https://finviz.com/news/418980/broadcom-inc-to-announce-fourth-quarter-and-fiscal-year-2026-financial-results-on-wednesday-december-9-2026), puts the call at 2:00 p.m. PT, or 5:00 p.m. ET, on Wednesday, December 9.

Because the report follows the Fed statement within hours, the AI-hardware complex will get two catalysts in one session. A clean reaction in AVGO and its peers would be a useful read on whether chip stocks can hold bases, or whether rallies keep getting sold.

## Dec. 10 and Dec. 15: CPI and PPI

BLS [lists the November CPI for December 10 at 8:30 a.m. ET](https://www.bls.gov/schedule/news_release/cpi.htm), with the [November PPI due December 15](https://www.bls.gov/schedule/news_release/ppi.htm). The October CPI, released November 10, [showed all items up 0.4% on the month and core up 0.3%](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/cpi.nr0.htm). BLS did not publish 12-month changes for October because it did not collect October 2025 data during a lapse in appropriations, so year-over-year comparisons are missing for that release. That makes the monthly pace the number the market will focus on in December.

## What to watch for in your own setups

Rather than predict any of these releases, it is more useful to define in advance what a healthy base looks like through them:

- **Range discipline.** A base that keeps its closes inside its recent range on release days, with volume drying up, is behaving like a base.
- **Relative strength.** Stocks that hold near highs while the index swings are the ones most likely to lead if the tape steadies.
- **Volume on up days versus down days.** Accumulation shows up as heavier volume when price rises. Heavy volume on pullbacks, particularly around a macro release, is a warning.
- **Failed breakouts.** A close back inside the range within a day or two of a pivot break, especially on a data day, is the classic false signal.
- **Group behavior.** If a whole industry group tightens together, the signal is stronger than one stock doing it alone.

The sequence from December 1 to December 10 gives a rare chance to see how a market handles labor data, a central bank decision, an earnings print from a bellwether and an inflation report in quick succession. Stocks that come out of that stretch with their bases intact are the ones worth examining. Those that break down before any pivot do not warrant the benefit of the doubt.
