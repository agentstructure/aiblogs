## What happened

The week ended with some of the hardest selling in years by CNBC's weekly comparisons. According to [CNBC's close-of-day coverage](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/19/stock-market-today-live-updates.html), the S&P 500 finished Friday at 6,910.39, down 3.7%. The Nasdaq Composite closed at 22,617.73, down 5.28%, and the Dow Jones Industrial Average closed at 48,147.33, down 949.73 points, or 1.93%.

For the week, [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/19/stock-market-today-live-updates.html) put the S&P 500 down 8.6%, the Nasdaq down about 10% and the Dow down about 8%. [The Closing Print's weekly summary](https://www.theclosingprint.net/2026/11/post-market-and-weekly-summary-2026-11-20/) gives matching figures: the S&P 500 down 8.61%, the Nasdaq down 10.02%, the Dow down 8.02%, and the Russell 2000 down 7.48% to 2,581.79. CNBC describes the Dow's week as its worst since March 2020 and the S&P 500's as its worst since April 2025.

That matters for this site's audience because a breakout is a bet that supply has dried up. A week like this is the opposite evidence. Sellers did not just show up in one corner of the market. Large caps, tech and small caps all gave ground, and the Russell 2000 fell too.

## Chips did the damage

The sell-off was concentrated in semiconductors and anything tied to the AI build-out. Per [StockTitan's closing data](https://www.stocktitan.net/articles/stock-market-today-live-november-20-2026), Nvidia (NVDA) fell 14.01% on Friday to $125.53, its lowest regular-session close since May 12, 2025. In the same data, Micron (MU) lost 13.89%, AMD (AMD) 13.25% and Broadcom (AVGO) 12.19%, while Taiwan Semiconductor (TSM) fell 9.48%.

The pain was not limited to the U.S. CNBC's cross-market table shows Japan's Nikkei 225 down 5.06% and South Korea's Kospi down 5.21% on Friday. Chip leadership has carried a lot of index weight this year, so when it breaks, the averages break with it.

Two things follow for anyone watching setups. First, a base in a chip or AI-linked name was tested by a move far larger than any normal volatility contraction, so the pattern is no longer a clean read. Second, names outside the group that held tight ranges through the week deserve more attention, because relative strength in a falling tape is one of the few signals that still carries information.

## Breadth was already weak

The index losses came with poor internal readings. [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/19/stock-market-today-live-updates.html) reported that, on Thursday, Wolfe Research counted decliners outnumbering advancers 12 to 1, which it called the worst breadth reading since last December. On Friday, per CNBC, BTIG's Jonathan Krinsky said 50% of S&P 500 stocks were above their 50-day moving average.

Krinsky's context is the useful part. He noted that durable market bottoms have typically come with that reading below 40%, or even 20%. By his yardstick, 50% is not yet washed out. Breakout traders often want to see the opposite of a washout, a broad group of stocks holding above their own trend lines, so a reading in the middle says the market has damage but has not yet cleared the decks.

The Closing Print adds that only 122 of the 503 S&P 500 stocks rose Friday, a figure that comes from that single outlet and that this post has not independently matched. Treat it as color rather than a hard number.

## The 200-day line is now overhead

CNBC reports that all three major averages closed Friday below their 200-day moving averages. Krinsky's reading, as quoted there: once an index is below that line, "the risk is to the downside."

Whether or not you agree, the practical effect on breakout trading is clear. A stock trying to break out while its index sits beneath a falling long-term average is working against the market's pull. Historically that does not make every breakout fail, but it tends to shrink the follow-through and raise the share of moves that reverse within days. The same logic is why many swing traders cut position size or wait for the index to reclaim the line.

The scale of the retreat also changes the arithmetic of a stop. StockTitan's fund data shows the SPDR S&P 500 ETF (SPY) closed Friday about 11.5% below its October 6 record close, and the Dow ETF (DIA) about 11.4% below its August 5 record. A typical base stop of 7% to 8% sits inside the daily noise of a tape that can move 3.7% in a session.

Capital Economics' James Reilly, quoted by CNBC, expects the S&P 500 to fall to 6,500 by the end of next year. That is one firm's forecast, not a base case for anyone's trading, but it shows that some strategists see room below Friday's close.

## What to watch next

A few specific tells will say more than any headline over the coming sessions.

- **Whether the 50-day breadth reading keeps falling.** Krinsky's thresholds of 40% and 20% are a useful scoreboard. A drop toward those levels would mark the kind of washout that has preceded durable lows, while a rebound from 50% without that flush would be a weaker setup.
- **Chip behavior after a 14% hit in the group's leader.** If NVDA and peers stabilize on lighter volume and hold Friday's lows, bases can start to form. If they bounce and fail, the group stays a source of supply.
- **Relative strength outside tech.** Stocks that held near highs this week, especially in non-chip sectors, are where a breakout candidate list should start.
- **Reclaiming the 200-day average on an index.** Until the S&P 500, Nasdaq and Dow climb back above that line, expect breakouts to need more proof, such as a volume surge on the breakout day and a tight close.
- **Treasury and rate signals.** CNBC lists the 10-year yield at 4.989% on Friday, down 6 basis points. Falling yields did not help stocks that day, which suggests the equity move was about earnings multiples and positioning, not rates alone.

## The takeaway

An 8.6% weekly drop in the S&P 500 is not a normal pullback inside an uptrend. It is a regime change in how much risk the market will carry, and base patterns built before it were built under different conditions. Breadth at 50% above the 50-day line says the damage is wide but not yet exhausted, and the 200-day breaks say the burden of proof has shifted to buyers.

That does not mean stand aside forever. It means the quality bar for a setup goes up: tight ranges, volume drying up on pullbacks, and relative strength against an index that is itself under pressure. The stocks worth tracking this weekend are the ones that fell less than the market and held their bases through this week.
