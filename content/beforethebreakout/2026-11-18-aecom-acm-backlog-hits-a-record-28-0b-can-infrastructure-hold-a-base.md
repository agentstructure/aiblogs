## What AECOM reported

AECOM (ACM), the infrastructure consulting and engineering firm, [released fiscal fourth-quarter results on November 16](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000868857/000110465926128300/tm2629784d1_ex99-1.htm). The headline for anyone watching the group is backlog: $28.0 billion, up 13% from $24.8 billion a year earlier and a record. Design-only backlog reached $26.1 billion, against $23.3 billion.

Wins were strong too. The company booked a record $13.4 billion for the full year, a book-to-burn ratio of 1.3x. In the fourth quarter alone, wins were $2.9 billion and book-to-burn was 1.1x, slower than the full-year pace.

Profitability improved where it was measured on an adjusted basis. Adjusted EPS was $1.63 against $1.36 a year earlier, up 20%. Adjusted EBITDA was $358 million, up 9% from $328.7 million, and the adjusted EBITDA margin was 19.2%, up 170 basis points. Free cash flow in the quarter was $274 million, against $134.1 million.

The picture is not clean. Quarterly revenue was $3,878 million, down 7% from $4,175 million. Net service revenue (NSR), the measure that strips out pass-through costs, was $1,920 million and grew 3% after adjusting for fewer working days, with the design business up 6%. For the full year, diluted EPS was $2.84, down 41%, and free cash flow was $344 million against $685 million. The release ties part of that weakness to a $337 million pre-tax charge booked in the third quarter for a higher projected cost to complete a construction management project.

## The tape it landed on

The earnings arrived on a poor day for the broad market. CNBC's live coverage has [the Dow down about 1.6%, the S&P 500 down about 1% to its lowest close since July 29, and the Nasdaq Composite down about 0.7%](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/17/stock-market-today-live-updates.html) on Tuesday, November 17. Different outlets print slightly different closing figures, so treat those as rounded.

In that session, [Stock Titan's closing movers table](https://stocktitan.net/articles/stock-market-today-live-november-17-2026) listed AECOM up 11.08%. That is a single secondhand source for a one-day move, so check it against your own chart before leaning on it. What matters for a base-watcher is the setting: a stock that gaps up when the index is breaking down is showing relative strength, but one session does not make a breakout.

For this audience, the useful question is the same one asked of any gap. Did the move come on heavy volume, does the close hold near the top of the day's range, and does the stock then pause in a tight, quiet range above the gap rather than giving it back? A gap that is defended for several sessions is information. A gap that fills in a week is an earnings pop.

## Why backlog matters more than revenue here

Engineering and consulting firms sell time and expertise on multi-year programs, so backlog is the closest thing to a forward order book. A record $28.0 billion, with Americas backlog [up 10% to a record and International up 21%](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000868857/000110465926128300/tm2629784d1_ex99-1.htm), supports the case that demand for infrastructure design work is intact even as reported revenue shrinks.

The release shows NSR growing while gross revenue declined, so NSR and backlog are the better gauges of underlying demand. Readers should hold both views: the order book argues for demand, while the revenue decline and the large construction management charge are the reasons the stock was not a market favorite going in.

That construction management exposure is the open risk. The release says two projects are performing in line with expectations, phase one of one has reached substantial completion, and the second is expected to be delivered near the end of fiscal second quarter 2027. It also says about $500 million of cash will be used on those projects in fiscal 2027.

## The fiscal 2027 outlook

Management's fiscal 2027 guidance, [as stated in the release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000868857/000110465926128300/tm2629784d1_ex99-1.htm):

- Organic NSR growth of 4% to 6%
- Adjusted EPS of $6.15 to $6.35
- Adjusted EBITDA of $1,350 million to $1,390 million
- Adjusted EBITDA margin of 17.6%
- Free cash flow of about $300 million, including the roughly $500 million construction management drag

The release also says the company is increasing its quarterly dividend 10% to $0.34 per share, payable January 22, 2027 to holders of record on January 6, and that no share repurchase was announced. Capital allocation is prioritized to organic growth and the dividend.

Note the shape of the free cash flow guide. At about $300 million, it is below the $344 million delivered in fiscal 2026 and well below the $685 million of fiscal 2025, because of the construction management cash use. The company says its target of 100% or better free cash flow conversion should return once those headwinds subside, which it expects by the end of the first half of fiscal 2027. That makes the first two quarters of the new fiscal year the proving period.

## What to watch next

Three things decide whether this becomes a base or a one-day event.

**Price and volume behavior after the gap.** Look at whether the reported advance held through the following sessions and whether pullbacks come on lighter volume than the advance. Heavy selling back into the gap area would argue the move was a relief reaction.

**Whether peers confirm.** A single stock leading is a story; a group leading is rotation. Infrastructure engineering and construction names that report in the coming weeks will show whether investors are rewarding backlog growth across the group or only at AECOM. Compare how each one trades relative to the major indexes on down days.

**Backlog conversion.** Book-to-burn slowed to 1.1x in the fourth quarter from 1.3x for the year. Another quarter at or above 1.0x keeps the order book growing; a slide below it would undercut the record-backlog headline.

The setup is straightforward to describe and hard to trade: strong orders, improving adjusted margins and a visible path to cash recovery, against falling reported revenue, a charge-hit year and an index that is pressing lower. The next few weeks of price action will say which side the market is weighting.
