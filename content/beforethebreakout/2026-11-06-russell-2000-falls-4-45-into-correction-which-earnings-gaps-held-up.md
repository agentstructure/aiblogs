## What happened

Thursday was the kind of session that rewrites a watchlist. The Russell 2000 fell 4.45% and [entered correction territory, according to CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/04/stock-market-today-live-updates.html). The Nasdaq Composite dropped 5.52% to 24,315.00, the S&P 500 lost 4.36% to 7,353.73, and the Dow shed 1,833.06 points, or 3.44%. CNBC reported that all three major averages had their worst day since April 2025.

The damage was concentrated in chips and AI infrastructure. CNBC noted that the iShares Semiconductor ETF (SOXX) had its worst day since March 2020. Broadcom (AVGO) fell about 10%, Nvidia (NVDA) about 9%, and AMD and Micron (MU) about 15% each. The S&P MidCap 400 fell 3.85%, per [Capp Notes' closing recap](https://cappnotes.substack.com/p/closing-look-thursday-november-5), which tied the selloff to weak chip earnings and fears of tighter data-center rules.

The backdrop matters. CNBC said the drop followed a midterm-related rally on Wednesday, when the Dow rose more than 1,500 points on the view that a Democratic sweep could slow the data-center buildout. It also reported that, per CME FedWatch, the odds of a December Fed rate hike slipped to 70.1% from 82.7% a day earlier. A live rate-hike debate is a headwind worth tracking for small caps.

## The names that went up

On a day when almost everything with a growth story was sold, a handful of small and mid caps rose. Capp Notes listed EPAM Systems (EPAM) up 20.63% and Enovis (ENOV) up 17.20% among the day's leaders. [Tickeron reported](https://tickeron.com/blogs/why-is-vericel-corporation-vcel-stock-up-11-92-today-29798/) Vericel (VCEL) up roughly 12%. Capp Notes also had AppLovin (APP) up 31.04%, but AppLovin is a large-cap and trades on a different set of drivers, so this piece sets it aside.

A stock rising 12% to 20% in a 4% down tape is relative strength in the plainest sense. But a one-day gap is an event, not a base. The useful question is what the company reported, what the chart looked like going in, and where the gap leaves the stock relative to its prior range.

## Vericel (VCEL): strong numbers, already extended

Tickeron's summary of Vericel's third quarter shows a clean beat. Revenue was $85.4 million, up 26% year over year, against roughly $78 million expected. Adjusted diluted EPS was $0.27 versus a $0.06 consensus. Burn-care revenue hit a record $18 million, up 52%. The company raised full-year revenue guidance to $344 million to $346 million from $330 million to $340 million, its third straight quarterly raise, and reported about $236 million in cash with no debt.

Fundamentals are the easy half. Tickeron also reported that the stock rose about 26% over three days and had gained more than 10% the session before earnings, with its upper Bollinger Band broken on November 4. Those are Tickeron's calculations and its model-based "odds" language should be ignored, but the shape is plain: Vericel ran before the report and gapped after it. A stock that climbs ahead of earnings and gaps again has used up much of its low-risk entry zone. Tickeron's own article flags overbought readings. Note too that its text (about 11.9%) and its price widget (a $51.78 close, up 13.85%) disagree slightly, so treat the exact gain as approximate.

What to watch: whether VCEL holds the top of Thursday's range on a pullback, and whether volume dries up during the pause. A gap that holds on quiet trading is a base forming on a higher shelf. A gap that fills on heavy selling was a spike.

## EPAM (EPAM): a re-rating, not a growth surge

EPAM's quarter was not spectacular on growth. According to its [8-K exhibit](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001352010/000135201026000048/exhibit99_q3x2026.htm), revenue was $1.426 billion, up 2.3% year over year, with non-GAAP EPS of $3.51. The company narrowed full-year revenue growth guidance to 3.5% to 3.7%, with organic constant-currency growth of 2.5% to 2.7%.

The more likely driver of a 20.63% move is capital return. EPAM raised its buyback authorization by $1.5 billion, plans a $500 million accelerated share repurchase, and committed to return at least 50% of free cash flow to shareholders annually from 2027 through 2029. Capp Notes did not give a reason for the move, so the link between the buyback and the price is an inference, not a reported fact.

For a swing trader the lesson is different from Vericel's. Low revenue growth with large buybacks is a valuation story. Those gaps can hold when the buyer is the company itself, but they depend on the stock not being dragged back by a market-wide selloff. Watch whether EPAM closes inside or above the gap day's range over the next few sessions.

## Enovis (ENOV): a smaller beat, a bigger reaction

Enovis's [earnings release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001420800/000142080026000052/q32026earningspressrelease.htm) showed third-quarter sales up 2% reported and 5% organic, net income of $3 million, and adjusted EBITDA of $104 million, or 18.5% of sales. The company said it was raising full-year guidance for adjusted EBITDA and adjusted EPS, and its board approved a new $200 million share repurchase authorization. I could not verify the new guidance figures, so the size of the raise is not reported here.

Capp Notes had the stock up 17.20% without citing a cause. The release suggests a profitability-and-buyback reading, but modest organic growth and a thin GAAP profit mean the margin story has to keep proving itself.

## How to use this

Three things separate a tradeable post-earnings gap from a headline:

- **Pre-gap setup.** Was the stock basing or already extended? Vericel was extended; the other two were not described in the sources used here.
- **Reason for the move.** Raised guidance on accelerating growth carries more weight than a buyback on slow growth, though both can work.
- **Behavior on day two to five.** Holding gains while the index stays under pressure is the signal. Giving the gap back as soon as the tape steadies is the warning.

The market context also argues for patience. A correction in the Russell 2000 means many bases that looked clean last week are now broken or stretched, and the VIX briefly reached 28.09 on Thursday, its highest since March 31, per CNBC. After a day like this, breakouts attempted on the first bounce tend to be tested harder than usual. That is a general pattern, not a forecast for any one name.

## What to watch next

- Whether the Russell 2000 stabilizes or follows through lower. A second heavy-volume down day would change how much weight any single-stock gap deserves.
- Chip and AI-infrastructure names. They led the selloff, and their next move shapes the risk appetite that small caps borrow.
- The gap-day lows in VCEL, EPAM and ENOV. Those levels are where each story gets tested.
- Fed expectations. The December hike odds CNBC cited are a live variable for rate-sensitive small caps.

This is information and commentary, not investment advice. Check every level against your own charts and data.
