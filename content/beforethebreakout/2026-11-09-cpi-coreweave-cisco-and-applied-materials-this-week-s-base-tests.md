## The week in one glance

Most of this week's market-moving information arrives in two clusters. The first is Tuesday, November 10, when the October Consumer Price Index prints at 8:30 a.m. ET and CoreWeave (CRWV) reports after the close. The second is Thursday, November 12, when Cisco (CSCO), Applied Materials (AMAT) and Cerebras Systems (CBRS) all report after the bell. Producer prices follow on Friday at 8:30 a.m. ET, according to [CNBC's week-ahead calendar](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/06/stock-market-next-week-outlook-for-nov-9-13-2026.html).

For traders who focus on bases, that structure matters. A tight range going into a known event is a coiled spring only if the event resolves in the stock's favor. Otherwise it is just a gap waiting to happen. The useful question this week is not which name will break out. It is which setups have already shown they can absorb bad news, and which have been waiting for a catalyst to rescue them.

## Tuesday: CPI first, then CoreWeave

According to early estimates averaged by FactSet and cited by CNBC, October CPI is expected to show prices up 3.9% from a year earlier, versus 3.7% in the prior reading. Core inflation is expected to have cooled somewhat on a monthly basis. That mix is awkward. A faster headline number with a softer core reading can be read either way, and the first move after 8:30 a.m. often reverses once desks digest the details.

That is why it helps to separate the data reaction from the trend. A stock that gaps on the print and then holds the opening range into the afternoon is telling you something different from one that spikes and fades inside an hour. Index-level reactions tend to drag everything together for the first few minutes, and the stocks with real demand behind them usually reveal themselves later in the session.

Tuesday evening, CoreWeave reports at 5:00 p.m. ET, per [Stock Titan's calendar](https://www.stocktitan.net/articles/earnings-calendar-week-of-november-9-2026), where the consensus EPS figure is a loss of $1.45 a share. A company still losing money is judged on its guidance, capacity and customer commentary rather than the headline loss. CoreWeave is also a bellwether for the AI infrastructure group, so its report can move names that have nothing to do with its own results. Other AI-linked names on the calendar include Nebius Group (NBIS), which reports before the open Thursday with a consensus loss of $0.61 a share, per the same calendar.

What to watch: whether suppliers and peers that were consolidating respond in sympathy, and whether that response is on above-average volume. Sympathy moves that fail within a day or two are the weakest kind of breakout.

## Thursday: a hardware double header

Cisco and Applied Materials both report after the close on Thursday at 4:30 p.m. ET, according to Stock Titan, which lists both as confirmed. Consensus EPS (Nasdaq's figures) is $1.15 for Cisco and $4.06 for Applied Materials. On guidance, the calendar says Cisco has pointed to revenue of $18.0 billion to $18.2 billion, and Applied Materials to about $10.25 billion, plus or minus $500 million. CNBC's calendar separately lists Applied Materials for Thursday.

The two companies sit at different points in the tech stack. Applied Materials sells the equipment used to make chips, so its order commentary is read as a forward signal for semiconductor capital spending. Cisco sells networking gear and is read as a gauge of enterprise and data-center demand. When both report in one evening, the next morning gives a clean read on whether capital is still flowing toward AI hardware, or whether the group is being treated as stretched.

Cerebras Systems reports the same evening at 5:00 p.m. ET with a consensus loss of $0.13 a share, per Stock Titan. A newer name with a short public history has less of a base to defend, so its chart reaction is worth treating with extra caution.

Note the timing difference: the reports land after the close, so the first look at price is the next morning's open, and any gap will form before you can act on an alert. Stops and position sizing that assumed a smooth path are the usual casualties.

## A framework for reading the reaction

Earnings gaps are the cleanest test a base ever gets, and a few habits make the read less noisy.

- **Compare the gap to the base.** A gap that clears a multi-week range on heavy volume is a different event from a gap that merely recovers a recent drop.
- **Watch day two.** The first session is dominated by positioning. The second shows whether buyers are willing to pay up without a headline.
- **Check relative strength.** In a week with a macro print on Tuesday, a stock that holds up on a weak tape deserves more attention than one that only rises when the index does.
- **Respect missing information.** Consensus numbers vary by data provider. Stock Titan uses Nasdaq's figures, and other outlets quote slightly different estimates for the same companies, so treat any single consensus number as approximate.

## What to watch next

Three things decide how this week reads. First, whether the CPI reaction holds into the close on Tuesday. Second, whether CoreWeave's report shifts the tone for AI infrastructure names heading into Thursday. Third, whether Cisco and Applied Materials confirm demand or raise questions about it on Friday morning, when PPI also arrives.

CNBC also notes that Nvidia reports later in the month, outside this week's window. Until then, the hardware group is trading on these reports and on inflation data rather than on its largest name. That gap can leave stocks drifting without a clear driver, which is when bases are easiest to misread.

None of this is a recommendation to buy or sell anything. It is a map of when the information arrives, so that the next time a chart breaks, you already know which catalyst caused it and how much of the move was the event rather than the setup.
