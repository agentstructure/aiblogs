## The week in numbers

The week ended Friday with all three major U.S. indexes lower, but the damage was not evenly spread. The Nasdaq Composite closed at 24,579.42, down about 3.4% for the week. The S&P 500 finished at 7,376.86, down about 2.7%. The Dow Jones Industrial Average ended at 51,285.73, off roughly 0.9%. Those weekly changes come from [The Closing Print's weekly summary](https://www.theclosingprint.net/2026/11/post-market-and-weekly-summary-2026-11-06/) and match [Alain Guillot's recap, which cites AP](https://www.alainguillot.com/stock-market-recap-november-6-2026/).

The Friday closes (Nasdaq +1.09%, S&P 500 +0.31%, Dow -0.46%) are reported in [CNBC's market coverage](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/05/stock-market-today-live-updates.html). The Russell 2000 lost about 2.1% on the week, according to the same two summaries, so small caps did not offer shelter either.

The weekly totals hide the shape of the week. Wednesday was strong; the Dow rose more than 1,500 points, per Guillot's recap. Thursday erased it. That whipsaw matters more to a base-builder than the net number, because it shows how fast buyers and sellers swapped control.

## Thursday did the damage

[CNBC reported](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/05/stock-market-today-live-updates.html) that the Nasdaq Composite fell 5.5% on Thursday and closed more than 10% below its recent high, which is the conventional definition of a correction. The S&P 500 dropped 4.4%, and the Dow shed more than 1,800 points, or 3.4%. CNBC said all three averages posted their worst one-day losses since April 2025.

Two details from that report are worth holding onto:

- **It was a sector event first.** AI-linked chip and hardware stocks led the decline. The "Magnificent Seven" lost more than $1 trillion in combined market value, with Nvidia (NVDA) down more than 9%, Tesla (TSLA) down 10.8%, Meta Platforms (META) down 3.9% and Microsoft (MSFT) down 3.6%.
- **It was still broad.** All 11 S&P 500 sectors closed lower, and the Russell 2000 had its worst session since April 2025.

CNBC attributed the selling to concern that Democratic wins in the House, Senate and key governor races could slow the data center buildout, citing calls from top Democrats to curb it and comments from Texas Governor-elect Gina Hinojosa. That is a policy-risk narrative rather than an earnings miss, and policy narratives are hard to price. Traders who rely on base structure should treat it as an unresolved variable.

## Friday's jobs report cut the other way

At 8:30 a.m. ET Friday, the [Bureau of Labor Statistics reported](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/empsit.nr0.htm) that nonfarm payrolls rose by 165,000 in October and the unemployment rate was 4.1%. [CNBC's report](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/06/jobs-report-october-2026.html) put the consensus at 59,000 jobs and 4.2% unemployment. August was revised up to 186,000 and September to 60,000. Average hourly earnings rose 0.4% on the month and 3.1% over the year, against estimates of 0.3% and 2.9%.

Good news on jobs was not good news for rate-sensitive stocks. CNBC said traders lifted the probability of another quarter-point Fed hike at the December 8-9 meeting to 76.8%, per CME Group's FedWatch tool. [A companion CNBC piece](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/06/treasury-yields-inch-lower-as-investors-look-ahead-to-key-jobs-data-.html) said that was up from more than 66% the day before, and that the 10-year Treasury yield rose more than 2 basis points to 4.864%.

So the tape now has two forces pulling in opposite directions. A strong labor market supports earnings, but it also keeps the Fed on a tightening path, which pressures the long-duration growth names that dominate many breakout watchlists.

## What the rebound did and did not repair

The Nasdaq's 1.09% Friday gain recovered a fraction of Thursday's 5.5% loss. Working backward from Friday's close and percentage change, Thursday's Nasdaq close was roughly 24,300 and the S&P 500's roughly 7,350. Those are my own back-calculations, not reported figures, but they give two reference points for whether Friday's bounce can hold. A rebound that stalls at or below Thursday's close would look different from one that pushes through it.

Breadth did not confirm the bounce. The Closing Print's summary counted 184 S&P 500 stocks up and 318 down on Friday, even as the index gained. That is one source's tally and I could not verify it against an exchange feed, but it fits the pattern of a rebound led by a handful of chip and mega-cap names rather than a broad return of buyers. The same summary showed the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index up 4.2% on the day, which is the kind of concentrated bounce that tends to look better in the index than in the median stock.

For breakout traders, the practical read is about odds, not direction:

- **Failure rates rise after a correction-sized shock.** When the leading index breaks 10% from its high, new pivots in leading groups have less overhead support and more supply to absorb.
- **Defensive and non-AI bases are not immune.** With every sector down Thursday, a stock that held its base is the exception worth studying.
- **Volume tells the story of any recovery.** I did not find a reliable volume figure for Friday, so judge it on your own platform rather than the headline index move.

## What to watch next

**Does the bounce hold Thursday's close?** The Nasdaq and S&P 500 closes noted above are the nearest reference points. Reclaiming them on rising volume would be a first sign of repair; a failure would put Thursday's low back in play.

**Does breadth improve?** Advancers outnumbering decliners on an up day, and new highs expanding instead of contracting, would show buying beyond the mega-cap chip names.

**How the rate market settles.** With a December hike priced at roughly three in four, according to CME FedWatch, any shift in that probability will move growth stocks. The next Fed decision lands December 9.

**Whether the data center debate escalates.** The selloff was tied to political news. Further statements on the buildout, or on how it would be curbed, could matter for AI hardware leaders more than the next earnings report.

The week left the market in an unusual spot: a correction in the Nasdaq, a hawkish Fed backdrop and a labor market that refuses to soften. For those who wait for bases to tighten before acting, that mix argues for patience and smaller expectations about how many setups will work. This is commentary, not advice, and none of the figures above should be read as a recommendation to buy or sell anything.
