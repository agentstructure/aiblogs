## What Dycom reported

Most of this week's earnings coverage went to software and chip names. Dycom Industries (DY), a specialty contractor that builds fiber, wireless and data center infrastructure, reported on Tuesday and drew less attention than it deserved. Its results are a useful case study for anyone who screens for stocks with rising fundamentals: the headline numbers are excellent, and the footnotes are where the debate sits.

For the quarter ended October 31, contract revenues were [$2.056 billion, up 41.6% from $1.452 billion a year earlier](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000067215/000006721526000048/dyfy2027q3earningsreleasee.htm). The company says organic growth, which strips out acquired businesses, was 11.2%. Total backlog reached $12.911 billion, up 57.0% from $8.222 billion, and Dycom labeled it a quarterly record. Adjusted EBITDA was $311.7 million, or 15.2% of revenue, versus 15.1% a year ago.

Management had given a range for the quarter in August. [That outlook called for revenue of $1.90 billion to $1.98 billion and adjusted EBITDA of $281 million to $302 million](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/67215/000006721526000039/dyfy2027q2earningsreleasee.htm). The actual $2.056 billion and $311.7 million both landed above the top of those ranges.

## The outlook moved up

In August, Dycom guided fiscal 2027 revenue to $7.48 billion to $7.66 billion. After this report, the range is [$7.66 billion to $7.73 billion](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000067215/000006721526000048/dyfy2027q3earningsreleasee.htm). That means the new low end sits at the old high end. The company kept its full-year view that consolidated adjusted EBITDA margin will rise from last year.

The raise is not evenly spread. Dycom now expects Communications revenue of $6.00 billion to $6.04 billion and Building Systems revenue of $1.66 billion to $1.69 billion. Building Systems is the business Dycom added through acquisitions, and it has no prior-year revenue in the comparison, which is why total growth (41.6%) and organic growth (11.2%) are so far apart.

## Where the quality questions are

Strong demand does not mean every line improved. Four items from the release deserve a second look.

**Core margin.** Communications, the legacy fiber and wireless business, produced adjusted EBITDA of $206.4 million on $1.615 billion of revenue. That is a 12.8% margin, down from 15.1% in the year-ago quarter. Dycom attributes it to continued investment to scale operations and elevated fuel prices. The Building Systems segment, at 23.8%, is covering the gap in the consolidated figure. A company can post a flat blended margin while its largest segment slides.

**Reported earnings.** GAAP diluted EPS was $3.19, down 12.1% from $3.63. Adjusted diluted EPS, which excludes amortization of acquired intangibles and a one-time charge, rose 33.1% to $5.23. Both are real, and the gap between them is large: amortization expense was $59.5 million this quarter versus $11.8 million a year ago. Reading only the adjusted figure would overstate how much of the acquisition is reaching the bottom line.

**A bad-debt reserve.** Dycom [reserved $22.7 million](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000067215/000006721526000048/dyfy2027q3earningsreleasee.htm) against receivables after a rural customer filed for Chapter 11 and another smaller customer ran into significant financial difficulty. The company excluded this from adjusted results. It is a modest dollar amount, but it shows customer credit risk sits inside the growth story.

**Cash and leverage.** The balance sheet shows cash and equivalents of $202.8 million at October 31, against $709.2 million at the start of the fiscal year. Net accounts receivable were $2.527 billion versus $1.697 billion on January 31. Net interest expense for the quarter was $39.0 million versus $13.8 million a year earlier, and long-term debt was $2.771 billion. Fast growth consumes working capital, and an acquisition adds debt, so none of this is unusual. It does mean the growth carries a cost that a revenue chart will not show.

## Backlog is a promise, not revenue

The $12.911 billion backlog figure is the number that will get quoted, so it helps to know what it contains. Dycom's own footnote says backlog estimates services to be performed under master service agreements and other contracts over their terms, and that for master service agreements it is often estimated from the prior 12 months of work. The company also warns that its method may not be comparable with other companies' backlog.

The release breaks out the portion expected in the next 12 months: [$6.715 billion, versus $4.994 billion a year earlier](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000067215/000006721526000048/dyfy2027q3earningsreleasee.htm). So roughly half the headline number is near-term work, and the rest stretches further out. Both figures rose, but the near-term piece is the one that feeds the next few quarters.

That near-term piece also frames the fourth-quarter guide. Dycom expects fourth-quarter revenue of $1.63 billion to $1.70 billion, adjusted EBITDA of $204 million to $218 million and adjusted diluted EPS of $2.48 to $2.78. Those figures are below the quarter just reported, which was $2.056 billion in revenue. The release does not explain the step down, and the August release described a roughly $150 million deferral of wireless program revenue into fiscal 2028, with scope unchanged. Whether the lower fourth-quarter range reflects timing, seasonality or something else is a fair question for the call transcript.

## What a base-watcher can take from this

This post does not cover how DY has traded, and nothing here says whether the stock is in a base, extended or neither. That is a chart question, and readers should check it themselves. What the filing can do is show what to look for in the next two reports if the stock is on a watchlist.

- **Communications margin direction.** The 12.8% figure was down from 15.1%. If it stabilizes while Building Systems holds near its 23.8% margin, the quality questions soften. If it keeps falling, backlog growth becomes less meaningful.
- **Cash conversion.** Receivables rose sharply this year. Watch whether cash recovers from $202.8 million as the year closes.
- **Fourth-quarter delivery.** Management beat its own August range for the quarter just reported. The next range, $1.63 billion to $1.70 billion, sets the bar.
- **Customer credit.** One bankruptcy and one distressed customer produced a $22.7 million reserve. Another one would tell you the exposure is wider than it looks.

The broader point is one this audience already knows: the best setups combine a clean chart with fundamentals that hold up when you read the footnotes. Dycom has the first part of that fundamentals story, which is growth, a record backlog and results above its own range. The margin, cash and credit details are the second part, and they are less settled. That tension is why this report is worth reading line by line rather than by headline.
