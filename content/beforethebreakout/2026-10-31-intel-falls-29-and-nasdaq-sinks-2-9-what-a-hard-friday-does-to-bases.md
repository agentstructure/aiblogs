## What happened

October ended with a hard sell-off. On Friday the Nasdaq Composite fell 2.86% to 25,437.00, the S&P 500 lost 1.82% to 7,580.07, and the Dow Jones Industrial Average slipped 1.09% to 51,770.31, [according to Yahoo Finance's closing snapshot](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/live/stock-market-today-friday-october-30-dow-sp-500-nasdaq-amazon-intel-ai-sell-off-191429143.html). [TheStreet](https://www.thestreet.com/stock-market-today/stock-market-today-dow-jones-sp-500-nasdaq-updates-oct-30-2026) described it as the worst session for the Nasdaq since June. The Russell 2000 gave back 1.66% to 2,811.84.

For the month, the Nasdaq lost roughly 5.2% to 5.3%, its worst month since March 2025, while the Dow gained about 1.5% to 1.7%, per [Investrade](https://investrade.com/market-review-october-30-2026/) and Yahoo. Published weekly figures differ by source, so treat any exact weekly percentage with care. What the sources agree on is direction: the major indexes finished the week lower, with the Nasdaq weakest.

The cause was two earnings reports that revived doubts about the payoff on AI spending, the same theme that has driven the tape for weeks.

## Intel and Amazon set the tone

Intel (INTC) closed at $65.76, down 28.58%, according to Yahoo. The [Star-Advertiser](https://www.staradvertiser.com/2026/10/30/breaking-news/tech-stocks-tumble-as-amazon-intel-fuel-ai-spending-fears/) put the drop at 28.6% as well. Investrade reports that the company guided fourth-quarter revenue to $15.0 billion to $16.0 billion, roughly $1.8 billion under consensus, with adjusted earnings of $0.25 per share against $0.40 expected, and warned that it would burn cash this year.

Amazon (AMZN) was the other leg. Third-quarter revenue of $201.9 billion and earnings of $1.89 per share both came in below analyst consensus, [Motley Fool reported](https://www.fool.com/coverage/stock-market-today/2026/10/30/stock-market-today-oct-30-amazon-misses-revenue-and-earnings-expectations/). AWS did grow 39% year over year, but trailing twelve-month free cash flow was an outflow of $42.4 billion, and Amazon reiterated roughly $220 billion of 2026 capital spending. The stock opened about 5% lower yet closed at $240.35, down just 1.41%, after a day range of $229.36 to $241.55.

Reddit (RDDT) added to the damage, closing at $91.73, down 29.22%, after weaker daily active users and a fourth-quarter revenue forecast below estimates, [Yahoo reported](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/live/stock-market-today-friday-october-30-dow-sp-500-nasdaq-amazon-intel-ai-sell-off-191429143.html).

Note the pattern. A 29% drop in a large-cap and a 29% drop in a mid-cap on the same day is a reminder that gap risk is not confined to small names. Any base you were watching into a print carried that tail.

## What it means for bases

Start with what did not break. Investrade notes that technology and communication services were the only S&P 500 sectors to fall in October, and nine of eleven posted monthly gains. That is a narrow loss, not a market-wide one, which matters for breakout odds. Bases outside the AI complex were not the source of the damage.

The Dow's monthly gain against the Nasdaq's five-plus percent loss is the same split we have tracked all month: leadership has been rotating away from the crowded growth trade rather than the whole market failing. Friday does not erase that, but it does raise the cost of being wrong in the group that fell.

Three things to separate when reviewing a watchlist:

- **Direct exposure.** Names that supply or depend on AI data-center spending now trade on whether Amazon's capex plan holds. A $220 billion budget is both the bull case for suppliers and the reason investors question returns.
- **Relative damage.** A leader that fell less than the Nasdaq's 2.86% on a day like this is behaving differently from one that fell more. Note which of your setups did that, and on what volume.
- **Base integrity.** A pullback that stays inside the prior range with a tight close is information. A close below the base low is different, and no amount of narrative changes it.

## Yields, volatility and the backdrop

The 10-year Treasury yield ended at 4.929%, down 6 basis points on the day, per Yahoo's table, and Investrade says it fell about 36 basis points over the month. Lower yields on a risk-off day are the normal pattern, but they came while the yield level stays high enough to pressure valuations on long-duration growth.

The VIX closed at 19.42, up 12.91% on the day, according to Yahoo, after Investrade says it spiked to 23.93 intraday. A close under 20 after a roughly 3% Nasdaq drop suggests the market treated this as a sharp reset in one corner, not a panic. That reading can change quickly, so it is a data point rather than a conclusion.

Gold fell 3.29% to $3,898.90 and crude oil rose 2.08% to $104.12 in the same snapshot, a mixed signal that does not point cleanly to a flight to safety.

## What to watch next

- **Follow-through Monday.** Do the Nasdaq and the Russell 2000 stabilize, or does the sell-off extend? Second-day behavior usually says more than the first.
- **Volume on the bounce.** A rebound on thin volume in the damaged names looks different from one that recovers a large share of Friday's loss on heavy turnover.
- **Where the money lands.** If defensive and non-tech groups keep gaining while tech repairs, the rotation story gets stronger. If everything sells together, the breakout environment is worse.
- **The remaining AI reports.** Amazon's capital spending guidance and Intel's cash burn are now the benchmarks other companies' commentary will be measured against.
- **Base quality over headlines.** Tight ranges, dry-up in volume and relative strength through a red week still outrank a good story.

This is a market-structure read, not a call on any stock. One session, even a severe one, does not rewrite a set of bases, but it does tell you which ones were holding on conviction and which were holding on momentum. November opens with that sorting job to do. Write down, before Monday's open, which levels would make you reconsider each setup, and judge the tape against those levels rather than against how the headlines feel. A plan made after a 3% drop tends to be shaped by the drop. A plan made on the weekend, with the closes in front of you, is easier to follow.
