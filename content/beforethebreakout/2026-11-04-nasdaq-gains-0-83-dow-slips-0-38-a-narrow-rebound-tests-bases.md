## What happened

Tuesday's close looked like relief on the surface and mixed underneath. According to [Motley Fool's market close](https://www.fool.com/coverage/stock-market-today/2026/11/03/stock-market-today-nov-3-markets-edge-higher-as-semiconductor-stocks-rebound/), the S&P 500 rose 0.36% to 7,568.92, the Nasdaq Composite gained 0.83% to 25,372.01, and the Dow Jones Industrial Average fell 0.38% to 51,786.72. [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/02/stock-market-today-live-updates.html) reports the S&P 500 ended a three-day losing streak, and that Apple (AAPL) weighed on the Dow after a weak margin forecast and iPad sales that missed estimates.

That split, with the Nasdaq up and the Dow down, is a clue about where the buying came from. Semiconductors led. [Capp Notes](https://cappnotes.substack.com/p/closing-look-tuesday-november-3-2026) puts the PHLX Semiconductor Index up 2.63%, with Broadcom (AVGO) up 3.07%, Micron (MU) up 2.58% and Nvidia (NVDA) up 2.29%. The [Stock Titan close](https://www.stocktitan.net/articles/stock-market-today-live-november-3-2026) shows the same direction, with AMD (AMD) up more than 3% ahead of its earnings report, and Broadcom, Micron and Nvidia in the same range.

I could not find a second source for the 2.63% semiconductor index figure, so treat it as approximate. The direction, a clear chip bounce, is confirmed by CNBC and Stock Titan.

## The rotation underneath the bounce

For a sector-rotation check, the useful part is what fell. Capp Notes lists closing sector changes: Technology +1.31%, Communication Services +1.18% and Industrials +0.95% led, while Consumer Staples (-2.07%) and Materials (-2.01%) were weakest. Energy lost 0.85% and Consumer Discretionary 0.70%. CNBC's afternoon read agrees on the shape: information technology up about 1.4%, six of 11 sectors down, materials down about 1.5% and staples down about 1.3% at that point.

Only one source gave me the closing sector table, so the exact percentages carry that caveat. What two sources agree on is the ordering, tech and industrials up, staples and materials down.

Two things stand out for anyone watching group leadership.

First, defensive groups did not catch a bid. When staples fall about 2% on a day the index rises, money is not hiding there. It is moving toward the beaten-up growth names that fell hard last week, which is a rebound pattern more than a new-leadership pattern.

Second, energy slipped even with oil still elevated. CNBC reports Brent settled down 1.12% at $103.60 a barrel and West Texas Intermediate down 0.38% at $96.09, on speculation about progress in U.S.-Iran negotiations. Energy bases that were trending on crude's strength have to absorb that headline sensitivity.

## Breadth did not confirm the headline

The index gain hides a weaker internal picture. Per Capp Notes, 551 stocks advanced against 941 decliners across the S&P 500, S&P 400, S&P 600 and Nasdaq-100 members, roughly 1.7 to 1 on the downside. The equal-weight S&P 500 fell 0.33%, trailing the cap-weighted index by about seven-tenths of a point. CNBC's intraday count of S&P 500 stocks showed the same lean, 291 decliners to 211 advancers.

This is the pattern base-builders should recognize. A cap-weighted index can rise on a handful of heavyweights while the typical stock slips. For a breakout trader, the question is not whether the S&P 500 had an up day. It is whether stocks inside constructive bases saw volume expand on the way up, or whether the move was concentrated in names that were already extended or already broken.

CNBC also notes the Nasdaq-100 rose 0.7% to 28,180.26 but remains in correction territory, about 10% below its recent high. A stock trying to form a base while its benchmark is in correction is working against the current. Bases that tighten and hold their lows while the index is still repairing are the ones to flag. Bases that merely bounce with the index are not telling you much yet.

## The deal behind the chip noise

Chips also had company news to digest. In a [filing with the SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001633978/000119312526442758/d163169dex991.htm), Marvell Technology (MRVL) and Lumentum (LITE) announced on November 2 a definitive agreement for Marvell to acquire Lumentum in an all-stock transaction. The release puts the implied enterprise value at $115 billion, or roughly $1,132 per Lumentum share, a premium of about 23.9% to unaffected closing prices as of October 28. Lumentum holders would receive 4.500 Marvell shares per share and the two sides expect to close in the first half of 2028.

CNBC reports Lumentum fell more than 18% on Monday after the announcement. That is an unusual reaction for an acquisition target, and it matters here for a structural reason: the deal is all-stock with a fixed exchange ratio, so Lumentum's price is tied to how Marvell trades, not to a cash price. Marvell's own chart is therefore the one that matters for both names. Deal headlines can break a base in one session regardless of how clean the pattern looked beforehand.

## Yields and the calendar

Rates remain a headwind for any growth-heavy base. Capp Notes has the 10-year Treasury yield up 4 basis points to 4.95%, the 30-year up 4 basis points to 5.32% and the 2-year up 2 basis points to 4.61%. Motley Fool puts the 10-year at 4.97% late in the day. The sources differ by a couple of basis points, which is normal across data feeds, but both put the 10-year just below 5%.

The calendar adds more variability. Voting for the U.S. midterm elections took place Tuesday, and CNBC says prediction markets put the odds of Democrats winning the Senate near 75%, with some strategists flagging that a Democratic sweep could weigh on AI names if it is not already priced in. Others, CNBC notes, argue Republican control could bring short-term gains. Nobody knows how that resolves, so expect the possibility of a gap in either direction.

Several large reports also landed after the close. Motley Fool notes AMD jumped more than 9% in after-hours trading and Super Micro Computer surged after an earnings beat, and Stock Titan shows Arista Networks (ANET) higher and Skyworks (SWKS) sharply lower in extended trading. After-hours prints are thin and often reverse by the open, so they are context, not confirmation.

## What to watch next

- **Whether the chip bounce has follow-through.** One 2.6% day in semiconductors after a hard stretch is a rebound. A second and third session with rising volume and higher lows is the first evidence of a base.
- **Equal-weight versus cap-weight.** If the equal-weight S&P 500 keeps lagging, the rally is narrow. Closing that gap would be the better breadth signal.
- **Defensive groups.** Staples and materials falling about 2% is the weakest reading on the board. Watch whether they stabilize or whether the selling extends, because that tells you whether rotation is out of defense or out of the market.
- **Event gaps.** Earnings reactions and any election-driven gap can invalidate a tight range overnight. Where a base's pivot and stop levels sit relative to a likely gap matters more than the pattern's neatness.
- **The 10-year near 5%.** A move through it would pressure long-duration growth names first.

Tuesday gave chips a bounce and everyone else a mixed tape. This is analysis of market conditions, not advice to buy or sell any security.
