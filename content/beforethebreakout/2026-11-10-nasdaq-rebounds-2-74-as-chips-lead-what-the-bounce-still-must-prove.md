## What happened

Monday gave bulls the kind of session they had been waiting a week for. The Nasdaq Composite gained 2.74% to 25,253.14, the S&P 500 rose 2.35% to 7,549.94 and the Dow added 2.15% to 52,389.75, according to [TradingKey's close report](https://www.tradingkey.com/analysis/stocks/us-stocks/262297090-us-stock-nasdaq-dow-rise-anthropic-ipo-nvidia-spacex-tesla-tradingkey). [Yahoo Finance's live coverage](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/live/stock-market-today-monday-november-9-dow-sp-500-nasdaq-nvidia-090535419.html) put the same three moves at roughly 2.2%, 2.3% and 2.7%, and listed lower energy prices and a renewed appetite for AI stocks as the drivers. It also noted that all three indexes had fallen the week before.

Chips did the heavy lifting. The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index (SOX) rose 6.85% to 10,033.88, with 29 of its 30 components advancing, TradingKey reported. Advanced Micro Devices (AMD) gained 13.26%, Applied Materials (AMAT) 11.37%, Lam Research (LRCX) 10.41% and Broadcom (AVGO) 8.78%. Arm Holdings (ARM), Texas Instruments (TXN), Micron (MU), ASML and Intel (INTC) each rose between roughly 5.5% and 6.7%.

The exception was Nvidia (NVDA), which fell 4.27% to $178.67. TradingKey tied the drop to news about CEO Jensen Huang that led the board to open an independent review; the company said Huang remains CEO. Bernstein called it a "key-person risk," per the same report.

## Put the bounce in context

A 6.85% day looks large until you place it next to the drop that came before it. According to a [24/7 Wall St. report republished by AlphaMaven](https://alpha-maven.com/news/hedge-fund/chip-stocks-just-had-their-second-worst-day-on-record), the SOX fell 12.25% on Thursday, Nov. 5, from 10,268.65 to 9,011.14. That article cites a Reuters columnist calling it the second-largest one-day decline on record. I could not open the original Reuters piece, so treat the "second-largest" ranking as relayed.

The arithmetic is worth doing yourself. From 9,011.14 to 10,033.88 is a gain of about 11.4% over two sessions (my calculation, using the figures above). But 10,033.88 is still about 2.3% below Wednesday's close of 10,268.65. Put differently, a 12.25% loss needs a gain of roughly 14% to fully repair, and the index has recovered most of that distance but not all of it.

That matters for anyone watching chip bases. Stocks that fell 10% or more in a day rarely rebuild a clean, tight pattern in forty-eight hours. A V-shaped retracement can look strong while leaving the underlying structure damaged: a wide, loose range with a big down bar in the middle of it. The practical question is whether the next several sessions produce narrow ranges and orderly pullbacks, or another round of wide, violent bars.

## Friday's tell: the bounce was narrow

The most useful detail from last week came from the Friday session. [CoinCentral's recap](https://coincentral.com/?p=216687) reported that the ProShares S&P 500 Ex-Technology ETF ended Friday flat, while the Nasdaq gained 1.1% to 24,579.42. eToro's Eto Lale Akoner said the rebound suggested retail investors were buying the dip in tech, but that she took "a very cautious view" because the buying did not spread beyond the sector.

Monday looked broader at the index level, since the Dow and S&P 500 both gained more than 2%. But index gains can flatter a tape when a handful of heavyweights lead. I could not find a sourced count of advancers and decliners across the whole market for Monday, so I am not claiming broad participation. The only breadth figure I can verify is inside the semiconductor group itself, where 29 of 30 SOX names rose.

Cross-check this on your own screens. A healthy rebound shows up in the equal-weight indexes, small caps and sectors outside technology, not only in the largest chip names.

## Relative strength: who bounced hardest, and who didn't

Relative strength is where Monday gets interesting. [CoinCentral](https://coincentral.com/?p=216687) reported that AMD jumped 10.8% on Friday. Add Monday's 13.26% and the stock compounded to a gain of roughly 25% in two sessions (my calculation). Yahoo Finance attributed Monday's move to an Oppenheimer upgrade to Buy; I could not find a second source for that, so it is attributed rather than confirmed. Whatever the cause, a stock that gains that much that fast is extended from any base it was building, and the useful levels are the prior highs and the gap, not chasing the candle.

Nvidia is the opposite case. It rose 3.9% on Friday, per CoinCentral, then reversed on Monday as the one major chip name to decline. Yahoo noted that the stock had trimmed early losses of about 8% to close down about 4%. When the group rallies hard and its largest member falls on company-specific news, relative strength rankings reshuffle. Names that held up on the way down and rose on the way back up gain standing against the index. Names that bounced only because the whole group bounced do not.

Two questions worth keeping in a notebook for each chip stock on your list:

- Did it close back above the midpoint of Thursday's range, or only recover part of it?
- Did Monday's volume expand relative to its recent average? None of the reports I reviewed gave volume figures for individual stocks, so this one needs your own charts.

## The macro backdrop that keeps this fragile

Chips were not the only moving part. Brent crude was down to roughly $91 to $94 a barrel, according to Yahoo, after falling below $100 on Friday per CoinCentral. Lower oil helps the inflation story, which is why Tuesday's October CPI report is the next test. Bloomberg's survey of economists, cited by Yahoo, called for headline CPI to rise 0.3% on the month, down from 0.6% in September, and for core to rise 0.2%. Check the actual release and how the index futures react before drawing conclusions from this piece.

Rates remain a live risk. TradingKey noted that Richmond Fed President Thomas Barkin signaled the possibility of further rate hikes. The jobs report added to that tension: CoinCentral said the Labor Department reported 165,000 October jobs against an expectation of 65,000, with September revised to 60,000. Strong hiring is good for earnings but makes the case for easier policy harder.

## What to watch next

This is commentary, not a recommendation, but here is the checklist a swing trader might use to separate a recovery from a relief bounce:

- **Retests hold.** After a shock day, the first pullback toward the gap or the prior day's close is informative. Higher lows on lighter volume are constructive. A close back under Friday's levels is not.
- **Breadth confirms.** Look for equal-weight and small-cap indexes participating, and for non-tech sectors rising alongside the chips.
- **Leadership persists.** If the same names lead for several sessions, that is relative strength. If leadership rotates every day, the tape is still trading headlines.
- **The gap to Wednesday closes.** The SOX needs roughly another 2.3% to return to its pre-rout close. Reclaiming that level on firm volume would say more than the 6.85% print did.
- **The Nvidia story stays contained.** Yahoo reported that the broader market largely shrugged off the news. If the group's largest member keeps lagging, watch whether suppliers and peers follow.

One day of green does not rebuild a base. It does tell you where the buyers showed up, and who they skipped. The next few sessions will show whether they come back.
