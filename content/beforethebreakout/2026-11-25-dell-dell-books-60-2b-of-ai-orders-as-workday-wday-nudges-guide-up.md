## Two reports, one night, two different stories

Dell Technologies (DELL) and Workday (WDAY) both reported after the close on Tuesday, Nov. 24, and the two releases read like a rotation chart in prose. One company is sitting on a record pile of AI hardware orders. The other beat its own targets but is guiding to slower growth. For traders who watch which groups are leading and which names are tightening up inside them, the pairing is more useful than either report alone.

This post sticks to what the companies themselves published. It does not cover how either stock traded afterward, because that reaction is the real test and it had not happened when the releases landed. The goal is to lay out what the numbers say, so you know what the charts need to confirm.

## Dell: the AI backlog keeps compounding

Dell's [third-quarter release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001571996/000157199626000050/exhibit991earnings8kq3fy27.htm) for the quarter ended Oct. 30 shows revenue of $54.9 billion, a record, up 103% from a year earlier. Diluted EPS was $8.82, up 287%, and non-GAAP diluted EPS was $9.45, up 265%.

The AI figures carry the story. Dell said it booked $60.2 billion of AI orders in the quarter and ended with a record $132.3 billion AI backlog. Revenue from AI-optimized servers was $22.0 billion, up 291%. The Infrastructure Solutions Group, Dell's server and storage segment, reported revenue of $42.0 billion, up 198%, and operating income of $7.1 billion, up 308%.

Dell also raised its full-year outlook, and the prior numbers are in the same release:

- Full-year fiscal 2027 revenue guidance of $202.0 billion, up 78%, versus the previous $192.0 billion.
- Non-GAAP diluted EPS guidance of $29.68, versus the previous $25.50.
- AI-optimized server revenue guidance of $78.0 billion, up 216%, versus the previous $74.0 billion.

For the fourth quarter, Dell guided to revenue of $56.3 billion, up 69%, and non-GAAP diluted EPS of $8.40.

Two details deserve a closer read. First, the quarterly AI server revenue of $22.0 billion is a fraction of the $132.3 billion backlog, which tells you that most of the work is still ahead of Dell, not behind it. Second, the release is candid about what could go wrong. Its forward-looking statements list access to capital markets by Dell or its customers and counterparty default risk among the risks, and Jeff Clarke's quote says the orders were "each rigorously vetted for customer quality and access to capital, land and power." Management is flagging that financing and power are the constraints, not demand.

Dell also announced that its board approved an additional $25.0 billion share repurchase authorization, and said it returned a record $4.9 billion to shareholders in the quarter through buybacks and dividends.

## Workday: a beat, a small raise, and a slower slope

Workday's [third-quarter release](https://newsroom.workday.com/2026-11-24-Workday-Announces-Fiscal-2027-Third-Quarter-Financial-Results) tells a steadier but slower story. Total revenue was $2.705 billion, up 11.2%. Subscription revenue was $2.532 billion, up 12.8%. Non-GAAP operating margin was 31.4%, versus 28.5% a year earlier, and non-GAAP diluted EPS was $2.82, versus $2.32.

Against its own targets, the quarter was clean. In its [second-quarter release](https://investor.workday.com/news-and-events/press-releases/news-details/2026/Workday-Announces-Fiscal-2027-Second-Quarter-Financial-Results/default.aspx), Workday guided third-quarter subscription revenue to $2.515 billion, growth of 12%, with a non-GAAP operating margin of 30.0%. The reported figures of $2.532 billion, 12.8% and 31.4% cleared all three.

The full-year guide moved up, but only slightly. Workday's second-quarter release called for fiscal 2027 subscription revenue of $9.940 billion to $9.950 billion. The new guidance is $9.958 billion, growth of 13%, with a non-GAAP operating margin of 31.5% versus 31.0% before. That is a raise above the top of the prior range, though a narrow one.

The slope is what a base-builder should notice. Second-quarter subscription revenue growth was 13.9%. The third quarter came in at 12.8%. Workday's fourth-quarter guidance calls for subscription revenue of $2.600 billion, growth of 10%. A company can beat and raise while the growth rate steps down, and the market tends to price the direction of the rate of change as much as the beat.

Workday's argument for acceleration is AI. CEO Aneel Bhusri said AI products drove nearly $150 million in new annual contract value, more than three times what the company delivered a year ago, and the release says more than 2,200 core customers have purchased at least one AI product, up nearly 25% from last quarter. Workday also repurchased about 3.8 million Class A shares for $750 million and recorded $51 million of restructuring expenses in the quarter. Free cash flow was $691 million, versus $550 million a year earlier.

## What the split says about rotation

The two reports describe two different positions on the same AI cycle. Dell is a picks-and-shovels story with an order book large enough that its own fourth-quarter guidance implies growth of 69%. Workday is an application-layer story, where AI is a product line growing quickly from a small base inside a business growing in the low double digits and guiding lower.

That does not make one a winner and the other a loser. It does set different expectations for how each chart should behave. A stock whose numbers are accelerating can absorb a pullback and still hold a base. A stock whose growth rate is stepping down has less room for error, and a flat, tight range there can be a pause rather than coiling.

## What to watch next

A few checks will help separate signal from noise over the next several sessions:

- **Volume on the reaction day.** A gap on earnings that holds on heavy volume carries more weight than one that fades by the close. Look at where each stock finishes relative to its gap, not where it opens.
- **Peers in the same group.** If Dell's results lift other AI hardware and server names that have been basing, that is group strength. If only Dell moves, it is a single-stock event.
- **Software behavior.** If Workday's results pull other application software names higher, rotation into the group is gaining traction. If they trade apart from it, the slower growth rate is still the dominant story.
- **Financing and power commentary.** Dell's own risk language puts customer access to capital and power at the center. Any change in tone from large AI customers is the first place a crack would appear.
- **Fourth-quarter prints.** Dell has guided to $56.3 billion in revenue and Workday to $2.600 billion in subscription revenue. Both give you a yardstick for the next report.

This is information and commentary, not a recommendation. The numbers above come directly from company releases, and the price action that follows is the only evidence that matters for whether a setup is working.
