## The scanner says one thing, the tape says another

Every swing trader has a volume scanner open. The appeal is obvious: a base that finally attracts heavy buying is often how a breakout announces itself. But Monday's list is a useful reminder that raw relative volume (RVOL) is a starting filter, not a signal.

[Tapeboard's unusual-volume scanner](https://tapeboard.com/movers/unusual-volume), as of October 12 and delayed 15 minutes, listed 20 tickers. Seventeen of them traded under $5. Nine were under $2. Euro Tech (CLWT) printed an RVOL above 12,500x on 70.9 million shares at $1.85, up 36.0%, on news of a planned joint venture. MNDR was up 190.6% on 191.6 million shares, and the page's own summary notes major dilution. FCHL rose 95.7% while the page cites a class action alleging a pump-and-dump scheme and a Nasdaq deficiency notice.

None of that looks like a stock quietly building a base. These are event-driven spikes in thinly priced shares, where volume is a symptom of a headline rather than evidence of accumulation by larger holders. The page also warns that its AI summaries sometimes disagree with its table on price change, which is another reason to confirm on a live chart before treating any line as fact.

## The one name that fit the profile

Only three tickers on the list traded above $5: CTNM at $8.84, SKK at $5.34, and TRAX at $33.11. CTNM was down 33.9% on 9.8x volume, so volume there marked a breakdown, not a setup.

TRAX is the more interesting read. It rose 4.0% on 7.3 million shares, an RVOL of 9.6x, after positive Phase 1b celiac disease results for ANB033, according to the scanner. A modest gain on very heavy volume after a catalyst is a different pattern from a 100% spike: it can mean the market absorbed news without a blow-off. Whether it matters depends on what happens next. Does it hold the day's range on lighter volume over the following sessions, or give it all back? That is the question a base-watcher asks, and one day cannot answer it.

## Where relative strength actually showed up

Look at the sectors instead. In [Tapeboard's October 12 close](https://tapeboard.com/blog/market-pulse-2026-10-12), the S&P 500 finished at 7,807.24, down 0.06%. The Nasdaq Composite fell 0.44% to 27,245.90, the Russell 2000 slipped 0.45% to 2,794.48, and the Dow gained 0.38% to 51,850.55. The page called it a defensive rotation.

Beneath that, Real Estate (XLRE) rose 2.62%, Energy (XLE) 1.43% and Consumer Staples (XLP) 1.17%, while Technology (XLK) fell 1.22%. Year to date, XLE is up 44.60% and XLK 36.08%, against 13.83% for the S&P 500. Financials (XLF), at +0.22% year to date, and Consumer Discretionary (XLY), at -5.14%, are the laggards in that table.

The pattern matters because it is consistent. On [October 8](https://tapeboard.com/blog/market-pulse-2026-10-08), XLE rose 2.34% and XLP 1.98% while XLK dropped 2.09%, with WTI crude at $91.07. By October 12, WTI was $93.31. Energy was among the top sectors on both days, and crude was higher on both. Leadership that repeats across sessions, rather than appearing once, is what separates a rotation from a one-day bounce.

## Reading leadership without chasing it

For a trader hunting pre-breakout setups, the sector table is a better first screen than the volume list. Stocks in the strongest groups that are holding tight ranges on declining volume, then showing a volume uptick near the top of the base, have the group working for them. A stock with a great chart in a group that is rolling over faces a headwind.

Monday also showed the other side. Five hardware and networking names fell about 10% together: Cerebras Systems (CBRS) fell 10.69% to $146.62, Semtech (SMTC) 10.15% to $168.28, Ciena (CIEN) 9.95% to $405.01, Fastly (FSLY) 9.80% to $26.43 and Lumentum (LITE) 9.78% to $995.41. When a whole complex sells off on the same day, individual bases in that group deserve extra scrutiny, because the selling is about the group and not each company.

A few practical filters follow from this:

- Price and liquidity first. A screen that excludes sub-$5 shares removes most of Monday's list and the dilution risk that comes with it.
- Ask what caused the volume. News-driven spikes are a different animal from quiet accumulation.
- Compare the stock to its group and the index. Strength that holds on a down day for the Nasdaq says more than a gain on an up day.
- Check the close. Heavy volume with a weak close is distribution, whatever the headline said.

## What to watch next

The broader tape is not weak. The S&P 500 closed Friday at 7,811.54, up 1.2% for the week, [according to the October 9 market wrap](https://www.lanacion.com.ar/usa/how-major-us-stock-indexes-fared-friday-1092026-nid09102026/), and finished Monday only about 0.06% lower. But the Russell 2000 fell 0.9% last week and another 0.45% Monday, so small-cap participation remains thin.

Tapeboard flagged two items for October 13: the five hardware losers and energy's leadership with WTI above $93. If the semiconductor and networking weakness stops, those bases can be re-examined. If it spreads, relative strength will keep migrating toward defensive groups and away from the year's leaders.

The 10-year Treasury yield at 5.24% is also worth keeping in view, since it was 5.23% on October 8 and has not given growth stocks much relief.

The takeaway is a habit rather than a trade. Before acting on any volume alert, write down the catalyst, the price level, the group's direction and where the stock closed relative to its range. If you cannot fill in all four lines, the alert has not yet earned a place on the watchlist.

None of this is a call on any stock. It is a way to sort a noisy scanner into names worth studying and names worth ignoring.
