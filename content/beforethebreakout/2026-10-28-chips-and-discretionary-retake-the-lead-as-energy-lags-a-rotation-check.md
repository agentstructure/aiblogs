## What the tape did

Tuesday reversed a good part of the pattern traders have been working with for weeks. Technology led the S&P 500 for a second straight session, with consumer discretionary and materials close behind. Energy and consumer staples sat at the bottom.

The index moves were solid rather than dramatic. According to [Investrade's closing recap](https://investrade.com/market-review-october-27-2026/), the S&P 500 gained 0.60% to 7,722, the Nasdaq rose 1.18%, and the Russell 2000 added 1.16% to 2,818. The Dow was close to flat, up 0.03%. [Yahoo Finance's live coverage](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/live/stock-market-today-tuesday-october-27-dow-sp-500-nasdaq-tech-sector-160557664.html) described the same shape: the Dow barely above the flat line while the Nasdaq outran the S&P 500.

The sector ranking, as tallied in [one Substack close summary](https://davidmeberhart.substack.com/p/scs-tuesday-market-close-technology), looked like this:

- Technology: +1.7%
- Consumer discretionary: +1.0%
- Materials: +0.8%
- Communication services and financials: -0.3% each
- Consumer staples: -0.9%
- Energy: -2.0%

Treat the exact percentages with some care, since that is a single secondhand tally and I could not find the full sector table on a primary data page. The direction is not in doubt. Investrade's recap also lists tech, industrials, discretionary and materials higher, real estate and staples lagging, and energy down more than 2%. The Motley Fool's midday report likewise put technology and basic materials on top, with consumer defensive and real estate names slipping.

## Chips are doing the heavy lifting

The same Substack summary shows the semiconductor ETF (SMH) up 3.0%. Yahoo Finance noted that chip stocks rallied again and that on Monday 57 of 60 stocks in a semiconductor basket rose. The Fool attributed the bid to investors returning to chipmakers and AI names ahead of several mega-cap earnings reports this week.

For a base-focused trader, two days of strength in a group is information, not confirmation. A group that has just bounced hard off weakness will contain many names that are extended from a low but still sit under their prior highs. The setups worth tracking are the ones where price has recovered into the upper half of a multi-week range on contracting daily ranges, rather than names that simply gapped up with the group.

The cleaner test comes in a few sessions. If chips hold most of this move on lighter volume, that is constructive. If they give it back on the first sign of trouble in Alphabet or Microsoft results, the bounce was a trade, not a turn.

## Energy gives back, and oil explains it

Energy was the weakest sector. Investrade shows WTI crude settled at $99.90 a barrel, down 1.83%, and Brent at $113.88, down 1.67%. Yahoo's coverage framed the sector move as following crude lower.

A 2% single-day slide matters mostly for how the group behaves next. Pullbacks that stop at a rising short-term average and then dry up in volume are normal inside an uptrend. A slide that breaks the lower end of a recent range on rising volume changes the group from leader to repair job. One day does not settle it, and with oil still near $100 the group is not broken by any standard that can be sourced here. Watch whether the individual names hold their last pivot lows while the sector ETF digests.

Consumer staples tell a similar story at smaller scale. Yahoo reported that staples were on track for their best month in nearly five years in October as semiconductors fell. That is a single source and I have not been able to confirm it independently, so read it as context rather than a data point. What can be said is that staples slipped on Tuesday as money moved back toward growth.

## Breadth says the rotation is not a broad advance

This is the part that keeps the day in perspective. The same Substack tally shows 58.8% of issues advancing against 38.0% declining, which is healthy for a single session. But the participation figures underneath are weak:

- 35.7% of stocks are above their 50-day moving average
- 39.3% are above their 200-day moving average
- 102 new 52-week highs against 284 new 52-week lows

New lows fell from 444 the day before and new highs fell from 139, so both columns shrank. Fewer stocks are making extremes in either direction, which fits a market that is waiting for a catalyst rather than trending. A tape where roughly six in ten stocks sit below their 50-day line does not offer an easy supply of clean bases. The ones that exist tend to be concentrated in whatever is leading, which is why the chip, discretionary and materials groups deserve the attention.

The Russell 2000 matters here too. Small caps rising 1.16% alongside the Nasdaq is a better sign than tech rallying alone, because it suggests some willingness to take risk outside the largest names. It is one session, and the group has been choppy, so the useful question is whether small caps hold the gain through the Fed meeting.

## What to watch next

The calendar is crowded. The Fed's two-day meeting concludes Wednesday, with a decision at 2:00 p.m. ET according to Yahoo Finance, and most market participants expect rates to be held. Alphabet and Microsoft report after the close the same day. Investrade's recap shows the 10-year Treasury yield at 4.992% and the 30-year at 5.304%, levels that keep any hawkish surprise relevant to long-duration growth names, which are exactly the groups that just led.

A practical checklist for the next few sessions:

1. **Does leadership persist?** Tech, discretionary and materials need to hold gains on pullbacks. A one-day reversal that fades by Thursday is a bounce.
2. **Does breadth improve?** Percent above the 50-day average climbing from the mid-30s toward 50 would show the rotation widening beyond a handful of groups.
3. **Do lows keep shrinking?** New 52-week lows falling from 444 to 284 is a start. Another leg down would suggest selling pressure is exhausting.
4. **How does energy behave near support?** Quiet, low-volume digestion is different from a break.
5. **Do earnings gaps hold?** Names that gap on results should be judged by whether they hold the gap day's low over the following week, as with any other base.

None of this is a signal to do anything. It is a map of where the tape is currently strong, where it is weak, and what evidence would change that. Tuesday handed the lead back to growth, but the underlying participation numbers say the market is still more selective than the headline index gains suggest. Bases form in that kind of tape, slowly, and the groups leading now are where to start looking for them.
