## Three catalysts, one trade

Most weeks, earnings, economic data and a big new listing compete for attention. This week they point at the same corner of the market. Nvidia (NVDA) reports its fiscal third quarter after the close on Tuesday. The Fed releases minutes from its October meeting on Wednesday. And Anthropic is expected to begin trading on Thursday, a deal that pulls several chip names into the story at once.

For traders watching bases in semiconductors and AI infrastructure, the useful question is not which way the headlines lean. It is what each event could do to the shape of a setup: whether a range keeps tightening, whether a gap holds, and whether volume confirms the move.

## Nvidia: the anchor of the week

Nvidia's [conference call is set for Tuesday, November 17 at 2 p.m. PT (5 p.m. ET)](https://investor.nvidia.com/news/press-release-details/2026/NVIDIA-Sets-Conference-Call-for-Third-Quarter-Financial-Results/), covering the quarter that ended October 25. The company says CFO Colette Kress's written commentary will be posted right after results come out, so the numbers and the guidance arrive before the call starts.

The stock arrives under pressure. [CNBC reports](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/13/stock-market-next-week-outlook-for-nov-16-20-2026.html) that Nvidia has dropped more than 30% in six weeks, wiping out roughly $2 trillion in market value, and fell about 13% in the latest week. That is a stock far below its highs, not one building a tight base near them. A reaction in either direction therefore has a different meaning than it would for a leader coiled near a pivot.

Analysts quoted in the coverage are focused on forward guidance more than the quarter itself. [Vital Knowledge, cited by Investing.com](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/nvidia-results-ahead-week-fed-093249400.html), says Nvidia will be under pressure to reiterate its projection of 70% sales growth for the next fiscal year, and warns that any post-earnings rally may meet selling. Consensus estimates for the quarter differ by a few cents across data providers, so check them against the official release rather than anchoring on one number.

Because Nvidia is a heavy weight in the major indexes and sets the tone for suppliers, customers and competitors, its reaction tends to spill into AMD (AMD), Broadcom (AVGO), Micron (MU) and Taiwan Semiconductor (TSM). Those are the names where a clean, low-volume pullback into support would be most informative.

## Fed minutes and retail sales: the rate backdrop

Two data points frame the rate backdrop. [October retail sales](https://www.heygotrade.com/en/news/weekly-economic-outlook-2026-11-16/) are due Tuesday at 8:30 a.m. ET, with a consensus of +0.2% month over month versus 0.8% in September, according to FXEmpire as cited by HeyGoTrade. A soft number would look like cooling demand; a firm one keeps rate pressure on.

On Wednesday at 2:00 p.m. ET, the Fed publishes [minutes from its October 27-28 meeting](https://www.heygotrade.com/en/news/weekly-economic-outlook-2026-11-16/). HeyGoTrade says investors are watching for signs of support for a December rate hike. Fed Chair Kevin Warsh also [moderates a panel on Monday](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/nvidia-results-ahead-week-fed-093249400.html), which Deutsche Bank analysts expect to center on AI, productivity and the labor market. Warsh has long avoided giving markets rate guidance, according to the same report, so the minutes may carry more signal than his remarks.

Why this matters for bases: growth stocks with long-dated earnings are the most sensitive to rate expectations. If the minutes read as hawkish, expect pressure on the same AI names that Nvidia's report is meant to rescue, and it can turn an otherwise valid earnings gap into a fade.

## Anthropic: a supply event for AI names

Anthropic [set its IPO range at $70 to $78 per share](https://www.rttnews.com/3712632/anthropic-sets-ipo-price-range-at-70-78-per-share-launches-roadshow.aspx) on November 10, according to RTTNews. The offering is 825 million Class A shares (801.35 million from the company and 23.65 million from selling stockholders), with an option for the underwriters to sell up to 123.75 million more. It is planned for the Nasdaq Global Select Market under the ticker ANT. A concurrent $26 billion private placement at the IPO price goes to AMD, Broadcom, Google, Micron, Microsoft, Nvidia and TSMC Partners.

At the stated share count and range, the base offering works out to roughly $57.8 billion to $64.4 billion (an estimate from the filing's figures, not a number the company reported). [CNBC reports](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/13/stock-market-next-week-outlook-for-nov-16-20-2026.html) that pricing is expected Wednesday and trading Thursday, with a valuation of about $1.2 trillion, down from $2 trillion earlier this fall. Those dates and the valuation are the outlet's reporting rather than something the company has confirmed, and they can change.

For a swing trader, a deal this size is a liquidity event. Capital raised in one place can come out of others, and the group of investors buying into the private placement overlaps with the chip stocks many traders are watching. Treat the first days of ANT trading as a read on risk appetite for AI, not as a signal about any single base.

## What to watch in the setups

**Nvidia's reaction day.** The first session after the report is the one to mark. A gap that holds on heavy volume is a different picture from a gap that closes by the afternoon. With the stock well below its highs, even a strong reaction mostly repairs damage; it does not by itself create a breakout.

**Peer behavior.** Check whether AMD, Broadcom and Micron move with Nvidia or ignore it. Names that hold their ranges on Tuesday and Wednesday while Nvidia swings are showing relative strength.

**Volume in the quiet days.** A tightening range on drying volume ahead of the catalysts is the classic setup. Expansion on Tuesday that fails to follow through on Wednesday's minutes suggests the move was event-driven.

**Thursday's listing.** If the IPO prices as planned, watch whether AI infrastructure names absorb the supply or sell off to make room.

Later in the week, other events add noise. Palo Alto Networks (PANW) reports after the close on Wednesday, and Intuit (INTU) reports Thursday after the close, according to [HeyGoTrade](https://www.heygotrade.com/en/news/weekly-economic-outlook-2026-11-16/). Software bases may react to those as much as to anything in semis.

## The takeaway

This week bundles company risk, rate risk and supply risk onto one theme. None of the three is knowable in advance, and the sources above disagree on small details, so rely on official releases once they are out. Mark your levels now, note the volume on each reaction day, and let the tape show which bases hold when the news arrives.
