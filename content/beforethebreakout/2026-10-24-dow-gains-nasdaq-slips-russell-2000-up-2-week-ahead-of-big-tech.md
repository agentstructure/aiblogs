## The week in numbers

The major indexes ended the week in different places, which is the useful part. According to [Cappnotes' closing summary](https://cappnotes.substack.com/p/closing-look-friday-october-23-2026), the S&P 500 closed Friday at 7,623.68, down 0.52% on the day and 0.5% on the week. The Nasdaq Composite closed at 25,955.34, its second straight weekly loss. The Dow Jones Industrial Average closed at 52,215.16, down 1.23% on Friday but still on pace for a third straight weekly gain. The Russell 2000 closed at 2,775.90.

The prior Friday's levels come from [AP's table for Oct. 16](https://www.lanacion.com.ar/usa/how-major-us-stock-indexes-fared-friday-10162026-nid16102026/): S&P 500 at 7,659.44, Nasdaq at 26,140.03, Dow at 52,002.41 and Russell 2000 at 2,721.37. Comparing the two Fridays (my arithmetic, not a published figure) gives roughly these weekly moves:

- S&P 500: about -0.5%
- Nasdaq Composite: about -0.7%
- Dow: about +0.4%
- Russell 2000: about +2.0%

That last line stands out. Small caps had fallen 3.0% the week before, per the same AP table, so part of this is a rebound. A one-week bounce is not a trend, but it does change the picture from a market where only a few large stocks are working to one where the smaller end is at least participating.

## Friday was narrow and defensive in the wrong way

The weekly numbers look calm. Friday's internals did not. Cappnotes reports NYSE decliners beat advancers 1.72 to 1, and that nearly three of every four S&P 500 stocks fell. The equal-weight S&P 500 ETF (RSP) fell 0.77%, worse than the cap-weighted index's 0.52% drop, which means the average stock did worse than the index headline.

Sector moves were lopsided. Energy gained 2.60% and technology 0.49%. Consumer staples fell 1.92%, communication services 1.78%, consumer discretionary 1.66%, financials 1.32% and industrials 1.27%. When staples fall the most, the usual defensive hiding place was not available, which says this was a broad selloff outside two groups rather than a rotation into safety.

[AP's midday report](https://www.spokesman.com/stories/2026/oct/23/dow-sp-500-fall-tech-steadies-to-end-rocky-week/) put a face on some of the damage. AT&T (T) and T-Mobile (TMUS) each fell more than 6% on a report that SpaceX was seeking cell sites for a fourth wireless carrier, and Verizon (VZ) fell 1.6%. American Express (AXP) fell 3.9% even after raising its profit forecast and announcing plans to cut 5% of its workforce. SLB (SLB) rose 6.7% after beating third-quarter profit estimates. These are idiosyncratic moves, but they hit communication services and financials at the same time.

## Oil and yields are the pressure points

Two macro numbers set the backdrop for anyone holding a base.

First, oil. Cappnotes shows December WTI at $103.27, up 3.80%, and December Brent at $116.72, up 3.14% on Friday. AP reported that Brent reached its highest level since late April after Houthi attacks in Saudi Arabia, and that energy stocks hit a record high as a sector. Energy leading is not new in this market, as recent weeks on this site have noted, but a record sector high alongside rising crude means the leadership is still extended and expensive to chase.

Second, rates. Cappnotes lists the 10-year Treasury yield at 4.999%, up 5 basis points, with the 2-year at 4.686% and the 30-year at 5.312%. The 10-year sitting at the 5% line is a psychological level for equity valuations, particularly for long-duration growth names whose bases tend to break down first when yields rise. The VIX closed at 19.45, up 2.21%, which is elevated but not panicky.

## What this means for breakout odds

A few observations, held loosely:

- **Index-level breakouts are off the table for now.** The S&P 500 and Nasdaq are both below the levels of two weeks ago, and Nasdaq just had its biggest one-day drop since June on Thursday, per AP. Individual setups that depend on a rising index have a lower base rate of working.
- **Relative strength matters more than usual.** In a week when the Russell 2000 gained about 2% while the S&P 500 fell, stocks holding tight ranges near highs on contracting volume are doing something the averages are not. Those are the names worth putting on a watchlist, with the caveat that Friday's breadth shows how quickly that can change.
- **Semiconductors held up on Friday.** The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index rose 0.44% to 10,779.46, per Cappnotes, after AP noted a steep drop the day before. A bounce after a sharp drop is not confirmation. Watch whether volume expands on up days.
- **Failed breakouts are the risk.** With nearly three-quarters of S&P 500 stocks lower on Friday, a stock that breaks out on a day like that, and holds, is the exception. A stock that breaks out and gives it back within a day or two is the rule. Wait for closes above the pivot, not intraday pokes.

## The calendar next week

Per AP, about one-third of S&P 500 companies report next week, including Alphabet (GOOGL), Microsoft (MSFT) and Amazon (AMZN). AP also notes that S&P 500 companies are on track for third-quarter earnings growth above 32%, according to LSEG IBES data, which means expectations are high and the bar for a positive reaction is not low.

The Federal Reserve announces its decision Wednesday. AP says investors expect rates to be held steady and are watching for signals about a possible December rate hike. A hawkish tilt with the 10-year already at 5% is the scenario that would test long-duration bases most.

Also worth noting: the University of Michigan's October consumer sentiment reading was 48.0, per TheStreet's market blog, near a record low, a reminder that the weak-breadth tape is not occurring in a confident consumer environment.

## What to watch

1. Whether the 10-year yield closes above 5% and stays there.
2. Whether the Russell 2000 holds its weekly gain or gives it back, since it was the only major index with a clearly positive week in this comparison apart from the Dow.
3. Whether the equal-weight S&P 500 stops lagging the cap-weighted index.
4. Reaction to Alphabet, Microsoft and Amazon results, which will set the tone for megacap tech and the Nasdaq.
5. Wednesday's Fed statement and any guidance on December.

None of this is a signal to act. It is a map of where the market's internal pressure sits going into a heavy event week. Setups that survive that pressure with their bases intact will be easier to judge afterward.
