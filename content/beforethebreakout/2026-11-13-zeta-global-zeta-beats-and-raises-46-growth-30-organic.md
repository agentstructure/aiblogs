## What happened

Zeta Global (ZETA) reported third-quarter results on November 9, and the headline was a number swing traders like: [revenue of $492.7 million, up 46% from $337.2 million a year earlier](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001851003/000119312526453558/zeta-ex99_1.htm). The company said that beat the midpoint of its own guidance by $22 million, or 5%, and called it its 21st consecutive "beat and raise" quarter.

It also raised the full-year outlook. Revenue guidance now sits at $1,853 million to $1,857 million, up $37 million at the midpoint from $1,818 million. Adjusted EBITDA guidance moved to $414.1 million to $415.2 million from a prior midpoint of $405.2 million, and free cash flow guidance rose to $270.6 million to $271.6 million from $255.3 million, all per the same release.

The stock has been acting like it knows. Trefis data for Wednesday, November 11 [listed ZETA among small-cap names at 52-week highs](https://www.trefis.com/stock/spy/articles/624515/the-52-week-high-list-33-small-cap-names-on-wednesday-2/2026-11-12), with a market value of about $9.4 billion, a gain of 18.8% over one week and 89.7% over one year. Trefis defines small cap as $2 billion to $10 billion, so ZETA is near the top edge of the group this site covers.

## The number that matters more than 46%

Headline growth of 46% overstates the underlying pace, and the company says so itself. The release backs out two items: $48.0 million of revenue from Marigold's Enterprise Business and $7.4 million of political candidate revenue. Excluding both, growth was 30%.

The arithmetic is simple. Revenue of $492.7 million less $55.4 million leaves roughly $437 million of core revenue. Marigold alone is about 10% of the quarter's sales, and political spending is under 2%.

The Marigold piece has a clock on it. Zeta [closed the acquisition on November 24, 2025](https://www.itiger.com/news/2588418370), so the fourth quarter of 2025 included only about five weeks of Marigold revenue. That means the acquired revenue is already in the base for part of the current quarter, and the company's own guidance reflects it. Fourth-quarter revenue guidance of $521 million to $525 million implies 32% to 33% growth, down from 46% in Q3.

That deceleration is mostly arithmetic, not demand. Still, it is what traders should expect when the next report arrives: reported growth will step down while organic growth is the figure to compare. If the organic rate holds near 30%, the step-down is benign. If it slips, the market will notice.

## Profitability is catching up to growth

The quality of the quarter is in the margin and cash lines, not only revenue.

- **Adjusted EBITDA:** $121.0 million, a 24.6% margin, up 140 basis points from a year earlier.
- **GAAP net income:** $38.3 million, or $0.14 per diluted share. Zeta says it is also positive year to date, at $33.2 million.
- **Free cash flow:** $81.5 million, up 73% year over year, on operating cash flow of $91.3 million, up 58%.

Zeta pairs growth and margin in a metric it calls the Rule of 71, adding revenue growth to adjusted EBITDA margin. Q3 reached it at 46% plus 24.6%. That is the company's own yardstick, not an industry standard, and it flatters a quarter in which acquired revenue inflated the growth term. Swapping in the 30% organic rate, the simple sum is closer to 55. That is still a strong combination, but a more honest one to carry forward.

Customer data points the same way. Zeta reported 212 customers spending at least $1 million on a trailing twelve-month basis, up 18% from a year earlier, with average revenue per such customer of $2.0 million, up 22%. Growth in both the count and the size of large accounts is the pattern that supports durable organic growth rather than one-off campaigns.

Fourth-quarter adjusted EBITDA guidance of $135.2 million to $136.3 million implies a margin of 25.7% to 26.2%, which would be higher than Q3's 24.6%.

## What the balance sheet shows

Cash and equivalents were $340.1 million at quarter-end against long-term borrowings of $243.9 million, according to the release. Zeta repurchased $85.5 million of shares in the first nine months of 2026. The release does not announce a new buyback authorization, so the repurchase pace is a trend to track, not a program to count on.

One more detail for the careful reader: the GAAP EPS guidance of $0.22 to $0.24 excludes a possible one-time, non-cash tax benefit from a valuation allowance release. If that benefit lands, reported GAAP earnings could look sharply better than the underlying run rate, and it should not be mistaken for operating improvement.

## What the chart has to prove

A stock that is up 89.7% in a year and 18.8% in a week has already made its move for many traders. The question for a base-builder is whether the post-earnings area holds. Information, not advice, on what to watch:

- **Behavior after the gap.** Constructive action after a beat-and-raise tends to show shallow pullbacks on lighter volume, with buyers stepping in near the first consolidation area. A fast give-back on heavy volume would say the news was priced in.
- **Tightening range.** Several days of narrowing daily ranges near the highs is the setup this site tracks. A single strong week is not a base.
- **Market backdrop.** Trefis counted 33 small-cap names at 52-week highs on November 11, so ZETA is not alone. Whether it holds up against that crowd, rather than just the index, is the more useful test.
- **Organic growth in February.** The next print is the real test. Reported growth should fall toward 32% to 33% on the guidance; the useful comparison is whether organic growth stays near 30%.

## The takeaway

Zeta is a clean example of why reading the footnotes matters. The 46% headline is real, but 30% organic growth is the figure to use, and the Marigold comparison will fade from the reported numbers in the coming quarters. What supports the story is the combination of rising guidance, expanding margins, positive GAAP income and growing large-customer counts. What the chart still has to show is that buyers will defend the new highs. This is analysis of a public report, not a recommendation to buy or sell.
