## What happened

Applied Aerospace & Defense (AADX), a supplier of space, rotorcraft and precision-strike components that listed on the New York Stock Exchange in June, reported third-quarter results on Nov. 11. The headline numbers looked strong. Revenue was a record $182.1 million, up 48.6% from $122.5 million a year earlier, according to [the company's release as republished by 01net](https://www.01net.it/applied-aerospace-defense-reports-third-quarter-2026-financial-results/). Adjusted EBITDA rose 37.4% to $39.7 million. Contract backlog reached $1.26 billion at Sept. 30, which the company rounds to "nearly $1.3 billion."

Then came the cut. Full-year 2026 guidance is now $660 million to $670 million in revenue and $137 million to $142 million in adjusted EBITDA. The release does not state the prior ranges, so the size of the reduction cannot be measured from the company's own text. What the company did say is why: timing and supplier constraints, according to the [GuruFocus summary of the call](https://www.gurufocus.com/news/9193958/applied-aerospace-defense-inc-aadx-q3-2026-earnings-call-highlights-record-13-billion-backlog-and-486-revenue-growth-offset-by-margin-pressure-and-lowered-guidance).

That pairing, a record backlog beside a lower forecast, is common among newly public defense and space suppliers. It is a reminder that backlog measures orders, not shipments.

## Two kinds of growth

The 48.6% headline needs a haircut. Organic growth, which excludes 2026 acquisitions, was 20.3%, or $24.9 million of the roughly $59.6 million increase, per the release. Acquisitions supplied the rest.

The segment table shows where that shows up. Advanced Sensing and Precision Strike Systems revenue jumped to $48.7 million from $12.4 million. Space and Launch Systems grew to $39.1 million from $29.9 million. Defense Aviation and Airborne Systems, the largest segment, rose to $94.2 million from $80.3 million.

Bookings were $254 million, a book-to-bill ratio of 1.4x, according to GuruFocus. A ratio above 1.0x means orders exceeded revenue in the quarter, which is why the backlog grew about 12% from the prior quarter. Orders are arriving. The question is how quickly parts can be built and shipped.

## Why the outlook came down

Management pointed to several causes, as reported by [Motley Fool's call transcript](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/11/11/applied-aero-aadx-q3-2026-earnings-call-transcript/) and GuruFocus.

- **Supply constraints.** The company cited "constraints with existing qualified suppliers, particularly on forgings, castings and certain specialized fasteners," and slow qualification of additional suppliers. An executive said the constraints are "not across the board" and tied to specific programs.
- **Approvals.** Between $15 million and $20 million of rotorcraft aftermarket revenue moved from the fourth quarter into the first half of 2027. GuruFocus attributes the slip to delayed U.S. government approval of updated technical data packages.
- **Customer funding.** On the call, an executive said: "Some awards are taking longer as our prime customers wait for funding and firm orders under broader framework agreements."

That last item connects to the budget backdrop this site has tracked. The Pentagon is operating under a stopgap funding measure, and framework agreements are not firm orders. A supplier sitting beneath the primes feels that delay before the headlines do.

## Margins and cash

Gross profit was $42.7 million, a margin of about 23.5% by our calculation from the reported figures, versus $34.3 million and about 28.0% a year earlier. Adjusted EBITDA margin slipped to 21.8% from 23.6%. Management attributed pressure to start-up costs for new program ramps and capacity expansions, per GuruFocus. The call transcript says the fourth-quarter gross margin should be near 26%, about 200 basis points below original expectations.

Net income was $30.6 million, but that includes a $24.2 million income tax benefit, per the release. Strip it out and the figure is about $6.4 million, our arithmetic. Over nine months the company reported a net loss of $138.5 million, compared with a $9.2 million loss a year earlier.

Cash is the sharper issue. Free cash flow was negative $9.5 million in the quarter: $11.5 million of operating cash flow less $21.0 million of capital spending. Through nine months, free cash flow was negative $113.2 million, versus negative $32.7 million a year earlier. Capital spending for the nine months was $42.6 million, against $10.4 million. Management expects positive free cash flow in the fourth quarter as spending moderates, according to GuruFocus. That is a forecast, and the company will have to deliver it.

## The balance sheet after the IPO

AADX priced its IPO at $20.00 a share, selling 32.5 million shares for $650 million in gross proceeds, according to the [424B4 prospectus](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0002118195/000119312526256356/d25758d424b4.htm). About $604.3 million of net proceeds was earmarked mainly to repay a revolver and term loans.

On the call, debt was put at $409.5 million at Sept. 30, down from $643 million at year-end and just over $1 billion before the IPO. Cash was $11.5 million. Net leverage was about 2.7x on a pro forma trailing basis, the transcript says; the 01net release copy does not report a leverage ratio, so treat that as a call figure. About $120 million was available under the revolver.

Lower debt gives the company room. It also means little cash cushion if the supply problems drag on and the cash-flow turn slips.

## What it means for the group

Three points matter for readers following the supply chain beneath the defense primes and space launchers.

First, the named customers show how broad the exposure is. The transcript names SpaceX, Blue Origin and Anduril's CCA Fury, as well as Raytheon, Bell and Lockheed Martin (LMT). No revenue shares were given. Rotorcraft aftermarket is about a quarter of revenue and mostly sole-source, per the transcript.

Second, the company expects Falcon 9 volumes to moderate, partly offset by New Glenn growth, according to the call. Launch cadence at two customers now shapes part of its space segment.

Third, 2027 visibility is decent. Motley Fool's takeaways say the backlog covers about 75% of expected 2027 revenue and that the company expects gross margin near 26% into the first half of 2027, returning to the high 20s in the second half. These are management expectations, and the transcript itself shows some ambiguity in how the 2027 order math was framed, so we leave the dollar split aside.

## What to watch

- **The fourth-quarter cash flow.** Positive free cash flow was promised. Capital spending near 4% to 5% of revenue is what management calls normal, per the transcript.
- **Supplier qualification.** Whether forgings, castings and fasteners loosen is the main swing for the 2026 revenue range.
- **Technical data approvals.** The $15 million to $20 million rotorcraft slip depends on government sign-off.
- **Framework-to-firm conversion.** If the primes' funding stays stuck, delayed awards would push into the 2027 backlog coverage the company is counting on.

This is information, not a recommendation to buy or sell any security.
