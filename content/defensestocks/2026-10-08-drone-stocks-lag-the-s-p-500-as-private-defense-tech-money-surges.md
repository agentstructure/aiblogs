## The gap between private enthusiasm and public prices

Public drone stocks have had a rough 2026, and private markets are moving the other way. According to a [24/7 Wall St. comparison published October 2](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/10/02/which-drone-stock-is-dominating-in-2026-red-cat-ondas-or-aerovironment/), Red Cat Holdings (RCAT) is down about 20% year to date, Ondas (ONDS) is down 26% and AeroVironment (AVAV) is down 42%. The SPDR S&P 500 ETF (SPY) is up 13% over the same stretch, and the REX Drone ETF (DRNZ) is down 9%.

Meanwhile, a [Dealroom forecast reported by Defense News on October 5](https://defensenews.com/global/europe/2026/10/05/european-defense-startups-to-raise-record-105-billion-in-2026-dealroom-forecasts) projects European defense startups will raise a record $10.5 billion in 2026, roughly four times the $2.6 billion raised a year earlier. Funding had already reached $7.4 billion by September 20, against $1.5 billion at the same point last year.

For investors in listed names, that contrast is the story. Capital is flooding into the category, but the listed pure plays are not getting credit for it, at least not yet.

## What the three public names are reporting

The operating results are less grim than the share prices. AeroVironment reported quarterly revenue of $480.5 million on September 9, ahead of expectations, and kept its full-year outlook. The stock still carries the heaviest drawdown of the group. The same 24/7 Wall St. piece notes the U.S. government canceled a large antenna contract supporting a Space Force program earlier in the year. A [MarketBeat analysis from July](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/drone-stocks-down-defense-backlogs-115500190.html) put the lost contract at $1.7 billion of long-term outlook, while also citing a $1.2 billion fully funded backlog, a 1.4 book-to-bill ratio in the fiscal fourth quarter, and a $500 million counter-drone IDIQ from the Domestic Shield Program.

AeroVironment also had a run of positive headlines in September: a $464.8 million U.S. Army laser award on September 3 and its first international LOCUST laser order on September 10, according to the headlines listed alongside the 24/7 Wall St. article. Directed energy and counter-drone work is now a meaningful part of the AVAV story, not just small loitering munitions.

Red Cat reported first-half 2026 sales of $35.7 million and maintained a full-year revenue target starting at $150 million. That arithmetic matters: the company needs the large majority of its annual revenue in the second half. The U.S. Army accounted for roughly half of first-half revenue, with Japan and Europe the next-largest customers. The July MarketBeat piece also flags that China banned exports of critical components to Red Cat and several other U.S. companies, a supply chain risk for any small drone maker that depends on imported parts.

Ondas raised its full-year revenue outlook after stronger quarterly results and reported a pro forma order backlog of $757 million, a figure that includes businesses it acquired during the year. Pro forma backlog built through acquisitions is a different quality of number than organic bookings, and investors should read it that way.


## Why the budget backdrop has not been enough

The demand case on paper is strong. The July MarketBeat article cites a Pentagon fiscal 2027 request containing nearly $75 billion for unmanned systems and counter-drone technology. Kratos Defense (KTOS) is cited there with a backlog above $2 billion at the end of the first quarter, about 72% funded.

Yet the stocks have lagged, and the likely reasons are practical rather than mysterious. Revenue is concentrated in a few customers and programs, so a single cancellation moves the whole outlook. Guidance for the smaller names is back-half weighted, which leaves little room for slippage. And budget requests are not obligations: until funds are awarded as contracts, they do not show up in backlog.

## Europe is catching up, and that matters for the listed names

The Dealroom data points to a shift in where new defense technology is being financed. The U.S. share of defense startup funding is 75%, its lowest ever, while the EU share has risen to 21% from 15%. Dealroom's own framing is "Europe catching up rather than the U.S. slowing." Defense now accounts for 15% of EU venture funding, up from 6.2% a year earlier.

Mega rounds drive the totals, accounting for more than 85% of European defense venture funding this year, including Helsing's $1.8 billion Series E in July. Helsing and Quantum Systems are together valued at $25.7 billion. Drones and autonomous systems take 74% of EU defense VC and 57% of U.S. defense VC.

Within Europe, unmanned aerial vehicles lead at $4.7 billion, followed by Earth observation and satellite imagery at $1.8 billion, anti-drone solutions at $1.2 billion and launch vehicles at $1.2 billion.

The implication for U.S.-listed investors is competitive as much as bullish. Well-funded private rivals can bid for the same allied budgets that AeroVironment, Red Cat and Ondas are targeting, and they are not subject to quarterly pressure. It also raises the odds that private valuations eventually reach public markets through IPOs, which would give investors a direct comparison with today's depressed listed multiples.

## What to watch next

- **Third-quarter results.** Red Cat's second-half ramp toward its $150 million-plus target is the clearest near-term test of whether the backlog converts to revenue.
- **Backlog quality at Ondas.** How much of the $757 million is organic versus acquired, and how quickly it turns into revenue.
- **Counter-drone and laser awards.** Follow-on orders after AeroVironment's Army and LOCUST wins would show whether directed energy is a durable line.
- **Component supply.** Any further Chinese export restrictions would hit small U.S. drone makers hardest.
- **Private-to-public flow.** A large European or U.S. defense-tech listing would reset how the market values this group.
