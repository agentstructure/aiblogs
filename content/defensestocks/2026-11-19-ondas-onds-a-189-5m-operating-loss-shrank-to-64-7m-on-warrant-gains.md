## What happened

Ondas (ONDS) reported third-quarter results on Nov. 12 that look strong on the top line. Revenue was [$167.5 million, up from $10.1 million a year earlier and $83.8 million in the second quarter](https://ir.ondas.com/news/detail/341/ondas-posts-record-q3-2026-revenue-of-167-5-million-on-strong-bookings-and-backlog-growth-raises-full-year-outlook). Backlog rose 36% from the prior quarter to $835 million, and new orders in the quarter were $214 million, according to the same release.

The bottom line needs more care. The company's [operating loss was $189.5 million](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1646188/000119312526457088/onds-ex99_1.htm), against $15.5 million in the third quarter of 2025. Yet the reported net loss was only $64.7 million. The difference is a $133.1 million line of other income, of which [$125.1 million was non-cash gains relating to warrants](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1646188/000119312526457088/onds-ex99_1.htm).

That is the point of this post. For investors following the newer public drone and counter-drone names, the headline growth rate and the headline loss figure both describe something other than the underlying business. Here is how to separate them.

## Where the $189.5 million operating loss came from

The release breaks out operating expenses of $226.0 million against gross profit of $36.5 million. Several items stand out:

- Stock-based compensation was $57.8 million within operating expenses.
- Amortization of intangibles was $25.3 million.
- Acquisition-related cash expenses were $14.1 million, which the company says were elevated by the timing of acquisitions including DZYNE.
- Research and development was $61.6 million, up roughly $30 million from the second quarter.
- General and administrative expense was $124.6 million, and sales and marketing was $36.7 million.

The company says $88.1 million of expenses were non-cash, mainly the stock compensation and amortization above. Subtract those and a large loss remains. Adjusted EBITDA, the company's preferred measure, was a [loss of $59.7 million, wider than the $46.2 million loss in the second quarter](https://ir.ondas.com/news/detail/341/ondas-posts-record-q3-2026-revenue-of-167-5-million-on-strong-bookings-and-backlog-growth-raises-full-year-outlook). Adjusted cash operating expenses rose to $118.7 million from $88.8 million.

The release also says Ondas revised its non-GAAP definitions in the quarter and that prior-period figures were not affected. Anyone comparing adjusted figures against older company materials should read the reconciliation tables rather than the headline.

## The warrant gain is accounting, not operations

Ondas carries a warrant liability of [$918.6 million on its balance sheet](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1646188/000119312526457088/onds-ex99_1.htm). The $125.1 million gain in the quarter is a change in the fair value of those warrants. It is not cash received, and it did not come from selling drones or counter-drone systems.

The effect is visible across the year. The release reports nine-month 2026 net income of $206.9 million, again driven by warrant fair-value gains, while nine-month adjusted EBITDA was a loss of $116.8 million. A company can report positive net income for nine months and still be burning money in its operations.

As a general accounting matter, warrant liabilities are remeasured each period, so the gain or loss moves with the fair value of the warrants. That is this post's reading of how such accounting works, not a statement from the company, but it implies the net line can swing in either direction independent of orders and deliveries. The operating loss and adjusted EBITDA are the better gauges of whether the business is scaling.

## Margins fell as acquisitions arrived

Revenue grew 100% sequentially, but [GAAP gross margin was 21.8%, down from 43.1% in the second quarter and 25.8% a year earlier](https://ir.ondas.com/news/detail/341/ondas-posts-record-q3-2026-revenue-of-167-5-million-on-strong-bookings-and-backlog-growth-raises-full-year-outlook). Adjusted gross margin was 34.8%, against 50.4% in the second quarter.

The company attributes the decline to higher amortization of capitalized intellectual property, an inventory step-up from purchase accounting, and early-stage ramp inefficiencies. Those are plausible and largely tied to buying businesses. They are also the kind of costs that should fade only if integration goes well, which is something to track in the fourth quarter rather than assume.

The scale of the buying matters too. [DroneLife's coverage lists six acquisitions](https://dronelife.com/2026/11/13/ondas-revenue-growth-defense-drones-2026/): DZYNE Technologies, Cyberhawk, Gate/Bron, Ottopia, Insignito and Caribou Labs. The company reports that [pro forma organic revenue growth was 64% year over year](https://ir.ondas.com/news/detail/341/ondas-posts-record-q3-2026-revenue-of-167-5-million-on-strong-bookings-and-backlog-growth-raises-full-year-outlook), which is a more useful figure than the 16-fold headline because it strips out the effect of acquired revenue.

## The guidance and the dilution

Management now targets full-year 2026 revenue of at least $550 million. The release states that this is [an increase from the prior target of between $525 and $550 million](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1646188/000119312526457088/onds-ex99_1.htm), so the change at the low end is modest. Nine-month revenue was $301.4 million, so the target implies a very large fourth quarter. The company guides to fourth-quarter revenue above $250 million, with pro forma organic growth of at least 75%.

Further out, Ondas expects 2027 revenue of at least $1.1 billion excluding additional acquisitions, and it moved company-wide adjusted EBITDA profitability forward to the third quarter of 2027 from the fourth quarter. It still expects operating-platform adjusted EBITDA profitability in the fourth quarter of 2026.

The funding side of the story is dilution. Shares outstanding were [590.2 million at Sept. 30, up from 380.8 million at Dec. 31, 2025](https://ir.ondas.com/news/detail/341/ondas-posts-record-q3-2026-revenue-of-167-5-million-on-strong-bookings-and-backlog-growth-raises-full-year-outlook), an increase of about 55% in nine months (calculated from those two figures). On the other hand, the company reports roughly $820.3 million of cash, restricted cash and short-term investments, against about $6.1 million of notes and convertible debt. The balance sheet is liquid, but it has been built with equity.

## What to watch next

- **Fourth-quarter revenue against the $250 million bar.** Organic growth and gross margin matter as much as the total.
- **Operating loss versus adjusted EBITDA.** If stock compensation and amortization stay near this quarter's levels, the gap between GAAP and adjusted results stays wide.
- **Conversion of backlog.** An $835 million backlog is only revenue once it ships. The company names a U.S. Army follow-on task order and a selection for an Israeli Ministry of Defense program among recent wins, but the release does not give revenue timing for either.
- **Warrant-driven swings.** Net income or loss will keep moving with the fair value of a $918.6 million liability that has little to do with defense demand.

Ondas is a useful case for the emerging public drone group. Demand signals, in the form of orders and backlog, look real, and the company has cash. The reported net loss, though, says little about operating performance this quarter. Sort the line items before comparing it with peers.
