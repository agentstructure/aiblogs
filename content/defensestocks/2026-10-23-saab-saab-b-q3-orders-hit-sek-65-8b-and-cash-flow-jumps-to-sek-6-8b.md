## The headline numbers

Saab (SAAB B) reported third-quarter results today, and the biggest line is orders. According to [Saab's release](https://www.saab.com/newsroom/press-releases/2026/saab-q3-results-2026positioned-to-capture-growing-demand), order bookings were SEK 65,825 million, against SEK 20,861 million a year earlier. That is more than triple.

The other lines also moved the right way:

- **Sales:** SEK 20,553 million versus SEK 15,871 million, an organic growth rate of 30.6% (18.3% a year ago).
- **EBIT:** SEK 1,945 million versus SEK 1,374 million, up 42%, for a margin of 9.5% (8.7%).
- **Net income:** SEK 1,432 million; earnings per share SEK 2.57 (SEK 1.77).
- **Net liquidity:** SEK 8,679 million, compared with SEK 3,989 million at the end of 2025.

CEO Micael Johansson called it "a quarter marked by high organic sales growth, significantly increased cash flow and strong order bookings."

## The cash test was the real question

Ahead of the report, the debate was less about orders, which were largely known, and more about conversion. Inderes' [Oct. 19 preview](https://www.inderes.se/en/analyst-comments/saab-q326-preview-delivery-pace-put-to-the-seasonal-test) said the focus was "squarely on execution," and flagged cash flow because free cash flow had been negative recently on working capital build. It suggested advance payments on large contracts could help.

They did. Operational cash flow was SEK 6,785 million, versus SEK 142 million in the same quarter last year. That is the number that separates a growing order book from a business that funds its own ramp-up. It is also the figure most likely to be one-off in character: advance payments on big contracts tend to arrive up front and unwind as work is delivered. One strong quarter does not establish a run rate, and the release text we reviewed does not break out how much came from customer advances.

## Versus expectations

Inderes' preview carried a forecast of roughly SEK 18.8 billion in sales and consensus of SEK 19.9 billion. Reported sales of SEK 20.6 billion cleared both. On earnings the picture is closer: consensus adjusted EBIT in the preview was SEK 1,929 million (a 9.7% margin), while Saab's headline EBIT was SEK 1,945 million at 9.5%. The two may not be on an identical basis, since the release summary we reviewed gives reported EBIT only, so treat that comparison as approximate. The sales and cash lines are the cleaner beats.

The preview also expected about SEK 56 billion of order intake, of which around SEK 47.5 billion was already announced. The actual SEK 65.8 billion came in well above that.

## Where the orders came from

The release points to three large awards: SEK 24.6 billion for Gripen E for Ukraine, SEK 10.1 billion for GlobalEye for an undisclosed customer, and SEK 8.7 billion for systems for German frigates. Together they account for SEK 43.4 billion of the quarter's bookings.

For investors, the caveat is timing. Inderes described these as long-cycle orders with deliveries mostly from 2029. They lock in a multi-year revenue base but do not change near-term sales much. They also strengthen the case that Saab's capacity, not demand, is the constraint on growth.

The release also refers to a strong order backlog but, in the text reviewed, gives no backlog figure or outlook language, so those are worth checking in the full interim report.

## The wider European picture

Saab's quarter lands in a sector that has been de-rated. [Morningstar](https://global.morningstar.com/en-gb/stocks/when-will-defense-stocks-rebound) notes that Rheinmetall (RHM) and Renk (R3NK) have fallen as much as 50% from their 2025 peaks, while Leonardo (LDO), Thales (HO) and BAE Systems (BA.) are each roughly 20% below earlier-year levels. Its analysts argue government spending pledges have not yet turned into firm orders, and that converting orders into deliveries remains the open question. Morningstar's analyst expects sentiment to turn more constructive toward the end of 2026, with stronger gains in 2027 as procurement accelerates, especially in Germany. That is one firm's view, not a forecast to lean on.

Saab's results speak directly to that skepticism: orders arrived, and so did cash.

Rheinmetall is the next big test. In its [August half-year report](https://www.teleborsa.it/News/2026/08/06/rheinmetall-nel-semestre-volano-ricavi-39percent-e-utili-74percent-ma-abbassa-guidance-79.html), it posted revenue up 39% to EUR 5.2 billion, operating result up 74% to EUR 786 million and a backlog of EUR 80.5 billion. But it also cancelled the F126 frigate program, which cuts Naval Systems sales by up to EUR 300 million in 2026, and lowered full-year revenue guidance to EUR 13.7 to 14.2 billion while keeping its roughly 19% operating margin target. Its official [EQS notice](https://www.marketscreener.com/news/rheinmetall-ag-preliminary-announcement-of-the-publication-of-quarterly-reports-and-quarterly-inter-ce7e58dade8ef621) lists the Q3 report for Nov. 5 (one calendar on the same page shows Nov. 4).

## What to watch next

- **The full Saab interim report:** backlog size, the split of the cash inflow between advances and underlying operations, and any guidance wording.
- **Whether cash holds:** a second quarter of strong operating cash flow would matter more than the first.
- **Margin mix:** the preview named ramp-up costs, T-7A start-up costs and low-margin A26 submarine work as drags. A 9.5% margin suggests they were manageable this quarter.
- **Rheinmetall on Nov. 5:** whether order intake and margins confirm that the sector's valuation reset has gone far enough, or whether delivery slippage like the F126 cancellation spreads.
