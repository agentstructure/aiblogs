## What happened

BWX Technologies (BWXT), a supplier of nuclear components to the U.S. Navy, reported third-quarter results on Nov. 2 that look strong at the top line. Revenue rose 10% to $953.0 million, GAAP operating income rose 17% to $132.1 million, and free cash flow jumped 59% to $150.5 million, according to the [company's earnings release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001486957/000148695726000053/bwxt_93026xerexhibit991.htm). Total backlog stood at $8,556.2 million, up from $7,389.1 million a year earlier.

The headline backlog number hides a split that matters for anyone using BWXT as a proxy for Navy nuclear demand. Comparing the Q3 release with the [Q2 release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001486957/000148695726000041/bwxt_63026xerexhibit991.htm), government backlog moved from $6.80 billion at June 30 to $6,481.8 million at Sept. 30, a decline of about $316 million in one quarter. Commercial backlog moved the other way, from $1.60 billion to $2,074.4 million.

This post uses "backlog" as the company defines it in those releases and "bookings" as new orders recorded in the quarter. Book-to-bill, a ratio of bookings to revenue, is my own arithmetic from the reported figures, not a company metric.

## Why the government number fell

Backlog is a simple stock-and-flow identity. It rises when new orders arrive and falls as work is billed. In Q3, Government Operations booked $323.0 million of new orders and recognized $639.1 million of revenue. The gap is about $316 million, which matches the drop in government backlog almost exactly. That implies a government book-to-bill of roughly 0.51x.

The year-ago comparison explains why. In Q3 2025 the segment booked $2,086.1 million, a quarter with large government awards. Total company bookings were $538.2 million this quarter against $2,245.4 million a year ago, a 76% decline. Q2 2026 bookings were $648.9 million, so the order rate has been below the roughly $900 million to $950 million quarterly revenue run rate for two straight quarters.

That does not mean demand has faded. BWXT [announced in May](https://investors.bwxt.com/news-releases/news-release-details/bwxt-announces-14-billion-contracts-naval-nuclear-propulsion) more than $1.4 billion in naval nuclear propulsion contracts, including a $1.285 billion long-lead material award that [Virginia Business](https://virginiabusiness.com/bwxt-wins-1-4b-in-u-s-naval-nuclear-propulsion-program-contracts/) described as the first of five annual task orders through 2030. Naval awards arrive in large, irregular blocks, and a quarter without one will always look weak against a quarter with one. The more useful test is the trailing-year pattern, not a single quarter.

## What lifted the total

If government backlog fell by $316 million, how did total backlog rise at all? Commercial Operations booked $215.2 million against $314.2 million of revenue, a shortfall of about $99 million, yet commercial backlog rose by roughly $474 million. Orders do not explain that increase.

One possible contributor is the Precision Components Group acquisition, which [the 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001486957/000148695726000051/bwxt-20260930.htm) says closed July 1, 2026 for approximately $216.8 million and is reported in Commercial Operations. The portion of the filing I could read does not state how much backlog it added, so treat the link as a hypothesis, not a finding. The takeaway is narrower: the roughly $158 million sequential rise in total backlog came from the commercial side and not from new government orders.

Commercial revenue is also where the growth is. It rose 25% to $314.2 million, against 4% for Government Operations at $639.1 million. Government operating income still grew faster, up 26% to $122.6 million, which points to margin improvement and not volume.

## Guidance and the 2027 setup

BWXT left 2026 revenue guidance at about $3.8 billion and narrowed the rest of its outlook. Non-GAAP earnings per share is now expected at $4.72 to $4.77, versus $4.70 to $4.80 before. Free cash flow is $350 million to $360 million, versus $345 million to $360 million. Adjusted EBITDA is about $667 million, inside the earlier $662 million to $672 million range. Narrowing a range this late in the year is a signal of visibility, not of a step change.

For 2027, the company gave a preliminary outlook with no dollar figures: high-single-digit revenue growth, and high-single-digit to low-double-digit growth in adjusted EBITDA and non-GAAP EPS. Against roughly $3.8 billion of 2026 revenue, high-single-digit growth would imply something near $4.1 billion, though that is arithmetic and not company guidance.

The release also named two awards that point beyond ships. BWXT's advanced reactor technology was selected for the U.S. Army's Janus Program at Fort Campbell, and for a transportable nuclear power plant project by Prodigy Clean Energy in Canada. The release gives no dollar value for either. CEO Rex Geveden said the company is in "the early-stages of a multi-decadal investment cycle."

The 10-Q also says BWXT agreed on July 31, 2026 to sell its medical business to Nordic Capital in a transaction valued at up to $800 million, with $700.4 million of assets classified as held for sale. That reshapes the commercial segment and is worth tracking alongside the backlog figures.

## What to watch next

- **The next naval award.** Government backlog only rebuilds if the Navy places its next large task orders. Watch for announcements of Virginia-class, Columbia-class and carrier component awards, and whether Q4 bookings return to revenue or better.
- **The backlog bridge.** Ask management how much of the commercial backlog increase came from the Precision Components Group acquisition, and how much from organic orders. The answer changes how the 25% commercial growth should be read.
- **Whether government growth stays at 4%.** Revenue of $639.1 million is up only 4% year over year. A 2027 outlook of high-single-digit total growth leans on the commercial segment or a government acceleration.
- **The medical sale.** Closing terms and use of proceeds will shape capital allocation, including any further acquisitions in the nuclear supply chain.

## Why it matters for defense investors

BWXT is a useful read-through for Navy nuclear demand, but one quarter of low government bookings is not a trend, given how lumpy naval awards are. It is a reminder that headline backlog totals can rise for reasons unrelated to new defense orders, and that the segment split is where the signal sits.
