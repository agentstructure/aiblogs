## The week's biggest awards went to a private company

The largest Pentagon announcements this week were not won by a listed prime. Two of the three headline awards went to Anduril Industries, which is privately held, and that matters for investors trying to translate contract news into public-market exposure.

On the Navy side, the Department of War announced a $6.6 billion submarine component yard at Sparrows Point in Baltimore County, Maryland, according to [GovCon Wire](https://www.govconwire.com/articles/anduril-navy-arsenal-2-sparrows-point-shipyard-trump-hegseth). Anduril is putting up $3.7 billion and the Navy $2.9 billion, paid through a contract with payments tied to demonstrated production outcomes. On the Army side, Anduril was [tapped for up to $1.8 billion](https://www.govconwire.com/articles/anduril-army-ngc2-contract-i-corps) over five years to field the Next-Generation Command and Control (NGC2) data layer, starting with I Corps, with a base period of $162.8 million.

Neither deal puts revenue directly into a ticker. But both depend on, or sit next to, companies that do trade.

## Arsenal-2: the listed shipbuilders are the customers

The Sparrows Point yard is designed to feed the existing submarine builders. General Dynamics (GD), through Electric Boat, and Huntington Ingalls (HII), through Newport News Shipbuilding, will receive the yard's modules for final assembly. Those two companies jointly build Columbia-class and Virginia-class submarines.

The reason is capacity. Vice Adm. Rob Gaucher, the Navy's director of submarine programs, said the enterprise currently delivers about half the tonnage required to reach one Columbia-class and two Virginia-class hulls per year. He said Arsenal-2 would add up to 9 million production hours per year, a 15% increase in total capacity. Initial work covers precision torpedo tubes and impulse tanks, with more complex rafted module assemblies later.

For GD and HII, the read-through is more nuanced than a simple win. Outsourced module capacity could relieve a bottleneck that has constrained their own throughput. It also introduces a new, venture-backed supplier into a supply chain they have long controlled. The yard is not expected to open until 2030; site preparation and permitting have started, and construction is awaiting approvals. Anduril is also building a 160,000-square-foot facility in Orange County, California, to validate manufacturing methods and produce Virginia-class test assets and torpedo tubes in the meantime. Any near-term effect on GD or HII results is therefore small.

One more detail worth noting: President Trump described a 40% stake for American taxpayers in the shipyard, along with the prospect of $21 billion in future purchases from the site. Those are claims made at the announcement, not contracted figures, and they are worth tracking as paperwork emerges.

## Lockheed Martin gets a long-dated electronic warfare order

The cleanest listed-company win in the batch is at Lockheed Martin (LMT). The Navy modified a contract for production of AN/SLQ-32(V)6 systems, the core of the Surface Electronic Warfare Improvement Program (SEWIP) Block II, [per GovCon Wire](https://www.govconwire.com/articles/lockheed-martin-navy-sewip-modification). The award dates to Sept. 30 and was announced on Monday.

The headline number needs care. The base value is $209 million; with options, the cumulative potential value is $1.71 billion. Work runs to September 2029. Lockheed Martin Rotary and Mission Systems will do 78% of it at Liverpool, New York, with the rest in Lansdale, Pennsylvania. The $209 million obligated at award was funded mainly from FY2026 shipbuilding and conversion money ($129.5 million) and other procurement funds.

The practical point for investors: only the obligated amount is firm backlog. Options are possible future revenue, and the gap between $209 million and $1.71 billion is large. The same report notes that NAVSEA solicited a potential $7.13 billion multiple-award contract in February for AN/SLQ-32 production, design agent engineering and system integration, so the program's competitive structure is still evolving.

## BWXT: small award, steady franchise

BWX Technologies (BWXT) announced a roughly $189 million Navy contract through its Nuclear Fuel Services unit in Erwin, Tennessee, according to [GovCon Wire](https://www.govconwire.com/articles/bwxt-189m-navy-nuclear-fuel-contract). The company will manufacture and deliver reactor fuel under the Naval Nuclear Propulsion Program, which supports five submarine classes (Columbia, Ohio, Virginia, Seawolf and Los Angeles) and two carrier classes (Ford and Nimitz). Work is scheduled to finish in August 2027.

The award is modest next to the others, but it shows how recurring naval supply contracts work. BWXT disclosed a $174 million Navy reactor fuel contract in October 2025 covering supply through summer 2026, so this looks like the renewal of a recurring annual need rather than a new program. The step up from $174 million to $189 million is small, and the more important signal is that submarine production ambitions, including the Arsenal-2 effort above, ultimately require more reactor fuel to follow.

## What it means for listed-stock exposure

Three themes stand out.

First, the Army's NGC2 award shows how software-defined command and control is shifting spend toward non-traditional vendors. Anduril says the award moves NGC2 from division-level prototyping to operational deployment, and the Army plans to field it to all 11 divisions over the five-year Future Years Defense Program, at two to three divisions per year. Legacy primes that sell communications and sensor hardware into those formations are exposed to how open the architecture really is, since it is designed to let the Army add technology from multiple vendors.

Second, the submarine industrial base remains the Navy's tightest constraint. Arsenal-2, the BWXT fuel order and the SEWIP production run all point to sustained funding for undersea and surface-combatant programs, but each pays out over years, not quarters.

Third, headline totals overstate near-term revenue. The Anduril yard is $6.6 billion across two funders and opens in 2030. The SEWIP figure is mostly options. The NGC2 figure is mostly options too, with $162.8 million in the base period.

## What to watch next

- Whether GD and HII comment on how Arsenal-2 modules fit their production schedules in upcoming earnings calls.
- Approvals for construction at Sparrows Point, which is still pending.
- Which vendors win positions in the potential $7.13 billion AN/SLQ-32 multiple-award contract.
- Whether the Army exercises NGC2 options beyond the base period and how soon it adds divisions after I Corps.
- Further Navy reactor fuel awards to BWXT as the next fiscal-year cycle approaches.
