## What happened

Two of the most closely watched newly public space and defense-tech names reported third-quarter results within three days of each other, and the pattern is the same. Backlog is at a record, guidance is intact or higher, and cash is flowing out the door.

Voyager Technologies (VOYG) [reported on Nov. 2](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001788060/000162828026068886/q32026-earningsreleasexex9.htm) net sales of $88.7 million, up 124.1% from a year earlier and 68% from the prior quarter. Bookings were $116 million, a 1.3x book-to-bill ratio, and backlog reached $742 million. Free cash flow for the quarter was negative $47.8 million.

Redwire (RDW) [reported on Nov. 4](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001819810/000181981026000139/exhibit991redwire09302026e.htm) revenue of $109.0 million, up 5.4% from $103.4 million, and a backlog of $619.7 million. Free cash flow for the quarter was negative $66.4 million.

Add the two free cash flow figures and the pair consumed about $114 million in one quarter. That is the number in the headline: the sum of the two companies' reported Q3 free cash flow, not a figure either company reports itself.

## Voyager: backlog tripled, but much of it was bought

Voyager's headline backlog number is striking. The company describes $742 million as a record, up 293% year over year and 121% from $336 million at June 30. But the release states that $347 million of that came from the Astrobotic acquisition. Roughly 47% of the total backlog, in other words, arrived through a deal rather than through the sales organization.

The release also cites a $298 million NASA award secured through Astrobotic, which expands the lunar backlog. It says Griffin-1 acceptance testing is complete, with launch targeted for late 2026. A lander launch is the kind of milestone that moves a lunar delivery business from backlog to revenue, and it is also a single event where a delay would push that revenue out.

Profitability is where the quarter looks different from the growth rate. Gross profit was $10.2 million, an 11.5% gross margin versus 15.4% a year earlier. The net loss attributable to Voyager was $55.7 million, or $0.91 per share, and adjusted EBITDA was negative $44.9 million. Over nine months, free cash flow was negative $187.4 million and adjusted EBITDA was negative $115.7 million.

The balance sheet is the cushion. Voyager reported $553.6 million in cash and $815.7 million in total liquidity, including $262.1 million of available revolver capacity, against $841.1 million of net convertible notes. At the Q3 pace of free cash outflow, liquidity covers a long runway, but the convertible debt is a fixed claim that grows more important if the company must keep funding losses.

## Redwire: margin doubled, revenue barely moved

Redwire's quarter was the reverse. Backlog rose to $619.7 million from $411.2 million at the end of 2025, and the company calls the figure nearly 75% higher year over year. Q3 contract awards were $188.7 million, producing a 1.73 book-to-bill ratio. The mix is $379.6 million in Space and $240.1 million in Defense Tech.

Revenue growth was modest. Space revenue rose to $61.3 million from $46.5 million, but Defense Tech fell to $47.8 million from $56.9 million. Defense Tech is where the Stalker drone platform sits, and the release notes follow-on Stalker orders from customers including the U.S. National Guard. So orders are coming, but the segment shrank year over year this quarter.

Margin was the bright spot. GAAP gross margin was 32.3%, double the 16.3% a year earlier, and net loss narrowed to $23.2 million from $41.2 million. Even so, adjusted EBITDA was negative $5.3 million, worse than negative $2.6 million a year ago, as R&D expense rose 87.1% to $14.4 million.

Redwire's cash position looks very different from where it started the year: $488.9 million in cash and restricted cash at Sept. 30, compared with $95.2 million at Dec. 31. The release excerpt reviewed here does not explain the source of that increase, so this post does not attribute it. What the filing does show is that free cash flow was negative $114.4 million over nine months, so the year-to-date burn is real even with the larger balance.

## The Q4 math

Both companies are asking investors to look past a weak cash quarter to a stronger finish. The guidance arithmetic shows how much work that is.

Redwire [reaffirmed](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001819810/000181981026000139/exhibit991redwire09302026e.htm) full-year revenue of $450 million to $500 million. Nine-month revenue was $323.1 million. Subtracting gives an implied fourth quarter of roughly $127 million to $177 million, against $109.0 million in Q3. The top of that range would be about 62% above Q3.

Voyager raised the low end of its full-year range by $10 million, to $285 million to $305 million. Nine-month net sales were $176.7 million, so the implied fourth quarter is roughly $108 million to $128 million, against $88.7 million in Q3, a step up of about 22% to 44%. These implied figures are our arithmetic from the companies' own numbers, not company guidance for the quarter.

Redwire's range is the wider and less certain of the two. The quarter's modest 5.4% growth shows the lag. A backlog that grows faster than revenue is a good sign only if it eventually converts.

## What to watch next

- **Conversion.** Whether Redwire's Q4 revenue lands inside its implied range, and whether Defense Tech returns to growth.
- **Griffin-1.** Voyager's lunar lander launch, targeted for late 2026, is the nearest hard milestone for its Astrobotic business.
- **Starlab and NASA's LEO program.** Voyager reports about $700 million in signed commercial reservations for Starlab, up from roughly $500 million last quarter, and 39 milestones achieved with $212.5 million in cumulative milestone cash receipts ($1.5 million of it in Q3). The release says NASA's Commercial LEO Destination Phase 2 request for proposals came out in early October, with submissions due in December 2026 and an award expected in spring 2027.
- **Gross margin at Voyager.** The 11.5% figure fell from 15.4%, and the company needs it to rise as Astrobotic work becomes revenue.
- **Cash burn per quarter.** Both companies have large cash balances, but Q3's combined outflow is the baseline against which any improvement should be measured.

## Why it matters for the sector

Investors who follow defense tech and space often use backlog as a shortcut for future earnings. These two reports show its limits. One backlog was lifted by an acquisition, the other came with flat-to-weak segment revenue, and both companies are spending heavily on development. Backlog counts what customers have agreed to buy, while free cash flow and gross margin show whether the business can deliver it at a profit.

None of this says the backlogs are low quality. NASA and defense customers are real, and both companies are growing orders. But the gap between a $742 million or $619.7 million backlog and a negative cash quarter is what the next two reports will have to close. This is information and commentary, not a recommendation on either stock.
