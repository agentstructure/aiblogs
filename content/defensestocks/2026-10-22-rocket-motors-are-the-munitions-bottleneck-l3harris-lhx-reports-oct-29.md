## What happened

Missiles get the headlines, but the part that often limits how many you can build is the motor inside them. Over two days at the AUSA conference this month, three separate announcements pointed at the same chokepoint.

On Oct. 12, L3Harris (LHX) said [Lockheed Martin (LMT) awarded it a $250 million follow-on contract](https://www.l3harris.com/newsroom/press-release/2026/10/l3harris-awarded-solid-rocket-motor-contract-record-production) for standard-range Guided Multiple Launch Rocket System (GMLRS) solid rocket motors. The company calls it the highest quantity in program history. It did not give a unit count, but said it has doubled GMLRS motor output in recent years. The work will be fulfilled at a 60,000-square-foot facility in Camden, Arkansas, which L3Harris said is due to open later in 2026.

The same day, Leidos (LDOS) [selected X-Bow Systems](https://finviz.com/news/401085/leidos-selects-x-bow-to-provide-rocket-motors-for-us-armys-low-cost-cruise-missile) to develop and produce booster motors for the Army's Low-Cost Containerized Munition. X-Bow, which is privately held, uses additive manufacturing and plans full-rate production in 2027. Its CEO put the logic bluntly: modern conflict "is decided by how many rounds you can put downrange, not how exquisite each one is."

On Oct. 13, Lockheed [signed a cooperation agreement with Avio USA](https://breakingdefense.com/2026/10/avio-lockheed-sign-agreement-for-solid-rocket-motor-production/), the U.S. arm of Italy's Avio. Avio USA will build and qualify motors for Extended Range GMLRS and the Precision Strike Missile (PrSM), where it would become a second motor source. No dollar value was disclosed.

## Why motors matter

A solid rocket motor is a poor candidate for a quick capacity increase. It takes specialized propellant handling, qualification testing, and plants that cost years to build. That is visible in the Avio timeline: its Hurt, Virginia plant broke ground in September 2026, but motor production is scheduled to start in 2029.

The Pentagon has already put money behind the problem. According to [Breaking Defense](https://breakingdefense.com/2026/07/l3harris-postpones-missile-unit-ipo-until-mid-2027/), L3Harris is one of only two domestic suppliers of large solid rocket motors, alongside Northrop Grumman (NOC). In late July the Department of War signed [two seven-year framework agreements with L3Harris](https://www.executivegov.com/articles/dow-l3harris-pac-3-mse-thaad-propulsion-agreements): one expects PAC-3 MSE propulsion production to nearly triple, the other expects THAAD propulsion production to quadruple. Both are described as the company's largest awards of their kind, with definitization expected later this year. That followed a $1 billion Pentagon strategic investment in L3Harris's missile solutions business in April, aimed at modernizing motor facilities in Arkansas, Alabama and Virginia.

A week later, [DefenseScoop reported](https://defensescoop.com/2026/08/03/pentagon-inks-3b-framework-agreement-for-patriot-thaad-components/) a combined $3 billion, seven-year framework with Northrop Grumman: $2 billion for Patriot solid rocket motors, which makes Northrop a second supplier, and $1 billion for THAAD structural components. The undersecretary quoted in the report said the aim was to support "the tripling of PAC-3 and quadrupling of THAAD interceptor production."

## The caveat investors should keep in view

Framework agreements are not funded orders. CSIS analyst Tom Karako, quoted by DefenseScoop, called the Northrop deals "agreements to agree," not contracts, and said none of it is real until appropriations arrive. The Pentagon is also running under a stopgap budget, as we covered earlier this month, so the distance between a headline multiple and booked revenue is real.

Two further points cut against reading these as pure upside:

- **Qualification risk.** Avio's Extended Range GMLRS motors are still pending qualification, and its Mk104 motor for the Standard Missile family has a critical design review slated for spring 2027. Second sources help only once they are certified.
- **Timing.** New capacity arrives in 2027 to 2029. Near-term output depends on existing plants.

## The L3Harris angle

L3Harris is the clearest public-market proxy for this theme, and it has a catalyst soon: it [reports third-quarter results before the open on Thursday, Oct. 29](https://www.l3harris.com/newsroom/press-release/2026/10/l3harris-sets-date-third-quarter-2026-earnings-release), with a call at 10:30 a.m. ET.

The missile business is also at the center of a corporate question. L3Harris planned to spin off and list the unit, but [postponed the IPO to mid-2027 at the earliest](https://breakingdefense.com/2026/07/l3harris-postpones-missile-unit-ipo-until-mid-2027/). CEO Chris Kubasik said "the market conditions do not reflect the value we're building," and executives also cited budget uncertainty and the midterm elections. On the same call, the company said Missile Solutions revenue was about $2 billion in the first half of 2026, up from $1.7 billion a year earlier, and that it is negotiating more than $20 billion in new contracts, which would triple the unit's backlog.

Those are management's statements, and the contracts in negotiation are not yet signed. That makes the Oct. 29 call a useful checkpoint on how much of the framework volume has been definitized.

## What to watch next

- **Definitization.** Whether the PAC-3 and THAAD propulsion frameworks become priced contracts by year-end, as L3Harris said to expect.
- **L3Harris's Oct. 29 commentary** on Missile Solutions backlog, Camden's opening, and the IPO timeline.
- **Appropriations.** A full-year defense bill is what converts "agreements to agree" into funded orders for LHX, NOC and the primes that depend on them.
- **Second-source qualification** at Avio USA and the pace at which newer entrants like X-Bow move from selection to full-rate production.

For investors in the sector, the takeaway is structural rather than tactical: interceptor and rocket output is gated by propulsion, so awards to motor suppliers are an early read on how fast the primes can actually deliver. This is information, not a recommendation on any security.
