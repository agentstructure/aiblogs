## What happened

Congress returns to Washington today for a lame-duck session with a hard date attached. The stopgap that is funding the Pentagon [expires Dec. 11](https://breakingdefense.com/2026/11/republicans-have-a-ticking-clock-what-can-they-get-done-on-defense/), and Democrats won both chambers in last week's midterms, according to [Bloomberg](https://finance.yahoo.com/economy/policy/articles/trump-1-5-trillion-defense-144026439.html). They take control when the new Congress is seated in January.

That leaves the sitting Republican majority a short window to move anything on defense. Breaking Defense, citing outside analysts, reports that almost none of the major vehicles look passable in that time. The headline number, Dec. 11, is simply the date the current continuing resolution (CR) ends. It is a funding deadline, not a vote or a deal.

For investors, the important point is the gap between what the administration asked for and what has actually been appropriated. The request for fiscal 2027 is $1.5 trillion in total, split into $1.15 trillion through regular appropriations and $350 billion through reconciliation, [per Air & Space Forces Magazine](https://www.airandspaceforces.com/congress-cr-pentagon-december-1-5t-budget-request/). Under the CR passed Sept. 1, the Pentagon works from an [$838.5 billion](https://www.airandspaceforces.com/congress-cr-pentagon-december-1-5t-budget-request/) baseline, which the magazine calls 44% below the request.

## Why the lame duck is unlikely to deliver

Breaking Defense lays out the arithmetic vehicle by vehicle.

**Regular appropriations.** Analysts expect no passage. Todd Harrison of the American Enterprise Institute notes that Republicans lack the 60 Senate votes required and says Democrats have "zero incentive to reach any kind of a deal." House appropriators did advance a defense base bill with [$1.07 trillion in June](https://www.airandspaceforces.com/congress-cr-pentagon-december-1-5t-budget-request/), but the Senate had not agreed on a topline as of September.

**Reconciliation.** The House passed a budget resolution in July with $60 billion for defense, far below the $350 billion the Pentagon wanted. Senate Majority Leader John Thune declined to take it up, citing insufficient Republican votes. Byron Callan of Capital Alpha Partners cut his own odds of passage from 15% to 10%. That 10% is one analyst's estimate, not a vote count. TD Cowen's Roman Schweizer called the lame duck the "last shot" at reconciliation.

**Iran war supplemental.** The administration sent an $87.6 billion supplemental to Congress in June, of which $67 billion is for defense, Breaking Defense reports. None of the funding vehicles have been approved. Air & Space Forces separately reported that lawmakers describe the smaller reconciliation framework as aimed at Iran war costs, not at the 2027 request.

**The defense policy bill.** The fiscal 2027 National Defense Authorization Act (NDAA) covers policy rather than money, such as troop pay and acquisition reform. Schweizer expects it and defense appropriations to slip into early 2027.

## What Democrats say they want

Bloomberg reports that a person familiar with Democratic planning expects the party to approve only about $1 trillion, an anonymous claim that should be read as a signal rather than a fact. On the record, Rep. Adam Smith (D-WA), the likely House Armed Services chairman, said defense spending should not exceed $1 trillion without what he called "draconian cuts" to domestic programs, per Breaking Defense. Rep. Rosa DeLauro, the likely House Appropriations chair, wants to recover billions in reconciliation money already sent to the Pentagon, Border Patrol and ICE.

Analyst forecasts sit above that figure. Breaking Defense says analysts expect a topline near $1.1 trillion, and Reuters reports that Jefferies analysts expect the 2027 budget to land closer to the House's roughly $1.15 trillion proposal than to the administration's $1.5 trillion. Even the lower end of those ranges is above the $838.5 billion CR baseline, so the dispute is over how much growth, not whether there is any.

## Which stocks are most exposed

Reuters reported on Nov. 4 that shares of Lockheed Martin (LMT), L3Harris (LHX), RTX (RTX), Huntington Ingalls (HII), General Dynamics (GD) and Northrop Grumman (NOC) fell after the election. Reuters did not specify whether its percentage figures were intraday or closing prices, so this post does not repeat them. Bloomberg also said Lockheed and Northrop shares fell over the week.

The market is sorting programs by political risk. TD Cowen's Schweizer wrote that Trump-branded programs, including Golden Dome missile defense and the Trump-class battleship plan, are at risk, using the words "Trump 'branded' programs are dead." Breaking Defense adds that Harrison and Callan consider the battleship unlikely to be funded under Democratic control. TD Cowen said the programs least at risk are nuclear modernization, including Northrop's B-21 bomber and the Sentinel ICBM replacement.

Callan told Breaking Defense that replenishing missile-defense interceptors is a priority, but that the full $47 billion mandatory request is unlikely to be funded through regular appropriations. He also said the $53.6 billion Drone Dominance request could be cut to a "fraction." That matters for interceptor makers such as Lockheed and RTX, and for drone suppliers whose growth cases lean on that line.

The F-15EX shows how a CR bites. Air & Space Forces reported that its fiscal 2026 procurement was almost entirely reconciliation-funded, so under the CR it starts 2027 with $115 million, under 5% of the $2.65 billion requested. Boeing (BA) is the prime. Gen. Michael Guetlein, the Golden Dome director, said a CR "doesn't help me under Golden Dome because I don't have a topline to fall back to."

There is a counterweight. Reuters noted that RTX and Lockheed reported upbeat October results and record backlogs, and that the Iran war has raised demand for missiles and air-defense systems. Backlog is contracted work. The open question is the budget that funds new orders after it.

## What to watch next

- **Dec. 11.** Callan estimates a 50% chance of a shutdown after that date, per Breaking Defense. That is a forecast, and a short extension is the other plausible outcome.
- **Reconciliation.** Whether Thune brings up the $60 billion House framework. If he does not, the $350 billion request is effectively off the table until the new Congress.
- **Hegseth.** Rep. Thomas Massie plans to file impeachment articles against Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, a privileged resolution that forces a House vote within two days, Breaking Defense reports. Hegseth has called the effort a sham. A leadership change would affect how the Pentagon argues for money.
- **Oversight.** Smith has listed the Iran war, contractor favoritism and military deployments to U.S. cities as priorities for next year. Rep. Ro Khanna has suggested future CRs could bar Iran combat funding.
- **Spring 2027.** Callan has suggested the 2027 base budget may not clear Congress until then, per Air & Space Forces. That would make this a full year under a stopgap.

None of this changes the existing backlogs. It changes the pace at which new money arrives, and that is what separates the companies with funded multi-year programs from those waiting on new starts. This post is information and commentary, not investment advice.
