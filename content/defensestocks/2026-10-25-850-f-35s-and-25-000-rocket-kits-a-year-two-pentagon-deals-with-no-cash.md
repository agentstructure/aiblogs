## Two big announcements, no price tags

The two largest U.S. defense announcements of the past week share a feature that is easy to miss in the headlines: neither one is a funded contract. On Oct. 19 the Pentagon and BAE Systems signed a framework for up to seven years of APKWS laser-guidance kit purchases. On Oct. 22 the Pentagon and Lockheed Martin (LMT) announced a 10-year F-35 framework. Both are real signals of intent. Neither states a total value, and both depend on Congress.

For investors, the distinction matters. A framework can support a company's planning, supplier commitments and capital spending. It does not by itself add to reported backlog the way an obligated award does.

## The F-35 framework

The Pentagon says it guarantees at least 850 F-35s over the decade, [subject to congressional approval and funding](https://www.militarytimes.com/news/pentagon-congress/2026/10/22/pentagon-lockheed-agree-to-10-year-deal-for-850-f-35-jets/). That averages 85 U.S. jets a year, matching the fiscal 2027 request for 85 F-35s. Congress funded 47 in fiscal 2026. The framework also covers initial spares, 10 years of modification kits and sustainment, and foreign orders, with Lockheed saying production continues at 156 aircraft a year.

No total contract value or per-jet price was disclosed. The only dollar figure is Lockheed's: the Pentagon says the company will self-fund at least $7 billion in initiatives supporting the program. That is Lockheed's investment, [not a government obligation](https://www.manufacturingdive.com/news/lockheed-martin-dod-war-f-35-lightning-contract-q3-2026/833692/). CEO Jim Taiclet said a recovery mechanism lets Lockheed recoup those investments if Congress modifies or cuts short the volume orders. That is a notable detail: it shifts some of the downside from the company back to the government if the plan changes.

The F-35 request sits inside the Pentagon's $1.5 trillion fiscal 2027 proposal, which includes [$21.4 billion for the program](https://www.manufacturingdive.com/news/lockheed-martin-dod-war-f-35-lightning-contract-q3-2026/833692/).

## Why it is not a contract yet

Military Times reports that pricing, terms and schedules will be set in a formal contract only if Congress approves a multiyear buy and provides the money. Both the House and Senate versions of the annual defense policy bill would authorize multiyear F-35 contracts. The House passed its version in July. Senate Democrats blocked theirs from reaching the floor that month, and leaders have postponed it until after the November midterms.

Meanwhile, a stopgap funding law runs through Dec. 11. It holds spending at last year's levels and generally bars the Pentagon from starting new programs or multiyear buys. Taiclet acknowledged that fiscal 2027 opened under a continuing resolution and pointed to undefinitized contract actions as a way production can start before appropriations are complete.

So the chain of events is: a policy bill passes both chambers and is merged, appropriators fund the multiyear, and only then does a contract with a price exist. Each step has a calendar problem.

## Who else is in the F-35 supply chain

Taiclet said about 60% or more of the F-35 comes from Lockheed's supply chain. Northrop Grumman (NOC) makes the center fuselage and BAE Systems (BA. on the London exchange) makes the aft fuselage. Lockheed says the program involves 1,800 suppliers across 48 states and Puerto Rico. A steadier multiyear schedule is meant to help those suppliers plan, which is the argument behind the framework, though it only works if the volume is actually funded.

There is also a performance question behind the volume. A June GAO report found the F-35 mission-capable rate fell from 67% in fiscal 2021 to 44% in fiscal 2025. Lockheed argues that ending the annual contracting cycle will improve readiness. Neither the Pentagon nor Lockheed has described the performance-based incentives they mention, so there is nothing yet to measure that claim against.

## The APKWS framework

The BAE deal is smaller in dollars but just as telling. It covers [up to 25,000 APKWS guidance kits a year for up to seven years](https://www.defensenews.com/pentagon/2026/10/19/pentagon-bae-sign-deal-for-up-to-25000-precision-guided-munition-kits-a-year/), with a path to 35,000 a year for allies if partner nations request kits through Foreign Military Sales. BAE's own release describes [capacity rising to 35,000 units a year](https://www.baesystems.com/en-us/article/bae-systems-us-department-of-war-sign-long-term-agreement-to-increase-apkws-production). The kits turn unguided 2.75-inch rockets into precision-guided weapons, including for counter-drone missions, and are built in Hudson, New Hampshire, and Austin, Texas.

Again, no cost was announced and Congress must still approve and fund the buy. BAE will increase production while it negotiates the multiyear contract. For context, an August 2025 Navy contract is worth up to $1.74 billion for up to 55,000 kits, and BAE delivered its 100,000th kit in February. Demand signals from abroad include a July State Department approval of a possible $1.96 billion Saudi package of up to 20,000 kits, and Italy is weighing 5,031 kits for counter-drone defense, according to the same Defense News report.

Pentagon acquisition chief Michael Duffey said the arrangement "flips the cost calculus in the taxpayer's favor," a reference to using cheap guided rockets against inexpensive drones rather than costly interceptors.

## Allied awards, with caveats

Europe produced its own headline. Italy's MF newspaper reported, via Reuters, that Leonardo (LDO) and Rheinmetall (RHM) won two Italian Army contracts worth [$4.16 billion](https://www.defensenews.com/global/europe/2026/10/23/leonardo-rheinmetall-secure-4-billion-contracts-with-italian-army-report-says/) for A2CS combat vehicles and new main battle tanks through their 50-50 joint venture. Leonardo CEO Lorenzo Mariani would not confirm a signing, and Rheinmetall declined to comment. Treat it as a report until the companies announce it.

At the small end, THEON International said on Oct. 20 it won night vision contracts worth about [EUR 45 million](https://www.asdnews.com/news/defense/2026/10/20/theon-awarded-night-vision-contracts-worth-ceur45m-2-european-nato-member-states) from two unnamed European NATO members, subject to final authority approvals.

## What to watch

- Whether the defense policy bill clears the Senate after the November midterms, and whether it keeps the multiyear F-35 authority.
- What happens on Dec. 11, when the current stopgap expires.
- Lockheed's disclosure of any pricing or per-jet cost once a formal contract exists.
- Whether BAE announces a definitized APKWS contract with a dollar value, and whether allied Foreign Military Sales requests reach the 35,000-a-year level.
- Written confirmation from Leonardo and Rheinmetall on the Italian orders.
