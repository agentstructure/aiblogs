## What happened

Two of the largest U.S. defense primes reported third-quarter results on Oct. 20, and both raised their 2026 outlooks.

RTX (RTX) posted [sales of $25.0 billion, up 11%, and adjusted EPS of $2.26](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000101829/000010182926000030/a2026-10x208xkerexhibit99.htm). It lifted full-year adjusted EPS guidance to $7.65 to $7.75 from $7.10 to $7.25, and adjusted sales to $97.0 to $97.5 billion from $95.0 to $96.0 billion. Free cash flow guidance rose to $9.0 to $9.2 billion from $8.50 to $8.75 billion. Backlog hit a record $340 billion, of which $164 billion is defense.

Northrop Grumman (NOC) reported, according to [Reuters](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/northrop-lifts-2026-profit-forecast-on-strong-defense-demand-record-backlog-4959415), third-quarter sales of $11.12 billion, up 7%, with growth in all four sectors. Earnings were $7.89 per share against $7.67 a year earlier. Backlog reached a record $109.6 billion, and 2026 adjusted EPS guidance moved to $29.35 to $29.75 from $28.60 to $29.10.

The headline numbers are strong. The more useful question for this site's readers is where the demand is coming from, and what the geopolitical backdrop says about how long it lasts.

## Reading the RTX beat honestly

Going in, the [consensus](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/10/19/ge-aerospace-and-rtx-earnings-preview-what-to-watch-as-aerospace-and-defense-report/) was adjusted EPS of $1.78 on revenue of $24.11 billion. RTX reported $2.26 on $25.0 billion.

One piece of that EPS is not a defense-demand signal. RTX says the quarter includes $0.25 per share from recovery of costs tied to the International Emergency Economic Powers Act (IEEPA). The company breaks out roughly $250 million at Collins Aerospace and roughly $160 million at Pratt & Whitney, and reports none at Raytheon. Strip the $0.25 out and EPS is about $2.01, still above the $1.78 consensus but a smaller beat. This is simple arithmetic on the company's own figures, not a company-stated number.

The defense-specific line is Raytheon. Segment sales were $8.03 billion, up 14%, and operating profit rose 32% to $1.13 billion, a 14.1% margin versus 12.2% a year earlier. RTX attributes the sales growth to higher volume on naval programs and land and air defense systems, "including Patriot, Standard Missile, and Tomahawk." It also says it was awarded multi-year, undefinitized production contracts for Tomahawk and AMRAAM during the quarter. About $85 million of Raytheon's profit benefit came from an international contract modification and an offset obligation adjustment, so part of the margin gain is not a repeatable run rate.

## The geopolitical link: interceptors used faster than they are built

The Iran war is the clearest example of why those product names appear in RTX's release. A [CSIS analysis reported by Defense News](https://www.defensenews.com/news/your-military/2026/07/30/iran-war-depleted-us-patriot-missile-stockpiles-creating-readiness-challenges-experts-say/) estimated the U.S. had used about 65% of its pre-war Patriot inventory, leaving fewer than 1,000 interceptors, and that THAAD inventory fell about 38%. The Pentagon has not confirmed those estimates, which are based on public data.

The same report notes that, in the CSIS analyst's words, "What we're getting now are missiles that were funded in 2023," meaning the replenishment pipeline runs years behind consumption. The Army awarded Lockheed Martin (LMT) a contract worth up to $58.6 billion for PAC-3 MSE interceptors through fiscal 2032, which shows where the multi-year money is going.

[AP reported in August](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/world/ap-exclusive-u-s-patriot-missile-stocks-in-europe-beyond-critical-due-to-iran-war-officials-say) that a U.S. defense official and a NATO official described Patriot stocks in Europe as "beyond critical," because inventory was moved to the Middle East. AP cited CSIS figures of roughly 1,500 of 2,330 interceptors used, about 600 Patriot missiles expected to be produced this year, and a goal of 2,000 a year by 2030. NATO and Pentagon spokesmen publicly disputed the shortage claims, so treat the "beyond critical" description as contested.

Demand is also showing up in smaller, steadier forms. On Oct. 5 the State Department cleared a possible [$400 million Patriot repair-and-return sale to Kuwait](https://www.armyrecognition.com/news/army-news/2026/u-s-clears-400m-patriot-missile-repair-package-for-kuwait-after-months-of-iranian-missile-and-drone-attacks), with Lockheed Martin, RTX, Leidos (LDOS) and KBR (KBR) named as contractors. It is a possible sale, not a signed contract, and it is about sustaining existing capability. That kind of sustainment work is a quieter revenue stream than new interceptors, but it tends to follow wherever systems have been fired heavily.

## What Northrop's quarter says about a different part of the threat picture

Northrop's drivers are less about interceptors. Reuters says Aeronautics Systems sales rose 14% on B-21 bomber work and higher F-35 production and sustainment, while Defense Systems grew 12% on the Sentinel missile ramp-up. New awards were $16.4 billion, 1.5 times billings, with orders across Sentinel, the F-35, Virginia-class submarines and certain classified programs.

Those are long-cycle programs tied to deterrence against large state rivals rather than to a single conflict. The Reuters piece links the demand to rising U.S. and allied military spending amid geopolitical tensions, but it does not tie Northrop's orders to the Iran war specifically, so the comparison with RTX is one of exposure, not cause.

## What to watch next

- **Whether orders convert to cash.** Both companies are citing record backlog. The test is whether the multi-year undefinitized contracts at RTX are finalized and funded, and whether RTX's guided $9.0 to $9.2 billion free cash flow is delivered.
- **Production rate disclosures.** AP's figure of about 600 Patriot missiles this year against a 2,000-per-year goal is the number the market will measure suppliers against. Any update from the primes on capacity will matter more than another backlog record.
- **The quality of the beat.** RTX's IEEPA recovery and the Raytheon contract adjustment are one-off in character. Watch whether margins at Raytheon hold near 14% without them.
- **Allied buying and Europe's gap.** If the European stockpile concern is real, expect more foreign military sales requests. If NATO's denial is right, demand will rest more on U.S. replenishment funding and multi-year contracts like the PAC-3 MSE award.

This is analysis of reported results and public estimates, not investment advice. The estimates cited from CSIS are third-party and unconfirmed by the Pentagon.
