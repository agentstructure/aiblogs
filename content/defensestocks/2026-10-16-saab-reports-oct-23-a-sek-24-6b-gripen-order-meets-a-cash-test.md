## The date and the setup

Saab (SAAB-B.ST; OTC: SAABY) publishes its January to September interim report on Friday, Oct. 23, at 07:30 CET, with a presentation at 10:00 CET led by CEO Micael Johansson and CFO Anna Wijkander, according to the [company's invitation](https://www.placera.se/pressmeddelanden/saab-invitation-to-saabs-q3-2026-interim-report-presentation-20261009). It is the first big European defense print of the third-quarter season, ahead of Rheinmetall (RHM.DE), which has told the market it will report on [Nov. 5](https://www.marketscreener.com/news/rheinmetall-ag-preliminary-announcement-of-the-publication-of-quarterly-reports-and-quarterly-inter-ce7e58dade8ef621).

For anyone tracking the European rearmament cycle, Saab is a clean read. It sells fighters, submarines, missiles, radars and combat systems, so its numbers show how fast government budgets are converting into signed contracts and then into deliveries. The quarter ahead has one known headline item. Whether it moves the picture depends on what sits around it.

## The order that should land in Q3

On June 30, Saab and Sweden signed a contract for 16 Gripen E fighters for Ukraine, worth about SEK 24.6 billion, with spare parts and associated equipment. Deliveries are scheduled for 2029 to 2030, and Saab said the order [will be booked in the third quarter](https://www.saab.com/newsroom/press-releases/2026/saab-signs-contract-for-gripen-e-for-ukraine). The press release does not say how the deal is financed, so this piece does not either.

Analysts were already counting on it. Inderes, in its [July preview](https://www.inderes.se/analyst-comments/saab-q226-preview-backlog-conversion-continues-to-set-the-tone), put the Ukraine contract at SEK 24.6 billion and expected it to be booked in Q3. That means Oct. 23 is unlikely to surprise on the existence of the order. The questions are timing of revenue recognition (deliveries are three years out or more, so this is backlog, not near-term sales) and what else came in beside it.

## What Q2 showed, and what it hid

Saab's [Q2 report](https://news.cision.com/saab/r/saab-q2-results-2026--strong-order-bookings-reinforce-market-position,c4375551) was strong on the surface:

- Order intake was SEK 68,393 million, against SEK 28,403 million a year earlier.
- Sales were SEK 25,453 million (SEK 19,786 million), with organic growth of 29.8%.
- Operating income rose 41% to SEK 2,794 million, an 11.0% margin versus 10.0%.
- EPS was SEK 3.96 versus SEK 2.83.

The same release says the SEK 47 billion Polish submarine order was booked in the quarter. Strip that out and the remainder is roughly SEK 21.4 billion, below the prior-year figure of SEK 28.4 billion. That is my arithmetic, and the prior-year quarter may itself have contained large awards, so it is not proof of weakness. But it shows how much a single naval program can swing headline intake, and why the underlying flow of small and mid-sized orders is worth checking in the Q3 report.

The same logic applies to Q3. With a SEK 24.6 billion Gripen order expected in the quarter, the reported number will likely look large again. A reader trying to judge demand breadth should look at orders excluding the largest one or two contracts.

## The cash line is the one to watch

The least comfortable number in Q2 was cash. Operational cash flow was SEK -62 million, an improvement from SEK -1,136 million a year earlier but still negative in a quarter where operating income was SEK 2.8 billion. Net liquidity fell to SEK 2,523 million from SEK 3,989 million at the end of 2025.

That gap is typical of a company growing near 30% organically: it builds inventory, funds capacity and waits for customer milestone payments. Saab has said it continues to expand capacity. Johansson described demand in the Q2 release as high, with customers investing in "both immediate and long-term needs." Growth of this kind is only as good as the cash it eventually turns into. If Q3 shows operating cash flow turning clearly positive, it supports the case that the backlog is converting. If it stays near zero while sales climb again, the market will ask when.

Inderes also flagged two margin risks before the Q2 print: start-up costs on the T-7A trainer program in Aeronautics and possible delivery delays in Dynamics pushing into the second half. Those are second-half questions, so the Q3 margin line and any commentary on Dynamics deliveries matter.

## How it fits the wider European picture

Saab is not the only name showing the same pattern of record orders and a long conversion runway. In its H1 results, Hensoldt (HAG.DE) reported order intake of EUR 2,812 million, roughly double the prior year, and a record backlog of EUR 10.356 billion, according to a [results summary](https://www.grosswald.org/data/articles/hensoldt-leonardo-h1-2026-results-order-intake-doubles-10-4-billion-backlog-leonardo-guidance-upgrade.json). Its revenue rose 23.6%, and it confirmed rather than raised full-year guidance. The same source's headline says Leonardo (LDO.MI) lifted its 2026 guidance; this piece relies on the summary for Hensoldt only.

The common thread is that order books are growing faster than revenue. That is what a rearmament cycle looks like early on. It also means the debate is shifting from "are governments ordering?" to "how quickly can suppliers deliver, and at what margin?"

## What to watch on Oct. 23

- **Order intake ex-large contracts.** Is the flow beyond the Gripen E deal steady or lumpy?
- **Operating cash flow.** A move from near zero to clearly positive would address the Q2 concern.
- **Margin versus 11.0%.** Watch Aeronautics start-up costs and any Dynamics delivery slippage.
- **Organic growth versus the Q2 rate of 29.8%.** Does capacity expansion keep sales near 30%?
- **Backlog and delivery commentary.** The Gripen E order adds work for 2029 to 2030, so the questions are about production slots and supplier capacity.

This is information, not a recommendation. Saab's report will give the first hard evidence this quarter of whether European defense order books are turning into cash, and Rheinmetall's Nov. 5 report is the next test.
