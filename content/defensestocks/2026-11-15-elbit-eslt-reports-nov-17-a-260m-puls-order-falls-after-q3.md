## What happened

On Nov. 12, Elbit Systems (ESLT) [announced](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/elbit-systems-awarded-260-million-contract-to-supply-puls-rocket-artillery-systems-to-a-european-nato-country-302937528.html) a contract worth about $260 million to supply its Precise & Universal Launching System (PULS) rocket artillery systems and munitions to a European NATO member. The contract runs five years. The release does not name the customer.

PULS fires unguided rockets, precision-guided munitions and missiles of various ranges from wheeled or tracked vehicles. The company's chief executive, Bezhalel "Butzi" Machlis, said in the release that continued selection of PULS by European and NATO customers reflects the system's operational maturity and versatility.

The timing matters for anyone reading Elbit's next report. The company [said on Oct. 28](https://www.prnewswire.com/il/news-releases/elbit-systems-to-report-third-quarter-2026-financial-results-on-november-17-2026-302921851.html) that it will publish third-quarter results on Tuesday, Nov. 17, with a call at 9:00 a.m. Eastern. The third quarter ended Sept. 30. A contract announced Nov. 12 falls in the fourth quarter, so it cannot be in the backlog figure Elbit reports on Tuesday.

## Sizing the order against the backlog

Elbit's last reported backlog was $32.0 billion as of June 30, according to its [second-quarter results](https://www.aviation-defence-universe.com/elbit-systems-reports-second-quarter-2026-results/). Against that figure, $260 million is roughly 0.8%. That is simple arithmetic on two reported numbers, not a company-stated share.

A five-year term also means the revenue arrives slowly. Spread evenly, $260 million would be about $52 million a year, though the release gives no delivery schedule, and defense deliveries are rarely flat. For a company that reported $2,287.1 million of revenue in a single quarter, the order is meaningful as a signal of European demand and small as a driver of any one year's sales.

This is also not the only recent PULS award. In April, Greece [signed a roughly $750 million deal](https://breakingdefense.com/2026/04/greece-inks-750m-deal-with-israel-for-elbits-puls-rocket-artillery-systems/) with Israel's Ministry of Defense covering launchers, training rockets, precision-guided rockets and loitering munitions, with four years of implementation and a further ten years of follow-on support, according to Breaking Defense. On the second-quarter call, management [described the Greek award](https://www.elbitsystems.com/sites/default/files/2026-08/Q2_2026%20%20transcript%20.pdf) as strengthening PULS' position as a leading rocket artillery solution in Europe. Because it was announced April 6, inside the second quarter, it was presumably already reflected in the $32.0 billion.

Calibre Defence [reported](https://www.calibredefence.co.uk/elbit-puls-extra-mr-contract-european-customer/) that Elbit announced another PULS and Extra-MR rocket order from an unnamed European customer on Oct. 19, valued at $235 million with delivery over three years. That figure rests on that report alone, and I have not confirmed it against Elbit's own release. If it is accurate, the two post-quarter PULS awards together are about $495 million, which would be added to fourth-quarter orders rather than the third.

## What the last quarter established

The second quarter gives Tuesday's report a clear baseline. From the [call transcript](https://www.elbitsystems.com/sites/default/files/2026-08/Q2_2026%20%20transcript%20.pdf):

- Revenue rose 15.9% to $2,287.1 million from $1,973 million a year earlier.
- Backlog reached $32.0 billion. Management said the quarterly increase was driven predominantly by international customers, mainly in Europe, and the chief financial officer cited 34% year-over-year backlog growth against 16% revenue growth.
- About 73% of the backlog came from outside Israel.
- About 42% was scheduled to be performed in the rest of 2026 and in 2027, with the rest in 2028 and beyond. On a $32.0 billion base, that is roughly $13.4 billion by the end of 2027 and $18.6 billion after.
- Europe contributed 25% of second-quarter revenue, North America 20%, Asia-Pacific 14% and Israel 37%. Management attributed the Israeli share partly to inventory replenishment after the conflict with Iran that it said ended at the beginning of April.
- GAAP net income was [$173.6 million](https://www.aviation-defence-universe.com/elbit-systems-reports-second-quarter-2026-results/). Cash from operations was $237 million and free cash flow was $150 million, against $71 million a year earlier, with cash conversion of 86%.

Those last figures are the useful counterweight to the order headlines. Elbit has been converting backlog into cash in the second quarter, helped by what management called a strong increase in contract liabilities, which typically reflects customer advances. Whether that holds is a separate question from whether orders keep arriving.

## Why it matters for investors in European rearmament

PULS is a case study in how European ground-fires budgets are being spent. Buyers are choosing a mature, export-proven launcher that can sit on vehicles they already own, rather than waiting for domestic systems. Elbit has also teamed with KNDS on EuroPULS, a German joint venture to market the system to European customers, according to the second-quarter call. That structure is aimed at the political question of buying Israeli-designed hardware with local industrial content.

The concentration risk is on the other side of the ledger. Single-country orders are lumpy, unnamed customers make them hard to track, and a five-year performance window means a backlog dollar booked today is not a sales dollar for years. Elbit's own scheduling data shows the same pattern: more than half of its backlog sits in 2028 or later.

None of this requires the new order to be large. The relevant question for the shares is whether Europe keeps supplying a steady run of mid-sized awards, because the second-quarter increase in backlog was attributed mainly to that region. Three PULS announcements in roughly seven months, from April 6 to Nov. 12, fit that pattern, even though only two of them are confirmed here from primary sources.

## What to watch Tuesday

- **Backlog versus $32.0 billion.** Whether the September 30 figure is higher, flat or lower will show how much Europe kept ordering before the Greek award had fully settled in. Watch the share from outside Israel against the 73% baseline.
- **Revenue against $2,287.1 million.** The company noted continued sequential revenue growth in the second quarter. Another sequential rise would show conversion is keeping pace with backlog.
- **Free cash flow and contract liabilities.** The second quarter's $150 million benefited from customer advances. A reversal there would matter more than any single award.
- **Management's comments on October and November orders.** Executives may reference the Nov. 12 PULS award and any other post-quarter wins on the call, and the earlier of those would shape how the fourth-quarter backlog starts.
- **Regional mix.** Europe was 25% of second-quarter revenue. A higher figure would line up with the order flow; a lower one would point to the lag between signing and delivery.
