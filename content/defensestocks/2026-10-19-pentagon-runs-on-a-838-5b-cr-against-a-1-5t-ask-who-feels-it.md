## The gap between the ask and the cash

The Pentagon began fiscal 2027 on Oct. 1 without a defense appropriations bill. President Trump [signed H.R. 6500](https://www.whitehouse.gov/briefings-statements/2026/09/congressional-bill-h-r-6500-signed-into-law/) on Sept. 2, a continuing resolution that funds the government through Dec. 11, 2026. The House passed it 370-48 and the Senate had passed it 90-6 on Aug. 8, according to the [Committee for a Responsible Federal Budget](https://www.crfb.org/blogs/appropriations-watch-fy-2027).

For defense contractors the headline is the size of the mismatch. The administration wants $1.5 trillion for fiscal 2027: $1.15 trillion through regular appropriations and $350 billion through reconciliation. Under the CR, the Pentagon [operates on a 2026 baseline of $838.5 billion](https://www.airandspaceforces.com/congress-cr-pentagon-december-1-5t-budget-request/), which Air & Space Forces Magazine calculates is 44 percent below the request. The CRFB puts enacted fiscal 2026 defense funding at $838.7 billion and the base request at $1.154 trillion, before the $350 billion reconciliation ask.

That is a gap of roughly $311 billion on the base budget alone. It will not close before the November midterms.

## What a CR actually blocks

A stopgap is not a cut, but it freezes the calendar. As [CSIS explains](https://www.csis.org/index%2ephp/analysis/tracking-fy-2027-defense-appropriations-reconciliation-and-supplemental-request), under a CR the Defense Department cannot start new programs or raise production rates without a specific exception, called an anomaly. A CR also covers only regular discretionary programs, not reconciliation money. CSIS notes DOD has started 21 of the last 27 fiscal years under a CR or shutdown, and the last time it began a year with full-year appropriations was fiscal 2019.

So the first-order effect is timing, not total dollars. Contracts that depend on a funding increase, a new start or a faster production line wait. Contracts funded at last year's rate continue.

The F-15EX shows how this works. Air & Space Forces Magazine reports the program starts fiscal 2027 with $115 million under the CR, less than 5 percent of the $2.65 billion requested. F-15EX is a Boeing (BA) program, so the company's fighter line is a direct example of a request that has outrun the stopgap. A funding delay of that kind usually shows up as slower orders or lumpier timing at the prime and its suppliers, not an immediate hit to reported backlog.

## Golden Dome and autonomy depend on reconciliation

The programs most exposed are those that lean on the reconciliation request, since a CR does nothing for them. Space Force Gen. Michael Guetlein, who leads Golden Dome, warned that [a CR "doesn't help me under Golden Dome because I don't have a topline to fall back to."](https://www.airandspaceforces.com/congress-cr-pentagon-december-1-5t-budget-request/) CSIS reports that DOD requested $17.1 billion of the $17.9 billion Golden Dome total through reconciliation.

[Breaking Defense](https://breakingdefense.com/2026/09/with-reconciliation-uncertain-pentagon-eyeing-plan-to-retool-fy27-priorities-with-congress/) reports that Guetlein warned Golden Dome work could halt in October without funding, and that the leader of the drone task force, Lt. Gen. Steven Marks, warned the Defense Autonomous Warfare Group could lose momentum. That group's full request is $53.6 billion, and a Pentagon official said it may not be fully funded. The same report says the Pentagon is preparing a plan, if the full $350 billion does not pass, to work with lawmakers on moving key priorities into the base budget. That would mean cutting other investments or deferring some to fiscal 2028. Acting comptroller Jules Hurst called it too premature to say what the trade-offs would be.

For investors this sorts the sector into two groups:

- **Programs already funded and in production**, including those backed by earlier multiyear contracts or the roughly $157 billion in defense money from the 2025 One Big Beautiful Bill Act, which is available through fiscal 2029 per CSIS. These are largely insulated from the CR.
- **Programs that need new money in fiscal 2027**, including Golden Dome, large autonomy efforts and production increases. These carry the timing risk.

Companies are not neatly split between the two. Large primes such as Lockheed Martin (LMT), RTX (RTX), Northrop Grumman (NOC), General Dynamics (GD) and Boeing (BA) hold both kinds of work, so the useful question for each is how much of its growth plan sits in the second bucket.

## Reconciliation is the real swing factor

The $350 billion reconciliation request is where the uncertainty concentrates. CSIS reports the House passed a fiscal 2027 budget resolution in July with $60 billion for defense, and the Senate's version also carries $60 billion, though its vote was postponed past the August recess and Majority Leader John Thune and others were skeptical a bill could pass before the midterms. Air & Space Forces Magazine adds that lawmakers describe the $60 billion as replenishment for the Iran war rather than fiscal 2027 needs. That is a small fraction of what was requested.

Akin Gump's [Sept. 29 note](https://www.akingump.com/en/insights/blogs/the-approps-drop/omb-reaches-for-congresss-purse-strings-but-december-is-coming) describes a possible "Reconciliation 3.0" package with a defense component of nearly $100 billion if Republicans pursue one after the midterms. It also says Congress will have roughly five legislative weeks after the election before the CR expires, and that Senate appropriators are not expected to act on fiscal 2027 bills before Election Day.

## What to watch

- **The Dec. 11 deadline.** Another CR, an omnibus or a shutdown are the possible paths. Capital Alpha Partners' Byron Callan told Air & Space Forces Magazine the base budget may not pass until spring 2027.
- **Anomaly requests.** Any exceptions the Pentagon negotiates for munitions, shipbuilding or Golden Dome would signal where Congress will allow early spending.
- **The NDAA.** The House passed its version 216-212 at about $1.15 trillion, and CSIS says the Senate's cloture vote failed 50-46 in July. The authorization bill sets policy rather than funding, but its final topline is a marker for how far Congress is willing to go.
- **Reconciliation size.** A figure near $60 billion versus one near $100 billion or more would change the outlook for the programs that depend on it.
- **Cash conversion language on earnings calls.** Management comments on fiscal 2027 timing, order phasing and whether guidance assumes a long CR are the quickest read on exposure.

## The bottom line

A long CR rarely breaks a defense contractor's year, but this one lands against a very large requested increase. The biggest uncertainty is not the $838.5 billion that is flowing but the roughly $660 billion difference between that and the request, and especially the $350 billion that depends on a reconciliation bill nobody has scheduled. Until Congress picks a path, expect budget headlines to move sentiment more than contract awards do.
