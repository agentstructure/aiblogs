## What happened

Boeing (BA) reported third-quarter results on Oct. 27, and the headline for defense investors is the Defense, Space & Security (BDS) segment. According to the [earnings release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000012927/000162828026067411/a202609sep308kprex991.htm), BDS revenue rose 15% to $7.94 billion and backlog reached a record $100.5 billion, with 23% of it from customers outside the U.S.

Earnings from operations came in at $492 million, a 6.2% margin. A year earlier the segment earned $114 million on $6.90 billion of revenue, a 1.7% margin, based on the figures in the [10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000012927/000162828026067508/ba-20260930.htm).

That is a sharp swing, and it is where the careful reading starts. The release states that the 6.2% margin includes a $252 million gain on the sale of HRL Laboratories.

## Stripping out the one-time gain

Remove the $252 million and BDS earned roughly $240 million on $7.94 billion of revenue. That is about a 3.0% margin by our arithmetic, not a figure Boeing reports. It is still well above the 1.7% of a year ago, so the underlying recovery is real. But it is half the headline margin, and it is thin for a segment with a $100 billion order book.

The nine-month numbers tell the same story. BDS revenue was $23.02 billion against $19.82 billion in the first nine months of 2025, while earnings from operations were $710 million against $379 million, a margin of about 3.1% on our calculation. The 10-Q says substantially all BDS contracts are long-term contracts, which means margins depend on cost estimates holding. A single adverse revision to a fixed-price program can erase a quarter of operating profit.

Neither the release nor the portion of the 10-Q we reviewed itemized a program charge for the quarter. One item worth watching is the 10-Q's note that unbilled receivables rose from $9.16 billion to $10.18 billion, "primarily driven by revenue recognized in excess of billings at BDS." Revenue is being recognized faster than cash is being billed, a normal feature of development-heavy work but one that holds back cash conversion.

## The F/A-XX win is not in the $100.5 billion

The most strategically important defense event of the quarter is not in the backlog at all. On Sept. 29 the Navy [selected Boeing](https://www.defensenews.com/industry/techwatch/2026/09/29/us-navy-selects-boeing-to-build-next-generation-fa-xx-fighter/) to build the F/A-XX, its sixth-generation carrier-based fighter. Defense News reported a value of $20 billion for the full-scale development phase, which covers multiple test aircraft, and said Northrop Grumman (NOC) was passed over in late-stage negotiations. The jet is meant to begin replacing F/A-18E/F Super Hornets and EA-18G Growlers in the 2030s.

Boeing's release is explicit that the order is not yet in backlog, pending completion of the source selection and evaluation review. Two points follow for anyone tracking the numbers. First, the record $100.5 billion excludes the award, so reported backlog could step up when it is booked. Second, the $20 billion figure comes from a single trade-press report, and Boeing's own filing does not state a value, so the amount that eventually lands in backlog could differ.

A development contract also converts to revenue slowly. Test aircraft and systems integration spread across years, and the profitable production phase would come later. The award matters for franchise value more than for the next four quarters.

## How it compares with Northrop

The cleanest comparison is Northrop Grumman, which [reported on Oct. 20](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001133421/000113342126000037/noc-09302026xearningsrelea.htm). Its sales rose 7% to $11.12 billion, segment operating margin was 12.1% against 12.3% a year earlier, and backlog stood at $109.6 billion after $16.4 billion of net awards, a 1.5x book-to-bill. Northrop raised its 2026 segment operating income guidance to $4.94 billion to $5.04 billion and its adjusted EPS guidance to $29.35 to $29.75.

Set side by side, the two have similar backlogs, but Northrop converts them into a segment margin around 12%, while Boeing's defense unit earns closer to 3% before the one-time gain. Boeing's growth is faster, 15% against 7%, and it has the F/A-XX win Northrop lost. Northrop has the margin and the guidance raise. The comparison is imperfect, since Boeing's BDS includes space and development programs with different risk profiles, but it frames what investors are being asked to wait for.

## The rest of Boeing matters to the defense story

BDS is only part of the company. Total backlog hit a record $745 billion, but $611.8 billion of that is commercial airplanes. The commercial unit delivered 161 airplanes in the quarter and posted a (6.5)% operating margin. Company core loss per share was $0.34, and free cash flow was $464 million on $1.5 billion of operating cash flow, which included about $0.7 billion of Department of Justice settlement payments.

For defense-focused readers the link is balance sheet capacity. A company still burning through commercial margin pressure has less room to absorb a fixed-price defense overrun, and Boeing is also moving assets out: the 10-Q lists Insitu within assets held for sale at Sept. 30. The release also notes a seven-year PAC-3 seeker production expansion contract, a modest but relevant sign of Boeing's exposure to the interceptor demand that has dominated sector coverage this month. It reported seven KC-46 deliveries in the quarter and 15 year to date.

The release contains no formal financial guidance, so there is no defense outlook to benchmark against.

## What to watch next

- **Booking of F/A-XX.** Watch for the quarter in which Boeing adds the award to backlog and what value it states. That will settle the gap between the reported $20 billion and the filed number.
- **Margin without the gain.** The next BDS print will have no HRL sale to lean on. Whether margin holds near 3% or moves toward Northrop's level is the real test of the recovery.
- **Program charges.** A quarter without an itemized charge is not a trend. Fixed-price development work remains the main source of downside surprises.
- **Cash conversion.** Rising unbilled receivables at BDS should eventually turn into billings. If they keep growing faster than revenue, free cash flow will lag the earnings story.
- **Insitu's sale.** Completion would reshape the segment's revenue base and could affect comparisons.

The takeaway is that Boeing's defense unit is growing and has the largest order book in its recent history, but the quality of earnings is lower than the 6.2% margin implies. This is information and commentary only, not investment advice.
