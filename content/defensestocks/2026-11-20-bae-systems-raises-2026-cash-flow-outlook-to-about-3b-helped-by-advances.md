## What BAE changed on Nov. 12

BAE Systems (BA.) used a trading update on Nov. 12 to raise one number and leave the others alone. The company now expects 2026 free cash flow of about £3.0 billion, against the "more than £2.0 billion" it guided in July, and the [market update](https://www.baesystems.com/en/article/market-update-nov-2026) puts 2025 at £2.2 billion. Sales, underlying EBIT and underlying EPS guidance did not move.

That sequence matters. In its [July half-year results](https://www.baesystems.com/en/article/2026-half-year-results), BAE had already lifted its free cash flow outlook from more than £1.3 billion to more than £2.0 billion, alongside higher sales, EBIT and EPS ranges. So the cash outlook has now been raised twice from the earlier £1.3 billion floor, while the profit ranges were last raised in July.

The guidance ranges BAE restated in November are sales growth of 8% to 10%, underlying EBIT growth of 10% to 12% and underlying EPS growth of 11% to 13%, all at constant currency and a rate of $1.32 to the pound. The release says the year-to-date average rate is about $1.34. BAE also gives a sensitivity: a five-cent move in the pound against the dollar shifts sales by about £500 million, underlying EBIT by about £70 million and underlying EPS by about 1.4p.

## Why advances are the footnote to read first

BAE is explicit about where part of the cash upgrade comes from. The £3.0 billion figure "includes some benefit from customer advances received to date" but excludes any material advances that might arrive before year-end, according to the [November update](https://www.baesystems.com/en/article/market-update-nov-2026).

The half-year results show how large that effect can be. First-half free cash flow was £1,791 million, against an outflow of £368 million a year earlier, and the company said the inflow reflected a high level of customer advances. It also said no material advances were received in the comparison period. Operating cash flow on the IFRS basis was £2,243 million against £74 million.

None of this makes the cash less real. An advance is cash the customer has paid, and it can fund the working capital that a production ramp needs. But advances are timing. They are paid before work is delivered and unwind as it is performed. A company that guides cash up while profit guidance is flat is telling investors that the cash line is being moved by contract terms as much as by earnings.

For investors tracking European and allied defense, the practical question is how much of the £3.0 billion repeats in 2027. BAE has not said. The full-year results on Feb. 19, 2027 are the next point where the company can describe what it expects once advances stop being a tailwind.

## Orders behind the cash

The cash story rests on an order book that keeps growing. BAE said it has secured £33 billion of orders so far in 2026 and expects further agreements before year-end. The update lists the main awards:

- Air: about £1.6 billion for the Global Combat Air Programme and about £1.5 billion in MBDA-related orders.
- Maritime: about £4.3 billion for the Dreadnought submarine programme and about A$1.3 billion for an Australian over-the-horizon radar system for Canada.
- Electronic Systems: about $1.0 billion of munitions funding, including APKWS and THAAD seekers.
- Platforms & Services: about $0.8 billion for US Army Armored Multi-Purpose Vehicles and about $0.5 billion for Army ammunition plants.

Order intake a year earlier was £27.2 billion at the same point, according to [interactive investor](https://www.ii.co.uk/analysis-commentary/ii-view-bae-systems-upgrade-demonstrates-strong-demand-ii540874), which I could not match to a BAE document, so treat that comparison as secondhand. At the half-year, BAE reported an order backlog of £84.0 billion, up from £83.6 billion at the end of 2025. Backlog barely moved in the first six months despite £16.4 billion of orders, which is a reminder that a large backlog converts to sales over years, not quarters.

The mix is useful. The US lines, munitions, seekers and ammunition plants, tie BAE to the same Pentagon funding fights that govern the American primes. The UK lines, Dreadnought and the fighter program, tie it to British budgets. The European exposure the company cites, missiles, air defense, land vehicles and combat air, is where rearmament spending is expected to show up next.

## Where the weak spot is

BAE says Electronic Systems, Air and Platforms & Services are trading strongly. Maritime is the exception: growth and margins are subdued as the Type 26 frigate programme moves through its first-in-class stage, in the company's words. The large Dreadnought award therefore does not offset a near-term margin drag in the same segment. Investors who want to judge the quality of the group's 10% to 12% EBIT growth target should look at how the other segments carry Maritime.

## What the market is pricing

The market reaction was muted. Interactive investor reports that the shares gave up early gains after the update and were up a little over 10% for the year, roughly in line with the FTSE 100. It notes the forward price-to-earnings ratio is above its three-year and 10-year averages, and that Morgan Stanley kept an overweight rating while trimming its price target to £25 from £26.20, citing lower peer multiples. These are one outlet's summary of analyst moves, not BAE disclosures.

The valuation point is the useful one: with the multiple above its averages, the cash conversion question carries more weight. BAE at least states the advance effect rather than leaving it to be inferred.

## What to watch next

Three items will tell readers whether the cash upgrade is durable:

1. **Further advances before year-end.** BAE excluded any material advances still to come, so more would add to the £3.0 billion.
2. **The Dec. 2 dividend.** The 2026 interim dividend is payable on Dec. 2, and the company expects to return about £1.6 billion to shareholders in 2026, including about £500 million of buybacks. That shows how much of the cash is going out.
3. **Feb. 19, 2027.** Full-year results should show the actual 2026 free cash flow, split between earnings and advances, and give the first read on 2027.
