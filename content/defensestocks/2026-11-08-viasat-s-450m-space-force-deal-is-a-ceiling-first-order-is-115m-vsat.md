## What happened

On Thursday, Nov. 5, the Space Force's Space Systems Command (SSC), working with the Pentagon's Golden Dome office, [announced a contract with Viasat (VSAT)](https://defensescoop.com/2026/11/06/pentagon-mission-operations-center-space-data-network/) to design, field and scale a Mission Operations Center (MOC) for the Space Data Network Backbone. It is a competitively awarded indefinite delivery/indefinite quantity (IDIQ) contract with a ceiling of up to $450 million. The first task order is $115 million.

The headline number needs a careful reading. The $450 million is the most the government may order under the contract. It is not money committed to Viasat. Only the $115 million task order is a defined piece of work so far, and [the reports do not disclose the contract's period of performance](https://www.satellitetoday.com/government-military/2026/11/06/viasat-receives-up-to-450-million-contract-for-sdn-b-mission-operations-center/) or where the center will be built. The first order is about 26% of the ceiling.

## What the center actually does

The MOC is a software-and-operations job, not a satellite build. [SSC describes it](https://breakingdefense.com/2026/11/dod-taps-viasat-to-field-operations-center-for-golden-dome-satellite-data-relay-network/) as bringing together "mission integration, network operations, and data transport management across a growing multi-vendor architecture." Breaking Defense likens it to a software-run telephone exchange: it orchestrates traffic across several satellite constellations and routes data by priority in real time.

Viasat's stated role is "network and mission system integration partner" as SSC expands what it calls an open-architecture ecosystem across multiple space-segment providers. Lt. Col. Alexa Eggert, the SDN Backbone materiel leader, called the MOC ["a crucial step in maturing our Space Data Network into a fully operational, multi-vendor capability."](https://defensescoop.com/2026/11/06/pentagon-mission-operations-center-space-data-network/) SSC says it is expected to advance priorities including the Golden Dome missile defense architecture.

## Where the award sits in a bigger network

The backbone itself belongs to someone else. In May, SSC [awarded SpaceX $2.29 billion](https://defensescoop.com/2026/05/27/space-force-awards-spacex-contract-backbone-satcom-network/) under an Other Transaction Authority agreement for the SDN Backbone, with a fully operational prototype due by the end of 2027. By that measure, Viasat's ceiling is roughly 20% of SpaceX's award, and Viasat is positioned as the neutral layer that ties vendors together rather than the owner of the constellation.

Other companies are already in the architecture. In August, SSC [awarded $60 million in Other Transaction agreements](https://www.satellitetoday.com/government-military/2026/11/06/viasat-receives-up-to-450-million-contract-for-sdn-b-mission-operations-center/) to Amazon LEO for Government, Lockheed Martin (LMT), Northrop Grumman (NOC), Rocket Lab (RKLB) and York Space Systems to test links between commercial satellites and the future network. In October, York received two SDN task orders. The same article cites the Aerospace Corporation as saying the Space Force budgeted $4.5 billion for the Space Data Network in fiscal 2027. Against that figure, the Viasat ceiling is 10%.

The network also has budget consequences beyond Viasat. DefenseScoop reported that if Congress approves, the backbone would fulfill the Space Force's Tranche 3 Transport Layer requirements, which is why the service is seeking to zero out that separate funding line in fiscal 2027. In other words, the Space Data Network is meant to replace a planned program, not simply add to the pile, and the contractors named so far are the ones positioned to capture that shift. The backbone was previously known as MILNET, and early reports described a constellation of more than 480 satellites, though the final count has not been published.

For investors in the sector, the practical point is that the Space Data Network is being built as a multi-vendor system with one integrating hub. That structure can spread work across several listed names, but it also means no single vendor owns the whole stack.

## What it means for Viasat

Viasat reported fiscal second-quarter 2027 results on Nov. 5, the same day as the award. [Via Satellite reported](https://www.satellitetoday.com/connectivity/2026/11/06/viasat-q2-defense-growth-aviation-headwinds-and-new-compensation-incentives/) total revenue of $1.2 billion, up 3%; adjusted EBITDA of $370 million, down 4%; and a net loss of $129 million. Communication Services revenue was $815 million, down 3%. The Defense and Advanced Technologies (DAT) segment was the growth driver at $365 million, up 20%, led by tactical networking products and information security and cyber defense. The segment's EBITDA improved 57% from a year earlier. The two segment figures sum to about the $1.2 billion total, which is consistent with $365 million being DAT revenue, though that article does not label it explicitly.

Set the award against that base. The $115 million first order equals about 31% of one quarter of DAT revenue, but it is not a quarterly figure: with no disclosed period of performance, it cannot be converted into annual revenue. The full $450 million ceiling would equal roughly 1.2 times one quarter of DAT revenue, again with no timeline. The reports on the award do not say how Viasat expects it to affect results, and the Via Satellite earnings piece does not mention the Space Force or the SDN at all.

That leaves Viasat with a government segment that is growing fast against a consumer and aviation business that is not. Whether this award changes the mix depends on how many task orders follow the first one.

## Risks and open questions

Three things are unresolved. First, ceiling versus orders: IDIQ ceilings often end up well above what is actually ordered, so the first task order is the figure to anchor on. Second, funding: the Space Force plans to [seek nearly $1.5 billion for the backbone](https://www.satellitetoday.com/government-military/2026/06/10/space-force-eyes-nearly-1-5b-for-space-data-network-backbone-in-future-reconciliation-bill/) in a future fiscal 2027 reconciliation bill, split into $685 million to expand the constellation and $800 million for adding vendors. That money is a request, not an appropriation. Third, scope: neither the period of performance nor the MOC location has been disclosed.

## What to watch next

- Whether SSC issues a second task order under the Viasat contract, and its size.
- Whether Congress includes the roughly $1.5 billion backbone request in a reconciliation bill.
- Any disclosure of the contract's period of performance or the center's location.
- Whether the five August test participants, including Lockheed Martin, Northrop Grumman and Rocket Lab, win follow-on Space Data Network work.
- SpaceX's progress toward the end-2027 prototype deadline, since the MOC exists to orchestrate that network.
