## What happened

Kratos Defense & Security Solutions (KTOS) reported third-quarter results on Nov. 5, and the order book was the story. The company booked $1.037 billion of new orders in the quarter, a book-to-bill ratio of 2.2 to 1, meaning it won $2.20 of new business for every $1.00 of revenue it recognized, [according to its earnings release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001069258/000106925826000085/ktos202609278kexhibit991.htm). Backlog rose to $2.650 billion at Sept. 27 from $2.084 billion at June 28, an increase of about $566 million, or 27%, in one quarter.

Revenue was $471.8 million, up 35.7% from $347.6 million a year earlier, with 23.2% of that growth organic. Adjusted EBITDA, a non-GAAP measure, was $50.9 million, a 10.8% margin versus 8.9% a year ago. Adjusted earnings were $0.24 per share against $0.14. On a GAAP basis, though, Kratos posted a net loss of $0.2 million, compared with net income of $8.7 million a year earlier.

Management raised its full-year adjusted EBITDA guidance to $176 million to $180 million and tightened revenue guidance to $1.78 billion to $1.81 billion. It also gave an initial 2027 forecast of $2.1 billion to $2.2 billion in revenue and $230 million to $240 million in adjusted EBITDA.

## The backlog is bigger, but cash is still going out

Backlog is a promise of future revenue, not cash in hand. The company's own filing shows how long that conversion takes. In its [10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001069258/000106925826000088/ktos-20260927.htm), Kratos says it expects about 16% of the $2.650 billion to be recognized in fiscal 2026, about 50% in fiscal 2027 and the rest after that. By our arithmetic, that is roughly $1.3 billion in 2027, well short of the $2.1 billion to $2.2 billion revenue forecast, so the 2027 number also depends on orders not yet won.

Of the total, $2.067 billion is funded and $582.9 million is not, per the release. Funded means the government has obligated money to the contract. Unfunded backlog can still be reduced or cancelled.

Cash flow lags the income statement. Operating cash flow was negative $5.2 million in Q3 and negative $43.6 million for the nine months. Capital expenditures were $30.2 million in the quarter, so free cash flow, which is operating cash flow minus capex, was negative $29.4 million. Over nine months, capex was $67.3 million, which puts the combined cash outflow from operations and investment near $111 million (our calculation from the 10-Q).

That did not threaten the balance sheet, because Kratos raised equity. The 10-Q shows net proceeds from stock issuance of $1.348 billion in the nine months, and cash and equivalents of $1.404 billion at quarter end, up from $560.6 million at the end of 2025. The share count was 187.7 million at Sept. 27 and 188.3 million on the cover of the 10-Q as of Oct. 30. Existing holders paid for the growth through dilution rather than through internally generated cash.

Working capital is absorbing some of the spending. Inventory rose to $244.5 million from $188.2 million at the end of fiscal 2025, mostly raw materials. Finance lease liabilities roughly doubled from about $99 million to $190 million, and amortization of acquired intangibles reached $11.0 million in the quarter from $2.5 million a year earlier, which helps explain the GAAP loss alongside a healthy adjusted figure.

## Where the growth came from, and where it did not

The strength is concentrated. Kratos Government Solutions, the larger segment, grew revenue 52.5% to $397.2 million and had a book-to-bill of 2.5 to 1. Its backlog jumped to $2.292 billion from $1.710 billion in Q2. The 10-Q attributes the backlog increase to awards in space, satellite and training, defense rocket support, microwave products and unmanned systems. Chief executive Eric DeMarco pointed to "notable strength in our hypersonic and space businesses."

The Unmanned Systems segment, home of the Valkyrie drone that dominates the bull case for many investors, went the other way. Revenue fell to $74.6 million from $87.2 million, and book-to-bill was 0.8 to 1, so orders trailed sales. Backlog slipped to $357.4 million from $374.6 million. The prior-year quarter included $16.1 million from an international Valkyrie shipment, which makes the comparison harder. The release says work continues on a second lot of 12 Valkyrie aircraft, but the quarter's order growth did not come from drones.

The quarter's order strength came from hypersonics, space and services, while the autonomy segment shrank year over year.

Customer concentration is high. Using the revenue-by-customer table in the 10-Q, U.S. government customers accounted for about 75% of Q3 revenue ($355.7 million of $471.8 million, our calculation). Kratos also cites a bid and proposal pipeline of $15.5 billion, which is the number to hold management to if 2027 is to look like the forecast.

## What it means for ETF holders

Kratos is a modest position in the ETFs most investors use to own defense. In the Global X Defense Tech ETF (SHLD), it was about 1.04% of the fund as of Nov. 4, [according to StockAnalysis](https://stockanalysis.com/etf/shld/holdings/). The fund is concentrated elsewhere: [Global X's holdings page](https://www.globalxetfs.com/funds/shld/) shows Palantir (PLTR) at 10.45%, RTX (RTX) at 9.53%, Lockheed Martin (LMT) at 9.49%, General Dynamics (GD) at 9.19% and Northrop Grumman (NOC) at 8.42%, which together are about 47% of assets (our sum).

The State Street SPDR S&P Aerospace & Defense ETF (XAR) tracks a modified equal-weighted index, and its [top ten holdings](https://www.ssga.com/us/en/intermediary/etfs/state-street-spdr-sp-aerospace-defense-etf-xar) range from 3.21% to 3.57% each as of Nov. 5. Kratos is not among them, so its weight is lower than 3.21%, though the page does not state the exact figure, and we could not confirm its weight in the iShares U.S. Aerospace & Defense ETF (ITA).

The practical point for portfolio construction is that a fast-growing small-cap contractor can double its backlog story without moving a cap-weighted fund much. A company at roughly 1% of SHLD adds only about one basis point of fund return for every 1% the stock moves, while the top five holdings, mostly mature primes with large existing backlogs, dominate it. This is a description of how the funds are built, not a recommendation to hold either.

## What to watch next

Three items will show whether the backlog turns into cash. First, Q4 guidance calls for revenue of $480 million to $510 million and adjusted EBITDA of $48 million to $52 million, which is roughly flat on Q3, so the step-up to the 2027 forecast has to come from conversion of the new awards. Second, free cash flow: another negative quarter with $1.4 billion of cash on hand is manageable, but investors will want to see the trend turn. Third, the Unmanned Systems book-to-bill, which needs to climb back above 1 for the Valkyrie narrative to match the reported numbers.

This echoes our Nov. 5 post on Redwire (RDW) and Voyager (VOYG), small-cap space names that also reported record backlogs while burning cash. The pattern is consistent across the group: orders are racing ahead of cash conversion, and the equity market has been funding the gap.
