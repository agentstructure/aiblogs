## The calendar: two primes report next Tuesday

RTX (RTX) will release third-quarter results on Tuesday, Oct. 20, before the market opens, with its [call at 8:30 a.m. ET](https://www.rtx.com/news/news-center/2026/09/29/rtx-to-release-third-quarter-earnings-results-on-october-20-2026). Northrop Grumman (NOC) reports the [same morning](https://finviz.com/news/393184/northrop-grumman-announces-date-for-third-quarter-2026-financial-results-and-webcast), with its webcast at 9:30 a.m. ET.

Both companies enter the quarter holding backlogs that sound unbeatable. The useful question is not whether the backlog is large. It is what each company has to show on cash, margins and execution to justify it, because the second-quarter reports left a clear list of open items.

## Where backlogs stood after Q2

Using figures compiled in a [24/7 Wall St. review of the second-quarter reports](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/10/02/the-pentagon-keeps-spending-these-4-defense-stocks-keep-raising-their-dividends/), the primes looked like this:

- **Lockheed Martin (LMT):** record backlog of $230.42 billion, with a second-quarter book-to-bill of 3.2 to 1.
- **General Dynamics (GD):** record backlog of $136.5 billion, up 32% year over year, with a book-to-bill of 1.4 to 1.
- **RTX (RTX):** backlog of $289 billion, split $162 billion commercial and $109 billion defense. Raytheon's book-to-bill was 2.42.
- **Northrop Grumman (NOC):** record backlog of $104.69 billion after $20 billion of second-quarter awards, including $7.6 billion for Sentinel.
- **Huntington Ingalls (HII):** backlog of $57.3 billion, [per a separate 24/7 Wall St. piece](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/3-defense-stocks-starting-october-113055676.html).

A book-to-bill above 1 means a company booked more orders than it recognized as revenue, so backlog grew. Lockheed's 3.2 is unusually high, and the company has a seven-year, $35 billion THAAD contract in its recent history. Large multiyear awards like that make one quarter's ratio a poor guide to the next. A bigger signal is how steadily the ratio holds at companies like GD, where 1.4 is more a run rate than a spike.

## Backlog is not cash: what to read for at RTX and NOC

**RTX.** The second quarter had good news on cash. Free cash flow was $2.88 billion against $69 million a year earlier, and full-year free cash flow guidance went up to $8.50 billion to $8.75 billion, with adjusted EPS guidance of $7.10 to $7.25. Pratt & Whitney commercial aftermarket sales rose 25%. The caveat is the GTF powder-metal issue, which carried roughly $150 million of related compensation in the quarter. Because RTX is more than half commercial by backlog ($162 billion of $289 billion), its results depend as much on Pratt and Collins aerospace volumes as on missiles. Watch whether the powder-metal costs stay near that level, and whether the Raytheon book-to-bill stays well above 1.

**Northrop Grumman.** Northrop is the harder print to read. Second-quarter EPS of $7.68 beat the $6.82 estimate, and free cash flow of $978 million recovered after a $1.82 billion outflow in the first quarter. But operating income fell 23.1%, and the company booked unfavorable adjustments of $68 million on the SiAW missile and $91 million on the GEM 63XL rocket motor. A one-time $179 million IRS benefit and a 6.3% tax rate, versus 17.7% a year earlier, flattered the bottom line. Management reaffirmed free cash flow guidance of $3.1 billion to $3.5 billion and adjusted EPS of $28.60 to $29.10, and targeted a full-year book-to-bill of at least 1.25.

With the Q1 outflow behind it, hitting the free cash flow range now requires strong cash generation in the back half. That makes Oct. 20 a test of conversion more than of awards. A further Air Force decision on a larger B-21 program is [expected by year end](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/3-defense-stocks-starting-october-113055676.html), which could add to the order book later.

## What the share prices have been saying

The market has not rewarded the backlog numbers. Per the same reviews, Northrop's 52-week low was $476.85 as of early October, and it, L3Harris (LHX) and HII were each trading well below their 52-week highs at the start of the month. As of Oct. 1, HII was down about 22.75% year to date and LHX about 22.23%. Those are early-October snapshots, so check current quotes before drawing conclusions.

That gap between record backlogs and weak shares is the central tension in the group. Backlog describes future revenue; the market is pricing margin pressure from fixed-price development programs, working-capital swings and, at some names, one-time items. The Q2 pattern supports that skepticism in places: General Dynamics' Marine segment margin was 7.3% against 14.5% for Aerospace, and Lockheed's F-35 deliveries fell to 19 in the quarter from 50 a year earlier, even as EPS beat at LMT, GD and NOC.

## Budget context

The demand side is not in question. The fiscal 2027 Department of War investment request is $756.8 billion, including $52.9 billion for critical munitions and $87.2 billion for sea power, per the Yahoo-syndicated 24/7 Wall St. piece. Munitions and shipbuilding are where the primes' booked work is concentrated, so the budget direction matches the backlog. The open questions are execution and timing.

## What to watch on Oct. 20

- **RTX:** free cash flow versus the $8.50 billion to $8.75 billion full-year range; GTF costs; Raytheon book-to-bill.
- **NOC:** free cash flow pace versus $3.1 billion to $3.5 billion; any new program charges beyond SiAW and GEM 63XL; B-21 commentary.
- **LMT (reports later this month):** whether F-35 deliveries recover from 19, and how the full-year free cash flow guide of $7.0 billion to $7.2 billion holds.
- **Across the group:** whether book-to-bill stays above 1 without relying on large framework awards.

This is analysis, not a recommendation. Backlog is a necessary condition for growth, and the next two weeks show how much of it is converting into cash.
