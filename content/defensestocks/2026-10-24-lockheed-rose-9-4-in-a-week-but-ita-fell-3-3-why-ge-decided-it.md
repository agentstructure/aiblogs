## The week the earnings did not show up in the ETFs

Defense earnings came in strong this week. Lockheed Martin (LMT) closed Friday at $604.18, up 9.4% from the prior Friday's $552.09. Northrop Grumman (NOC) closed at $551.44, up 6.1% from $519.86. Yet the iShares U.S. Aerospace & Defense ETF (ITA) closed Friday at $202.23, down 3.3% from $209.07 a week earlier, and the SPDR S&P Aerospace & Defense ETF (XAR) fell 3.0%, from $224.53 to $217.79. All weekly moves here are close-to-close from [StockAnalysis price histories](https://stockanalysis.com/etf/ita/history/) for Oct. 16 to Oct. 23.

The Global X Defense Tech ETF (SHLD) went the other way, rising 0.7% from $61.48 to $61.89, per its [price history](https://stockanalysis.com/etf/shld/history/).

We covered ITA, XAR and SHLD valuations on Oct. 10 and Oct. 17. This piece asks a different question: when the primes report, what actually decides how a fund reacts? The answer is mostly arithmetic about weights.

## What the primes reported

Three companies reported on Tuesday, Oct. 20, and Lockheed followed on Thursday, Oct. 22.

RTX (RTX) posted Q3 sales of $25.0B, a record backlog of $340B, and raised 2026 adjusted EPS guidance to $7.65 to $7.75 from $7.10 to $7.25, per [MarketScreener's earnings roundup](https://www.marketscreener.com/news/defense-earnings-week-opens-with-three-raised-forecasts-and-two-record-backlogs-ce785cd9d981fe23). Northrop reported Q3 sales of $11.1B, a $109.6B backlog, and raised its 2026 EPS range to $29.35 to $29.75, according to the same report. NOC rose 4.57% to $534.00 that day, [Yahoo Finance reported](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/northrop-grumman-noc-stock-jumps-231610479.html).

GE Aerospace (GE) also reported on Oct. 20. Its orders rose 49% to $19.0B and it raised its 2026 adjusted EPS range to $8.00 to $8.10. The stock still fell 3.67% to $299.87 that day, per MarketScreener.

Lockheed's [Q3 release](https://investors.lockheedmartin.com/news-releases/news-release-details/lockheed-martin-reports-third-quarter-2026-financial-results) showed sales of $20.7B, up 11%, diluted EPS of $7.97 and a record $292.0B backlog, which includes $52.5B tied to the PAC-3 MSE interceptor award. It raised 2026 guidance to about $80.0B to $82.0B in sales and $30.35 to $30.75 in EPS. The company also flagged that it is still negotiating F-35 Lot 20 and later production contracts, and that its costs began exceeding currently authorized funding in Q3.

LMT closed Oct. 22 at $589.91, up 7.0% from $551.51, and added another 2.4% on Friday. Early on Oct. 22 it was up about 9%, per [24/7 Wall St.](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/10/22/lockheed-martin-rises-9-as-record-backlog-lifts-outlook-rtx-gains-2-northrop-grumman-advances-3/), so the day faded somewhat from its morning level.

## Why ITA fell while LMT and NOC rose

The iShares fund page and holdings file, both dated Oct. 22, show the weights. ITA holds 50 equities and charges 0.37%. Its top holdings, per [iShares](https://www.ishares.com/us/products/239502/ishares-u-s-aerospace-defense-etf/latest-holdings.csv):

- GE Aerospace (GE): 19.95%
- RTX (RTX): 17.19%
- Boeing (BA): 7.64%
- Lockheed Martin (LMT): 5.40%
- Northrop Grumman (NOC): 5.14%

Two companies, GE and RTX, make up 37.1% of the fund. Lockheed and Northrop together are 10.5%.

Now look at the week. GE closed Oct. 16 at $313.83 and Oct. 23 at $285.64, a drop of 9.0%, per its [price history](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/ge/history/). RTX slipped 1.5%, from $193.92 to $191.01, per its [price history](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/rtx/history/). A 20% position falling 9% costs the fund roughly 1.8 percentage points before anything else moves. A 5.4% position rising 9.4% adds roughly half a point. That is back-of-envelope math using current weights, not an attribution report, but it points the right way.

ITA is often described as a defense fund. This week it traded like a commercial aerospace fund with a defense sleeve, because GE is not a prime and its engine business is driven by commercial orders and aftermarket demand. GE's own defense unit had orders of $4.3B on revenue of $3.5B in Q3, per MarketScreener, a fraction of the $19.0B in total orders.

## XAR and SHLD answer the same question differently

XAR, per [State Street's fund page](https://www.ssga.com/us/en/intermediary/etfs/spdr-sp-aerospace-defense-etf-xar), also holds 50 names, with net assets of $4.86B and a 0.35% gross expense ratio. Its top ten holdings range only from 3.06% to 3.59% as of Oct. 22. Lockheed is the largest at 3.59%, Northrop 3.42% and RTX 3.14%. Together the three are about 10.2% of the fund, and GE does not appear in the top ten, so it is below 3.06%.

XAR still fell 3.0%, almost in step with ITA, even though no single stock can swing it much. Its top ten also includes TransDigm (TDG), Honeywell Aerospace, HEICO, Textron (TXT) and Huntington Ingalls (HII), so it carries aerospace supplier and commercial exposure alongside the primes. Lockheed's gain therefore could not offset the rest of the fund. We did not pull XAR's full holdings file, so we can't say which names drove the decline.

SHLD is the outlier. Its [fund page](https://www.globalxetfs.com/funds/shld/) lists Palantir (PLTR) at 12.01%, Lockheed at 9.27% and RTX at 9.22% as of Oct. 22, with 50 holdings, $6.46B in net assets and a 0.50% expense ratio. Lockheed and RTX together are 18.5% of SHLD, against 22.6% for the same pair in ITA. We did not verify what lifted the fund, and Palantir's role in particular is unconfirmed here, so treat the 0.7% gain as a fact, not as an attribution.

## What to take from it

Three points follow for anyone comparing funds.

First, read the top five holdings before reading the fund's name. In ITA, two companies are 37.1% of assets, so a single earnings reaction at GE can outweigh a double-digit move at Lockheed.

Second, earnings-week leadership can sit in one stock. Lockheed's rise came with a raised outlook and a $292.0B backlog. But ITA's own price shows the stock rise was not enough: ITA closed Oct. 22 at $204.48, down 0.6% from $205.79, on the same day LMT closed up 7.0%. Early in the session the fund was up 0.21%, per 24/7 Wall St., so it also reversed intraday.

Third, the earnings calendar is not finished. L3Harris (LHX) reports Oct. 29 with a record $42B backlog as of Q2, per MarketScreener. LHX is 3.72% of ITA and 3.24% of XAR in the holdings tables above. The fund reaction will again depend on how much of each fund that name represents.

The funding backdrop has not changed either. MarketScreener notes that the government runs on a continuing resolution through Dec. 11, which bars the Pentagon from using FY2027 money to speed production above FY2026 rates, against a $1.5 trillion FY2027 request. Strong guidance from the primes has so far been priced against that constraint, and the fund-level outcome depends on which of those primes the fund actually owns.
