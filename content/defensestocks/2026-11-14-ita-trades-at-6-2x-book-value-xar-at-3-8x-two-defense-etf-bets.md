## The number to start with

Two of the biggest defense ETFs each hold 50 stocks, and the market charges very different prices for them. The iShares U.S. Aerospace & Defense ETF (ITA) had a price-to-book ratio of 6.23 as of Nov. 12, [according to iShares](https://www.ishares.com/us/products/239502/ishares-us-aerospace-defense-etf). The State Street SPDR S&P Aerospace & Defense ETF (XAR) stood at 3.80 on the same date, [per State Street](https://www.ssga.com/us/en/intermediary/etfs/state-street-spdr-sp-aerospace-defense-etf-xar). That is a gap of about 1.6 times for funds that sit in the same sector.

Price-to-book is the fund's underlying stock prices divided by the book value of those companies' equity, aggregated across holdings. A headline "6.2x book" says nothing about whether a fund is expensive or cheap in absolute terms. It does show how differently the two weighting schemes land on the same industry.

The comparison matters this week because the sector is repricing its policy risk. Midterm results pushed several defense names lower, and investors holding a basket now have to decide which basket they actually own.

## Same sector, different construction

The valuation gap likely reflects construction, though we did not verify each index's weighting rules. XAR's published weights are nearly flat: a small supplier gets almost the same weight as a prime. State Street's Nov. 12 holdings show it: the top ten positions run from 3.44% (Lockheed Martin, LMT) down to 3.10% (Karman Holdings), and together they add up to about 32.3% of the fund, by our addition of the published weights. Other top-ten names include Huntington Ingalls, Honeywell Aerospace, HEICO, L3Harris (LHX), StandardAero, TransDigm, Northrop Grumman (NOC) and Textron.

The Global X Defense Tech ETF (SHLD) is built differently again. According to Global X's fund page, Palantir was about 10.5% of net assets as of Nov. 3, Lockheed Martin about 9.8% and RTX (RTX) about 9.4%. Those three alone come to roughly 29.7%, from a fund that holds up to 50 companies. SHLD is closer to a concentrated bet on a handful of names than the equal-weighted XAR, even though both are sold as defense exposure.

Fees follow the same split. ITA's expense ratio is 0.37% [per iShares](https://www.ishares.com/us/products/239502/ishares-us-aerospace-defense-etf), XAR's gross ratio is 0.35% [per State Street](https://www.ssga.com/us/en/intermediary/etfs/state-street-spdr-sp-aerospace-defense-etf-xar), and SHLD's is 0.50% [per its SEC prospectus summary](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1432353/000143235326000477/a497k-defensetech.htm). On $10,000 held for a year, that is $37, $35 and $50 respectively, before any trading costs.

## Price-to-earnings: a caution on bases

ITA's price-to-earnings ratio was 36.49 as of Nov. 12, per iShares. XAR's was 25.92 on a forward, first-fiscal-year basis, with 29.18 for its index, per State Street. These are not like-for-like. iShares' page, as we retrieved it, did not state whether its figure uses trailing or forward earnings, and the two providers calculate fund-level ratios differently. The price-to-book pair is the cleaner comparison, though it carries its own caveat: book value understates asset-light, software-heavy and intangible-heavy companies, which can inflate the ratio for funds that hold more of them.

Readers comparing funds should check each issuer's stated methodology rather than lining the two P/E figures up.

## Performance: dates do not match

Returns tell a mixed story, and the reporting dates differ, so treat them as rough context.

- ITA's NAV total return was 0.48% year to date as of Nov. 12, per iShares. Its NAV was $71.4852 on Nov. 13, against a 52-week range of $66.09 to $84.41, which puts it about 15% below the high and about 8% above the low.
- XAR's NAV return was -5.50% year to date as of Oct. 31, and -8.43% over one year, per State Street.
- SHLD was down about 8% year to date, according to a Nov. 6 Stocktwits report. We could not confirm that figure against Global X directly.

ITA's price history also has a break in it. iShares notified a forward stock split that began trading on a split-adjusted basis on Nov. 5, according to the fund's iShares listing. Anyone comparing ITA's price today with the numbers quoted in older coverage should adjust for it.

Assets under management moved the other way from performance. ITA's net assets were $12.02 billion on Nov. 13, per iShares, compared with about $11.72 billion on Nov. 6 in iShares data. Part of that is price movement rather than new money, because the same page does not report flows, so we cannot say whether investors added to the fund.

## Why politics is in the ETF debate

The policy backdrop is why fund construction matters now. The administration's fiscal 2027 request is $1.5 trillion, built from [about $1.15 trillion in base spending plus $350 billion through reconciliation](https://breakingdefense.com/2026/04/trump-to-propose-1-5-trillion-defense-budget-banking-on-350-billion-from-reconciliation/), as Breaking Defense reported in April. [CSIS has noted](https://www.csis.org/analysis/fy-2027-defense-funding-potential-post-midterm-outcomes) that Democratic control of either chamber could give lawmakers reason to wait and negotiate funding levels in the next Congress.

After the election, Stocktwits reported on Nov. 6 that Lockheed Martin, L3Harris, RTX and Northrop Grumman all closed lower the day after the vote. It quoted TD Cowen analyst Roman Schweizer saying "Trump branded programs are dead," naming Golden Dome and the Golden Fleet, and calling nuclear modernization programs the "least at risk." That is one analyst's view relayed by a single outlet, and we have not seen the original note, so read it as opinion rather than fact.

The relevance to funds is concentration. A concentrated SHLD carries heavy exposure to the primes that sit at the center of a budget fight. A near-flat XAR spreads exposure across suppliers such as HEICO, TransDigm and StandardAero, which depend more on commercial aerospace cycles than on any single appropriations bill. None of that predicts which fund performs better. It describes what each one owns.

## What to watch next

- **Lame-duck action.** What Congress does on defense funding before the new session shapes whether the reconciliation figure in the request is realistic.
- **Reconciliation size.** Whether Congress funds anything close to the $350 billion requested is the biggest swing factor for procurement-heavy names.
- **Fund data refreshes.** Both iShares and State Street update valuation ratios daily, so the 6.23 and 3.80 price-to-book figures will move. Watch whether the spread narrows or widens as prices adjust.
- **Flows.** Issuer flow data would show whether the ITA asset increase reflects new money or just price changes.
