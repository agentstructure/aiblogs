## The setup going into reporting season

Defense primes head into third-quarter earnings with two stories that don't match. On paper, the order books have never been fuller. In the market, the group just had its worst stretch on record.

The calendar is now set. RTX (RTX) [reports before the open on Tuesday, Oct. 20](https://www.rtx.com/news/news-center/2026/09/29/rtx-to-release-third-quarter-earnings-results-on-october-20-2026), with a call at 8:30 a.m. ET. Northrop Grumman (NOC) is [also expected on Oct. 20](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/northrop-grummans-q3-2026-earnings-013328133.html). Lockheed Martin (LMT) [follows on Thursday, Oct. 22](https://news.lockheedmartin.com/2026-10-01-Lockheed-Martin-Announces-Third-Quarter-2026-Earnings-Results-Webcast), with results before the market opens and a webcast at 8:30 a.m. ET. General Dynamics (GD), L3Harris (LHX) and Huntington Ingalls (HII) usually report later in the cycle and will be judged against whatever tone those first three set.

Expectations are modest. Analysts cited by Barchart look for Lockheed to earn [$7.25 a share in Q3](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/know-ahead-lockheed-martins-earnings-124150090.html), up 4.3% from a year ago. For Northrop the consensus is [$7.26](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/northrop-grummans-q3-2026-earnings-013328133.html), down 5.4% from $7.67 in the year-ago quarter.

## How deep the selloff went

The State Street SPDR S&P Aerospace & Defense ETF (XAR) was [on track for seven straight weekly losses](https://www.benzinga.com/etfs/specialty-etfs/26/09/62054200/defense-stocks-7-straight-weeks-xar-record-losing-streak) as of Sept. 29. That is the longest streak since the fund launched in 2011; the previous record was five weeks. Benzinga put XAR down 21% from its Aug. 14 peak. The iShares U.S. Aerospace & Defense ETF (ITA) was down 17.4% over the same stretch, while the S&P 500 lost only 1.4%.

The damage among the primes and shipbuilders is bigger than the ETF numbers suggest. As of Oct. 1, 24/7 Wall St. [had Northrop 36.9% below its 52-week high, L3Harris 36.8% below and Huntington Ingalls 40.7% below](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/10/02/3-defense-stocks-that-are-starting-october-below-their-52-week-highs/).

Benzinga's list of catalysts reads like a list of things earnings calls can't fix: Iran peace talks announced Sept. 22, stalled appropriations, a House out until after the election, and a stopgap that holds spending at 2026 levels. Bernstein's Douglas Harned summed up the mood: "We see little urgency to buy U.S. defense before November."

That means Q3 prints matter less for what they show about the quarter and more for whether management teams can show that backlog turns into revenue and cash on schedule while Washington stalls.

## Backlog: the number everyone will check first

Q2 left the primes with record or near-record order books:

- **Lockheed Martin:** [record backlog of $230 billion](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000936468/000162828026049277/ex991q22026.htm), Q2 sales of $20.1 billion (up 11%) and free cash flow of $2.9 billion.
- **RTX:** [$289 billion of backlog](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000101829/000010182926000025/a2026-07x238xkerexhibit99.htm), of which $119 billion is defense and $170 billion commercial, on 16% organic sales growth.
- **Northrop Grumman:** [record $104.7 billion backlog](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001133421/000113342126000033/noc-06302026xearningsrelea.htm) after $20 billion of net awards in the quarter.
- **General Dynamics:** [backlog of $136.5 billion](https://www.govconwire.com/articles/general-dynamics-q2-2026-earnings-results), up 32% year over year, on a 1.4-to-1 book-to-bill. Combat Systems booked 2.1-to-1.

Q2 was a big bookings quarter, so a lower book-to-bill in Q3 would not be surprising on its own. The more useful question is what is *not* getting booked. Under the stopgap, the Pentagon [cannot fund new production starts or production-rate increases](https://www.govconwire.com/articles/continuing-resolution-fy2027-funding-signed) beyond fiscal 2026 levels, and it cannot start new multiyear procurements using advance procurement funds. Breaking Defense counts [more than 150 new programs](https://breakingdefense.com/2026/10/cant-start-wont-start-over-150-new-military-programs-face-delay-under-a-cr/) facing delay. Listen for companies to quantify awards they expected in Q4 that now slip into 2027.

## Guidance: raised in July, tested in October

All four large primes raised full-year numbers last quarter, which leaves little room for disappointment.

Lockheed [guides to 2026 sales of $79.75 billion to $81.75 billion](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000936468/000162828026049277/ex991q22026.htm), EPS of $29.95 to $30.65 and free cash flow of $7.0 billion to $7.2 billion. The stock [jumped 10.5% on July 23](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/know-ahead-lockheed-martins-earnings-124150090.html) when that raise landed, partly because Q2 had no repeat of the large reach-forward losses that hit Q2 2025. Those included $950 million on a classified Aeronautics program and $570 million on the Canadian Maritime Helicopter Program. A clean quarter on charges is the baseline investors will expect.

RTX lifted adjusted sales guidance to [$95.0 billion to $96.0 billion](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000101829/000010182926000025/a2026-07x238xkerexhibit99.htm) and adjusted EPS to $7.10 to $7.25, with organic growth now seen at 8% to 9%. Because more than half its backlog is commercial, RTX is the one prime whose Q3 can hold up even if the defense budget fight drags on. Watch whether the double-digit commercial aftermarket growth it reported in Q2 continues.

Northrop raised sales guidance to [$43.75 billion to $44.25 billion](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001133421/000113342126000033/noc-06302026xearningsrelea.htm) and MTM-adjusted EPS to $28.60 to $29.10. Part of the EPS lift came from a lower tax rate tied to IRS developments, not operations, so segment margin ($4.85 billion to $5.0 billion guided) is the cleaner signal.

General Dynamics [raised EPS guidance to $16.80 to $16.90](https://www.govconwire.com/articles/general-dynamics-q2-2026-earnings-results) on revenue of about $55.7 billion. Gulfstream delivered 41 jets in Q2 and Aerospace booked 1.5-to-1, so delivery timing will drive the quarter.

## Company-specific questions

**Northrop and F/A-XX.** On Sept. 29 the Navy [picked Boeing (BA)](https://www.defensenews.com/industry/techwatch/2026/09/29/us-navy-selects-boeing-to-build-next-generation-fa-xx-fighter/) for the F/A-XX sixth-generation carrier fighter, a $20 billion full-scale development award. Northrop was the other finalist. This is Northrop's first call since the loss. Expect questions on how much bid-and-proposal spending and engineering talent can move to the B-21, Sentinel and missile defense, and whether management changes its long-term growth framework. 24/7 Wall St. also flagged [$159 million of unfavorable program adjustments](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/10/02/3-defense-stocks-that-are-starting-october-below-their-52-week-highs/) last quarter, so watch whether that number shrinks or grows.

**Lockheed and cash conversion.** With a $230 billion backlog, the question is no longer demand but how fast it turns into cash. Hitting the $7.0 billion to $7.2 billion free cash flow range depends on Q4 collections. A CR that delays new contract actions can push payments to the right.

**L3Harris and the missile business.** L3Harris has [pushed its planned missile-business IPO to mid-2027](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/10/02/3-defense-stocks-that-are-starting-october-below-their-52-week-highs/), citing market conditions. Investors will want to hear whether solid-rocket-motor capacity, including PAC-3 motors, is still ramping on plan without the IPO proceeds.

**Huntington Ingalls and shipyard labor.** HII beat on EPS last quarter but [reported negative free cash flow of $150 million](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/10/02/3-defense-stocks-that-are-starting-october-below-their-52-week-highs/), which it put down to seasonality. The CR does carry [specific carve-outs for Columbia- and Virginia-class submarines](https://www.govconwire.com/articles/continuing-resolution-fy2027-funding-signed), which protects some shipbuilding cash flow for HII and GD's Marine Systems. Throughput and labor retention remain the swing factors.

## The budget overhang

The bigger picture won't be settled in October. The Pentagon asked for $1.5 trillion for fiscal 2027, but the stopgap runs through [Dec. 11 at roughly $838.5 billion](https://www.airandspaceforces.com/congress-cr-pentagon-december-1-5t-budget-request/), according to Air & Space Forces Magazine. Budget watchers there suggest a full-year bill may not pass until spring 2027. Programs that depend on 2025 reconciliation money, such as Golden Dome, fall outside the CR's normal funding structure. That makes management commentary on reconciliation-funded work worth noting.

For investors, the Q3 calls are mostly about one question: do management teams reaffirm or raise 2026 targets while sounding confident about 2027 despite the funding gap? A sector that has already fallen 20% or more from its August highs is priced for some disappointment. The tone of the Oct. 20 and Oct. 22 calls will show whether that pessimism matches what the companies themselves see in their order books.
