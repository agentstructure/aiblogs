## The stopgap is not one number

Most coverage of the fiscal 2027 continuing resolution (CR) has focused on the topline: the Pentagon is operating at roughly [$838.5 billion](https://www.airandspaceforces.com/congress-cr-pentagon-december-1-5t-budget-request/) against a $1.5 trillion request. That gap is real, but it hides the more useful question for investors: which specific lines stop moving, and which ones were never covered by the baseline in the first place.

The CR took effect Oct. 1 and [runs through Dec. 11](https://breakingdefense.com/2026/10/cant-start-wont-start-over-150-new-military-programs-face-delay-under-a-cr/). Lawmakers can pass another one or face a shutdown. Under its terms, according to the [National Guard Association's summary](https://www.ngaus.org/newsroom/fiscal-2027-stopgap-budget-both-good-bad-news), defense funding is capped at the prior year's level, agencies cannot begin activities that were not funded last year, and production rate increases and certain multiyear procurements are barred. This is the fiscal 2027 start under a temporary measure that the Government Accountability Office counts as the [38th in 50 years](https://www.airandspaceforces.com/congress-cr-pentagon-december-1-5t-budget-request/).

Two mechanisms matter for companies: the "new start" ban, and the reconciliation gap.

## 150-plus programs that cannot begin

Breaking Defense searched the fiscal 2027 procurement and research budget documents for "new starts" and found [more than 150 results](https://breakingdefense.com/2026/10/cant-start-wont-start-over-150-new-military-programs-face-delay-under-a-cr/). The authors caution that the method is imperfect, so treat the count as a floor for attention, not a precise tally. The distribution is lopsided: 29 Army new starts, 17 Navy and Marine Corps, nine Space Force and roughly 100 in the Air Force.

Some of the named items are concrete dollar lines:

- **Army:** $145 million for the Holston Army Ammunition Plant in Tennessee, the largest procurement new start, and $921.9 million in research funding for THAAD plus $132.1 million for its AN/TPY-2 sensor. Sierra Nevada Corp.'s HADES spy plane carries $131.7 million for platform and system integration.
- **Navy:** About $1 billion in advance procurement and roughly $837 million in research for the Trump-class battleship, $4.4 billion for two submarine tenders, and $750 million to buy the first 12 Conventional Prompt Strike weapons.
- **Space Force:** $1.5 billion in fiscal 2027 for Autonomous Tactical Fabric, which the article says resembles the former JADC2 effort, and about $140 million in advance procurement for the third and fourth Evolved Strategic SATCOM satellites.
- **Air Force:** Hypersonic follow-ons, including a second ARRW increment, and the Looking Glass-Next airborne command post are among the delayed efforts.

The Collaborative Combat Aircraft is a useful illustration of why this is more than an accounting footnote. General Atomics and Anduril were selected earlier this year, but Brig. Gen. Timothy Helfrich said that without fiscal 2027 funding, production is limited: ["You can't go do production without production funding."](https://breakingdefense.com/2026/10/cant-start-wont-start-over-150-new-military-programs-face-delay-under-a-cr/) Chief of Space Operations Gen. Douglas Schiess put it more bluntly at a Sept. 15 briefing: "It's gonna slow us down, and the threat is not slowing down."

One caveat from the same reporting: FLRAA was originally listed as a $127.2 million procurement new start, but it was moved to research funding. Because that line has existed for years, it is not a new start and is not blocked. Budget structure, not just program importance, determines who is exposed.

## The reconciliation cliff: F-15EX and Golden Dome

The less obvious exposure comes from money that arrived outside the regular appropriations process. Funding from the 2025 One Big Beautiful Bill Act, passed through reconciliation, [is not covered by the CR](https://www.airandspaceforces.com/congress-cr-pentagon-december-1-5t-budget-request/). That creates a baseline problem: a program funded mostly by reconciliation in fiscal 2026 has almost nothing to continue at.

The F-15EX is the clearest case. Its fiscal 2026 procurement was nearly all reconciliation-funded, so under the CR it starts fiscal 2027 with a $115 million topline, less than 5% of the $2.65 billion requested. That bears on Boeing (BA), which in September [won a $2.4 billion deal for more F-15EX fighters](https://breakingdefense.com/2026/09/boeing-nabs-2-4-billion-deal-for-more-f-15ex-fighters/), according to Breaking Defense's headline. I could not retrieve the article body, so details of that award, including how it is funded, are not covered here. The point for readers is that a signed contract and a funded year-two line are different things, and the CR now sits between them.

Golden Dome has the same structural problem. Director Gen. Michael Guetlein said that [a CR "doesn't help me under Golden Dome because I don't have a topline to fall back to."](https://www.airandspaceforces.com/congress-cr-pentagon-december-1-5t-budget-request/) The administration planned $17.5 billion for fiscal 2027, but less than $400 million sits in the base budget, per [Breaking Defense](https://breakingdefense.com/2026/10/cant-start-wont-start-over-150-new-military-programs-face-delay-under-a-cr/). Guetlein told an August symposium that "if they don't figure it out, there's no Golden Dome, because there is no funding."

The Defense Autonomous Warfare Group shows the same pattern at larger scale: a $54.6 billion research request of which $1 billion is in the base budget and $53.6 billion depends on reconciliation.

## Why the reconciliation money is the swing factor

The $1.5 trillion request has two parts: $1.15 trillion through regular appropriations and $350 billion through reconciliation. House appropriators advanced a base defense bill in June at $1.07 trillion, the Senate has not agreed on a topline, and [neither chamber has moved on the $350 billion](https://www.airandspaceforces.com/congress-cr-pentagon-december-1-5t-budget-request/). The House did pass a framework for a smaller reconciliation package with $60 billion for defense, but lawmakers describe that money as addressing supplemental costs tied to the Iran war, not the fiscal 2027 requests.

Sen. Chris Coons (D-Conn.) estimated a gap of [$500 billion to $700 billion](https://breakingdefense.com/2026/10/cant-start-wont-start-over-150-new-military-programs-face-delay-under-a-cr/) between Pentagon plans and likely appropriations, because he doubts the $350 billion in reconciliation and a supplemental of more than $100 billion will materialize. That is one senator's estimate, not a forecast from the Pentagon or an independent scorekeeper, but it frames the downside case. Byron Callan of Capital Alpha Partners said the base 2027 budget may not clear Congress ["until spring 2027,"](https://www.airandspaceforces.com/congress-cr-pentagon-december-1-5t-budget-request/) six or more months late.

## What to watch

- **Dec. 11.** Another CR, a shutdown or an omnibus. Last year's 43-day shutdown left much of the National Guard's full-time force [without pay for weeks](https://www.ngaus.org/newsroom/fiscal-2027-stopgap-budget-both-good-bad-news), so the cost of a lapse is not abstract.
- **Anomalies.** Whether Congress adds exceptions for reconciliation-dependent lines such as F-15EX and Golden Dome, or lets them wait.
- **A post-midterm reconciliation bill.** Some Republicans want one, but intraparty disputes over how to pay for it make passage [uncertain](https://www.airandspaceforces.com/congress-cr-pentagon-december-1-5t-budget-request/).
- **Company commentary on timing.** Backlog is not the constraint at the primes. The question is whether awards slip into 2027 and how management teams describe that slippage.

The GAO has warned that long CRs create [bottlenecks](https://www.airandspaceforces.com/congress-cr-pentagon-december-1-5t-budget-request/) in contracting offices and vendor capacity, and it found that F-35 program officials estimate about 20% of their financial management staff's time goes to adjusting budgets under CR constraints. The practical takeaway: the headline gap between $838.5 billion and $1.5 trillion is less informative than program-level structure. Lines with new-start status or reconciliation dependence face the sharpest delay risk. Nothing here is a view on any stock; it is a map of where the stopgap bites.
