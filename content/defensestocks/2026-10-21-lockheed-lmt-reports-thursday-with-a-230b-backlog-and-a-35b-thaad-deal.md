## The calendar: one prime this week, four more next

Lockheed Martin (LMT) is the only major prime left to report this week. It releases third-quarter results before the market opens on [Thursday, Oct. 22, with a webcast at 8:30 a.m. ET](https://news.lockheedmartin.com/2026-10-01-Lockheed-Martin-Announces-Third-Quarter-2026-Earnings-Results-Webcast?asPDF=1). The rest of the group follows next week. L3Harris (LHX) and Howmet Aerospace (HWM) both report [Oct. 29](https://s205.q4cdn.com/565046976/files/doc_news/L3Harris-Sets-Date-for-Third-Quarter-2026-Earnings-Release-2026.pdf) ([Howmet's date](https://finviz.com/news/397643/howmet-aerospace-to-host-webcast-and-announce-third-quarter-2026-results)), and so does [Textron (TXT)](https://investingnews.com/textron-to-release-third-quarter-results-on-october-29-2026/). General Dynamics (GD) will hold its [call on Friday, Oct. 30 at 9 a.m. EDT](https://finviz.com/news/400930/general-dynamics-to-webcast-2026-third-quarter-financial-results-conference-call).

That makes Thursday the main read-through for the week. Lockheed's results will set the tone for how investors judge the others.

## What Tuesday already set up

RTX (RTX) and Northrop Grumman (NOC), which were covered here yesterday, set a high bar. RTX [raised its 2026 adjusted EPS outlook to $7.65 to $7.75 from $7.10 to $7.25](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000101829/000010182926000030/a2026-10x208xkerexhibit99.htm) and reported a $164 billion defense backlog. Northrop [lifted its MTM-adjusted EPS range to $29.35 to $29.75 from $28.60 to $29.10](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001133421/000113342126000037/noc-09302026xearningsrelea.htm) and booked a 1.5x book-to-bill. Both raised guidance, so a Lockheed print that merely holds its range will look soft by comparison.

## Where Lockheed stands going in

The second quarter, [reported July 23](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000936468/000162828026049277/ex991q22026.htm), was a clean one. Sales were $20.06 billion, up 11%. Diluted EPS was $7.94, against $1.46 a year earlier. Free cash flow was $2.9 billion.

The EPS comparison needs care. The year-earlier quarter carried $1.615 billion of reach-forward losses, mostly on a classified Aeronautics program and the Canadian Maritime Helicopter Program. The second quarter of 2026 had no such charges. So the jump in EPS says more about the absence of losses than about growth. The 11% sales gain is the cleaner signal.

For the third quarter, the year-ago bar is [$18.6 billion in sales and $6.95 of diluted EPS](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/936468/000162828025045582/ex991q32025.htm). Lockheed's full-year sales guidance is about $79.75 to $81.75 billion. The midpoint of $80.75 billion works out to about $20.2 billion a quarter, so a third quarter near the second quarter's $20.06 billion would keep the company on track. That is simple arithmetic on guidance, not a forecast.

## The backlog is the headline, and the missile segment drives it

Backlog hit a record [$230.4 billion at the end of June, up from $193.6 billion at year-end 2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000936468/000162828026049277/ex991q22026.htm). Most of that gain sits in one place. Missiles and Fire Control backlog [rose to $87.9 billion from $46.7 billion](https://news.lockheedmartin.com/2026-07-23-Lockheed-Martin-Reports-Second-Quarter-2026-Financial-Results) over the same period. The release says the figure includes a $35 billion multi-year Missile Defense Agency contract for THAAD.

The segment's sales rose 19% to $4.1 billion in the quarter, on production ramps for PAC-3, THAAD and the Precision Strike Missile (PrSM). Its operating profit was $594 million, a 14.5% margin.

A note on reading the number: a backlog figure tells you what has been contracted, not what has been funded or built. Watch whether the third quarter shows backlog growing again or flattening as the THAAD award is worked off. A flat number would not be alarming after a quarter this large, but it would end the run of record highs.

## What to watch Thursday

**Missile margins.** Lockheed's second-quarter release shows the missiles segment at a 14.5% margin while ramping fast. The question is how much margin the new THAAD and PAC-3 volume carries as output scales. Management commentary on mix is more useful than the headline number.

**Guidance direction.** Lockheed raised sales, segment profit, EPS and free cash flow in July, and lowered its capital spending plan to [$2.0 to $2.4 billion from $2.5 to $2.8 billion](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000936468/000162828026049277/ex991q22026.htm). RTX and Northrop both raised again in October. Whether Lockheed does too is the cleanest read on its execution.

**Aeronautics.** The segment recovered to a $760 million operating profit in the second quarter on $8.1 billion of sales, after a loss a year earlier. It is also where the largest past charges came from, so any new reach-forward loss would matter far more than a small miss in missiles.

**Cash conversion.** Second-quarter free cash flow was $2.9 billion. The full-year target is $7.0 to $7.2 billion, so the third quarter should show steady conversion.

## Segment comparisons to check

The year-ago quarter gives a concrete yardstick by segment. In the third quarter of 2025, Missiles and Fire Control had [$3.6 billion in sales, up 14%](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/936468/000162828025045582/ex991q32025.htm), and Aeronautics had $7.3 billion, up 12%, on F-35 production and sustainment. Against those bases, the second-quarter 2026 figures of $4.1 billion and $8.1 billion show the pace Lockheed needs to hold. Total backlog a year ago was $179.1 billion, so the company has added roughly $51 billion since then, based on the two reported figures.

Orders matter as much as sales. Lockheed's CEO cited [$65 billion of new orders in the second quarter](https://news.lockheedmartin.com/2026-07-23-Lockheed-Martin-Reports-Second-Quarter-2026-Financial-Results), a quarter that included the $35 billion THAAD contract. A quarter without a comparable mega-award will look smaller by comparison, and that is not necessarily a weakness. The release also mentioned an agreement with Rheinmetall to co-produce ATACMS in Europe, a sign that some of the growth is moving into international partnerships rather than only US production lines.

## How the peers compare

Lockheed is not the only prime with a missile-heavy story. RTX's Raytheon segment [grew sales 14% to $8.03 billion in the third quarter](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000101829/000010182926000030/a2026-10x208xkerexhibit99.htm) on Patriot, Standard Missile and Tomahawk volume, with a 14.1% adjusted operating margin. Northrop's sales grew [7% to $11.1 billion](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001133421/000113342126000037/noc-09302026xearningsrelea.htm). Lockheed's second-quarter missiles margin of 14.5% sits close to Raytheon's. If Lockheed's third-quarter margin in that segment slips while sales climb, it would suggest the new contracts are starting at lower profitability.

## The next read-through: General Dynamics

GD's last report, in July, showed what a different prime looks like. Backlog was [$136.5 billion, up 32% year over year, with a 1.4-to-1 book-to-bill](https://www.govconwire.com/articles/general-dynamics-q2-2026-earnings-results), and Combat Systems, its land-vehicle and munitions segment, posted a 2.1-to-1 ratio. GD [raised its 2026 EPS guidance to $16.80 to $16.90](https://www.govconwire.com/articles/general-dynamics-q2-2026-earnings-results). Its Oct. 30 report will show whether Marine Systems and Aerospace growth is still outrunning Combat Systems.

## What this means

Tuesday's results showed that demand is not the problem for defense primes. Backlogs are at or near records across RTX, Northrop and Lockheed. The question for the next two weeks is conversion: sales growth, margins and cash that justify those backlogs. Lockheed reports first among the remaining names, and its missile segment is the cleanest test of whether a surge in orders becomes profit.
