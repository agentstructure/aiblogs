## What happened

Saab (SAAB B) is the European rearmament story that most clearly shows what a flood of orders does to a balance sheet. Its [third-quarter interim report](https://www.saab.com/globalassets/cision/documents/2026/20261023-saab-q3-results-2026positioned-to-capture-growing-demand-en-0-5449176.pdf), published Oct. 23, put order bookings at SEK 65.8 billion, against SEK 20.9 billion a year earlier. Management put the book-to-bill ratio at 3.2x.

Orders are the headline, but cash is the more telling number. Operational cash flow in the quarter was SEK 6,785 million, compared with SEK 142 million in the third quarter of 2025. Saab says the increase was "mainly attributable to customer payments, which had a positive impact on working capital."

That matters because defense contractors are not valued on bookings alone. A big order is a promise; cash is what pays for factories, suppliers and engineers while the work gets done. This quarter, a meaningful part of that cash came from customers paying in advance.

## The quarter in numbers

Sales were SEK 20,553 million (SEK 15,871 million a year earlier), which Saab describes as organic growth of 30.6%. Operating income (EBIT) rose 42% to SEK 1,945 million, and the operating margin was 9.5% versus 8.7%. Earnings per share were SEK 2.57, up from SEK 1.77, according to the [same report](https://www.saab.com/globalassets/cision/documents/2026/20261023-saab-q3-results-2026positioned-to-capture-growing-demand-en-0-5449176.pdf).

The quarter's large orders totalled SEK 52,120 million, against SEK 8,321 million a year earlier. Three contracts were named: SEK 24.6 billion for 16 Gripen E fighters for Ukraine, SEK 10.1 billion for two GlobalEye aircraft for an undisclosed customer, and SEK 8.7 billion to equip German frigates. The report says the Gripen contract was signed with Sweden's Defence Materiel Administration on June 30, with deliveries scheduled for 2029 to 2030.

Cash, though, is where the quarter stands apart. The nine-month change in working capital added SEK 5,688 million to operating cash flow, against a SEK 3,366 million drag in the same period of 2025. Contract liabilities, which largely represent money customers have paid ahead of delivery, stood at SEK 46,611 million at Sept. 30, up SEK 12,753 million from year-end 2025. Saab attributes that rise mainly to customer payments.

## Poland's submarines show up in the cash line

The report links part of the cash surge to a specific program. In the Naval business area, third-quarter operational cash flow was SEK 5,981 million, up from SEK 232 million, and Saab says it was "driven by customer advances for the submarine programme for Poland."

That program is the Orka deal. According to the report, Saab signed a contract with the Polish State Treasury Armaments Agency on June 29 for three A26-type submarines, worth approximately SEK 47 billion, with final deliveries scheduled during 2038. Advances at the front of a contract that long can flatter cash flow for a while, because the spending follows the payments rather than the other way around.

The relationship is still developing. On Nov. 20, Saab [announced a licensing agreement](https://www.saab.com/newsroom/press-releases/2026/saab-signs-mortar-munitions-licensing-agreement-for-production-in-poland) with Dezamet, part of Poland's PGZ Group, for production and sales of MAPAM mortar munitions in Poland. Saab describes it as the first industrial cooperation and technology transfer commitment tied to Orka. No financial value, volumes or royalty terms were disclosed, so it is a signal of the program's industrial footprint rather than a number to model.

## The company's own caution

Saab does not present the cash figure as a new normal. The report says that, "due to the nature of Saab's customer contracts, deliveries and timing of customer milestone payments in large projects, large fluctuations in cash flow between quarters can occur."

It also says capacity expansion carries a cost. Saab is "increasing investments to expand its capacity and expand production volumes, which entails higher working capital," and describes this as putting "some pressure on operational cash flow." The balance sheet shows it: inventories rose SEK 3,179 million over the nine months to SEK 28,618 million, and net investments were SEK 6,536 million, against SEK 4,920 million a year earlier.

The risk section adds two more cautions. The report says orders in the defense market "can be deferred to the future based on financing and political factors," and that for certain subsystems Saab depends on a single supplier or very few.

Not all of the margin story is clean either. In the Naval area, the third-quarter operating margin was 6.1% versus 7.5%, which Saab says was impacted by costs related to capacity expansion and higher R&D. In Aeronautics, the report cites costs related to the T-7A trainer program as a partial offset to profit growth.

## Backlog: large, and mostly later

Saab's order backlog was SEK 364,223 million at Sept. 30, compared with SEK 274,532 million at the start of the year. Of that, 79% is international.

The report's backlog duration table shows how far out the work runs: SEK 27.9 billion remaining for 2026, SEK 102.1 billion for 2027, SEK 89.2 billion for 2028, SEK 62.0 billion for 2029, and SEK 83.0 billion after 2029. A backlog scheduled this far out is good for visibility, but it also explains why advances and working capital matter. Saab has to build capacity years before much of the revenue arrives.

One more item inflates the balance sheet without being operational. Financial investments rose SEK 6,223 million in the first nine months, mainly from the revaluation of Saab's shares in Helsing GmbH, which Saab says reflected a higher valuation in connection with a financing. That gain was recognized in other comprehensive income rather than in earnings, and it is separate from the cash story above.

## What to watch

- **Whether the cash holds.** Saab's nine-month free cash flow was SEK 5,053 million versus negative SEK 2,245 million a year earlier, but the company says the quarterly pattern can swing. A fourth quarter that gives back part of the advance-driven gain would fit its own warning.
- **Margins during the ramp.** Group EBIT margin improved, helped by more Surveillance sales, but Naval's slipped. Watch whether capacity costs keep trimming margins as new programs scale.
- **Conversion of pending deals.** Saab said it has signed a term sheet for the contract framework covering six GlobalEye aircraft following Canada's selection, and that NATO has said it will begin formal negotiations on up to ten GlobalEye systems. Neither is yet a booked order in the report.
- **Poland follow-through.** After the mortar license, Saab says it looks forward to "securing orders" to move the Orka industrial program to its next stage.
- **Portfolio changes.** The sale of the Public Safety Solutions business to Omda AS is expected to be completed in the fourth quarter.
