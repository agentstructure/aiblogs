## The headline number and what it measures

Elbit Systems (ESLT) reported third-quarter results on Nov. 17 with a record order backlog of $33.0 billion as of Sept. 30, 2026, according to its [press release](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/elbit-systems-reports-third-quarter-2026-results-302941770.html). The company said the increase in backlog during the quarter "came mainly from Europe," and that about 74% of the total is attributable to orders from outside Israel.

Revenue for the three months ended Sept. 30 was $2,252.1 million, against $1,921.6 million a year earlier, an increase of about 17% (the percentage is the company's own). GAAP diluted earnings per share were $3.70, versus $2.80; non-GAAP diluted EPS were $4.36, versus $3.35. GAAP operating income was $232.5 million, or 10.3% of revenue, compared with $171.4 million, or 8.9%.

It was a strong quarter. The more useful question for investors is where the revenue came from, because the geography of sales and the geography of backlog point in different directions.

## Backlog says Europe; revenue says Israel

The release breaks Q3 revenue out by customer region. Israel accounted for $845.4 million, up from $641.6 million a year earlier. That is a gain of about 32% by our arithmetic, and Israel rose from 33.4% of quarterly revenue to 37.5%. Europe was $556.9 million, against $536.0 million, a gain of roughly 4%, and its share fell from 27.9% to 24.7%. North America rose to $456.0 million from $406.6 million, and Asia-Pacific to $289.4 million from $266.2 million.

Put together, Israel supplied about $204 million of the roughly $330 million year-over-year increase in quarterly revenue, or about 62% (our calculation from the release's tables). Revenue from outside Israel grew about 10%, compared with the 17% total.

The segment commentary points the same way. Elbit attributed the 28% rise in Land and Armaments revenue "mainly" to ammunition and munition sales in Israel, and the 4% rise in Aerospace to UAV sales in Israel, partly offset by lower precision-guided munition sales in Asia-Pacific. The 32% rise in ISTAR and EW (intelligence, surveillance, target acquisition and reconnaissance, plus electronic warfare) came from airborne and land High Power Laser, electronic warfare and maritime systems in Asia-Pacific, and from electronic warfare and electro-optic systems in Israel. Europe's contribution was concentrated in C4I and Cyber, where revenue rose 11% on command and control systems sales in Europe.

Elbit says it has "experienced a continued material increase in the demand" from the Israel Ministry of Defense (IMOD) since the war began in October 2023, and that this demand may generate more orders. It does mean that the growth visible in this quarter's income statement is not the same growth that filled the backlog.

## How much of the backlog converts soon

Elbit said about 35% of the backlog is scheduled to be performed during the remainder of 2026 and 2027. Applied to $33.0 billion, that is roughly $11.6 billion over about five quarters (our arithmetic). The other roughly 65%, or about $21.4 billion, sits beyond 2027.

Long performance windows are visible in the contracts the release lists as announced since the quarter began: about $270 million for advanced ISR and targeting payloads for an international customer running up to six years, about $235 million for rockets and PULS systems for a European customer over three years, and about $260 million for PULS systems and munitions for a European NATO member over five years. The release does not say how much of each is already in the Sept. 30 backlog.

The company gave no split of the backlog by delivery year beyond the 35% figure, so the pace at which European orders turn into European revenue is the open question. Europe's 4% growth this quarter suggests that conversion has not yet arrived in the reported numbers.

## Cash, capital spending and the IMOD payment agreement

Cash generation was a bright spot. Operating cash flow for the first nine months was $862.5 million, compared with $461.0 million a year earlier. Elbit said third-quarter cash flow was affected mainly by "the strong increase in net income and an increase in contract liabilities." Contract liabilities generally include customer advances, so part of the cash strength reflects payments received ahead of delivery rather than completed work.

Chief executive Bezhalel Machlis said free cash flow was $276 million in the quarter, a cash conversion of over 150%. The release's tables do not present a free cash flow line, so the $276 million is management's figure; GAAP net income attributable to shareholders was $177.9 million.

Capital spending is also rising. Purchases of property, plant and equipment, net of grants, were $226.2 million for the nine months, against $128.4 million a year earlier, an increase of about 76% by our arithmetic. Machlis said the company is "expanding production capacity" and "increasing our capital investment." Heavier spending is one reason free cash flow may not stay at Q3 levels.

Separately, Elbit disclosed that in November 2026 it signed an agreement with the IMOD under which it "will be compensated for delayed payments by the IMOD since October 2023." The release gives no amount and no schedule. It is a detail worth tracking because the Israeli ministry is now a larger share of revenue than a year ago, and payment timing from a single customer affects cash conversion.

## What management expects, and what to watch

On outlook, the release says that "based on management's current estimates," Elbit expects high-teens revenue growth and continued margin expansion in 2027 compared with full-year 2026. The company adds that this expectation is "provided one time only" and that it does not intend to provide similar expectations on a recurring basis. That is a statement of management's current estimate, not a formal guidance range, and it should be read that way.

The release also carries a risk disclosure: the U.S.-Iran memorandum signed in June collapsed in early July, and as of the filing date there have been intermittent hostilities between the two. Elbit describes the situation as uncertain and notes that some operations have experienced supply chain and operational constraints.

Three things to watch in coming quarters:

- **Europe's revenue growth rate.** With the backlog build led by Europe, sales to European customers should accelerate if production capacity keeps pace. A repeat of low single-digit growth would suggest conversion is slower than the order intake implies.
- **The Israel share of revenue.** It rose to 37.5% in Q3 from 33.4%. If it keeps climbing, the company's revenue mix will lean toward its home customer even as the backlog leans away.
- **Cash conversion after the IMOD agreement.** The size of any compensation and whether contract liabilities keep rising will shape whether Q3's free cash flow is a run rate or a high point.

The takeaway is that backlog figures need to be read alongside delivery timing and cash, not in place of them. This post is information and commentary, not investment advice.
