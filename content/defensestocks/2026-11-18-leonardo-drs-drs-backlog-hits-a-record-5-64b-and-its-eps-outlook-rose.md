## What Leonardo DRS reported

Leonardo DRS (DRS) reported third-quarter 2026 results on Oct. 29. The headline numbers are strong, and the company's own [earnings release filed with the SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001833756/000183375626000042/q32026earningspressrelease.htm) lays them out in detail.

Revenue was $1,066 million, up 11% from $960 million a year earlier. Net earnings rose 60% to $115 million. Adjusted EBITDA reached $149 million, up 27%, which lifted the adjusted EBITDA margin to 14.0% from 12.2%.

The number that matters most to this site's audience is orders. Bookings were $1,639 million against $1,307 million a year earlier, a book-to-bill ratio of 1.5x. Funded backlog ended September at $5,640 million, up 21% from a year earlier, and the company described it as a record. Nine-month bookings were $3,609 million, a 1.3x book-to-bill, versus $3,151 million and 1.2x in the same period of 2025, per the [same filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001833756/000183375626000042/q32026earningspressrelease.htm).

One caveat on the backlog figure: DRS reports funded backlog only. The release does not give a total backlog that would include unfunded and option work, so this number is not directly comparable with the total backlogs primes such as General Dynamics (GD) report.

## Both segments grew, and orders ran ahead of sales

DRS reports two segments, and both posted a 1.5x book-to-bill in the quarter, per the [filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001833756/000183375626000042/q32026earningspressrelease.htm).

- **Advanced Sensing and Computing (ASC):** revenue rose 14% to $659 million. The company credits tactical radars and space sensing programs. Segment bookings were $1,017 million, up from $757 million. Segment adjusted EBITDA margin was 11.8%.
- **Integrated Mission Systems (IMS):** revenue rose 12% to $428 million, driven by naval power and propulsion programs. Bookings were $622 million, up from $550 million. Segment adjusted EBITDA margin was 16.6%, up from 13.8%.

IMS is the margin story. Its adjusted EBITDA grew 34% on 12% revenue growth, so most of the consolidated margin gain came from there. The company says bookings strength came from naval power and propulsion, tactical radars, force protection, and electric power and propulsion. The release names no individual contract awards, so readers cannot tie the quarter to a single large win.

## The guidance raise, and how much of it is tax

DRS raised its 2026 outlook, and the release states the prior ranges, so the word "raised" is accurate here. The changes, from the [filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001833756/000183375626000042/q32026earningspressrelease.htm):

| Measure | Prior | New |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $3,900M to $3,975M | $3,950M to $3,985M |
| Adjusted EBITDA | $525M to $540M | $545M to $555M |
| Adjusted diluted EPS | $1.34 to $1.39 | $1.50 to $1.53 |
| Tax rate | 16.5% | 13.0% |

The operating raise is real but modest. At the midpoints, revenue guidance moved up about 0.8% and adjusted EBITDA about 3.3%. Adjusted EPS moved up about 11%, from a $1.365 midpoint to $1.515.

The gap between those percentages is mostly the tax line. The expected tax rate fell 3.5 points, from 16.5% to 13.0%, while the share count assumption stayed at 269.0 million. A rough calculation shows the size of the effect. Taking the new EPS midpoint of $1.515 and a 13% tax rate implies about $1.74 of pre-tax earnings per share. Taxed at the old 16.5% rate, that would be roughly $1.45. On that arithmetic, about $0.06 of the $0.15 midpoint increase comes from tax and about $0.09 from operations. This is our estimate, not a company disclosure, and it ignores any adjustments in how the company computes adjusted EPS. The release does not explain why the tax rate fell.

The guidance also implies a strong fourth quarter. Nine-month revenue was $2,825 million and adjusted EBITDA was $382 million. The new full-year ranges therefore imply fourth-quarter revenue of roughly $1,125 million to $1,160 million and adjusted EBITDA of roughly $163 million to $173 million, against $1,066 million and $149 million in Q3. That is arithmetic from the filed figures, not a company forecast. It requires sequential growth in both revenue and margin. CEO John Baylouny tied the raise to the record funded backlog and "improved visibility into the fourth quarter."

## Cash is the weaker spot

Cash flow did not match the income statement. Operating cash flow was $96 million in Q3, down from $107 million, and free cash flow was $61 million, down from $77 million. The company attributes the decline to higher working capital needs. Free cash flow for the first nine months was negative $28 million, though that is better than negative $149 million a year earlier. DRS does not give free cash flow guidance in this release, so there is no company target to measure the fourth quarter against.

That matters because of the balance sheet. DRS ended September with $294 million in cash and $175 million in debt, with nothing drawn on its credit facility. It paid about $24 million in dividends in the quarter, bought back 288,633 shares for about $12 million, and declared a $0.09 dividend payable Nov. 25.

Now add the acquisition. On July 28 DRS agreed to buy Raft LLC, a mission-software company, for [$450 million in cash](https://pulse2.com/leonardo-drs-to-acquire-raft-in-450-million-deal), with closing expected in the fourth quarter pending regulatory approvals. A $450 million payment is larger than the cash DRS held at quarter end. The company has said it expects to fund the deal with cash on hand and its revolving credit facility, per [secondary reporting](https://pulse2.com/leonardo-drs-to-acquire-raft-in-450-million-deal) of its announcement, so debt will likely rise once the deal closes. The 2026 guidance excludes Raft, according to the earnings release.

## What to watch

Three things will show whether this quarter was a high-water mark or a step up.

First, conversion. Funded backlog of $5.64 billion is about 1.4 times the high end of full-year 2026 revenue guidance, but backlog only matters if it turns into cash. Watch whether fourth-quarter free cash flow recovers and whether the working capital drag reverses.

Second, the sequential ramp. Guidance requires fourth-quarter adjusted EBITDA well above the Q3 level. A result at the low end of the implied range would still meet the new outlook, so the range gives a useful yardstick for what counts as a beat.

Third, the Raft closing and its effect on leverage and on the 2027 outlook. Management has described the deal as adding software and AI capability to the product mix, and investors will want to see how that shows up in margins once it is consolidated.

The broader point for defense investors is that a record funded backlog and a guidance raise do not carry equal weight. The orders are the stronger signal. A meaningful share of the EPS gain comes from a tax assumption rather than from operations, and cash conversion remains the open question.
