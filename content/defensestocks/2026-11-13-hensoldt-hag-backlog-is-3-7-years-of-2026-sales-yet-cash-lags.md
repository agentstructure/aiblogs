## What the headline number means

Hensoldt (HAG), the German sensor and radar maker, reported on Nov. 5 that its order backlog hit a record €10,367 million at the end of September. The headline compares that backlog with the company's own 2026 revenue guidance of around €2,800 million. The ratio is about 3.7, so the backlog equals roughly 3.7 years of sales at this year's pace. That division is our arithmetic, not a company metric.

The scale of the order book is not in doubt. According to [Hensoldt's nine-month release](https://live.deutsche-boerse.com/news/EQS-News-HENSOLDT-increases-adjusted-EBITDA-by-33-and-refines-guidance-for-2026-upwards-2ff46b2e-7cbe-4a26-985d-10f58200c903), nine-month order intake was €3,564 million, up 76.7% from €2,017 million a year earlier. The backlog was up 46.1% from €7,096 million. Book-to-bill, orders divided by revenue, was 1.9x for the period.

The harder question is how fast that backlog turns into revenue and cash. Leonardo (LDO), reporting the same morning, offers a useful comparison.

## Hensoldt: margins up, cash still negative

Nine-month revenue rose 21.3% to €1,863 million. Hensoldt says core revenue excluding pass-through items grew 17.7%, so the reported figure is flattered somewhat by low-margin pass-through business. Adjusted EBITDA rose 33.0% to €281 million, a margin of 15.1% against 13.7% a year earlier. Adjusted EBIT was €177 million, up 45.4%.

By segment, Sensors booked €2,631 million of orders (+54.5%) and holds a record €7.4 billion backlog. Optronics booked €1,157 million (+252.7%), a book-to-bill of 3.3x, and its adjusted EBITDA margin rose to 12.6% from 5.1%.

Cash is the weak spot. Adjusted free cash flow for nine months was negative €104 million, only €15 million better than the negative €119 million a year earlier. Third-quarter free cash flow was positive at €32 million, and the third-quarter adjusted EBITDA margin was 20.6%, per the same release. In its [first-half report](https://www.hensoldt.net/news/hensoldt-doubles-order-intake-in-the-first-half-of-2026-%E2%80%93-revenue-and-earnings-rise-significantly), the company said free cash flow is typically at its lowest at this point in the year because of upfront investment ahead of fourth-quarter project milestones.

## What the refined guidance requires

Hensoldt's release headline says it "refines guidance for 2026 upwards." The changes are small. Revenue is now around €2,800 million, from around €2,750 million. The adjusted EBITDA margin is around 19.0%, from a range of 18.5% to 19.0%. Book-to-bill is now guided to the upper end of the 1.5x to 2.0x range. Cash conversion stays at around 50% of adjusted EBITDA.

Put those together and the fourth quarter has a lot to do. These are our calculations from the company's rounded figures, so treat them as approximate:

- Revenue of about €2,800 million less €1,863 million leaves roughly €940 million for Q4.
- A 19.0% margin on €2,800 million is about €530 million of adjusted EBITDA, so Q4 needs roughly €250 million, a margin near 27%. The best quarter so far this year was 20.6%.
- Cash conversion of 50% implies about €265 million of free cash flow for the year, against negative €104 million through September. That means roughly €370 million in Q4 alone.

Fourth-quarter weighting is normal in defense, and Hensoldt has flagged it. But the guide leaves little room for slippage in milestone payments. One upside is outside the guidance: the company says book-to-bill would be around 2.3x if the PEGASUS contract were awarded in 2026, but it expects that award in early 2027.

## Leonardo shows the same pattern

[Leonardo's nine-month release](https://www.leonardo.com/en/press-release-detail/-/detail/05-11-2026-leonardo-board-of-directors-approves-9m2026-results) shows new orders of €23.3 billion, up 28% (22.4% on a like-for-like basis excluding the Iveco Defence Vehicles business it bought in March). Backlog topped €60 billion for the first time, at €60.4 billion, about €6 billion of which came from that acquisition. Leonardo says the backlog covers more than 2.7 years of production. Revenue was €15.3 billion (+14.2%), and EBITA was €1,301 million (+37.7%, or +29.3% like-for-like).

Cash again lags. Free operating cash flow was negative €68 million, an improvement of 84% from a year ago. Before consolidating the acquired business it was positive €22 million. Net debt was €3,325 million, up 43.8%, after the roughly €1.6 billion purchase price.

Leonardo's own headline says its 2026 guidance was upgraded on orders: the order target moved to about €29.5 billion from about €28.2 billion. Every other measure was left unchanged from July. Revenue is still about €22.1 billion, EBITA about €2.21 billion, free operating cash flow about €1.37 billion and net debt about €2.2 billion, according to the [company's guidance page](https://www.leonardo.com/en/investors/guidance) and the release.

Again, the year-end figures imply a big fourth quarter. By our arithmetic from those numbers:

- Revenue of about €22.1 billion less €15.3 billion implies about €6.8 billion in Q4.
- Free operating cash flow of about €1.37 billion against negative €68 million implies roughly €1.44 billion in Q4.
- Net debt falling from €3.3 billion to about €2.2 billion implies a reduction of about €1.1 billion in three months.
- Orders of about €29.5 billion less €23.3 billion implies about €6.2 billion still to book.

Leonardo said the guidance excludes the announced Raft acquisition by its US unit, Leonardo DRS, and any other M&A, so a deal could change the net debt picture.

## Why it matters for investors

Both companies are showing the same shape. Government orders arrive quickly, margins improve as volume rises, and cash stays negative until late-year milestone payments land. For investors in European defense, the order book is no longer the open question. Execution and cash timing are.

Hensoldt's long-range targets lean on that conversion. At its [Capital Markets Day](https://www.hensoldt.net/news/capital-markets-day-hensoldt-confirms-revenue-ambition-of-6-billion-euros-for-2030-and-aims-for-10-billion-euros-in-2035) in London on Nov. 10, it reaffirmed €6 billion of revenue in 2030 and set a new ambition of €10 billion in 2035. It targets 15% to 20% annual growth from 2027 and 2028 and above 20% toward the end of the decade, an adjusted EBITDA margin of about 21% by 2030, and cash conversion of about 50% of adjusted EBITDA. Capex is expected to peak at about 5.5% of revenue in 2026 before easing to 4% to 4.5%.

Getting from roughly €2.8 billion this year to €6 billion in 2030 requires compound growth of about 21% a year, by our arithmetic, which is higher than the 15% to 20% the company guides for 2027 and 2028. It implies growth above 20% in the later years, which the company also says to expect. CEO Oliver Dörre said in the half-year report that it is now down to industrial execution to determine how fast orders become real capabilities.

## What to watch next

- Hensoldt's preliminary full-year 2026 results, scheduled for Feb. 24, 2027, will show whether Q4 delivered the roughly €370 million of cash that its guidance implies.
- Whether the PEGASUS award slips past early 2027.
- Leonardo's year-end net debt against the roughly €2.2 billion guide, and whether the Raft deal changes it.
- Whether the Q4 order target of about €6.2 billion at Leonardo is met.

None of this is investment advice. It is a reading of what two companies have reported and what their own guidance requires.
