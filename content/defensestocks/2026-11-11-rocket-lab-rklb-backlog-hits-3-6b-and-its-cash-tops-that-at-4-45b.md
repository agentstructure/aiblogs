## What the filing says

Rocket Lab (RKLB) reported third-quarter results on Nov. 9, and the headline number is backlog. The company says it ended the quarter with a record backlog of approximately $3.6 billion, "a 52% increase since end of Q2 2026," according to its [Form 8-K earnings release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001819994/000181999426000073/rklb-11092026ex991.htm). Chief executive Peter Beck puts the figure at $3.57 billion. The 52% is a quarter-over-quarter change, not a year-over-year one.

The prior quarter's figure was $2.36 billion, according to a [summary of the Q2 release](https://pulse2.com/rocket-lab-backlog-reaches-record-2-36-billion-as-new-space-systems-and-launch-contracts-top-1-billion/). Dividing $3.57 billion by $2.36 billion gives roughly 51%, in line with the company's rounding (my calculation).

Revenue was a record $267.8 million, up 73% from $155.1 million a year earlier, per the same release. Nine-month revenue was $702.2 million. For the fourth quarter, Rocket Lab guides to revenue of $270 million to $290 million.

## Where the new backlog came from

The release says "nearly $1 billion" of backlog came from new launch contracts signed in the third quarter alone. It also cites $470 million signed for Space Systems, which covers spacecraft and components. It lists a record 117 launches in backlog, and more than $515 million in new Neutron launch contracts, which lifted the Neutron manifest to 16 missions.

Add the roughly $1 billion of launch deals to the $470 million of Space Systems awards, subtract the $267.8 million of revenue recognized, and start from $2.36 billion: the result is about $3.56 billion, close to the reported figure (my calculation). The jump reflects a very large quarter of bookings, not a revision to old contracts.

For defense readers, the government content is the part to read closely. The release mentions the VICTUS HAZE responsive space mission for the U.S. Space Force, whose spacecraft launched and are operating, and says more than 55 spacecraft are in development and build for customers including the Space Development Agency and the Space Force. It also describes the company's work as spanning "constellation deployment to missile defense." The release does not disclose contract values, award dates or a government versus commercial split for the Q3 additions. The Q2 summary cited a 57% commercial and 43% government mix for that quarter's backlog, but Rocket Lab did not repeat that breakdown in the Q3 release.

## What backlog does not tell you

Backlog is not defined in the release, and it does not say how much converts to revenue within 12 months. The Q2 summary reported about 45.5% expected to be recognized within 12 months; no equivalent figure appears in the Q3 filing.

Scale helps frame it. At the Q3 revenue rate, $3.57 billion of backlog equals about 13 quarters of sales (my calculation). That is a long runway, but a large share of it is tied to Neutron, and Neutron has not flown.

The release says Neutron's first launch is "now targeted for no earlier than 2027." It also says first flight hardware has been delivered to Launch Complex 3. The company did not state in this release how far the date moved, so readers should treat the new date as a floor, not a commitment. Launch contracts signed at a $70 million base price for several Neutron missions only become revenue after the rocket flies.

## Profitability and cash

The business is still losing money. Third-quarter net loss was $79.7 million, or $0.12 per share, and adjusted EBITDA was a loss of $18.0 million, compared with a $26.3 million loss a year earlier. Non-GAAP gross margin was 38.4%. Fourth-quarter guidance calls for an adjusted EBITDA loss of $14 million to $20 million.

The cash flow statement in the filing covers nine months only. Operating cash flow over that period was negative $127.3 million and capital expenditure was $84.2 million, which implies free cash flow of about negative $211 million (my calculation). The release's highlights cite "positive GAAP operating cash flow of $7.2 million" for the quarter, but that figure cannot be checked against the tables in the filing.

The balance sheet is the larger story. Cash and cash equivalents were $4.45 billion at Sept. 30, up from $828.7 million at Dec. 31, 2025, and the company holds additional marketable securities. The filing shows $3.91 billion in proceeds from at-the-market equity offerings over the nine months. In other words, Rocket Lab now holds more cash than it has backlog, and most of that cash came from selling shares.

## The Iridium deal explains the cash

The release says the equity raise "fully funded" the pending acquisition of Iridium Communications (IRDM). Rocket Lab announced that deal on June 29 at about $8.0 billion in enterprise value, or $54 per Iridium share in cash and stock, according to [Inside GNSS](https://insidegnss.com/?p=197063). The same report says closing is expected in mid-2027, subject to Iridium shareholder approval and regulatory clearances, and that Rocket Lab had arranged a $3.6 billion bridge loan for the cash portion.

Iridium reported $871.7 million of 2025 revenue at a 57% OEBITDA margin, with more than 2.55 million active subscribers across government, defense, aviation, maritime and commercial markets. Inside GNSS also notes that the companies pitch Iridium's low-Earth-orbit network as a resilient positioning and timing option where GPS is degraded or unavailable.

This is worth separating from the backlog story. The $3.57 billion backlog does not include Iridium, and the cash on hand is largely earmarked for a deal that has not closed. Neither the cash balance nor the Iridium acquisition is evidence of operating strength in today's business.

## What to watch

Four items will show whether the backlog turns into earnings power:

- **Neutron's schedule.** Any further movement beyond 2027 would hit the part of the backlog with the least revenue visibility.
- **Government mix.** Whether Rocket Lab discloses the government share of its backlog again, and how much comes from Space Development Agency and Space Force work.
- **Cash conversion.** Whether operating cash flow stays positive on a full-quarter basis in Q4, which the company has not yet shown in its tables.
- **Iridium approvals.** Shareholder and regulatory milestones ahead of the mid-2027 target close.

For the defense-investing audience, the takeaway is narrow. A 52% quarterly backlog increase is a real signal of demand in launch and spacecraft, including from national security customers. It is also a number with no stated conversion schedule, resting partly on a rocket that has yet to fly. The cash is real, but it is mostly borrowed from future shareholders and set aside for a purchase. This is information, not a recommendation on any security.
