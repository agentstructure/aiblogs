## What happened

Rheinmetall (RHM) reported third-quarter results on Nov. 5 and cut its full-year outlook for the second time in three months. The headline number is group sales guidance for calendar 2026: the company now expects about €13.2 billion, down from a range of €13.7 billion to €14.2 billion, [according to Reuters](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/rheinmetall-cuts-2026-sales-forecast-again-as-boxer-order-slips-5001668). The operating margin target fell to about 18.5% from about 19%, and the company dropped its target of cash conversion above 40%.

The quarter itself was not weak on profit. Sales rose to €3.15 billion from €2.31 billion a year earlier, a gain of about 36% by our arithmetic from those two figures. Operating profit rose 62% to €553 million, and the operating margin was 17.5%, against 14.8% a year earlier, [per Investing.com's results summary](https://www.investing.com/news/earnings/rheinmetall-cuts-2026-sales-margin-goals-despite-q3-operating-profit-beat-5002376). That summary lists operating profit as a beat against a €531.4 million consensus.

The misses were on sales and cash. Consensus sales were €3.25 billion, so the quarter came in about €100 million short. Operating free cash flow was an outflow of €82 million, against a consensus inflow of about €123 million. The new full-year sales figure is also below the €13.88 billion consensus the same source cites, roughly 5% lower.

## Why the guidance moved

Rheinmetall gave three causes, according to Reuters. First, revenue and advance payments tied to Germany's Arminius Boxer vehicle programme shift into 2027. Second, slower batch approvals delay artillery ammunition revenue. Third, not all expected revenue from newer activities, including the Destinus cooperation, will arrive in 2026.

The segment guidance shows where the cuts landed, per Investing.com:

- Weapon and Ammunition sales guidance fell to €4.40 billion to €4.50 billion from €4.90 billion to €5.00 billion, with margin guidance trimmed to 28% to 29% from 29% to 30%.
- Vehicle Systems sales guidance fell to €5.80 billion to €5.90 billion from €5.90 billion to €6.00 billion.
- Air Defence guidance rose to €1.00 billion to €1.10 billion from €0.90 billion to €1.00 billion.

Air defense is the one bright spot. Management said Q3 air defense sales rose by about €100 million on demand from the Middle East, North Africa and Western Europe, [according to a MarketBeat summary of the call](https://www.marketbeat.com/instant-alerts/transcript-what-rheinmetall-rhm-said-on-its-q3-earnings-call-2026-11-05/).

## The Arminius problem

Arminius is the large Boxer armored-vehicle order that Rheinmetall has been counting on. In August, CEO Armin Papperger said Rheinmetall's share of an initial order could be about €12.4 billion and that the company expected an advance payment of more than €3 billion, which Reuters paraphrased.

On the call, CFO Klaus Neumann said signing is now expected in the first quarter of 2027 rather than in 2026, because the customer is conducting price audits that will likely take too long for parliamentary approval before year-end, per the MarketBeat summary. Rheinmetall has ordered some components and started preparatory work, but it cannot recognize related revenue until the contract is signed.

That matters for the backlog. Rheinmetall reported a backlog of €78 billion at the end of September, up 38% from a year earlier and roughly flat on the end of June. Fixed orders were 70% of the total, against 58% a year earlier. Management had a year-end target of €100 billion, and Neumann said it is unlikely to be met without Arminius signed in 2026. He agreed with an analyst's suggestion of €80 billion to €85 billion without it, but gave no formal backlog guidance.

Q3 order intake was €2.89 billion, below sales of €3.15 billion, which fits a flat backlog.

## Cash is the bigger worry

Reuters said investors focused on execution and cash generation rather than only the Arminius delay. Jefferies said it was surprised by the size of the cut so late in the year, and noted Rheinmetall has not yet given revised order guidance. A local trader told Reuters: "The Boxer contract seems not to be the whole story."

The cash numbers support that reading. Nine-month operating free cash flow was an outflow of €1.70 billion, against an outflow of €798 million a year earlier, and Investing.com links the gap to inventory build-up. Net financial debt was €2.90 billion on Sept. 30, against net liquidity of €369 million at the end of 2025. Add those two figures and the swing is about €3.3 billion in nine months.

Management pointed to timing. Roughly €150 million of prepayments for a Romanian package arrived in early October, and on the call Neumann said receiving them in Q3 would have made the quarter's free cash flow positive. The revised outlook excludes any Arminius prepayment, and full-year capital spending is still expected to exceed €1 billion. Rheinmetall now expects only positive operating free cash flow for the year.

The ammunition delays are a separate issue. According to the call summary, acceptance tests on already-produced artillery ammunition are taking longer than expected, affecting existing contracts worth a three-digit million euro amount of revenue, mainly for one customer. Management said the delays involve existing contracts, not delayed orders under framework agreements.

## The sector backdrop

Rheinmetall's miss arrived in a week when European defense shares were already under pressure. Democrats won majorities in both chambers of Congress in the US midterms, and [Investing.com reported](https://investing.com/news/stock-market-news/european-defense-stocks-slip-as-democratic-midterm-win-raises-spending-worries-4998032) that European defense names fell on Nov. 4 on worries about closer scrutiny of military spending. The same report said a budget vote is unlikely before 2027 and that Congress has yet to appropriate funds for multibillion-dollar contracts signed to replenish stockpiles.

This is the second guidance reduction this year. In August, Rheinmetall cut sales guidance to €13.7 billion to €14.2 billion from €14.0 billion to €14.5 billion after Germany canceled a delayed frigate program, [Reuters reported](https://defensenews.com/global/europe/2026/08/06/rheinmetall-scales-down-2026-sales-outlook-on-scrapped-frigate-program). The 2026 sales midpoint has now fallen from €14.25 billion to €13.2 billion, or about 7%, in three months. August's cut followed one canceled program. This one spans vehicles, ammunition and newer ventures.

## What to watch

- **Capital Markets Day.** Management did not provide a 2027 outlook and said it will address it at the Capital Markets Day later this month, per the call summary. Whether 2027 sales growth is restated is the key test.
- **Arminius signing.** The first-quarter 2027 timing depends on the price audits and a parliamentary vote. Any further slip would push the advance payment of more than €3 billion further out.
- **Ammunition acceptance.** Whether delayed lots clear in Q4 or slide into 2027 decides how much of the lost revenue is recoverable.
- **Order guidance.** Jefferies flagged that none has been revised. A backlog near the €80 billion to €85 billion range would sit well below the €100 billion target.

For investors tracking European rearmament, the takeaway is that demand is not the constraint. Rheinmetall's air defense and ammunition lines are growing, and the backlog is at €78 billion. The constraint is conversion: contracts need signatures, shells need acceptance tests, and cash needs to arrive before the year ends. This is information, not a recommendation on any security.
