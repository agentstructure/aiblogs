## The number most campaign pages hide

When founders benchmark a Regulation Crowdfunding raise, they usually look at an average. The July 2026 data from [Kingscrowd](https://kingscrowd.com/a-selective-crowdfunding-market-still-found-its-winners-july-2026/) shows why that is risky. Among Reg CF rounds that closed in the month, the average final investment was about $231,000. The median was about $95,000.

That gap is the story. A few large closes pull the average far above what a typical issuer collects. If you set a target by looking at the mean, you are benchmarking against the top of the distribution, not the middle.

The same report counts 66 rounds that closed out of more than 400 available that month, and 43 of those closed successfully. Nine reached at least 90% of their maximum target. Read plainly: roughly two of every three closing rounds succeeded, but only a small group filled most of the way to their ceiling.

## The market behind those closes

July was soft. Reg CF volume came to about $22.9 million, down 19.1% from June and 34.2% from July 2025, which Kingscrowd places near the low end of the last five years. [Crowdfund Insider's summary](https://www.crowdfundinsider.com/2026/08/302351-reg-cf-funding-drops-by-almost-20-in-july-reg-a-by-over-4) of the same Kingscrowd data puts the average raised per campaign at $64,700, down from $86,500 in June and $98,200 a year earlier. The two sources round differently, but they point the same way.

Activity did not fall with dollars. Crowdfund Insider reports 357 active monthly funding rounds in July, up from 331 in June and roughly flat on the 354 of a year earlier. More issuers are chasing less money, so the average campaign gets a smaller slice.

Money also pooled in a few places. DealMaker Securities led Reg CF with $7.34 million, or 32.1%, followed by Wefunder at $5.71 million (25.0%), StartEngine at $2.43 million and Netcapital at $2.19 million. The top four platforms held 77.2% of the total. At the top of the campaign list, Cytonics raised about $2.35 million on DealMaker in the month and Avadain about $2.16 million on Netcapital. Kingscrowd says only about 10% of active deals were raising more than $1 million.

## Reg A is even more concentrated

Regulation A showed the same shape at larger scale. July Reg A volume was about $43 million, and DealMaker enabled more than 85% of it. Four issuers (EnergyX at $17.25 million, Mode Mobile at $5.89 million, Frontieras North America at $4.82 million and Immersed at $3.87 million) accounted for about $31.8 million, or 73.9% of positive Reg A movement, according to Kingscrowd.

For a founder weighing the two routes, that matters. Reg A Tier 2 allows up to $75 million, but the realized averages are driven by a handful of large, well-promoted offerings. Kingscrowd also found in its Q1 study that [only 21.4% of Reg A offerings disclosed a valuation](https://lenderkit.com/blog/us-equity-crowdfunding-in-2026-reg-a-vs-reg-cf-data-shock-transparency-crisis-and-the-secs-next-move/), against 88.7% for Reg CF, per figures LenderKit cites from Crowdfund Insider. Investors who cannot see a price will often pass, so disclosure is a practical advantage for Reg CF issuers.

## What the data suggests about setting a target

None of this is a forecast, and the figures describe one month. Still, several practical points follow.

- **Anchor to the median, not the mean.** A $95,000 median close implies that a minimum target well below $100,000 matches what a typical successful round collects. A target built on the $231,000 average is more of a stretch than it looks.
- **Separate your minimum from your maximum.** Reg CF lets an issuer set a minimum to close and a higher cap. Only nine July closes got to 90% or more of their cap, so a cap is best treated as upside.
- **Count your own audience first.** Kingscrowd's read of the month is that campaigns with traction, a reachable audience and realistic targets still do well. Smaller raises can be meaningful, too: the report lists closes such as The Sports Bra at about $1.24 million on Republic alongside several in the low hundreds of thousands on smaller portals.
- **Expect a crowded field.** With 357 active rounds in July and more than 400 available, attention is the scarce resource.

Investor counts are a caution here. Kingscrowd reports 14,495 Reg CF investors in July against 13,786 a year earlier, but notes that coverage is inconsistent across platforms, so treat that comparison as rough.

## Recent months: a modest rebound

Kingscrowd's [September tracker](https://kingscrowd.com/monthly-raise/2026/09/) shows Reg CF commitments of about $17.6 million from 41 new deals in September, up from about $14.0 million from 44 new deals in August. October month to date stood at about $14.9 million from 24 new deals as of the page's capture, with 333 active Reg CF deals. The tracker's weekly list for Oct. 22 had Doroni Aerospace at about $2.2 million on DealMaker, well clear of the next four names, which ranged from roughly $179,000 to $246,000.

The tracker uses commitments and new-deal counts rather than closed capital, so it should not be compared directly with the July closing data above. The same page links to a headline citing $190.5 million in crowdfunding for Q3 2026; the underlying report was not available to review, so that figure is not relied on here.

## What to watch next

Three things will show whether the pattern holds. First, whether October's pace, already at $14.9 million with a week to go, pushes Reg CF back above its recent lows. Second, whether the median close moves up or the average falls toward it. Third, whether platform concentration continues, with DealMaker's share rising or Wefunder and StartEngine regaining ground.

For issuers, the lesson from July is modest but useful: in a market where the middle is much smaller than the average, an honest target and an existing audience matter more than a headline number.
