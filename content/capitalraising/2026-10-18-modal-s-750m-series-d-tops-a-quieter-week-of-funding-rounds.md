## The week in one number

The largest U.S. startup round announced this week was Modal Labs' $750 million Series D. The second largest was Icon's $200 million. After that, the field drops sharply: according to [Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/biggest-funding-rounds-investors-scaled-back-ai-biotech-modal/), only two financings reached the multiple hundreds of millions, and the tenth-biggest round, North.cloud's, was $60 million.

For founders planning a raise, that shape matters more than any single headline. Capital is still available in large amounts, but it is concentrating in companies that can show revenue at scale. The rounds below illustrate three different ways to make that case.

## Modal: usage-based growth, priced fast

Modal, which sells cloud infrastructure for running AI inference and other workloads, announced the $750 million Series D on Oct. 14. Accel led, and Kleiner Perkins joined, along with existing investors including Menlo Ventures, General Catalyst, Redpoint, Lux Capital, Amplify Partners and Bain Capital Ventures, according to [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/modal-750m-series-d-15-75bn-valuation). The valuation is $15.75 billion.

The step-up is the story. Modal's previous round was a $355 million raise in May at a $4.65 billion valuation, so the price has more than tripled in about five months. The company says its annualized revenue run rate now exceeds $1 billion, that usage tripled since the Series C, and that revenue grew more than 15 times over a year. Its sandboxes product, which lets AI agents run code in isolated environments, accounts for roughly $300 million of that annual figure. Named customers include DoorDash, Ramp, Linear, Cognition and Runway.

Two takeaways for other companies raising:

- **Usage-based revenue compounds visibly.** When customers pay for compute consumed, a growth curve shows up in the revenue line quickly, and investors can underwrite it without much narrative.
- **Fast follow-on rounds reprice everything.** A tripled valuation in five months is rare, and it is tied to a run rate that investors could check against customer demand. Founders should not read it as a benchmark for ordinary Series C-to-D steps.

The article does not specify how Modal will use the money, beyond plans for more products around training and fine-tuning models. Treat the round as a bet on demand for AI compute rather than on a defined spending plan.

## Icon: a Series D with a factory attached

Icon, the Austin, Texas builder of robotic construction systems best known for 3D-printed homes, announced a $200 million Series D on Oct. 13, co-led by Baillie Gifford and funds managed by BlackRock, per [Construction Dive](https://www.constructiondive.com/news/3d-printing-giant-icon-raises-funding/833001/). Crunchbase lists it as the week's second-largest round and reports that the company says it is on pace for four times revenue growth in 2026.

This raise is different from Modal's because the proceeds are tied to physical assets. Icon says the money will go toward starting production and manufacturing of its Titan printer at its Austin factory and opening a large research and development facility there. Titan lists at $899,000 and has a 27-foot build height. Icon says it has built more than 270 homes and structures, and the U.S. Army contracted it in January for $62.8 million to print 10 barracks at Fort Bliss.

The investor mix is worth noting. Baillie Gifford and BlackRock are large, long-horizon managers, the sort of investors that tend to appear when a company moves from building projects to manufacturing a product. Icon did not disclose its valuation in the coverage reviewed here, and one outlet reported a figure that this post could not confirm, so none is given.

For founders in hardware or construction tech, the lesson is the pairing: a government contract as proof of demand, then growth capital sized to a production line.

## Stand: a unicorn on a $70 million round

Stand, a San Francisco home insurer focused on wildfire and hurricane risk, announced a $70 million Series C on Oct. 15 at a $1 billion valuation, led by Saga Ventures, [Fortune reported](https://fortune.com/2026/10/15/stand-insurance-wildfire-unicorn-raises-70-million-1-billion-valuation/). Lowercarbon, Eclipse, Equal Ventures, Inspired Capital, Neotribe and Jackson Square Ventures also participated. The prior round was a Series B led by Eclipse about a year ago.

Stand says it now insures more than $10 billion of property, up from $1 billion a year earlier, and that it is growing about 15% a month. It builds a digital twin of each home to simulate disaster outcomes, sells through brokers and writes policies in California and Florida. Its pitch is that underwriting precision pays: the company says its model predicted wildfire outcomes about 85% of the time in a backtest of roughly 310 California homes, against 50% to 55% for conventional models. Fortune noted it could not independently verify that claim, and the sample is small.

The round is modest in size relative to the valuation. Founders who can show fast insured-value growth in a regulated, capital-heavy business can reach a $1 billion price without raising hundreds of millions. They also take on diligence about whether the claimed edge survives a bad catastrophe year, which a small backtest cannot settle.

## What to take from the week

- **Concentration at the top, depth below.** Crunchbase counted just two rounds in the hundreds of millions. The rest of the top ten ran from $60 million to $135 million, spread across biotech, healthcare software, AI tooling and insurance.
- **Proof points differ by sector.** Modal had a run rate, Icon had a government contract and a factory plan, Stand had insured-value growth. Match your evidence to what your category's investors actually underwrite.
- **Read valuations against the prior round.** Modal's repricing came with a verifiable revenue run rate. Where a company discloses neither revenue nor valuation, as Icon did not, the investor names and use of proceeds are the best signals available.
- **Company-reported metrics need discounting.** Run rate, growth rate and backtest accuracy are all self-reported in the coverage above. Expect diligence to test them.

## What to watch next

Whether the next few weeks bring more rounds in the hundreds of millions, or whether this week's thinner top end holds, will show how much of the recent giant-round pace was a cluster rather than a trend. Also watch whether Modal publishes how it will spend the proceeds, and whether Icon's Titan production start meets its manufacturing plan.
