## What the Q3 numbers say

Founders weighing a Regulation Crowdfunding (Reg CF) raise have been reading mixed signals all year. The latest quarterly data from Kingscrowd, a data and analytics firm that tracks online capital formation, gives a clearer picture, and it is not an encouraging one for small raises.

Kingscrowd reports that [investment crowdfunding recorded $190.5 million in Q3 2026](https://kingscrowd.com/reg-a-growth-helps-crowdfunding-reach-190-5-million-in-q3-2026/), up 1.9% from the previous quarter. That combined figure covers Reg CF and Regulation A (Reg A). Under the headline, though, the two exemptions moved differently. Per the same report, Reg CF [recorded $54.7 million and finished the quarter with a September rebound](https://kingscrowd.com/reg-a-growth-helps-crowdfunding-reach-190-5-million-in-q3-2026/). A search summary of the same report described it as Reg CF's weakest third quarter in five years, which we could not confirm on the page itself. Reg A supplied 71.3% of combined Q3 funding.

Read that carefully. A modest rise in the combined number was driven by the larger exemption, while the smaller one, the route most first-time and community-style issuers use, hit a five-year low for the period. These are figures from one data firm, and Kingscrowd's own tallies depend on what platforms and filings it can see, so treat them as a well-informed estimate rather than an official count.

## The year so far: fewer launches, more concentration

The Q3 result fits a pattern Kingscrowd flagged at mid-year. In its [H1 2026 report](https://kingscrowd.com/h1-2026-investment-crowdfunding-lower-volume-higher-concentration-selective-strength/), investors put $365.2 million into Reg CF and Reg A+ offerings, and combined volume fell 27.9% from the first half of 2025, though it still exceeded 2023 and 2024 levels. Reg CF launches dropped 31.2%, from 564 in H1 2025 to 388 in H1 2026. Reg A+ accounted for $225.7 million, or 61.8% of the half-year total.

The headline of that report, "Lower Volume, Higher Concentration, Selective Strength," is worth taking literally. Fewer companies tried to raise through Reg CF, and the dollars that did arrive were bunched into fewer places. Kingscrowd's [Q3 platform ranking](https://kingscrowd.com/reg-a-growth-helps-crowdfunding-reach-190-5-million-in-q3-2026/) shows the same thing at the intermediary level. DealMaker Securities led Reg CF with $18.28 million in the quarter, followed by Wefunder at $17.35 million and one other portal. Kingscrowd says those three together accounted for 82.4% of recorded Reg CF funding, up from 71.0% in Q3 2025.

For an issuer, that matters more than any single quarterly total. When three intermediaries hold the great majority of the dollars, the platform you pick is no longer a branding decision. It decides which investor lists, which email audiences and which community of repeat backers your campaign can reach.

## Platform filings and dollars tell different stories

Founders comparing portals often rely on filing counts, since those are easy to pull from the SEC's EDGAR system. Kingscrowd's dollar rankings and filing counts do not always point the same way. An [AngelSchool analysis of SEC Form C filings](https://www.angelschool.vc/blog/best-reg-cf-crowdfunding-platforms-for-investments) reports Wefunder at 234 deals over the last 12 months, down 14% from a year earlier, and says Netcapital's count fell 56%. Those are counts of offerings filed, not dollars raised, and the AngelSchool figures are a third-party estimate that has not been cross-checked against the platforms.

The takeaway is not that one portal is better. It is that a falling count of campaigns can sit alongside stable dollars at the top of the market. When many small campaigns drop out and a handful of larger ones continue, the average successful raise can hold up even as the number of attempts shrinks. In practical terms, a founder looking at a platform's "total raised to date" is looking at a number dominated by a small group of high performers, not at the typical outcome.

## Recent weeks: choppy, and hard to read

Kingscrowd's monthly and weekly reports show how uneven the recent run has been. Its [October 2026 funding report](https://kingscrowd.com/funding-report-for-october-2026/) lists $24.16M of total Reg CF net investment for the month, while the [week-ending 10/31 report](https://kingscrowd.com/funding-report-for-week-ending-10-31-2026/) shows $22.4M raised in October. The two do not match, and the pages do not explain the gap, so the safest reading is that October was in the low-to-mid $20 millions on Kingscrowd's measures, not a precise number. In the same October report, Wefunder and DealMaker together accounted for about 69% of commitments.

The [week ending November 14](https://kingscrowd.com/funding-report-for-week-ending-11-14-2026/) was reported at about $10.38M in total Reg CF net investment, with Wefunder showing 161 active rounds. A week like that illustrates the limits of single-period data. Totals can swing on one large offering closing or one platform recording commitments on a different schedule.

## What founders should do with this

None of this says crowdfunding has stopped working. Kingscrowd's mid-year exit and failure report found [15 failures and 11 exits through August 3, 2026](https://kingscrowd.com/mid-year-2026-investment-crowdfunding-exits-failures/), with failures down 57% from the same point in 2025. A thinner pipeline of issuers has not, on that evidence, produced a wave of collapses, although Kingscrowd itself calls failure counts a lagging indicator.

A few practical points follow for anyone planning a campaign this winter:

- **Benchmark against the right set.** Compare your target with what comparable issuers on your chosen platform actually closed, not with a platform's all-time total.
- **Treat platform choice as a distribution decision.** Ask each portal how many investors have backed more than one issuer there, and how it promotes live offerings, since the top three platforms now hold most of the dollars.
- **Plan for a longer raise.** With fewer launches overall but dollars concentrated, a campaign without an existing audience has a harder path to clear its minimum.
- **Consider whether Reg A fits.** Reg A supplied 71.3% of combined Q3 funding per Kingscrowd. It carries heavier legal and audit work, so it suits a company that is already larger, but it is where most of the money went.

## What to watch next

Kingscrowd's next quarterly update will show whether September's rebound in Reg CF was a turning point or a one-month blip. Also watch whether the top-three platform share keeps rising from 82.4%, and whether Reg CF launch counts stabilize near H1's 388. If volume recovers while concentration rises, small issuers will be competing for a narrower audience even in a healthy market. This post is information and commentary only, not investment advice.
