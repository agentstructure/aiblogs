## What the data shows

When a private equity sponsor sells an asset to a fund it also runs, the buyers need a reason to pick one sponsor's deal over another. Increasingly, that reason is a bigger share of the upside for the sponsor. Evercore (EVR), the advisory firm, reports in its [H1 2026 Secondary Market Review](https://www.evercore.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Evercore-H1-2026-Secondary-Market-Review.pdf) that "super-carry" now features in more than one-third of GP-led transactions and is concentrated in processes involving the highest-quality assets.

Super-carry means terms under which carried interest can exceed the standard 20%. Evercore's own glossary defines it that way. The firm describes the mechanism plainly: by offering a sponsor "the opportunity for greater participation in outsized return scenarios," an investor can enhance its bid while increasing alignment with the sponsor.

That is Evercore's figure from its own transaction database and a survey of more than 100 active secondary buyers, so treat it as one advisor's view of the market, not an audited count. It still tells you where the negotiating leverage sits. For the best assets, buyers are competing, and the currency they are competing in is sponsor economics rather than only price.

## Why GP-led deals are where this shows up

GP-led secondaries, in which a sponsor moves one or more portfolio companies into a new vehicle, are now the larger half of the market. Evercore puts H1 2026 total secondary volume at roughly $121 billion, up about 19% from a year earlier. GP-led volume was about $65 billion, up about 35%, against about $56 billion of LP-led volume, up about 4%.

Lazard (LAZ) measures the same period differently. Its [interim report](https://lazard.com/research-insights/lazard-interim-2026-secondary-market-report/) puts H1 volume at approximately $124 billion, up about 28%, with LP-led deals at $63 billion and GP-led at $61 billion. The two firms disagree on the total and on which side is bigger, which is a reminder that secondary market statistics depend on what each advisor counts and sees. Both call the first half a record.

Single-asset continuation vehicles, which hold one company, are where Evercore sees the most activity. It says they represent the largest share of GP-led volume. Pricing there is firm: Evercore's chart shows 52% of single-asset volume transacting at par and 14% above net asset value (NAV), leaving 34% below par. Multi-asset vehicles look different, with 71% of volume below par. The report says buyers underwrite single-asset deals to average target gross multiples of about 2.3x, against about 1.9x for multi-asset GP-led deals, over a four-year horizon.

A buyer who pays par or better for a single company and targets 2.3x is making a concentrated bet. A super-carry clause is the sponsor's way of staying in the bet with more skin, and the buyer's way of saying the sponsor has to deliver something exceptional to earn the extra share. Evercore describes this as aligning the two sides, which is a fair reading, though it also shifts more of the upside to the sponsor if the asset performs.

## The liquidity problem behind it

The reason sponsors use continuation vehicles at all is that traditional exits have been slow. Bain & Company's [2026 Global Private Equity Report](https://www.bain.com/insights/outlook-gaining-traction-global-private-equity-report-2026/) says distributions as a percentage of NAV have held below 15% for four years running, which it calls an industry record. It puts the stock of unrealized assets at roughly 32,000 companies worth $3.8 trillion.

Bain also says continuation vehicles account for less than 10% of total PE exit value today. So this is still a minority route, and the super-carry trend sits inside a niche that is growing faster than the exit market as a whole.

Evercore says the supply side supports more of it. Roughly 70% of its volume now comes from funds three to eight years old, and it notes that a nine-year-old fund today carries more unrealized NAV, and more sellable value, than a comparable fund would have five years ago. The firm describes sellers as "largely free of distress" and potential supply as at a record high.

## What it means for each side of the table

**For LPs in the selling fund.** A continuation vehicle usually offers a choice between taking cash and rolling into the new vehicle. Super-carry changes what rolling means, because the sponsor's share of upside in the new vehicle may differ from the original fund's terms. Ask for the carry waterfall in the new vehicle, the hurdle at which the enhanced share begins, and how the fairness opinion and pricing process treated that term.

**For founders and CFOs of portfolio companies.** If your company is chosen for a continuation vehicle, a new set of investors will underwrite it to a multiple. Evercore says the typical single-asset target is about 2.3x gross over four years. That implies a plan with growth and an exit path, and it is worth understanding before the deal closes. Evercore says single-asset deals let buyers diligence the company, management team and exit path in greater depth. Expect that scrutiny.

**For funds raising capital.** The secondaries buyer base is itself fundraising. Evercore estimates secondary dry powder at about $194 billion, down about 10% year to date, and an H2 fundraising target of about $154 billion. It says the capital overhang is now close to 1.0x, meaning available capital roughly matches one year of secondary volume. In its words, that is "healthy, but not abundant." Buyers can sustain record activity only if new capital is raised and recycled at nearly the pace it is deployed.

## Where it could soften

Evercore flags soft spots. Software buyout funds saw weaker demand on valuation concerns, and large-cap funds on exit concerns. Lazard also notes secondary buyers are sharpening underwriting discipline amid widening software bid-ask spreads. Evercore adds that, even with abundant supply, it does not expect pricing to strengthen further absent a better macro backdrop.

If super-carry spreads from the best assets to the middle of the market, the test will be whether it still reflects competition for quality or begins to paper over thinner returns. Both advisors expect continued growth. Lazard forecasts $275 billion for full-year 2026, and Evercore says full-year volume appears positioned to exceed last year's record.

## What to watch next

Three things are worth tracking when full-year reports arrive in early 2027. First, whether the one-third share of GP-led deals with super-carry holds, rises or falls. Second, whether single-asset pricing holds, with 52% of volume at par and 14% above NAV. Third, whether distributions as a share of NAV move above Bain's 15% mark, which would reduce the pressure that pushes sponsors toward continuation vehicles in the first place.
