## What happened

Mercor, the San Francisco company that connects AI developers with human experts for model training and evaluation, announced a $750 million Series D on November 10 at a $20 billion valuation, according to [AIWeekly's summary of the Reuters report](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/mercor-raises-750m-series-d-at-20b-doubling-in-a-year). That doubles the $10 billion valuation it carried when it raised a $350 million Series C in October 2025.

The investor list varies by outlet, which is common when a company names backers in pieces. AIWeekly lists Benchmark, funds managed by affiliates of Apollo Global Management, General Catalyst, NVIDIA (NVDA), Salesforce (CRM) and Zoom (ZM). [TechStartups](https://techstartups.com/?p=140368) names Nvidia, Salesforce, Benchmark, Felicis, General Catalyst, G Squared, Sapphire Ventures and Zoom, plus other unnamed investors. Both agree on Benchmark, General Catalyst, Nvidia, Salesforce and Zoom.

[Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/biggest-startup-funding-rounds-agentic-ai-infrastructure-mirendil-mercor/) ranked it the second-largest round of the week of November 7 to 13, noting the company was founded less than four years before the round.

## The detail in the footnotes

The headline number is $750 million. The more useful number is how much of it went into the company.

According to TechStartups, the round included a share sale that let some existing shareholders sell part of their holdings, and Mercor did not say how much of the $750 million is new capital versus proceeds from that sale. I could not confirm the split from a second source, so treat the new-money figure as unknown.

This matters because a "round size" can mean three different things:

- **Primary capital:** new money that goes onto the balance sheet and extends runway.
- **Secondary proceeds:** cash that goes to existing holders, usually employees and early investors.
- **Valuation:** the price per share that both of the above are struck at.

A $20 billion valuation is the same whether the primary is $750 million or $400 million. Dilution and runway are not. Founders reading a competitor's announcement should look for the primary figure before comparing it to their own raise.

## What the revenue figures do and don't say

AIWeekly reports $614 million in gross revenue for the first half of 2026, with an annualized run rate above $2 billion by June. [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/mercor-20-billion-valuation-deeptune-acquisition), citing CEO Brendan Foody, said in July that the run rate had passed $2 billion and had doubled in four months.

Those are gross figures. The Next Web noted that the $2 billion is gross billings, and that contractors keep 60 to 70 percent of billings, citing The Information. Based on that, it estimated net revenue of $600 million to $800 million, which would put the valuation at roughly 25 to 33 times net revenue. That multiple is The Next Web's estimate, not a company disclosure, and it was calculated from a July article on a valuation that was then only under discussion.

For a marketplace business, the gross-versus-net distinction is the main thing an investor diligences. It changes the multiple by a factor of three or more. Founders pitching a marketplace or services-heavy model should expect the same question and have the take rate ready.

## Where the money is going

Per TechStartups, Mercor said the funding "accelerates our investment in the infrastructure and research for evals and post-training," including simulated environments where AI agents can learn professional workflows. In July it announced it was acquiring Deeptune, which builds those kinds of environments, according to The Next Web. Terms were not disclosed.

That Deeptune deal carried a governance footnote worth knowing about. The Next Web reported that Foody had been an angel investor in Deeptune's $43 million round in March, and that public reporting had not established whether Mercor's board or outside investors reviewed his personal stake. Related-party transactions are routine at fast-growing companies, and the practical lesson for any founder is to document board review when one occurs.

The same article reported that a supply-chain attack on the open-source library LiteLLM in March 2026 exposed up to four terabytes of Mercor data, that Meta paused work with the company, and that Mercor called the impact "very limited." A $20 billion price eight months later suggests investors weighed that risk and went ahead.

## The rest of the week

Mercor was not alone in raising at scale. Crunchbase's list for November 7 to 13 shows a lineup tilted toward infrastructure and hardware as much as software:

- **Torus** raised $500 million in growth financing led by Magnetar Capital, for distributed power and compute infrastructure.
- **Path Robotics** raised a $300 million Series E at a $2.45 billion valuation, led by Georgian.
- **LangChain** was listed at $300 million in a Series C led by CapitalG at a $3 billion valuation. I could not find a second source for that figure, so treat it as Crunchbase's alone.
- **Nominal** raised $240 million at a $3 billion valuation, led by Lightspeed Venture Partners and Insight Partners.
- **Imprint** raised $130 million at a $1.9 billion valuation, led by Khosla Ventures, the only fintech deal in the top 10.

Outside the Crunchbase list, [Agile Robots](https://www.unite.ai/agile-robots-raises-500m-series-f-at-8-5b-valuation-to-expand-production/) announced a $500 million Series F at an $8.5 billion valuation led by SoftBank Group, with NVIDIA, Hitachi and T.Capital participating. The company said the round would expand its US presence and add production capacity, including in Germany.

## What to take from it

Three practical points for founders and CFOs raising now:

1. **Ask for the primary number.** When a round includes a tender, the announced size overstates the capital the company can spend. Model your own raise on primary dollars, and decide in advance whether you would allow a secondary component.
2. **Know your gross-to-net bridge.** If your revenue is billings with a large pass-through, investors will convert it to net before they apply a multiple.
3. **Expect cross-checks.** Reporting on the same deal differed on investors and detail. If your announcement lists backers, make sure every named firm has agreed to it.

## What to watch next

Mercor has not, in the sources reviewed here, published how much of the round was primary. If it does, or if a filing surfaces, that will show how much runway the round really bought. Also watch whether the larger rounds of this week close at the reported terms: the Crunchbase list is built from announcements and press reports, and valuations and amounts sometimes change in later filings.
