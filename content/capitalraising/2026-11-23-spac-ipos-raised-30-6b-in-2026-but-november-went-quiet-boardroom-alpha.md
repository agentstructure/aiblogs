## The headline number hides the trend

The year-to-date SPAC tally looks strong. As of Nov. 20, 2026, the data firm Boardroom Alpha's [SPAC Research tracker](https://www.boardroomalpha.com/spac) shows 158 SPAC IPOs this year, raising $30.6 billion. The same page counts 140 deals in the pipeline and 46 de-SPAC closes over the last 12 months.

The monthly series underneath tells a different story. In Boardroom Alpha's [Nov. 20 market update](https://www.boardroomalpha.com/research/spac-market-update-november-20-2026-tdac-afjk), SPAC IPO counts run 24 in January, 27 in February (the peak), 11 in March, 16 in April, 20 in May, 19 in June and 18 in July. Then the count drops to 12 in August, 5 in September and 6 in October. November, through the 20th, shows zero IPOs and $0.0 million raised. The same table says the first 20 days of October had 4 IPOs worth $491 million, and puts the 12-month average at 16 a month.

Other data providers count slightly differently. FTI Consulting's [Q3 2026 report](https://www.fticonsulting.com/insights/reports/ipo-spac-market-update-q3-2026), dated Oct. 29, puts third-quarter SPAC formations at 34, down from 51 in the second quarter. Those totals do not match Boardroom Alpha's monthly figures exactly, which is normal when trackers define a "formation" or a pricing date differently. The direction is the same in both: issuance peaked early in the year and has faded since.

One caveat on the zero. The tracker counts pricings, not filings. Renaissance Capital's [news feed](https://www.renaissancecapital.com/IPO-Center/News) lists SPAC filings in mid-November, including ZA Acquisition I (a $75 million filing on Nov. 9). Compute AI Acquisition (AICPU) [filed on Nov. 13](https://www.renaissancecapital.com/IPO-Center/News/122719/spac-compute-ai-acquisition-files-for-a-75-million-ipo-targeting-ai) for up to $75 million, offering 7.5 million units at $10 with a warrant exercisable at $11.50 and a right to half a share. Sponsors are still lining up new vehicles. They just haven't priced any this month, at least through Nov. 20.

## Where the activity went: extensions and redemptions

When fresh blank-check money slows, the vehicles already on the market become the story. Boardroom Alpha's daily briefs for Nov. 18 to 20 are mostly about deadline extensions and the cash that leaves trust accounts when shareholders vote.

Kochav Defense Acquisition Corp (KCHV) is the clearest example. According to the [Nov. 19 brief](https://www.boardroomalpha.com/research/spac-market-update-november-19-2026-kchv), shareholders approved an extension to Nov. 29, 2027. In the same vote, 21,128,865 shares were redeemed and $222,486,948 left the trust, which stood at $10.53 per share. The extension passed, but the vehicle carries into 2027 with less cash than it started with.

Aimei Health Technology (AFJK) extended to Dec. 6, 2027 with a far smaller outflow: 841,629 shares redeemed and $10,377,286 removed, at a trust value of $12.33 per share, per the [Nov. 20 brief](https://www.boardroomalpha.com/research/spac-market-update-november-20-2026-tdac-afjk). The gap between those two votes is the point. Redemption outcomes vary widely, and sponsors who need an extension have no reliable way to predict how much cash will stay.

Some are now paying to keep it. The same brief notes that FACT II Acquisition Corp (FACT) offered shares to investors who agreed to forgo redemptions, with the stated aim of preserving trust funds, and its vote extended the deadline from Nov. 27, 2026 to Nov. 25, 2027. Boardroom Alpha lists its trust at $10.83. Others are buying time in small increments: Quartzsea Acquisition Corp (QSEA) deposited $100,000 to extend by one month to Dec. 19, 2026, and Future Vision II Acquisition Corp (FVN) extended to Dec. 13, 2026 using a $65,000 sponsor note, per the [Nov. 18 brief](https://www.boardroomalpha.com/research/spac-market-update-november-18-2026-wenn-alcyf).

The tracker's own vote calendar shows four votes in the next seven days, one extension and three deal votes. Among them, Research Alliance Corp III (RACC) votes on Nov. 24 and Crown PropTech Acquisitions (CPTK) and Inflection Point Acquisition Corp VII (IPXG) vote on Nov. 25.

## Deals are still getting signed, with PIPE money doing the lifting

The merger pipeline has not stopped. Wen Acquisition Corp (WENN) said on Nov. 18 that battery maker Our Next Energy plans to merge at a $600 million pre-money valuation and is targeting $400 million in gross proceeds. Translational Development Acquisition Corp. (TDAC) and ProLogium amended their merger agreement on Nov. 20 to set a $3.9 billion equity value, and ProLogium corrected its proposed investment commitments from $85 million to up to $135 million at $10 per share.

Note how the financing is described. Boardroom Alpha's summary of Inflection Point VII's pending deal with Elroy Air cites a $175 million PIPE ahead of the shareholder vote, and the HCAC (Hall Chadwick Acquisition Corp) deal for REEcycle added a $15 million PIPE commitment, bringing total commitments to $17.1 million. In each case, outside capital is meant to backstop a trust that may shrink. That is the practical lesson of the redemption data: a SPAC's headline trust is the ceiling on cash at closing, not the expected amount.

Not every deal survives. The Nov. 18 brief lists one termination in the prior 10 days, between WINV and Embed Financial Group on Nov. 9.

## How the stock performs afterward

Boardroom Alpha's FAQ states that roughly 80% of recent de-SPACs trade below the SPAC's $10 trust value within a year, per its own distribution chart. That is the firm's claim and we could not reproduce it from public data, so treat it as a directional marker rather than a settled statistic. It does fit the pricing seen in the tracker's recent de-SPAC list, where Forge Nano (NANO), Evernorth Holdings (XRPN) and Super Apps Holdings (BBCI) are listed at $3.04, $6.84 and $1.15 respectively. Those are tracker snapshots, not closing prices on a fixed date.

## What to watch

For founders weighing a SPAC merger, the useful read is about cash certainty, not the count of SPAC IPOs. A 158-IPO year means many sponsors face deadlines in 2027 and 2028, and each extension vote is a chance for holders to take cash out. Ask what minimum cash the deal needs, how much is committed outside the trust, and who pays for the extension.

For sponsors, the question is whether the zero-IPO November is a pause or a regime change. Watch three things:

- Whether any SPAC prices before year-end, and at what size. Recent filings, such as Compute AI Acquisition's $75 million, sit at the low end of the $75 million to $100 million deals listed for October.
- How many of the 140 pipeline deals reach a vote, and how much trust cash survives. The December votes, including Dec. 1 for Kensington Capital Acquisition Corp VI (KCAC), are next in line.
- Whether more sponsors offer incentives to non-redeeming holders, as FACT II did.
