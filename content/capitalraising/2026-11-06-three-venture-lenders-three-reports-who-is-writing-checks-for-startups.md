## Three lenders, one quarter, very different results

Venture debt is usually discussed as a single market. The third-quarter reports from three publicly traded lenders, all filed within a week, show it is closer to three markets with different appetites, different funding costs and different terms for borrowers.

Hercules Capital (HTGC) reported on October 29. Trinity Capital (TRIN) and TriplePoint Venture Growth (TPVG) both reported on November 4. All three lend to venture-backed companies, so their numbers are a rare same-quarter read on what lenders are doing, not what trade guides say they do.

## Hercules and Trinity are growing; TriplePoint is shrinking its book

Hercules booked [$701.0 million of new debt and equity commitments in the third quarter](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001280784/000128078426000055/htgcq32026release.htm) and funded $572.9 million. Year to date, commitments are $3.44 billion, up 20.0% from a year earlier. Its pending pipeline of signed, non-binding term sheets stood at $571.7 million as of October 26.

Trinity reported [$880.5 million of new commitments and $614.1 million of gross fundings](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001786108/000119312526446244/trin-ex99_1.htm). Of the fundings, $431.9 million went to 15 new portfolio companies, so most of the quarter's money went to borrowers new to Trinity. Its portfolio is about $2.86 billion at fair value across 198 companies.

TriplePoint looks different. It made [$43.0 million of new debt commitments, but funded only $0.7 million of debt](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001580345/000158034526000037/tpvgq32026earningsrelease.htm) in the quarter. Repayments and the sale of an investment (Prodigy, for $43.8 million in cash) more than offset new lending, and total investment income fell to $20.2 million from $22.7 million a year earlier. After quarter-end it reported $60.0 million of new commitments closed and $57.4 million funded through November 3, so activity picked up, but from a low base.

A commitment is not cash in a borrower's account. Venture debt facilities often fund in tranches tied to milestones, so the gap between commitments and fundings is worth reading. TriplePoint's own release says $14.8 million of its $171.6 million in unfunded commitments depends on portfolio companies reaching milestones.

## Why the gap: credit quality, leverage and the cost of the lender's own money

Lenders pass their own balance-sheet constraints on to borrowers. Three data points explain much of the divergence.

**Net asset value.** Hercules reported [NAV of $12.26 per share, up 0.9% from $12.15 in the second quarter](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001280784/000128078426000055/htgcq32026release.htm). Trinity's NAV rose to $13.61 from $13.47. TriplePoint's net assets were $8.32 per share versus $8.73 at December 31, 2025, a decline of about 4.7% by our arithmetic, and it posted a net decrease in net assets from operations of $1.7 million for the quarter.

**Credit.** Trinity reported three non-accrual loans with about $17.2 million of fair value, or 0.7% of its debt investments. Hercules had two non-accruals, with a cost of $18.8 million. TriplePoint did not disclose non-accruals in its release, but its debt book is rated more cautiously: 23.8% of the debt portfolio at fair value sits in the "Yellow" category and 3.0% in "Orange," on its own internal scale. Internal scales differ by lender, so those figures should not be compared directly with the others.

**Access to capital.** Trinity sold shares through its at-the-market program at a weighted average of $17.82 in the third quarter, against a NAV of $13.61. By our arithmetic that is a premium of roughly 31%, which lets it raise equity cheaply and lend more. On October 5 it also [issued $350.0 million of 7.500% unsecured notes due 2032](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001786108/000119312526446244/trin-ex99_1.htm), using the proceeds to repay part of its bank facility. A lender that can fund itself at that cost and keep growing can underwrite more deals. A lender with a shrinking NAV and a stressed book has less room to.

## What the yields say about pricing

The three lenders report yield on different bases, so treat these as ranges, not a league table.

- Trinity: 15.2% weighted average effective yield on debt investments at cost.
- TriplePoint: 12.4% weighted average annualized portfolio yield on debt investments, with 11.2% yield at origination on its third-quarter fundings.
- Hercules: 12.3% GAAP effective yield on debt investments, with a non-GAAP "core yield" of 11.9%.

Trinity's higher number sits alongside a book that is 89.2% first-lien and 78.4% floating-rate, so the release does not attribute the gap to weaker collateral. Hercules reported that early loan repayments were [$148.3 million, down 74.1% from $572.1 million in the second quarter](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001280784/000128078426000055/htgcq32026release.htm). Fewer refinancings usually mean borrowers are staying with the debt they have, either because new equity rounds are not replacing it or because the loans are priced competitively. The release does not say which.

For a founder, the practical read is that all-in cost on a venture loan from these lenders is running in a low-to-mid teens range on a yield basis. Fees, warrants and prepayment terms come on top of the stated rate, and those are negotiable.

## What founders and CFOs should do with this

This is information, not advice, but the data suggests some questions to ask any prospective lender.

1. **Ask how the lender funds itself.** A lender with a stock trading above book value and access to unsecured notes can hold terms through a cycle. A lender with a falling NAV may tighten, slow fundings or push for faster prepayment.
2. **Ask about draw conditions.** TriplePoint's milestone-dependent commitments are a reminder that a headline facility size can overstate the cash you can actually draw.
3. **Compare the structure, not just the rate.** Warrant coverage, final payments, prepayment fees and covenants move the real cost more than a point of interest.
4. **Run more than one process.** The spread between 11.2% at origination for TriplePoint's recent fundings and 15.2% on Trinity's book is a reminder that pricing differs by lender and by borrower profile.

## What to watch next

Two lines in the releases are worth tracking. Hercules said it closed $219.6 million of commitments in the fourth quarter through October 26, with $89.2 million funded, which will show whether its momentum carries into year-end. TriplePoint's post-quarter activity ($60.0 million closed) will show whether its third quarter was a pause or a trend. The other metric is repayment volume: if early repayments at Hercules recover from $148.3 million, it would suggest equity rounds are again refinancing venture debt.

One caution on the sources. These are company-issued earnings releases, filed with the SEC, and they describe each lender's own portfolio. They are not a measure of the whole venture debt market, and lenders choose which metrics to emphasize. Read them as three data points, then check them against the term sheets in front of you.
