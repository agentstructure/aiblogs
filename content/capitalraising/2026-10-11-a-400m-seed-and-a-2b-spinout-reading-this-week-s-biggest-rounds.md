## The week in one table

Crunchbase's [list of the ten largest US rounds announced October 3 to 9](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/biggest-funding-rounds-ai-energy-cloud-axiom-typesafe/) reads like a catalogue of every corner of the AI economy, plus power and quantum. The top ten:

- **Axiom Solutions International** (Austin): $2B, at a $37.5B initial enterprise value, led by General Catalyst with Koch Equity.
- **TypeSafe AI** (San Francisco): $870M, a foundation model developer, with Andreessen Horowitz as largest investor and Sequoia Capital participating.
- **Oratomic** (Pasadena): $475M for fault-tolerant quantum computing, with Arch Venture Partners, Spark Capital, Khosla Ventures, Index Ventures, General Catalyst and Bezos Expeditions.
- **SignSplit** (New York): $400M seed at a $1B valuation from W Group.
- **Vinci** (Palo Alto): $250M Series B at $1.5B from Advent, Temasek and Xora.
- **Atomic Machines** (Emeryville): $250M raised to date as it emerged from stealth.
- **Voltus** (San Francisco): $225M Series D from Generation Investment Management, Activate Capital and Vitol.
- **Type One Energy** (Knoxville), **Ledgebrook** (Boston) and **Arena**: $200M each.

Headline totals matter less than what each deal reveals about structure and stage. Four stand out.

## Axiom: a spinout financed before it lists

The largest deal is not a classic venture round. Flex is separating its Cloud and Power Infrastructure segment as Axiom, and [Axiom sold $2B of Series A Convertible Preferred Stock](https://pulse2.com/axiom-secures-2-billion-convertible-preferred-investment-at-37-5-billion-enterprise-value/) to funds affiliated with General Catalyst and Koch Equity Development, plus unnamed co-investors.

The terms are the story. The preferred pays a 10% annual cash dividend before the separation. After it, the dividend drops to 6% if paid in cash or 7% if paid in kind, with possible increases after the fifth anniversary. General Catalyst gets the right to nominate one board director after the separation. Axiom is expected to become an independent, publicly traded company in the first quarter of 2027.

Proceeds may fund part of Axiom's acquisition of EPC Power, repay bridge financing tied to that deal, pay preferred dividends or cover general corporate purposes. Flex separately lined up committed term-loan financing for the rest of the purchase price.

For founders and CFOs, this is a template for pre-IPO capital: investors take a senior, income-paying security with a board seat instead of common equity at a negotiated multiple. Compare it with the venture-debt tradeoffs we covered earlier this week; the logic of paying a coupon to keep ownership is the same, applied at $2B scale.

## SignSplit: a $400M seed

SignSplit's raise is labeled a seed round at a $1B valuation, from W Group, for tools to protect, license and contribute human data. A seed that size breaks the usual stage vocabulary. It also shows why stage labels alone say little: the number to compare is how much capital arrives against how little operating history exists.

Our look at seed valuations earlier this week argued that terms deserve as much negotiation as price. A round this large makes the point sharper. Whatever the headline valuation, the governance rights, liquidation preferences and milestones attached to a check this size are where the real bargain sits.

## TypeSafe and Arena: valuations that compound in months

TypeSafe's $870M raise was [announced October 9 as a Series A at a $7.5B valuation](https://www.unite.ai/typesafe-ai-raises-870m-series-a-at-7-5b-valuation-to-ship-more-ai-models/), led by Andreessen Horowitz with Sequoia, existing investor DCVC and angels participating. A16z general partner Martin Casado joins the board. The company released its first model in early access on September 15, 2026, so the round was priced against less than a month of early access. Reports on adoption also differ: TypeSafe says a third of the Fortune 500 use its model, while a16z says 25%. Founders should note the gap, because investors' diligence rarely rests on a single stated figure.

Arena, the model-evaluation platform that grew out of the Berkeley research group behind Chatbot Arena, raised a [$200M Series B at $3.1B post-money, led by Lightspeed and Khosla Ventures](https://seedtable.com/companies/lmarena/funding-rounds/series-b-2026-10). Seedtable puts that at roughly double its valuation of about $1.7B in January 2026, and notes total funding of about $450M after a $100M seed in May 2025 and a $150M Series A in January 2026. Seedtable also flags two risks: investors who back the AI labs being ranked could undermine trust in the leaderboard, and the company will eventually need a paid business model.

## What a lower-profile round looks like

Outside the top ten, [Stuut raised a $52.5M Series B](https://www.finsmes.com/2026/10/stuut-raises-52-5m-in-series-b-funding.html) led by Insight Partners, with Andreessen Horowitz, Microsoft's M12 and Activant, bringing total funding to $93M. The New York company sells an AI platform for order-to-cash automation, covering collections, cash application, payments, disputes and credit. It plans to use the money to meet customer demand and expand into transaction financial infrastructure, including credit, lending and funds movement.

That is a more recognizable shape for most readers: a defined product for finance teams, a priced Series B, and a stated plan to move into adjacent lending. It is also a reminder that the megadeals are the exception.

## Why it matters

Three takeaways for people raising money:

1. **The cutoff keeps rising.** In Crunchbase's [list for September 12 to 18](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/biggest-funding-rounds-ai-space-fintech-temporal/), tenth place was Sling Therapeutics at $123M. This week, tenth place was $200M, and several rounds tied at that level. Comparisons to "the biggest rounds" now describe a market few companies can enter.
2. **Structure is doing more work.** Convertible preferred with a dividend, equity plus debt, and sizable seed rounds all appeared this week. Read the security, not just the headline.
3. **Capital is concentrating by theme.** Crunchbase's [venture coverage](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/) noted that third-quarter global venture funding totaled $159B across close to 6,000 funded startups, and its October 9 roundup observed that investors appear to be leaving few AI-related niches unfunded. If your business sits outside those themes, your comparables are different, and so should be your expectations on valuation and speed.

## What to watch

Watch whether Axiom's preferred terms become a reference point for other carve-outs heading to public markets ahead of the first quarter of 2027. Watch also whether stage labels keep stretching: a $400M seed and an $870M Series A make the next down-round or flat-round conversation harder, not easier. And for Arena-style businesses whose value rests on influence and data, the question is when paid revenue replaces investor enthusiasm.
