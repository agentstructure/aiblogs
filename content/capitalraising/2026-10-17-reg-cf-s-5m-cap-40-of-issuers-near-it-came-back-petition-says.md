## What happened

Regulation Crowdfunding lets a US company raise up to $5 million in any 12-month period from the general public through a registered intermediary. A petition now pending at the SEC asks for four times that: $20 million, indexed to inflation.

The filer is Crowdfund Capital Advisors, signed by principal Sherwood Neiss. [According to the SEC filing](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/petitions/2026/petn4-914.pdf), the petition was first sent to Chairman Paul Atkins on January 6, 2026, and resubmitted to the SEC Secretary on July 13, 2026 as File No. 4-914. A [third supplement dated October 9](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/petitions/2026/petn4-914-third-supplement.pdf) adds new data and responds to the public comments so far. The SEC also lists a separate petition, [File No. 4-889](https://www.sec.gov/rules-regulations/2026/03/4-889), titled "Rulemaking petition to modernize SEC Regulation Crowdfunding," which the 4-914 filing says seeks a similar cap increase.

The cap has moved before. It was $1 million at launch in 2016 and [rose to $5 million in 2021](https://thecrowdspace.com/blog/crowdfunding-weekly-digest-september-28-october-4-2026/), per CrowdSpace's digest. [Crowdfund Insider notes](https://www.crowdfundinsider.com/2026/01/257193-petition-filed-with-securities-and-exchange-commission-to-raise-reg-cf-funding-cap-to-20-million/) the SEC can change the limit by rule, without an act of Congress.

## What the petitioner's data says

Everything in this section comes from the petitioner's own database, which it calls CCLEAR and says corrects for incomplete issuer reporting. Treat it as advocacy evidence, not an SEC finding. The petition looks at issuers that raised roughly $4 million to $5 million in a single offering, meaning they approached the cap.

- 40.3% of those near-cap issuers later returned for follow-on Reg CF offerings, with a median gap of 20 months.
- Their median additional capital raised after that point was about $3.1 million.
- Together they raised more than $214 million after approaching the cap.
- 29.4% raised at least $1 million in later Reg CF offerings.

The October supplement adds more. Issuers whose stated maximum was within 2% of the $5 million cap came back for follow-ons at a rate of 28.8%, against 11.3% for other issuers. Among 722 issuers with two or more offerings that stated a valuation, the latest offering's median terms were 1.54 times the first. On geography, $1.82 billion of $2.908 billion in funded capital that could be assigned to a metro area, or 62.6%, went to companies outside Boston, Los Angeles, San Francisco and New York. All figures are as of October 8, 2026.

The argument built on this: the cap is a binding constraint that pushes successful issuers into serial offerings. Neiss has said a $10 million limit [could recreate some of the same inefficiencies](https://www.crowdfundinsider.com/2026/01/257193-petition-filed-with-securities-and-exchange-commission-to-raise-reg-cf-funding-cap-to-20-million/) and that issuers raising $10 million to $20 million tend to be post-revenue and more mature.

## The counterweight: most campaigns never get near the cap

The SEC's own staff data tells a different story about the typical issuer. The [SEC's DERA report](https://www.sec.gov/files/dera-reg-cf-2505.pdf) covers May 16, 2016 to December 31, 2024:

- 8,492 initiated offerings by 7,134 issuers, excluding 990 withdrawn.
- 3,869 offerings reported proceeds, totaling about $1.3 billion. The authors call that likely a lower-bound estimate.
- The average successful raise was about $346,000 and the median about $113,000.
- Among 3,253 unique issuers reporting proceeds, 8 (0.25%) later reached an IPO, 71 (2.2%) were acquired and 110 (3.4%) raised venture capital.

Against a median near $113,000, a $5 million ceiling is irrelevant for almost every campaign. The petitioner's case rests on a small, high-performing slice. That does not make it wrong. It does mean a higher cap would change the market for the top of the funnel, not for the median founder running a community round.

The petition's own sample is also selected: by definition it looks at issuers that already succeeded. It cannot say how a $20 million raise would perform with retail investors who are subject to individual investment limits, which the petition does not address.

## Why it matters now

The timing is not accidental. [Crowdfund Insider reports](https://www.crowdfundinsider.com/?p=312405) that Reg CF issuers raised $139.5 million in the first half of 2026, down 28% from the first half of 2025, and counts 73 FINRA-registered funding portals with only one new portal launched this year. Platforms are competing for fewer dollars. A higher cap would let them keep their best issuers longer instead of losing them to Reg A+ or traditional private rounds.

Procedure is the other reason. The October supplement says the comment record has ten letters, nine of them supportive, and points to an item called "Updating the Exempt Offering Pathways" on the SEC's October 2026 regulatory agenda as a possible vehicle for the change. It also cites the SEC's Regulation Crypto Assets proposal, which the filing says would permit up to $75 million in retail offerings per 12 months. We have not independently reviewed that proposal, and the petition's reading of it is the petitioner's own.

A petition is only a request. SEC staff review it and the Commission decides; it can open a rulemaking, seek comment first or do nothing. No timeline has been announced.

## What founders and investors should do with this

For founders planning a Reg CF round, the practical points are narrow:

- If your target is under a few million dollars, nothing changes. Plan against the current $5 million limit.
- If you expect to hit the cap, the petition's data suggests follow-on offerings are common, so plan the sequence of rounds rather than assuming one raise is your last.
- If you are comparing exemptions, watch whether a higher Reg CF cap narrows the gap with Reg A+ and Reg D 506(c) on cost and speed.

For investors, a larger cap means larger and more mature issuers on the same platforms, with the same individual investment limits. The DERA exit numbers are a reminder of base rates: an IPO outcome occurred for 0.25% of issuers with reported proceeds.

## What to watch next

- Whether the SEC's October agenda item on exempt offering pathways includes Reg CF limits.
- New comment letters on File No. 4-914, especially from critics, since the record so far is overwhelmingly supportive.
- Whether any proposal pairs a higher cap with the conforming Rule 12g-6 and Section 12(g) changes the petitioner requests.
- Fourth-quarter Reg CF volume, to see whether the 28% first-half decline persists.

This is information, not investment advice. Reg CF offerings are high-risk and illiquid, and investors can lose everything they put in.
