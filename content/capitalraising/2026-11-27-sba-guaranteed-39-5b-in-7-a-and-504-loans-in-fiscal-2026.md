## What the SBA reported

On Nov. 2, the Small Business Administration published its [fiscal 2026 results](https://legacy.sba.gov/article/2026/11/02/sba-fuels-main-street-growth-100-billion-delivered-fy26). The agency says it guaranteed 62,205 loans under its 7(a) and 504 programs, totaling $39.5 billion. It calls that the third-highest lending volume in its history.

The split matters for anyone choosing between the two products. The 7(a) program, the general-purpose workhorse, approved 56,130 loans totaling $32.4 billion. The 504 program, which finances long-term fixed assets such as real estate and equipment, approved 6,075 loans totaling $7.1 billion. The release says that works out to a combined 1,350 loans each week.

Other figures in the release are worth keeping in view for a capital-raising audience. The SBA says the two programs supported more than 9,700 startups, totaling $6.5 billion in financing. It also reports 10,314 rural loans totaling $6.5 billion, and a combined $7 billion in construction and manufacturing lending across 11,790 loans.

One caution on the headline. The release is titled "$100 Billion Delivered in FY26," but that number is a bundle. According to the release, it includes a record $60 billion in combined private capital and SBA leverage through its small business investment company (SBIC) program, plus disaster loans and entrepreneurial-development funding. The $39.5 billion in 7(a) and 504 guarantees is the figure that tells you how much guaranteed bank-loan capital reached borrowers.

## The shutdown gap

The SBA is explicit that the year was interrupted. Its release cites a 43-day federal shutdown that halted SBA lending for nearly two months of the fiscal year. The same release estimates the 2025 freeze stopped it from guaranteeing roughly $170 million in loans each business day, about $5 billion in financing in total. Those are the agency's own estimates, not an audited measure of lost lending, and the release does not fully reconcile how long the programs were closed.

For context, the SBA's [Sept. 30, 2025 release](https://legacy.sba.gov/article/2025/09/30/trump-sba-delivers-record-capital-small-businesses-fy25) on the prior fiscal year cited 84,400 combined loans totaling $44.8 billion, with 7(a) at 77,600 loans and $37 billion. That release was issued on the last day of the year, so the final tallies may differ slightly, and its component loan counts do not exactly sum to its stated total. Still, the direction is clear in the agency's own numbers: fewer loans and fewer dollars in fiscal 2026 than the year before, with a shutdown in the middle.

American Banker, [reporting in early October](https://www.americanbanker.com/news/sba-starts-new-fiscal-year-with-even-tighter-credit-box), described lending as declining in fiscal 2026 after three years of growth, and said 7(a) volume had been dipping. The shutdown is one explanation. The other is policy.

## A tighter rulebook from Oct. 1

The SBA's updated operating procedures, SOP 50 10 8.1, were [published Aug. 14 and took effect Oct. 1](https://www.doeren.com/viewpoint/sop-50-10-8-1-what-sba-lenders-should-be-thinking-about-before-oct-1), according to a lender-focused summary from accounting firm Doeren Mayhew. It replaces the prior procedures for both the 7(a) and 504 programs. Several changes affect how a founder or owner should prepare an application.

First, lenders must treat business cash flow as the primary source of repayment. Second, the debt service coverage ratio, the test of whether cash flow covers loan payments, is now "transaction-dependent," and for certain change-of-ownership deals lenders must use quality-of-earnings figures in the calculation. Third, acquisition and expansion deals priced at $3 million or more require a quality-of-earnings report supported by cash proof, covering the trailing 12 months and the last two fiscal years. That is a real cost for a buyer using an SBA loan to purchase a company, and it adds time.

Small loans got a different treatment. According to [Experian](https://www.experian.com/blogs/business-information/2026/07/22/the-sba-sbss-sunset/), the SBA stopped using the FICO Small Business Scoring Service to screen 7(a) Small Loans on March 1, 2026. Lenders must instead run a full credit analysis, including a credit memo, a debt service coverage ratio of at least 1.10x and a review of two months of operating-account statements. Doeren Mayhew's summary likewise notes a repayment analysis based on the two most recent months of bank statements.

American Banker characterizes the October changes as a further tightening of the SBA's credit box. The SBA has framed its own work as delivering volume despite the shutdown, so the two descriptions are not in conflict: volume can be the third-highest on record and still be falling while underwriting gets stricter.

## What it means for borrowers weighing debt options

Guaranteed bank debt is usually the cheapest outside capital a small business can get, which is why the shape of the program matters to anyone also considering venture debt, revenue-based financing or other alternative capital. Three practical points follow from the SBA's numbers and rules.

- **Documentation is the new gating item.** With scoring gone for small loans and quality-of-earnings reports required on larger acquisitions, the file a lender can assemble is now the product. Clean bank statements and a defensible cash-flow story carry more weight.
- **Timing risk is real.** A 43-day shutdown stopped guarantees for nearly two months. A founder whose closing depends on an SBA guarantee should have a fallback, such as a conventional term loan or a lender-funded bridge, if government operations stall again.
- **Acquirers feel it most.** The new quality-of-earnings requirement, which applies at $3 million and up, hits buyers financing a purchase, and those deals are where cash-flow analysis was already hardest.

None of this is a recommendation for or against any financing route. The point is that guaranteed lending has become more document-heavy at the same time its volume has slipped, and alternatives that price risk differently, whether faster and costlier or structured around revenue, tend to look more attractive when the cheapest option gets harder to close.

## What to watch next

Three things are worth tracking. First, whether the SBA's fiscal 2027 monthly activity data, posted on the SBA's data portal and in lender reports, shows loan counts recovering under the new procedures. Second, how lenders respond: a stricter credit box often pushes banks to concentrate on larger, cleaner borrowers, which would leave smaller and newer businesses looking elsewhere. Third, whether the agency publishes final fiscal 2026 figures that differ from this early release.

For now, the cleanest reading is the agency's own: $39.5 billion in 7(a) and 504 guarantees across 62,205 loans, a year interrupted by a shutdown, and a rulebook that asks borrowers to prove more before the loan closes.
